# THE BUCKS FREE PRESS,FRIDAY,AUGUST 25.1939.

# BUCKS TOO GOOD FOR HERTS AT HIGH WYCOMBE

# Baker In Form Again: Franklin Gets Nine Victims

A NOTHER fine innings by L.G. Baker and some fine work behind the stumps by W. B. Franklin, who claimed no fewer than nine victims, were the principal features of the game between Buckinghamshire and Hertfordshire, which was concluded at High Wycombe on Friday.

## W. B. FRANKLIN

On the first day, against some splendid bowling and sparkling fielding, the Herts batsmen could make very little headway. The in-swingers of Lund, in particular, caused trouble to the opening batsmen and he claimed four early wickets. Tee and Roberts, for Herts, made a fair start, but six wickets were down for 96. Butler and Young faced the bowling with more determination and put on 43 runs—the highest stand of the innings. No one seemed capable of staying long, and the innings closed for 134.

Bucks began confidently and started to put runs on at a good pace, but the score had only reached 22 when Franklin returned to the pavilion. Baker and Ratcliffe carried the score to 69, the latter having one good hit over the square leg boundary for six. With Peters in, a much longer stand was made despite frequent changes of bowling.

## Bright Cricket

Peters, who has adopted a more careful style of play this season, was at first content to let the runs come, but having played himself in, he went for the bowling, and with

Baker in a similar mood, runs came with delightful freedom.

The 100 was quickly reached, and the score mounted to 151 when Baker pulled the ball on to his stumps. He had helped Peters to add 82 runs by bright cricket. Walker, who followed, was immediately missed in the slips, but was out low in the same over. Peters was caught at the same total—three wickets down for the addition of only three runs.

Macindoe had a bright 19, but O'Callaghan failed. Batcock, who usually can be depended on for runs in a crisis, carried on with a bright 31, helped in a ninth-wicket stand of 40 by Lund. The innings closed 96 runs on.

## Bowlers On Top

When Herts started their second innings, it was soon evident that the bowlers were on top and wickets began to fall regularly.

Townley batted skilfully, and received support from Butler, who had done very well in the first innings. Fordham had the unenviable distinction of “bagging a brace.”

At this period, three wickets were down for 37. Butler and Townley kept the score moving steadily, but the fielding was so keen that runs did not come easily. Another smart catch at the wicket disposed of Butler.

On the resumption after lunch, Young, one of the heroes of the first innings, was out lbw to Batcock for the addition of only three runs and at 89 Townley was clean bowled by Rutter, who was now finding the pace of the wicket much more to his liking. Appleyard added a few, but the remaining wickets offered feeble resistance, and the innings closed for 107. Thus Bucks were set only 12 runs to get for victory, which they accomplished for the loss of only one wicket. Scores:

## SPORTS GOSSIP By ONLOOKER

## Old Rivals

Buckinghamshire's games with Norfolk at Ascott yesterday and at Norwich last week were well in keeping with the lively tradition of their annual fixtures with their east country neighbours.

No “local derbys” are contested more grimly than these games between Bucks and Norfolk. Bucks have twice wrecked Norfolk's championship hopes.

When Bucks won the championship for the fifth time last season, Norfolk were the only county to score more than 300 runs in one innings against them.

On that occasion D. F. Walker played a splendid innings of 167, but on the second day, it will be recalled, Norfolk only just managed to avoid defeat.

## Norfolk Improving

A week earlier, at Lakenham, Bucks gained a one run lead on the first innings, and Norfolk, needing 143 to win, were dismissed by Rutter and Lund for 91.

Of the 27 matches played between the two counties, Buckinghamshire have won 14 and lost only 5. Three of Norfolk's five wins have been obtained in the last three years. Norfolk's last success was at Lakeinham in 1937, when B. W. Rought-Rought and T. G. L. Ballance each made a century, and Bucks wickets fell so cheaply that Norfolk gained a convincing victory by an innings and 97 runs.

## Triumph For Franklin

Last week's match with Hertfordshire on the London Road ground provided a personal triumph for W. P. Franklin, the Bucks skipper, who claimed no fewer than nine victims behind the stumps. In the course of the game he caught seven and stumped two of the Hertfordshire batsmen.

The Buckinghamshire fielding was again extremely keen, and was certainly an important factor in the team's success.

## Wycombe Men's Claims

A High Wycombe supporter, who signs himself "A Paying Watcher," puts forward in a letter this week, the claim of Wycombe players for recognition in the Buckinghamshire team.

"After seeing the Berks and Herts matches," he writes, "and finding that High Wycombe have only one player in the county side, I must at least express indignation that the county selectors send to us players who are far below High Wycombe standard by the inclusion of Isherwood, O'Callaghan and Walker. The county side otherwise is a very good selection."

## Worth A Trial

" My opinion is that High Wycombe have two other players who could honestly be put forward for inclusion in the county side—Ryan and Frank Floyd. In next year's matches, if he improves a lot, young Tovey might be included.

"Now the question is — Have these players been put forward by the High Wycombe officials? I have made enquiries but cannot find if this has been done. But I do find that thirty people I have discussed this with on the ground are fully of the opinion that they should be given a chance."
