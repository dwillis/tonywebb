# HERTS ADVERTISER: FRIDAY 18 AUGUST 1939-2.

## LAST-WICKET STAND BAVES GAME.

A dogged last-wicket stand by C. J. S. Ffoot and I. W. Houghton saved the Old Berkhamstedians from defeat in their match with the Herts County Club at Bucklands on Friday. W. Brian Smith (48) and R. Is. Eade (73) batted well for the County Club. Wilson, who was severe on W. H. Marsh, hitting him for three sixes, and A. M. Olsson (57) gave the Old Berkhamstelians a good start, but after they had been separated wickets fell quickly, the ninth falling when the Old Boys were 42 in arrears. Ffoot and Houghton, however, defended doggedly to save the game. Scores:—

## BT. ALBANS' FINE VICTORY AT

## LUTON.

St. Albans brought off a capital performance on Saturday when they beat Luton by twenty-one runs at Wardown Park.

St. Albang batted first, but A. S. Hacker (219 and S. H. Rabone (46) were the only players to reach double figures against good bowling, particularly by R. B. Webb (7-6). St. Albang had eight men out for 64, but J. Holt (3 not out) played very steadily to keep his end up while S. H. Rabone added 23 for the ninth wicket.

Luton lost both their opening bats. men at nine. With F. Minshull (3-27), J. Holt (2-27) and S. H. Rahane (1-10) all bowling effectively five wickets were down for 36 and seven men were out for 51. A. J. Worboys aided by C. J. Partnerships tried hard to pull the game round. When they had raised the score to 70, Worboys, who had made 36, was caught by Weatherhead off Lismore (3-10), who finished off the innings for the addition of three runs by taking a return from Webb and getting Emery 1.b.w. ## OLD ALBANIANS' SURPRISE COLLAPSE.

The Old Albanians suffered a sensational one-run defeat on Saturday, when they opposed Shenley Hospital at Porter's Park, Shenley, thanks largely to a good knock of 42 by A. Clarke, totalled 98. M. A. Wigra (36), A. G. Owen (18) and K. A. Griffiths (16) gave the Old Boys a capital start; indeed they needed only nineteen for victory when the fourth wicket fell, but Brown (4-38) and Woodcock (2-21) brought about a surprising collapse. Eight wickets were down for 92. The ninth lifted the total to 97 and two runs were still required when W. Fenton, the last man, went in, but he was caught by Greenhaugh off Brown without addition to the total and Shenley succeeded, in a tense finish, by one run.
