# THE CHRONICLE FRIDAY AUGUST 18 1939

# CRICKET

# BERKSHIRE AGAIN DEFEATED

# AFTER FIRST INNINGS LEAD

## SUFFOLK'S FINE RECORD

Some high scoring was witnessed at Cintra Ground, lent by Messrs. Sutton and Sons, when Berkshire entertained Smolk for the first time at Reading in the Mnor Counties Championship. This was the most county match to take place at Cintra for a number of years. Suffolk helded a strong eleven, including seven players who had had experience of first-class county cricket, among them being Phil Mead, who played for England and Hampshire.

Winn.ng the toss, Berkshire went in to bat on a very easy wicket. Runs came very rapidly at first, but wickets also fell fairly regularly. Stokes was clean bowled by a short-pitched ball from Belle, whilst Wolton was smartly stumped after going forward to a ball from the same bowler. It was not until Timewell and Crosfield came together that anything of a stand was made. They carried the score to 89 before Timewell put up a high catch in the outfield. Crosfield, who had been missed at four, followed soon afterwards, having contributed a very useful 40 to the score. Hubble started well, but played on to his wicket when he seemed to be getting set. The best batting display was given by Cawton, who in his 93 hit five 6's and eight 4's. He punished Daley very severely, hitting him for three sixes and a four off consecutive balls. Once Cawston had been disposed of, however, the Berkshire tail failed to wag, and the last three wickets fell for the addition of only five runs. Perkins had been the most successful Suffolk bowler.

Suffolk were left with an hour to bat before close of play, and they made full use of their time, being only 81 re-behind the Berkshire total with six wicke's in hand. Porter compiled a useful 63 of their total. Mead was out to a brilliant catch by Wolton, who held a ball that was going away from him over his head.

Berkshire bowlers were more successful on Thursday morning, and they disposed of the remaining Suffolk batsmen in a little over an hour. Dedman once again was responsible for the collapse, claiming four of the remaining six wickets. His final analysis figures were four for 62. Altogether, Relt captured five wickets for 73 runs. The Suffolk total of 242 left Berkshire with a first innings lead of 18. Berkshire started their second innings disastrously, Stokes being stumped with only two on the board, and Timewell following pine runs later. Crisfield was the next victim, and the Suffolk bowlers seemed to be definitely on top. Until Hubble hit a four off Daley to make the score 26, ail of the runs had come in singles. Hubble managed to stay in whilst two other wickets fell, but he was bowled eventually by a ball from Perkins, which broke back and took his leg stump. Taylor indulged in some hard hitting, but was given three lives early in his innings, and was eventually bowled by Daley. Relt was caught off the first ball he received. The Berkshire innings closed for 83 runs, and had lasted for only an hour and three-quarters. Perkins had the remarkable bowling figures of six for 15, and altogether had taken 12 wickets for 73 in the match.

With plenty of time in which to compile 102 runs for victory, Suffolk had obtained the runs by the tea interval for the loss of only two wickets. Suffolk, who are at the top of the Minor Counties championship table, have now played six matches, won four, and won one on

## the first innings, there being no result in one game.
