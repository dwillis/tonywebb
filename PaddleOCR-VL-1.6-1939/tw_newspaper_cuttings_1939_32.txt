# Dorset Daily Echo: Wednesday 9 August 1939-2.

# TRIANGLE : DEFEAT PORTSMOUTH SIDE

## J. S. STONE IN FORM WITH THE BAT

In this annual fixture yesterday the Triangle received a good start from J. R. Skinner and J. S. Stone, who put on 38 before Skinner was bowled for 19. Baker (20) helped Stone to carry the score to 63, but three wickets fell for an additional three runs, but Canon Hurley and Stone took the figures to 111 before the latter was well caught on the boundary.

Hurley left at 142 for 30, but J. D. Sansom went on with the good work and had 26 not out when the innings closed for 167. There were few boundaries owing to the soft state of the field.

Portsmouth found the bowling of J_{x} Stone and Andrews hard to get away and the latter being in good form, seven wickets fell for 58. A late stand by Murgatroyd (10) and Elderfield (32) helped the tally along and when Meredith came in he was challenged to hit 6's off the Rector's bowling. He got one and put on 27 in two overs, but then hit over a straight one from his challenger and was bowled for 20 with the total at 137.

After the match Mr. Meredith thanked the Triangle officials for their hospitality, declared that this was one of the annual fixtures they all anticipated with pleasure and hoped it would continue for many years.

# BREWERY CRICKETERS VISIT EXETER

## DORCHESTER TEAM TURN THE TABLES

On Saturday the Dorchester Brewery cricket team visited Exeter to play the local Gas Co., who made a journey to Dorchester in the football season and gave the Brewers a lesson in football. However, on Saturday the tables were turned and the visitors won a very enjoyable game by 41 runs.

The visitors were officially welcomed during the tea interval by Mr. Rhead, the assistant manager. Winning the toss the Brewery decided to put the home team in (incidentally, they are in the final of the Exeter League Shield) and soon found themselves in difficulties, five wickets going down for 12 runs. The whole side were all out in just over an hour for 54 runs. However, the visitors found that runs were not easy to get and lost seven wickets before their opponents' total was passed. When the last wicket fell (both teams played 12 men) the score had reached 94.

## TEAMS ENTERTAINED

Following the match the teams were entertained at the Countess Wear Hotel when Mr. Dart, secretary to the Gas Co., and Mr. II. G. Read, on behalf of the Brewery, spoke of the way in which such fixtures between sports clubs promoted a better understanding between employees and hoped it would be possible for the fixtures between the Gas Co. and the Brewery to continue. In thanking their hosts for the very elaborate way in which the visitors had been looked after, Mr. Read thought they all ought to be very grateful to Mr. Loynes, hon. secretary of the Gas Co. Sports Club, for the very hard work he must have put in to make the visit such a success.

Four members of the bowling section of the Brewery took the opportunity of playing four members of the Gas Co. in the pleasant surroundings of the Exeter City Bowling Club, the game resulting in a win for the visitors by 21 shots to 9.
