When the League had progressed some five or six weeks congratulations were made in this column on the utter absence of drawn matches. The elements, however, during the latter part of the season have caused the drawn column to be a singularly heavy one. Leek heads the drawn list by having failed to complete eight matches out of its thirteen engagements, and had the club been engaged on Saturday last it would have created a record for no club since the institution of the League has drawn nine matches even in the completed season. Bearing this fact in mind, and bids fair to equal its performance of last year when it had against its name the huge number of drawn matches in the League. Although several clubs have only completed two-thirds of their engagements there have been more drawn matches this year than has ever been recorded before in the history of the North Staffordshire and District Cricket League. It must be borne in mind that the action of the League at the commencement of the season which lengthened the time for League matches would have resulted, had fair weather have been the rule instead of the exception, in causing more matches than usual to be played to a finish.

LEEK.
Leek first team had no match at all on Saturday. They were resting after making the record number of single points, with a hope that when they recommence it will run to two points. After the club week they will be able to do with them. Your readers will have noticed that there were no notes from Leek on Saturday. They were written, but the boy who was entrusted with the important mission of conveying them to the station forgot the way. Neither have been heard of since.
Last Saturday was a soaker. It was Bagshaw's benefit, and the match was against our old friends, Buxton, whose faces have not been seen in Leek for a few seasons, but of whose performances this season we offer congratulations. Everything promised a bumping gate, as the Leek team were not playing, and Kirkby, in a most generous manner, assisted his brother professional. But it was the wretched weather that came as the disappointment, a heavy shower coming on just previously to the time fixed for commencing.
The visitors came in grand style in a coach-and-four, and they reached the ground in good time. The spectators were but few, and it showed a good feeling to see so many of the prominent members of the Leek club on the ground. Leek won the toss, and that was about all that was done for an hour; but at last the sun came out, and the Temperance Band played a lively selection, and the game commenced.
Kirkby and Bagshaw took the bowling, but from some cause or other Bagshaw did "not come off," and after bowling some dozen overs, which were very expensive, he was taken off. Kirkby was in grand form, and he obtained five wickets for 30 runs—no mean performance against Buxton. Harry Ball made a mistake in missing Haws when that player had only scored 1 run, as his total was 34. Maddock was a patient bat, and kept up his wicket, but failed to add many to the score-sheet. Woodruff had a short and merry career, out of his hits for 8 was a treat. When eight wickets were down for 86 rain again set in, and the match was abandoned.
Leek Highfield have not yet sustained a defeat this season, and their application for permission to join the North Staffordshire League next season ought to be favourably considered.
Leek Reserve were playing Oakamoor on the Leek ground. Leek scored 61, of which number Dr. Haslehurst very successfully for 50, and G. Tudor 13. When 61 had been obtained for seven wickets, the innings was closed. Oakamoor did not bat long, obtaining 2 runs without the loss of a wicket, when rain prevented any further play.
The Church Club were engaged with Audley on their hundred acre enclosure. The visitors batted first. At least they were the only players who did bat, for when the score was 26 for seven wickets, the gentle rain descended, and the game was abandoned. Out of the 26 J. Leek was responsible for 23.
To-day there will be only one junior match—Leek, and it will be difficult to find eleven players left in the town to do duty, as the usual exodus commenced yesterday, and Leek this week-end could very properly be called "Owt o' notes," all the places of business having closed for a week.

KEELE.
This week your millions of readers will doubt the authenticity of these notes, as they don't bear the usual "at it again" figurehead. But for our friend Jew Peter Pluvius, I have no doubt we should have set up business as usual under the old sign, but as I wrote in my copy-book years and years ago, "the wind is tempered to the shorn lamb." So it was on Saturday, thanks to Jew Peter. For weeks, read Whitmore Rangers (including Whitmore, Silverdale, Newcastle, Porthill, and Madeley) for the shorn lamb read Keele, and the parable is complete. In other words we were in for the usual scorching, but for the rain which damped the match. We won the toss and elected to bat. The Rector and T. Jones opened to Lightfoot (Silverdale) and Maudsley (Porthill). Jones soon retired, Lightfoot being yards too fast for him, and H. Taylor also retired from the same cause. Then Burroughs joined the Rector (who was playing very steadily), and the first change took place, Glover going on vice Maudsley. Then the final change, and the game was over at 5.15. (33 for two wickets.) Burroughs 15 and the Rector 11 (both not out). By some unaccountable means Burroughs played for Keele, when he was put down for Whitmore. If I should just like to employ which the sport is, in bringing a team like Whitmore Rangers brought against a young and inexperienced team like Keele, in which, as a rule, the players are home grown. Truly, as was said in your columns recently, "Sport is dead," but win at any cost seems to be very much alive. At all events, thanks to our umpire and friend "Jew Peter," we gained one point, and he stopped the game at the right time with his watering-pot.
To-day, Norton-in-Hales at Keele.

BIGNALL END.
The above club journeyed to Chell last Saturday to play the return League fixture. We won the toss, and sent the home team in to bat. The match had not proceeded far when the rain came on, we stayed out as long as possible, but had to retire when we had succeeded in dismissing nine of the Chell men out for 59 runs. We were anxiously waiting for about an hour and a half for it to clear up so that we might knock up 60 runs and claim two points, as the fight for the championship is a close one.
Nantwich Second team had no match on and they got the advantage of us, we having to lose a point owing to the rain. That is if we should have won, I think from past experience, and the determination of our players we should have pulled off the match. However, we had to be content with a draw. The rain stopping the match when the two last men were in for Chell. Score 59.
M. Statham bowled excellently, taking six wickets for 12 runs, and J. Dale three for 31. To-day Audley.

CLOUGH HALL.
Rain again interfered with the cricket fixture in the League match against Tunstall on Saturday. Dobson lost the toss, and Tunstall went to the wickets. Bull and Webster faced the bowling of Warburton and Paskin. The wicket was a heavy one, and runs came slowly. None of the visiting batsmen made double figures, and their innings closed for a total of 44 when the rain prevented further play. The averages of the Clough bowling was as follows:—Warburton, 2-6; Paskin, 0-9; Broadhurst, 5-8; and Rothwell, who did remarkably well, 1-25.
To-day the team should play away at Silverdale.
Thursday's match against Tunstall was a disastrous one for the Hall. Tunstall, who batted first, put up a total of 99, and against such a score the chances of the home team were small indeed. The visiting bowlers were too much for the bowlers, and frequent changes were made. The Hall team succeeded in putting up 54—by no means bad. The bowling of Maddock was very destructive.

MARKET DRAYTON.
Rain fell pretty generally in this district on Saturday, and no cricket of note came under my notice. Drayton did turn out in their encounter with Whitchurch, but rain fell so heavily, and the ground became so saturated, that although it cleared up about five it was found impossible to proceed with the game. Drayton were assisted by Mr. J. P. Heywood-Lonsdale. The first three or four batsmen could do nothing against the bowling of Cliff, but J. Taylor (not out 13) and W. G. Stephenson (not out 3) broke the spell of fortune which had favoured the visitors. Stephenson was a grand success to Taylor in keeping up the wicket so splendidly, and not in this case, which we are apt to see in local teams, too eager for run-getting. At this period the game was abandoned, a very heavy storm coming on.
To-day Newport play the return at Drayton.

HIGH LANE.
On Saturday we visited St. Paul's, whose ground is situated in "the lake district." Cricket seemed out of the question when we started to the ground. On our arrival, however, the weather soon cleared up. The home side won the toss, and decided to bat, sending Adams and Owen to face the bowling of Whitehurst and Capey. Wickets fell thus: Two for 2, three for 4, four for 7, five for 7, six for 7, seven for 15, eight for 17, nine for 17 (only ten men batting); total, 19. Whitehurst bowled eleven overs, four maidens, taking seven wickets for 9 runs. Capey took two for 5. Heavy rain fell, and we found another pitch and decided to go on with the game. What a disastrous start! Three wickets went down for 1 run, Wallace catching Foster in the first over, and Cartwright bowling two men in the second over. More rain fell, but the sides agreed to continue the game. Runs came as well as could be expected on such a wicket, and St. Paul's seemed disinclined to go on. Barnett made the winning hit, but the game continued for three or four minutes, when the players could stand the wet no longer, all having a run for the pavilion with wet jackets.

LAWTON PARK.
Both fixtures on Saturday last with Middleport were postponed, owing to the rain. A start was made, but play was impossible, and the game ceased when Middleport had made 21 runs for the loss of five wickets. To-day Congleton play the Park a visit in a friendly encounter.

JUNIOR CRICKET GOSSIP.
Saturday last turned out most unfavourable for cricket, and with one or two exceptions no matches could be brought to a definite conclusion owing to the disastrous weather. It is useless attempting to describe any of the games which commenced, and only one match has reached me to hand where the full time was played out. This was Fenton Second v. Crewe Second. The game took place on the latter's ground, and the homesters had whipped together a powerful side including Evanson, Brannan, Heaps, and Lyon, of the first eleven. The homesters batted first and put together 75. Evanson played well for 25, as did Hamer for 14. George Boultstone was again in evidence with the ball, and captured six wickets for 30 runs, but had the fielding been up to the mark his analysis would have been much better. Boultstone, with his lobs, captured four wickets for 12 runs. I forget to mention that the players on each side numbered twelve. With an hour to get the runs, the visitors showed good form, and were unlucky in not winning, as they put together 66 for six wicks, and were thus robbed of victory, as time was called at six o'clock. Roberts batted splendidly for 27, not out, as did Winkle 15 and Harper 14.
