L A W T O N   P A R K.
This club won its protest against Silverdale, and
the match has to be replayed. As was pointed out
last week it is most important that the measurement
of the ground and pitch should be taken accurately,
and especially in League, Combination, or Alliance
fixtures, so as to avoid the unpleasantness which
arises from disputes. More need not be said regard-
ing the match. Saturday's match was again a
victory for the Park club, which so far has done well
in the Combination, not having lost a match.
To-day, the club meet Middleport, both at home and
away, and it is probable that another success will
have to be recorded.

P O R T H I L L   P A R K.
On Saturday Porthill at Tunstall gave about the
best batting display which they have ever given,
and their score of 152 for three wickets was a per-
formance which reflected credit on all those who
had a share in it. King justified the expectations
of all who have seen his form, and his score of 64
was made without fault. H. F. Wood once again
also proved his real value to his side, as he stayed
with King until the score was 91, of which his
share was 36. The previous week, against Lichfield
Garrick, these two men had made 30 without being
parted, when rain stopped play, so that in the two
games they had made 121, being separated. Almost
a record for North Staffordshire League cricket.
Dean followed in, and soon began to make matters
lively, and until I partially have stayed to help
Bennett some time out for a smart run out on the
part of the latest Tunstall acquisition. His score
of 21 was got in less than eighteen overs, and the
club that gets rid of him for less than 10 runs may be
considered fortunate. The first 120 runs were
scored on the rather small ground in just over the
hour, and then the rate of scoring was reduced some-
what, but that did not suit Bennett, and he paid
little respect to any of the bowlers, and in about
twenty-five minutes made his score of 52, not out, at
which point the innings was closed, leaving Tunstall
more time to bat than the visiting team had taken
to compile their rather formidable score.
Some mention has been made in former editions
to the impression that the Porthill batting sadly
lacked force and run-getting powers. This is
certainly no longer correct, as in last Saturday's
display no opportunity was neglected to score runs,
and the display altogether was singularly free from
fluky strokes. The Tunstall men saved their long
outing well, and the fielding all through was smart,
especially so in some instances.
The Tunstall innings was conspicuous for a very
good innings on the part of Walker, who made 28,
and a prolonged stand for the last wicket by Buckley
and Gater, who took the score from 55 to 102, as
which number Gater offered a chance in the long
field to King, which he promptly accepted. Bennett
accounted for four wickets, and the absence of W. S.
White, who was holidaying, gave King an oppor-
tunity to show that his bowling will be useful if he
is called on, as he also took four wickets.
All old members of the Porthill Club would notice
with pleasure that once again that sterling cricketer
Earnest Walker was back again in their ranks, after
an absence of seven or eight years, and although the
big performances of some of the other players did
not give him a chance on Saturday, there is no
doubt he will not be long in the team before he
makes his presence felt in the old style which so
many Wednesday people recall with pleasure.
It is also interesting to notice with what great
success Bennett made his first appearance in Derby-
shire county cricket this season, his aggregate of
45 and bowling analysis, both being the
best performances of the side v. Hamp-
shire. Porthill also supplied the Staffordshire
wicket-keeper in the person of J. Poole, and the
absence of these two, coupled with the holidays,
caused the team which was sent to Caverswall to be
mainly composed of second eleven men, who on the
whole acquitted themselves well, scoring 134 to
Caverswall 122, and seeing that King was once in a
way dismissed without scoring, the total must be
considered a good one. The Rev. F. W. Haden was
conspicuous by a very well played innings of 39,
other double figures being:—G. Evans 15, J. Dean
15, C. Wood 14, and S. Mawdesley 22 not out. A
very pleasant day was spent at Caverswall, the only
regret being that owing to the holidays so many of
the first team were absent. The reserve team
had a very easy victory on Saturday v.
Audley, scoring 104 for four wickets, and
disposing of their opponents for 39, S. Mawdesley
once again taking his full share of wickets. To-day,
Fenton visit Porthill, and for the first time for four
years, Briscoe plays against Porthill, with what
result may be looked for in another column. Next
Saturday we visit Silverdale, and the week after
the great fight of the season, Burslem at Porthill.

F E N T O N.
[BY "RAMBLER."]
Fenton visited Norton last week for the purpose
of annexing points, and were successful; at least,
this is what I believe. The scores were—Fenton 94,
Norton 56. This ought to entitle Fenton to count
two points. Norton will probably agree that it
does, but others may think otherwise.
Some people have an idea that I supported the
action of Fenton in their match v. Silverdale. How
this is arrived at I cannot imagine. I stated suffi-
ciently plain for an average reader to understand
that I blamed the rules, and that if the rules allowed
one team to "best" another nobody could blame the
players. This is, to my mind, clear enough. I am
of the same opinion now. Those who have seen a
match at Trent Bridge, when Notts have been playing
for a draw will never forget it. How to waste time
is shown to perfection. Players leaving the wickets
for a cup of tea and other such little tricks, disgust
those who go to see sport. The ways in which a
batsmen can waste time are numerous, and the same
remark applies to a bowler. I hold that the
umpires when neutral should be empowered to ex-
tend the time for such period as they consider has
been deliberately wasted.
The game of cricket is one of the oldest on record
which perhaps accounts for the old-fashioned ways
of its legislators. No serious effort is made to
advance with events. This is a mistake which is
made clearly apparent by the actions of the specta-
tors when a player like Stoddart goes in to bat. His
presence acts like magic on a crowd. There is no
"fooling around" about him. He goes in to get
runs not to waste time, and it must
be annoying to a sportsman like him when a bowler
uses the power given him by the rules to waste time
to the disadvantage of the batter.
The members of the Cricket League may think
proper such as I have suggested impracticable, or it
possible say they have no power in the matter.
That's just where the deadlock is. Nobody appears
willing to accept a move. Last year Mr. J. T.
Farrall suggested that (instead of there luck decid-
ing which team should have choice of innings) the
visitors should have the option of batting or fielding.
There was nothing in the world to prevent this being
done , in League matches, but the committee
wouldn't have it. The majority of the com-
mittee preferred to trust to luck to assist in
winning matches. Winning the toss has more to do
with a Saturday afternoon match than most people
imagine, and if a captain is fortunate enough to
guess the upper side of a penny in both matches he
stands a good chance of securing four points, or at
the least the opportunity of making his opponents
take the field the best portion of the time.
Luck is an element which ought not to be counted
upon as an assistance to secure victory, and although
not a thought-reader, I venture to think that 99 out
of every 100 spectators will agree with what I say.
On a bitterly cold day six or eight thousand people
will put in an appearance at Stoke to see a good
football match, because they know that they will get
a full hour and a half's sport. Waste of time is not
permitted there, and luck is reduced to a minimum.
The cleverest team as a rule wins the game, and
that is a lot more than can be said about present-day
cricket.
Having concluded the portion which was to be
continued, as per last week's issue, I will now revert
to the Fenton v. Norton match. Somehow or other,
no matter how badly Fenton is shaping against
other clubs, they rise to the occasion against Norton.
Last year Mount went with a damaged optic, and
although unfit to play he scored more runs than the
Norton team put together. On Saturday the team
was a sort of straggling. Winning the
toss, the usual pair took their places at
the wickets. Merritt played in his usual
careful style, and Turner applied the wood hard and
often, and had 16 to his credit when a separation
came. Briscoe made things lively, but eventually
stopped a ball with his foot, and retired at the
request of the umpire, with as much satisfaction as
is usual in such cases. Skelson, who has been study-
ing how to get ducks' eggs, unless, and other things
whilst in training at Lichfield, soon got an "outer,"
and the same remark applies to W. Turner and
Povey. Walton made a single, and things were
looking rosy for Norton, but Dean, Birks and
Rhodes altered the complexion of the game, the
score at the close reading 94.
Birks is to be complimented upon his success. Fate
has for some time been against the cheery Albion
batsman, but now a turn has been made all
Fentonians hope it will be a long way to the nearest
stopping place.
Norton commenced badly against the deliveries of
Turner and Briscoe, catches for once in a way being
accepted. Boulton was the only man on the side
to get into doubles, and his score shows what a
useful man he is. The end came with the total at
56, Fenton winning comfortably at the finish.
On Wednesday a fairly representative team
visited Caverswall, the paradise of cricketers, to
oppose Mr. W. E. Bowers' eleven. It was a day
out for the Turners, who put on 50 runs in about
half-an-hour, J. having 59 to his credit and W. 15.
Rhodes chipped in with a useful 15 and the other
eight divided a total score between them, the
respective portions varying from nothing to 7.
The home captain, assisted by Aynsley, Morgan,
and Booth, passed the Fenton score, stumps being
drawn before J. Morgan, Taylor, Young, and Blair
had a chance of showing what they could do. The
outing, as usual, was much enjoyed, and some of the
players are already consulting an almanac in the
hopes of finding out when Whit Week is due in
1896.

MOUNT PLEASANT.
On Saturday we went to Birches Head to meet
Tabernacle Mission. The Missionites batted first,
facing the bowling of Edwards and Rosamond. T.
Prince made a good stand, being finally caught by
Burdett after making 21 runs. The last wicket fell
for 72 runs.
On our side A. Smith and W. Edwards were in
good form, the former hitting in splendid style, and
making 52 runs, which included three 6's. The
innings closed for 80 runs, and we won by 8 runs.
Our second team received the Y.M.C.A. at Mount
Pleasant. The visitors won the toss, and had to thank
Hardwick and Salmon for their victory. Hardwick
made 34 and Salmon 11 runs out of a total of 61.
Hawkin's bowling was deadly, and Hardwick gave
four catches, but was missed each time. Our men
made a good stand. Phillips (18), Donkin (7), and
Simister (8) were the best. The innings closed with
the total at 51. It was a close game throughout,
and thoroughly enjoyed.
On Whit-Monday we were picked to play against
Hanley Tabernacle. Owing to the holidays only
three of our first team turned up, and consequently
we made a poor show. The Tabernacle ground was
in a fearful condition, and spoke results : Hanley
Tabernacle 55 (of which W. Richards made 23) ;
Mount Pleasant 9.

L E E K.
Lovers of cricket were well catered for last week
and the beginning of this week in Leek. Both the
Leek and the Leek Highfield teams had very good
engagements on Saturday, and they were both well
patronised, the attendances being about equal. A very
childish practical joke was carried out either in the
very late hours of the evening of Friday week or
the early hours of Saturday morning, and was
intended to do damage to the Leek Highfield
Cricket Club. Over almost all the posters of the
Highfield club, announcing their match against
Crewe, a slip of paper was affixed, having upon them
the words "Bath buns and milk gratis." Whoever
was the joker may take all the credit he may think
reflects upon him and be welcome to it.
LEEK CLUB.
Mr. Brunt and the other members of the executive
of the Leek Club are to be congratulated on the
opening on Saturday last of what I unhesitatingly
say is one of the best grounds in North Stafford-
shire. The attendance on the ground on Saturday
last was a large one, although the weather was
threatening. The arrangement for the accommo-
dation of the spectators was all that could be
desired, but the representatives of the press had to
sit outside, and rain falling several times made this
very unpleasant. I understand that a pavilion is to
be erected at a cost of about £350, probably the
press will be considered.
The captain of the Crewe team won the toss,
and made a great mistake in allowing the Leek
team to bat first on a good wicket, and they found
it out before the game was over. Brunt and Dr.
M'Clew batted first, and the doctor from the first
ball scored a single. Both batsmen played a safe
game, and the bowling was run on both Peters and
Henshall being taken off before a wicket was taken.
This good start had a visible effect upon the company,
the ladies, of whom there were a good number present,
congratulating the gentlemen upon the successful
start, and afternoon tea was soon indulged in, there
being an ample supply of all kinds of provisions.
Brunt was unfortunately out leg before, but
Kirkby played a splendid game, and soon ran up a
number of runs. The Doctor was caught out, and
Wardle was soon disposed of without any addition
to the score. Wright and Kirkby got together and
runs came rapidly, the visitors having now found out
their mistake in putting Leek in first. Wright played
a vigorous game, but Riley, who was bowling in
first rate style bowled him with a grand ball.
Kirkby ought to have obtained his 50 runs, but
Peters with a marvellous catch disposed of him for
45. He had played a good game. Backwell fool-
ishly ran himself out, and the innings closed with a
total of 111. Riley was the most successful bowler,
taking seven wickets for 38.
Crewe commenced with Osborne and Lyon, to the
bowling of A. H. Wardle and Kirkby. Osborne
was badly let off, Kirkby missing hitting the wickets
but he retrieved this mistake by having him caught
off the bowling by Cantrill. Rain then commenced
to fall rapidly, and it was a fortunate thing for
Crewe, as they had four good wickets down for 30.
A Notts eleven were the players on Monday and
Tuesday at Westwood-lane. It seems that there
had been some misunderstanding as to the right of
the visitors to be styled an eleven of Notts, and the
secretary of the Notts County team had been corre-
sponded with. Whoever they were they proved
themselves good players. They provided two days
good cricket. The attendance on both days must
have cheered the hearts of the Executive. Monday's
attendance was like that in the old time, when
Ashcombe Park used to play Leek. The ladies were
present in goodly numbers, and the bright colours
they wore added to the aspect.
There was one disappointment, and that was the
late start. The visitors had missed their train at
Stoke, and only arrived in time for luncheon.
A start was made at two o'clock, and it was soon
seen that the Leek team were in for an afternoon's
fielding. Goodacre and Whitlock played steady
cricket, and completely mastered the bowling. The
left-hander was, however, run out, the score standing
at 39. Bennett, who has played with Notts County,
joined Goodacre, and again the Leek bowling
was punished. Bennett has a very free style with him.
He obtained three boundaries, and the second
wicket which was smartly taken by Cantrill behind
the wickets fell for 75; Shelton, who was responsible
for the team visiting Leek, partnered Bennett, and
the score was taken to 91, when Wardle clean
bowled Bennett. Warne did not remain long. He
hit a 6 and a three, and Wright then displaced his
wicket. The innings closed for 146. The Leek
team had tried eight bowlers out of the team, and
Wardle and Wright were the most successful.
Leek commenced with Messrs. Heaton and Jones.
The former was soon out, but Jones and Kirkby
played good cricket, and the score was taken quickly
up. Jones played an exceptionally good innings,
some of his strokes being very pretty. Kirkby was
out leg before, and Wright did not stay long, the
day's play ending with 52 for three wickets. Jones
was not out 25, and the Rev. J. Darling not out 3.
The state of the game brought down a good number
of spectators on Tuesday, but the day's play was not
so interesting, the Leek batting falling off consider-
ably. Vickerstaff and C. Wardle were the highest
scorers, the total realising 126. The visitors replied
with 124 for eight wickets, and the game was drawn.
Whitlock retired 32 and Bell 35. A feature of the
game was the clever wicket-keeping of Cantrill.
LEEK HIGHFIELD.
This team were on Saturday at Rocester, and
right well they acquitted themselves. They had
their genial captain with them, with whose name
cricket in Leek has been so long associated.
Whether it was this reason or not the score
obtained by the Moorlanders was a grand one—212.
The veteran Heathcote was responsible for
22, and this is the best reply he could have
made to the inquiry as to whether he was past
cricket. The two most prominent bats were H.
Tatton (62) and Barnett (not out, 57). There will
want a good show of presumption to best the old Leek
club if this sort of scoring is to be continued. J.
Stonehewer obtained 34. The home team did not
bat, and it was perhaps as well.
