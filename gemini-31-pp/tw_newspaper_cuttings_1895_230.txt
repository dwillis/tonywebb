BERROW'S WORCESTER JOURNAL: SATURDAY 13 JULY 1895-2

WORCESTER CATHEDRAL SCHOOL v. MR. T. DAVIES'
XI.—This match was played on Saturday on the
School ground, and resulted in a win for the School
by 24 runs. The visitors went in first but save for
Messrs. P. P. Williams and R. W. Holland none made
any stand, and the innings closed for 47. The School,
with the help of a well-played innings of 27 of J. S
Liddell, and 15 by K. A. Sercombe, soon topped the
visitors' score, and eventually the score reached 72.
The second innings of the scratch team began
disastrously, as seven wickets were down for only 14
runs, but T. F. Rammell raised the score somewhat
before he had unfortunately to retire. Of the School
bowlers J. K. Deakin did best with an analysis of
seven wickets for 17. Score:—

ST. JOHN'S v. BROMSGROVE.—This match was
played at St. John's on Saturday, a capital game
resulting in favour of St. John's by 37 runs. Broms-
grove won the toss, but Mr. Beach Hicks and T. Straw
both bowled so well, and were so ably supported by
capital fielding, that the visitors were all disposed of
for 33 runs, Straw taking five wickets for 7 runs. St.
John's on taking the bat soon lost four wickets,
through the excellent bowling of Rolleston, for 15
runs, but on Mr. Young joining Mr. Curtis an in-
valuable stand was made, and, thanks to their excel-
lent batting, the score was taken to 61 before the next
wicket fell, Mr. Curtis playing capital cricket for his
16, and Mr. Young an equally good innings for 17.
Mr. Park also hit well for his score, and the innings
closed for 70. Rolleston bowled splendidly, and
Messrs. J. K. and R. Perkins shared the bowling at
the other end, and were well backed up by the excel-
lent fielding. Score:—

WORCESTER GRAMMAR SCHOOL v. HARTLEBURY
GRAMMAR SCHOOL.—Played at Flag Meadow on
Saturday. Hartlebury batted first and were all sent
back for 20. Of these Vale, who was top scorer with
nine, played very steadily. Thorp took eight wickets
for ten runs. Worcester's prospects did not look very
bright when two good wickets went for one bye, but
on Davis and Thorp being associated runs came more
briskly, and their opponents' total was passed with
four wickets down. Ultimately the whole side was
dismissed for 54. Davis playing very well for his 15.
Vale and Dorsett both bowled very well for Hartle-
bury. Worcester Grammar School have now played
nine, won six lost three. Score:—

HANLEY CASTLE GRAMMAR SCHOOL v. WORCESTER
CATHEDRAL VOLUNTARY CHOIR.—The return match
between these teams was played at Hanley Castle on
Saturday, and resulted, as in the first match, in a
victory for the Grammar School. The Choir batted
first, on a good pitch, but wickets fell rapidly before
the excellent bowling of Chadd, who took seven
wickets for 7 runs, and Hemus who took three
wickets for 4 runs. The innings realised 15 runs.
Chadd and Pope started well for the Grammar
School, making 22 runs for the first wicket. Hemus
and Hargraves also batted in good form against the
somewhat weak bowling of the Choir. After tea,
which was served on the ground, Chadd continued
batting in excellent style, and carried out his bat for
71 runs. Stumps were drawn at 6.30, when the
School had made 123 runs for six wickets. Score:—

CATHEDRAL GUILD v. BARBOURNE COLLEGE.—Played
at Diglis on Saturday. The Guild, with a weak eleven
in the field, managed to inflict a defeat on the College.
Winning the toss the Guild elected to bat, and com-
piled the respectable score of 91, Messrs. Wall, Evans,
A. Smith, and Bartlett being the chief contributors.
The College could only score 37, Potter compiling a
useful 14. Smith bowled well for the Guild.
