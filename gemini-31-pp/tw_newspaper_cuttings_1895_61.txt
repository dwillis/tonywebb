Birkenhead News : SATURDAY 14 SEPTEMBER 1895 - 2.

CRICKET NOTES.
Saturday saw the practical conclusion of the
season, the leading local clubs all finishing
their list of engagements. The Premier club
should have played Huyton, but as the latter's
ground was nothing short of a quagmire after
the heavy rain of the previous evening, the
match had perforce to be abandoned. It is
some time back now since the Parkites have
had such a successful season as the one just
concluded, as their position at the head of the
district competition readily testifies. Indepen-
dent, however, of their excellent record against
the district clubs, they have done more than
well against such powerful opponents as those
of Manchester, Leyland and Wigan, the latter
being beaten in both their engagements with
the Park club. It will be seen on reference to
the batting and bowling averages that T. R.
Hubback heads the list in both departments of
the game, a position which indicates what an
invaluable acquisition he has been to his club
with both bat and ball, independent of the great
service he has rendered behind the wicket.
R. Fielding Ould has secured the second
position in the batting list, being closely
followed by H. J. Fish, who, owing to a severe
illness has been prevented from taking part
in several important matches. On the whole
skipper Holden can hardly be said to have
played up to the form exhibited by him for
several previous years, and it looks a trifle
curious to see his name so low down
in the list as fourth, but still an average
of 31.11 for 26 innings is by no means bad, and
besides this he has rendered excellent service
with the ball, having captured 45 wickets at an
average cost of 9.20. Indeed he has seldom
bowled better than he has done this season,
and he is to be congratulated on his capital
record with the sphere. H. M. Grayson comes
out fairly well with an average of 19.1 for 16
innings, but Smith shows a great falling off as
compared with last year, his figures being 16.3
for 19 ventures. Town has done a little
better, he having an average of 18 for 13 inn-
ings, whilst others who figure rather low down,
but who may be confidently expected to do
much better in the future, include H. W. P.
Bennette, H. Salmon, and H. S. Cochrane.
That good old stonewaller, A. B. Wilson, does
not appear to have exercised his usual discre-
tion, for he comes very near to being the
"absolute," this unenviable position being
occupied by Captain Byrne. The brunt of the
bowling has of course being borne by Smith,
who has taken 88 wickets for 10.9, whilst C. E.
Steen's 48 have been secured at a rate of 13.7.
Town has had a singularly unfortunate season,
as although he has bowled uniformly well he
has been able to get but few wickets, 34 at a
cost of 22.11 being rather expensive.
The Rock Ferryites concluded a fairly suc-
cessful season on Saturday with a win over
Cheadle Hulme. Of the 25 matches they have
played, 11 have been won, 6 drawn, and 7 lost,
whilst one had the almost unique result of a tie.
A glance at the batting averages shows E. F.
Kearsey at the head of affairs with the nice
figures of 30.88 for nine innings, and it seems
rather a pity that his services are not more often
available for first team matches. A. J. Cripps
comes next, but his average compares unfavour-
able with that of Kearsey, there being a con-
siderable difference between 30.88 and 23,
albeit Cripps has played two more innings.
With the exception of his captain, A. Stott,
who is placed third, has taken part in more
matches than any other player in the team, and his
21.13 for 24 innings must be set down as dis-
tinctly creditable. G. G. Paton has an average
of 20.4 for 14 innings, whilst immediately fol-
lowing him comes "Capting" Ravenscroft who,
on the whole, has been far from fortunate, re-
peated failures reducing his figures to as low as
19.79 for 26 innings. Another batsman who
has not shown to great advantage this season is
T. R. Bretherton, whose figures of 18.78 for 20
attempts are not as good as might be expected
from such a capable willow wielder, whilst
the average of A. C. Blain is also a little disap-
pointing, this usually remarkably vigorous
batsman being comparatively low down in the
list with 18.33 for 18 innings. Needham's figures
are 17.28 for 23 attempts, the next in order of
merit being A. C. Bamford, who for 16 innings
has an average of 14.46. Surprisingly low down
is H. S. Bamford who, however, has not devoted
much time to the game, having only been at the
wickets on six occasions, for which he has an
average of 10.14. J. Bretherton, E. Stott, G.
B. Paterson, and E. C. Rylands are also allotted
places in the list, the last being Ryland
who, out of 14 innings, has been not out
no less than seven times, and would probably
have had a much higher average than 4.14 if he
had been given a chance of showing his
abilities with the bat. If he has not proved
himself a brilliant batsman, however, there can
be no question about his success with the ball,
for during the season he has captured no fewer
than 113 wickets at the moderate rate of 11.03
per wicket. J. Bretherton, though bowling
well, falls far behind Ryland, the fast
bowler only crediting himself with 83 wickets
at a cost of 12.36. With such strong amateur
bowling talent at its disposal, the club was not
required to call upon the services of Needham to
any great extent, but the pro has been very
useful at times, and has secured 26 wickets at
an average cost of 17.19.
Though the Oxtonites have been under a
cloud for the last two or three weeks, they
cannot be said to have fared badly on the
whole, as out of 28 matches played they have won
9 and drawn 13, being absolutely beaten on but
five occasions, one, the match referred to in my
remarks about Rock Ferry, resulting in a tie. On
Saturday, at Aigburth, they met with another
serious reverse, receiving a shocking trouncing
at the hands of the Liverpool representatives,
who disposed of them for 71, and knocked up
133 for the loss of seven wickets. The Oxton-
ites thus ended the season with three successive
losses. Their batting averages reveal the
fact that M. Court has not only scored
the most runs, but he has also the
best average, his figures being 32.67 for 27
innings, a really excellent record for this very
promising young player. The second position
on the list is filled by J. H. F. Grayson, with
27.87 for nine innings, but now that this
undoubtedly fine batsman has settled down and
definitely thrown in his lot with the Oxtonites,
we may expect to see a marked improvement in
his figures next year. Another old Park player
takes third honours, this being W. Turner, who
has developed into one of the mainstays of the
Oxton batting, this being evident by his
excellent average of 25.67 for 25 innings. The
Oxton skipper, C. F. Hutton has been a little
off colour occasionally during the season, and
has not scored quite so prolifically as he has
done in former years, his highest contribution
in an innings being 52, and his average 20.38
for 15 ventures. That the new pro. Bishop
will be a great acquisition to the Oxtonites next
season there can be but little doubt, for during
the short time he has been engaged he has done
wonderfully well with both bat and ball, having
a batting average of 19.29 for 9 innings, whilst
with the ball he has secured 48 wickets at a
cost of 9.4 per wicket. J. Curwen has scored
better and more consistently than he has done
for a considerable time, and his record of 19.12
for 20 innings indicates that his powers as a
batsman have not yet altogether left him. H.
T. Hibbert has only taken part in 12 matches,
for which his figures are 16.17, whilst for 19
innings M. Field has an average of 14.22.
Through an injury received early on in the
season, Smith was unable to take as active a
part in the campaign as could be wished, and
for his 12 innings he is rather low down on the
list with 13.52. H. Bickersteth has played
in ten matches, and has scarcely been so
successful in taking out his bat as he has been
in former seasons, consequently his average has
suffered somewhat, and his figures of 10.86 are
not calculated to make him unduly conceited.
G. H. Court, B. S. Smith, and P. L. Bromnell
are all close together with figures ranging from
10.33 to 8.18, L. Court bringing up the rear
with 3.86 for 2 innings. Bowling honours are
divided between amateurs and professionals,
Bishop coming out well with 41 wickets at a
cost of 9.4. Smith has proved a trifle more
expensive his figures being 63 for 9.62, but his
record is an excellent one for all that, more
especially in view of the accident he met with,
before referred to. G. H. Court has also
rendered splendid service with the ball,
capturing no less than 49 wickets, whilst
his younger brother L. Court has given signs of
future usefulness by securing 12 wickets for
3.86 per wicket.
Like that of Oxton, the Birkenhead Victoria
first string have been faring badly of late, and
also like Oxton they concluded their campaign
on Saturday with a disastrous defeat, the Bootle
representatives, who have done anything but
well themselves this year, achieving an easy
victory over the evidently disorganised Victorites,
who made a very feeble show with the bat, and
were all sent to the rightabout for 71. On the
whole the Victoria representatives can hardly
be congratulated on their season's record, for
out of 25 matches played they have only won
four, whilst 9 have been lost and 12 drawn. It
is not surprising to see J. Wylde at the head of
the batting averages with 25.14, for he has shown
consistent good form throughout the season.
Others who may be said to have done well are
W. T. Davies (22.14), F. Wright (19.4) W. H.
Routledge (18.7) and J. Macdonald (17.3), but
a falling off is noticeable in the batting of J.
Brocklebank, who is very low down on the list
with 14.6, though he has played in more matches
than any of his colleagues. J. Fish has been a
distinct acquisition with the leather and heads
the bowling averages with 27 wickets for a trifle
over 11 runs per wicket. So far as taking
wickets is concerned, however, J. Brocklebank
is an easy first, he having captured 42 at a cost
of 14.32. Several others of the team have had
a trial with the ball, these including J. West,
S. Wright, M. Sprat, J. Wylde, E. H. Carter,
and W. H. Routledge.
