OXFORD TIMES: SATURDAY 24 AUGUST 1895 - 4.

COUNTY CUP.
O.C.C.C. v. VICTORIA G.C.—This tie in the semi-final
round of the above competition was played before a
large number of spectators on the Hertford ground on
Saturday last. The City "skipper" was quite justified
in putting his opponents in first. The wicket just
suited W. S. Case, and in a little over an hour he had
got the side out, and made the remarkable analysis of
nine wickets for 22. As 50 was all the Victoria set
the City to win, the match looked all over bar shout-
ing; but greatly to the delight of the Victoria sup-
porters, Freeborn and Nutt were in their best form, and
four of the best of the City wickets were captured for
only 10 runs. Here, however, their successes ended,
for Moulder and A.W. Roberts made a brilliant stand,
and for exactly 100 minutes defied all the bowling
brought against them. Moulder's innings of 84 was
by far the best he has ever played for the City Club,
and with the exception of one chance in the long field
was quite faultless. The fielding of each team left a
lot to be desired, although up to the time their score
had been passed the fielding of the Victoria was very
clean. Play commenced at 1.30, when the home team,
having won the toss, decided to put their opponents in
to bat, on a drying wicket. Welbury and New were
the first pair of batsmen, and the attack was opened
by Case (pavilion end) and Goold, and in the former's
second over he got Welbury caught at the wicket, the
score being 13. With Freeborn in, New drove Case to
the boundary, and cut Goold for 4 and 3, but at 26
fell to a catch at slip. Gray played a ball into his
wicket at 30, and with only 4 added, both Ridgway
and Roberts were clean bowled by Case. Freeborn was
batting just an hour for 10 runs, but nobody else stayed
long, and the venture closed for 49. Case secured
9 wickets for 22 runs, a splendid performance. The
innings of the home team opened in a very sensational
manner. Freeborn and Nutt were the bowlers, and
when only 3 runs had been scored, Case was secured
in the long field. The score was gradually taken
towards double figures, but before that was effected
Sheard succumbed to a "yorker" from Nutt, and at 10
Freeborn got rid of Day and Phillips. Here A. W.
Roberts joined Moulder, the latter cut each bowler
twice for a trio, and Roberts got Nutt to leg for a
couple. At 29 Hewer relieved Nutt, and in his second
over Moulder was missed in the long field by New, a
mistake which proved very expensive to the Victoria.
At 32 Gray superseded Freeborn, and at 40 Hewer gave
way to Roberts. Moulder hit Gray twice for 3, and
Roberts for a couple, and then with a snick to the
boundary passed the Victoria total with 6 wickets in
hand. Nutt and Freeborn resumed bowling at 52, and
Moulder drove the latter for 2 and 3 in his first over,
and then hit Nutt to leg for 5, while Roberts, after a
long period of inactivity, cut him for a like number.
Moulder placed Freeborn twice through the slips for 3,
and on-drove him to the boundary. This brought on
New at 78, and each batsman got him away to leg for
4 in his first over. Moulder drove Nutt for 2 and 4,
which caused him to give way to Gray. Roberts drove
him each side the wicket for 4, and played him to leg
for a couple, bringing up the century at 3.50. At 103
Weller was given a trial, but Moulder cut him twice
to the boundary and drove him for 2 from his first
three balls, and in his second over Roberts cut him for
4 and 3. He shortly afterwards had his revenge, for
at 130 he got Roberts l-b-w for an invaluable innings
of 34. The pair had added 120 for the fifth wicket in
an hour and forty minutes. With G. A. Roberts in,
Moulder drove Weller for 3 and three 2's, but when
only 6 runs short of the century, he returned a ball to
Weller. He had been batting just two hours, and his
splendid innings of 94 included eight 4's, ten 3's, and
thirteen 2's. Goold, who filled the vacancy, hit each
bowler to the boundary, but then was smartly caught
by Roberts, and Dickenson arrived. G. A. Roberts
hit Weller to leg for 4 and 2, and Dickenson got him
away in the same position for 3, and drove him for 2,
which brought on Nutt at 160. Dickenson cut him
for 5, while G. A. Roberts got 4 from the other end.
At 177 Dickenson was easily caught and bowled, and
Pike came in, but after Roberts had cut his namesake
to the boundary, was clean bowled without scoring,
and Weippert being absent, the innings closed for 183,
leaving the home team winners by 134 runs. Score
and analysis:—

COWLEY v. THAME.—This match came off on Satur-
day, in splendid weather, and before a large
number of spectators. Cowley were fortunate enough
to win the toss, and decided to bat first. P. Rogers
and Arnold opened the match, and so well did they
bat that 55 runs were scored before the first wicket
fell, Arnold being caught at the wicket, after playing
a very steady innings for 25. P. Rogers on being
joined by P. King still played very correct cricket for
his 31 runs. Among the others who batted well were
A. Rogers, who hit up 40 in very smart fashion, W.
King (21), P. King (14), and C. Walker (12) also played
sound cricket. The Cowley batting was most consis-
tent all through the game, and not until they had
reached the very respectable total of 181 runs
were they all out. The bowling of A. Rogers, P.
Rogers, and W. King was first class, and the Thame
men were all out for 61, leaving Cowley winners by
100 runs. The only Thame batsmen to reach double
figures were W. Wells (11), W. Rowell (25), and G.
Holloway (16), Mr. Rowell especially batting well for
his runs. Score and analysis:—

CITY CUP.
CORPORATION (holders) v. CLARENDON PRESS.—
This match, played on Saturday, proved most exciting.
The Press batted first, and with the help of good play
by Simms and Eddles put together 89. Nash bowled
very finely, taking 9 wickets for 30. The Corporation
could do little till Jennings appeared, but notwith-
standing determined play by him and Kimber 9
wickets were down for 85. Tanner and Thomas proved
equal to the occasion and won the match by 6 runs.

game was played on the Oxford County School ground
on August 15, the home team being victorious by 67
runs. For the winners W. A. Wells batted finely,
