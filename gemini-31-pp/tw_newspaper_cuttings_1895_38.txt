WYCOMBE RESERVES v. WEST WYCOMBE.
This match was played at West Wycombe on Saturday
afternoon. The visitors went to the wicket first, but offered a
very feeble resistance to the bowling of Turner and Fletcher,
who each took five wickets for 10 runs. The home side, owing
to the good batting of Turner, J. Harman, and Fletcher,
reached a total of 79. For the Reserves Keen bowled in good
form, taking six wickets for 28 runs. In their second attempt
the Reserves did better, and passed their former score before
the third wicket fell ; but their total was not high enough to
avert defeat, as it left the home side only 32 to get to win,
which they accomplished with the loss of four wickets.

HIGH WYCOMBE v. WOOBURN INSTITUTE.
This match, which was played on the Wycombe ground last
Saturday afternoon, resulted in a draw that, if time had
permitted, would have ended in favour of the home team.
The visitors were without Barber, the Lano'ns, and others
who generally play for the Institute; but they had the
assistance of H. Bacon, who proved a tower of strength to
them in both departments of the game. Wycombe did not
play their full strength, more than half their number being
reserve men. The home side went in first, and a total of 156
was reached before they were all disposed of. The chief con-
tributor to this score was F. George, whose figures included a
4, six 3's, and seven 2's. Other good innings were played by
Bird, H. George, and Page who drove one of Bacon's deliveries
out of the field for 6. A word of praise ought to b given to
Raffety among the younger members of the team, who played
well for 21 ; his principal hits being a 4, three 3's, and three
2's. Bacon was the most successful in bowling, his eight
wickets costing a trifle over six runs apiece, and the las three
being obtained without a run being scored off him. For
Wooburn Institute, Bacon, Bowler, and Plumridge played
well, the second named hitting G. George for a 6. When
stumps were drawn the visitors had made 129 for seven
wickets, the last three of which had put on but eight runs
between them. Page was the most successful with the ball,
but his four wickets cost nearly 12 each. Score and

MARLOW v. MR. J. MONRO WALKER'S XI.
On Saturday Marlow met an eleven got together by Mr. J
Monro Walker, of Bisham Abbey, on the Marlow ground
The weather was delightfully fine, and an enjoyable game
resulted. The visitors won the toss, and took first innings.
Harbottle hit hard for 39, and with smaller contributions
from all the side except Lane (who was run out before he had
scored), the total reached 153. Harbottle hit a 9 (four for an
overthrow) and four 4's. Monro Walker was credited with
an 8 (four for an overthrow). Marlow lost Paine and Faulkner
cheaply, but with useful scores from Langley, Bower, Baillie,
and Want, the visitors' total was passed with six wickets
down. Eventually the innings closed for 183. Want--one of
the Australian visitors staying in Marlow--hit a 5, six 4's,
and three 3's in his dashing score of 48. Langley hit five 4's
in his 30. Mr. Walkers team went in a second time, and had
compiled 113 for six wickets, when stumps were drawn.
Marlow therefore won by 30 runs on the first innings. Full
score and analysis :-

WYCOMBE BELLE VUE WANDERERS v. HOLLOWAY'S
BOOT OPERATIVES C.C.
The return match between the above-named teams, which
took place on the Wycombe Rye last Saturday, excited great
interest. Mr. E. Coleman, who "skippered" the Wanderers,
turned out a formidable eleven, while the shoemakers were
but poorly represented, in fact at the last moment they had
to make up with five substitutes. The men of "wax and
leather" batted first, and ran up a creditable total of 66 :
Wilks, Humphrey, Collier, Lillyman, and Coles, contributing
their respective scores in fine style. Tibbles, with the ball, was
in rare form, his four wickets only costing a fraction over 4
runs a-piece ; Anger took 2 wickets for 20. The time even-
tually arrived for the Wanderers' turn at the wicket, Coltman
and Treove opening the others against the destructive
deliveries of Humphrey and Mead. Wickets 1 and 2 went
down for 16, and at this juncture it looked as though the
Wanderers' contingent would collapse, six wickets being
captured for 20, the last three of which not adding a run.
The score rose slowly, and when Thorne went in the
Wanderers' hopes revived, the great slogger piling on his 15
in double quick time. The end eventually arrived with the
total at 54, which left the operatives winners on the first
innings by 12 runs. Both Mead and Humphrey, who bowled
throughout the game, captured 4 wickets each for 26 runs
a-piece. The shoemen went in a second time, and at the close
of the venture 31 runs had been added for the loss of seven
wickets. After the match the teams heartily enjoyed a social
at the "Belle Vue," Gordon-road. Score :-
