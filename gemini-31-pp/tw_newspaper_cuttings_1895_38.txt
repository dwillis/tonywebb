WYCOMBE RESERVES v. WEST WYCOMBE.
This match was played at West Wycombe on Saturday afternoon. The visitors went to the wicket first, but offered a very feeble resistance to the bowling of Turner and Fletcher, who each took five wickets for 10 runs. The home side, owing to the good batting of Turner, A. Harman, and Fletcher, reached a total of 79. For the Reserves Keen bowled in good form, taking six wickets for 23 runs. In their second attempt the Reserves did better, and passed their former score before the third wicket fell; but their total was not high enough to avert defeat, as it left the home side only 32 to get to win, which they accomplished with the loss of four wickets. HIGH WYCOMBE v. WOOBURN INSTITUTE.
This match, which was played on the Wycombe ground last Saturday afternoon, resulted in a draw that, if time had permitted, would have ended in favour of the home team. The visitors were without Barlow, the Lunnons, and others who generally play for the Institute; but they had the assistance of H. Bacon, who proved a tower of strength to them in both departments of the game. Wycombe did not play their full strength, more than half their number being reserve men. The home side went in first, and a total of 156 was reached before they were all disposed of. The chief contributor to this score was F. George, whose figures included a 6, six 3's, and seven 2's. Other good innings were played by Bird, G. George, and Page who drove one of Bacon's deliveries out of the field for 6. A word of praise ought to be given to Raffety among the younger members of the team, who played well for 21; his principal hits being a 4, three 3's, and three 2's. Bacon was the most successful in bowling, his eight wickets costing a trifle over six runs apiece, and the last three being obtained without a run being scored off him. For Wooburn Institute, Bacon, Bowler, and Plumridge played well, the second named hitting G. George for a 6. When stumps were drawn the visitors had made 129 for seven wickets, the first three of which had put on but eight runs between them. Page was the most successful with the ball, but his four wickets cost nearly 12 each. Score and analysis:—

MARLOW v. MR. J. MONRO WALKER'S XI.
On Saturday Marlow met an eleven got together by Mr. J Monro Walker, of Bisham Abbey, on the Marlow ground The weather was delightfully fine, and an enjoyable game resulted. The visitors won the toss, and took first innings. Harbottle hit hard for 30, and with smaller contributions from all the side except Lane (who was run out before he had scored), the total reached 183. Harbottle hit a 9 (four for an overthrow) and four 4's. Monro Walker was credited with an 8 (four for an overthrow). Marlow lost Paine and Faulkner cheaply, but with useful scores from Langley, Bower, Baillie, and Want, the visitors' total was passed with six wickets down. Eventually the innings closed for 183. Want—one of the Australian visitors staying in Marlow—hit a 5, six 4's, and three 3's in his dashing score of 48. Langley hit five 4's in his 30. Mr. Walker's team went in a second time, and had compiled 113 for six wickets, when stumps were drawn. Marlow therefore won by 50 runs on the first innings. Full score and analysis:—

WYCOMBE CHURCH ROOM v. WYCOMBE ST. JOHN'S.
The third encounter between the above-named teams took place on the Wycombe Rye on Saturday last in splendid weather, and, after a pleasant and interesting game, resulted in a win for the Church Room by 37 runs on the first innings. The St. John's won the toss and elected to bat first, and compiled the moderate total of 30, F. Free (12), Burnham (8), and Ing (8) being the top scorers. The Church Room then went in, and replied with 67, C. Vickers (not out, 24), Seaton (14), and C. Twitchen (13) being the double-figure contributors. The St. John's then went in again, and showed better form, knocking up 60, W. Rolfe (11), Ing (12), and Ford (9) being the chief scorers. WYCOMBE BELLE VUE WANDERERS v. HOLLOWAY'S BOOT OPERATIVES C.C.
The return match between the above-named teams, which took place on the Wycombe Rye last Saturday, excited great interest. Mr. H. Coltman, who "skippered" the Wanderers, turned out a formidable eleven, while the shoemakers were but poorly represented, in fact at the last moment they had to make up with five substitutes. The men of "wax and leather" batted first, and ran up a creditable total of 66; Wilks, Humphrey, Collier, Lillyman, and Coles, contributing their respective scores in fine style. Tibbles, with the ball, was in rare fettle, his four wickets only costing a fraction over 4 runs a-piece; Anger took 2 wickets for 20. The time eventually arrived for the Wanderers' turn at the wicket, Coltman and Youers opening the attack against the destructive deliveries of Humphrey and Mead. Wickets 1 and 2 went down for 14, and at this juncture it looked as though the Wanderers' contingent would collapse, six wickets being captured for 29, the last three of which not adding a run. The score rose slowly, and when Thorne went in the Wanderers' hopes revived, the great slogger piling on his 15 in double quick time. The end eventually arrived with the total at 54, which left the operatives winners on the first innings by 12 runs. Both Mead and Humphrey, who bowled throughout the game, captured 4 wickets each for 26 runs a-piece. The shoemen went in a second time, and at the close of the venture 21 runs had been added for the loss of seven wickets. After the match the teams heartily enjoyed a social at the "Belle Vue," Gordon-road. Score:—
