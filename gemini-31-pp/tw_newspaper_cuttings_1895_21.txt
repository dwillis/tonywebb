GUILDHALL C.C. v NEWTOWN C.C. — These clubs met
on Saturday in their return fixture at Sandleford park. The
game, which ended in a win for Guildhall, was remarkable
for low scoring, the bowlers taking everything before them,
in addition to which the fielding of both sides was good.
Humphries and Lucas for Guildhall bowled splendidly, the
former capturing 12 wickets for as many runs, and the
latter 7 for 8. Allsop and Williamson took the bowling
honours for Newtown. The teams were entertained on
the ground, and a hearty vote of thanks was accorded
Lord Arthur and Lady Butler (who were present) for their
kindness, also to Mr Allsop. Score :—

PARISH CHURCH INSTITUTE V HIGHCLERE.—The return
match between these teams was played, by kind per-
mission, on the cricket ground on Saturday in splendid
weather. Highclere winning the toss decided to bat first, but
owing to the bowling of Gunning and Gayfer, the first four
wickets fell for 21, when Collins came in and made a useful
stand, necessitating several changes in the bowling. Still
runs came, Collins doing the lion's share, but Gunning going
on at the top end fairly beat him with a beauty, whilst the
Thirkills made some useful stands. After the interval the
Institute started batting with Cook and Pyke, the former
hitting his wicket, whilst Pyke contented himself by keeping
up his wicket. Maslin, the next man, made things more
lively putting on 18 in quick time. The others making
double figures were Pyke, Young and Gunning. Both teams
were entertained to tea, through the kindness of Miss L.
Godding and Mr H Godding. Score :—

SPEEN v. BURGHCLERE.—This match was played at Speen on
Saturday. Speen, winning the toss, sent Ashby and Freebody
to the wickets, the former being unfortunately bowled, the ball
hitting him on the chest and rebounding on to the wickets. A.
Smith then joined Freebody and a long stand was made Speen
putting on 148 before the innings was declared closed. Both
Smith and Freebody played good cricket for their respective
scores, 71 and 64. Burghclere then went in and totalled 63
before being dismissed. Sherman and Storie did best. Free-
body was most successful with the ball, obtaining five wickets.
Speen thus won a pleasant game by 85 runs and nine wickets.

NEWBURY V. 49TH REGIMENTAL DISTRICT.—The return match
between these twelves [unclear] was played on Wednesday on the
ground of the former in perfect cricket weather. The
soldiers, who had got together a strong team, were the first
to bat, Sergt. Bromley and Lieut. Croome facing the deliveries
of H. D. Wells and [unclear]. The score increased steadily,
and it was not until it had reached 73 that the first two
batsmen were separated. When the sixth wicket went down,
111 was registered, but the rest of the men were quickly dis-
missed, and the whole side were all out before lunch for 142.
Pte Smith, who showed fine form, was not out 53, his best hits
being four four's, five three's, and ten two's, the only other
batsmen to make a stand were Bromley (27), and Croome (31).
The home team opened their innings shortly after three
o'clock, E. F. Hill and Cave opposing the bowling of Badding
and Orsome, when runs came quickly. It was not until the
telegraph board showed 75 that Middleton secured the profes-
sional's wicket. The retiring batsman had made 39 by some
good cricket. H. D. Wells went in and Hill was directly
afterwards bowled by Bromley. Two for 76. Ingram joined
Wells and the two completely mastered the bowling, the re-
sult being that the visitors score was passed just
before five o'clock. Wells still continued to hit
freely, and though several changes of bowling were
tried they failed to bring about the desired effect.
Stumps had to be drawn at 5-15 owing to the Reading men
having to leave by the 5-34 train, the score standing at 193 for
two wickets. The two not-outs Wells (62) and Ingram (45)
gave a fine display of batting. They were at the wickets just
over an hour, during which time they put on 118 runs. The
match was watched with interest by a fairly large number of
on-lookers. Score:—
