GUILDHALL C.C. v NEWTOWN C.C. — These clubs met on Saturday in their return fixture at Sandleford park. The game, which ended in a win for Guildhall, was remarkable for low scoring, the bowlers taking everything before them, in addition to which the fielding of both sides was good. Humphries and Lucas for Guildhall bowled splendidly, the former capturing 12 wickets for as many runs, and the latter 7 for 8. Allsop and Williamson took the bowling honours for Newtown. The teams were entertained on the ground, and a hearty vote of thanks was accorded Lord Arthur and Lady Butler (who were present) for their kindness, also to Mr Allsop. Score:—

PARISH CHURCH INSTITUTE V HIGHCLERE.—The return match between these teams was played, by kind permission, on the cricket ground on Saturday in splendid weather. Highclere winning the toss decided to bat first, but owing to the bowling of Gunning and Gayfer, the first four wickets fell for 21, when Collins came in and made a useful stand, necessitating several changes in the bowling. Still runs came, Collins doing the lion's share, but Gunning going on at the top end fairly beat him with a beauty, whilst the Thirkills made some useful stands. After the interval the Institute started batting with Cook and Pyke, the former hitting his wicket, whilst Pyke contented himself by keeping up his wicket. Maslin, the next man, made things more lively putting on 18 in quick time. The others making double figures were Pyke, Young and Gunning. Both teams were entertained to tea, through the kindness of Miss L. Godding and Mr H Godding. Score:—

SPEEN v. BURGHCLERE.—This match was played at Speen on Saturday. Speen, winning the toss, sent Ashby and Freebody to the wickets, the former being unfortunately bowled; the ball hitting him on the chest and rebounding on to the wickets. A. NEWBURY V. 49TH REGIMENTAL DISTRICT.—The return match between these two elevens was played on Wednesday on the ground of the former in perfect cricket weather. The soldiers, who had got together a strong team, were the first to bat. Sergt Bromley and Staff-Armourer facing the deliveries of H. D. Wells and [unclear]. The score increased steadily, and it was not until it had reached 73 that the first two batsmen were separated. When the sixth wicket went down, 111 was registered, but the rest of the men were quickly dismissed, and the whole side were all out before lunch for 142. For Regiments Pte. Smith showed fine form; was not out 62, his best hits being four four's, five three's, and ten two's, the only other batsmen to make a stand were Bromley (27), and Croome (31). The home team opened their innings shortly after three o'clock, E. P. Hill and Cave opposing the bowling of Badding and Croome, when runs came quickly. It was not until the telegraph board showed 75 that Middleton secured the professional's wicket. The retiring batsman had made 39 by some good cricket. H. D. Wells went in and Hill was directly afterwards bowled by Bromley. Two for 76. Ingram joined Wells and the two completely mastered the bowling, the result being that the visitors' score was passed just before five o'clock. Wells still continued to hit freely, and though several changes of bowling were tried they failed to bring about the desired effect. Stumps had to be drawn at 5-15 owing to the Reading men having to leave by the 5-34 train, the score standing at 193 for two wickets. The two not-outs Wells (62) and Ingram (45) gave a fine display of batting. They were at the wickets just over an hour, during which time they put on 118 runs. The match was watched with interest by a fairly large number of on-lookers. Score:—
