To Fairfax Davies largely belongs the credit of
Norfolk's latest success over Oxfordshire. The
difference was so slight—a matter of 13 runs only—
after a first day's play, that the issue of the game
hung almost entirely upon the play during the second
day. The wicket played queerly, and none but
resolute batsmen, with a good defence, stood any
chance of making runs. It was at this stage of the
game that Fairfax Davies entered upon his long and
invaluable innings. He and Skrimshire put on 30 for
the first wicket, and after the departure of the Holt
man, Tillard helped him to take the score to
within one of the century. The next two wickets
gave little trouble, Barratt contributing 3 and the
Norfolk captain only a couple. Wilson, of Sutton
Valence fame, put together a dozen, and the last five
men gave even less trouble, Morley narrowly escaping
"a pair." Of the 182 put together by Norfolk, Mr.
Davies claimed more than half, and there can be little
doubt that his magnificent score of 94 turned the
scale in favour of Norfolk. His best hits were nine
4s and eight 3s.

Oxfordshire were set 170 to get to win, but they
never at any time during the innings looked like accom-
plishing this task. Shore, who failed to secure a single
wicket in the first innings, and Mack bowled finely;
and though five men reached double figures, the whole
side were dismissed for the moderate total of 100,
leaving Norfolk victorious by a margin of 69 runs.
Mack's figures for the match were excellent, while
both Shore and Morley bowled well in a single innings.

G. F. Blake once more proved himself a useful
member of the team. In addition to scoring 27 and 8,
he secured three men behind the sticks, whilst he only
gave away three byes in the first innings and two in
the second.

Last season Fairfax Davies headed the Norfolk
batting averages with an average of 27 for eight
innings, his highest effort being his 49 at Cambridge. In
the match, however, he was credited with 82 for one
completed innings.

Of the seven Inter-County matches played by
Norfolk so far this season, three have been won, two
drawn, and one lost. Victories have been obtained
over Oxfordshire, Hertfordshire, and Lincolnshire. The
drawn games have been with Lincolnshire and
Cambridgeshire, and the solitary defeat was ex-
perienced at the hands of Herts at Bishop's Stortford.

The last fixture of the season is now in progress as
I write. Cambridgeshire are well represented, but
as we got through this match last season with
some 80 odd runs to spare, and as the
team are in a winning frame of mind, there is
sufficient reason for believing that the results at the
finish of the season will be far more satisfactory, from
the point of view of winning matches, than was
predicted at the early part of the year.

The success of the Gloucestershire eleven in the field
this season has effected a marked improvement in the
gate receipts. In the Cheltenham Week, for instance,
the county beat all previous records, including that of
1890, when the Australians were a great attraction.
In that week, as in the one just passed, there was a
blank day, but the receipts have gone from £537 to
£701.

Last week I remarked upon the falling-off of local
century makers, and hinted at the possibility of some-
one doing the trick that week. As a matter of fact,
although I did not know it at the time, Phil Fryer was
well on the way for the century at the time the lines
were penned. That he eventually made 129 is now a
matter of ancient history. It was, I am told, a fine
performance, and I regret very much that I was un-
able to witness the all-round display of the Norfolk
team on that occasion. He experienced a piece of good
luck early in his career, the ball striking the stumps
and failing to dislodge the bails. One can hardly
describe that as a blemish. In all other respects the
innings was faultless.

P. A. Fryer's 129 against Oxfordshire last week is
the highest individual score obtained by any player in
the Norfolk matches this season, and, so far, it is the
only three figure innings. Last season A. E. Braund's
95, against a strong M.C.C. team at Yarmouth, stood
as the best for the season, Fryer's best effort being a
40 obtained under great difficulties at Bishop's Stort-
ford.

Norfolk made a grand beginning at Cambridge yes-
terday, and rain alone can save the Cambridgeshire
eleven from a heavy—possibly a single innings—defeat
to-day. On a bowler's wicket, Shore and Morley
carried everything before them, Cambridgeshire being
dismissed for the melancholy total of 56, of which
number Dan Hayward was responsible for 17. Norfolk
started none too well, losing F. Davies, J. F. Skrim-
shire, and C. Tillard for less than 20, but P. A. Fryer
and L. Barratt, between them, carried the score past
the home total. Morley again failed with the bat, and
his captain, although making a lengthy stay, scored
none too freely. The association of the Rev J C
Wilson and Ernest Fryer was, however, attended with
happy results, and the pair put on runs
at a smart pace. The St. Bart's man,
who is playing in the place of G. F. Blake, eventually
carried out his bat for 32, the highest individual
effort of the day. The innings closed for 163,
of which no fewer than 27 were extras. Cambridge-
shire played better in their second essay with the bat,
and Roberson, who is one of the soundest bats in the
team, and a Norfolk born man, to wit, played good
cricket for 32. At the call of time five wickets had
been disposed of for 82, so that the home county are in
the unenviable position of being still 45 runs behind
with only five wickets in hand. To all appearances
Cambridgeshire's chances are beyond all hope of
recovery, and an early victory for Norfolk may reason-
ably be expected.
