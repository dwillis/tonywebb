NEWCASTLE DAILY JOURNAL: THURSDAY 29 AUGUST 1895-2

CRICKET.

NOTICE TO CRICKETERS.
We shall be glad to receive the names and addresses of
club secretaries throughout Northumberland as early as
possible, in order that the resolution passed at the recent
County Club meeting held in Newcastle may be sent to
them. We shall also be glad to receive the name of any-
one willing to subscribe to the County Club.

MR CLAYTON'S XI. v. MR R. G. MORTIMER'S XI.
To-day Mr Clayton's eleven continue their tour by
playing a team raised by Mr R G Mortimer. The match
extends over to-morrow, and a start will be made at 11
o'clock this morning. The teams are:—
Mr Clayton's XI: F G H Clayton, A T B Dunn, W B
Anderson, H Philipson, B N Bosworth-Smith, M G
Barlow, J M Quinton, C G Pope, K A Woodward, H D
Corbet, and N Malcolmson.
Mr Mortimer's XI: R G Mortimer, J Harrell, C
Latimer, C M Henderson, R B Hoare, G Garnett, T
Gowans, H J Salmon, C F Blain, with Griffin and
another.

MR A BROWNE'S XI. v. MR H J SALMON'S XI.
Our readers will remember that a short time ago
"Custos" stated that the meeting between the teams of
Mr A Browne of Doxford Hall and Mr H J Salmon of
Newcastle at the Constabulary Ground on Monday and
Tuesday, September 2nd and 3rd, would provide two
days really first-class cricket. At that time the announce-
ment was made the brothers L C H and R O N Palaret,
as well as C B Fry, the famous Oxford triple Blue, were
expected to play in the match; but, unfortunately, none
of these celebrated cricketers can come north, and, con-
sequently, Mr Salmon has been more or less compelled to
confine himself to local talent. Still, from the names
given below, it will be seen that his team is a fairly strong
one, in both batting and bowling.
Mr Browne, on the other hand, has spread his net over a
wider area, and a better eleven than he is bringing has
not been seen on Tyneside, for it includes Wainwright,
the Yorkshire County bowler, and Pougher of Leicester-
shire, whose performances, especially against Surrey,
stamp him as one of the best bowlers of the present day.
Besides these, Pickett and F G Bull of Essex are included
in the team, so that, if Mr Salmon wins, his men will have
to surpass all their previous performances. The teams
are as follows:—
Mr Browne's XI.: A Browne (captain), C Perkins, S
Marshall, A R Fuller, F G Bull, G Elser, Lambert,
Pickett, Wainwright, Pougher, and another (probably
Briggs of Lancashire).
Mr Salmon's XI.: H J Salmon (captain), G A T
Salmon, C O Hall, J S Brown, S C Ferguson, A T B
Dunn, C F Blain, H Haslam, F G H Clayton, G Simp-
son, and the Rev E W R Walters.

MR A. C. MACLAREN'S AVERAGE.
Including the innings completed on Tuesday morning,
A. Maclaren's batting average is a fraction over 54, and
he thus runs a close race with "W. G.," who failed to
score for Gloucestershire on Monday. The figures are:
W. G. Grace, 54.20; A. C. Maclaren, 54.01.

MR BEWICKE'S XI. v. MR F C R CLAYTON'S XI.
(By "CUSTOS.")
The match between these teams was resumed at the
Close House ground, yesterday, but on account of the
rain no definite issue was arrived at. Though the early
part of the day was fine, heavy showers fell at intervals
throughout the afternoon, driving the players from the
field, and ultimately causing the game to be abandoned
as a draw, when, in their second innings, Mr Bewicke's
team had scored 35 for one wicket. Mr Clayton's eleven
were the greater sufferers from the wet weather, their
bowlers being thus severely handicapped; though at the
finish the rain undoubtedly saved them from defeat.
The change from slow to a fast wicket proved too much
for most of them, who often played late at Guttridge's
fast deliveries, and that bowler captured six of their
wickets for 32 runs; while Simpson's four cost 35. For
the home side Walters again batted well.
Prompt to time Anderson resumed bowling from the
railway end, Bosworth-Smith being the other bowler.
After four had been scored Guttridge was smartly taken
by Pope at extra-cover off Bosworth-Smith. Farmer
followed in but had only made three when he put one up
in the slips which Woodward took. Walters gave
Anderson a hot catch at mid-off, which was dropped, and
then, with an off drive to the boundary, he sent up the
"hundred." His partner, Salmon, opened with a lofty
2 through the slips, and, after adding another single, was
caught at long on by Quinton off the lefthander. C. Perkins
filled the vacancy, but lost Walters, who was taken at
short slip by Dunn; and C Bewicke, the last man, came
in. The end had now arrived, however, as in attempting
a short run the home captain was cut, the innings realising
112, or 28 behind the score of their opponents.
Dunn and Philipson began Mr Clayton's second innings
to the bowling of Guttridge (house end) and Simpson.
Philipson opened the scoring with a single, the first run
he has made on the tour. When he had scored three,
however, he was out to a brilliant lefthanded catch in
the slips by Simpson, off Guttridge. With Barlow in, steadi-
ness was the order, and the score had been carried to 30
before Dunn was caught at the wicket. Barlow quickly
followed him out, being clean bowled in trying to pull,
and hitting short, at one from Simpson. Woodward
filled the vacancy, but did not stay long, being caught at
mid-off by Haslam, so that four wickets were down for 37.

With 40 up Guttridge beat Anderson, and Clayton joined
Quinton. They stayed together for some time, when
Clayton played outside one from Simpson, and was
bowled (6 for 59); and an adjournment was made for
luncheon. A shower of rain caused this interval to be
prolonged for about half an hour. On resuming Bosworth-
Smith joined Quinton, opening his account with a cut for
two; but Guttridge hit his leg stump when he had scored
7 (7 for 65). Two runs later Simpson bowled Quinton.
Corbet, the newcomer, was sent back by Simpson after
making a single. Malcolmson was last man, and increased
the score by a couple of 4's when Pope fell to a catch by
Simpson in the slips, the total reaching 84, thus leaving
Mr Bewicke's team 113 runs to get to win.
A A Mewburn and Butler again opened the home
innings, Anderson and Bosworth-Smith sharing the
attack. Mawburn was bowled for a single, and then rain
caused a delay of half an hour. Walters was the next
batsman, starting with a 2 behind point. With 29 on
the board rain interrupted play once more, though only
for a few minutes. On turning out Dunn relieved Ander-
son at the railway end. However, before they had
bowled half a dozen overs rain drove them from the field,
and, though the players waited till 5.30 in the hope of
resuming, the shower continued, and play had to be
abandoned. Both Walters (21) and Butler (10) batted
steadily for their runs. Score:—
