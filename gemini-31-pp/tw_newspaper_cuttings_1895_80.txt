POOLE HERALD: THURSDAY 5 SEPTEMBER 1895 - 2

Bournemouth C.C. finished their season on Tuesday
last with an overwhelming victory over the newly re-
suscitated Lulworth Lobsters, Capt. W. S. Wyld sig-
nalising his last appearance for the club by a capital 59.
All told, the club has played 17 matches, of which 10
have been won, 5 drawn, and 2 lost. The defeats were
administered by Poole and Hinton Admiral. The draws
were with Hampshire Hogs, Hinton, Southampton
Deanery (2), and the M.C.C., and it is interesting to
note that except for an intervening win between the
first and second they all came together. The clubs
defeated were Poole, Downton College (2), Bourne-
mouth United, New Forest, Rev. Askew's Dorset XI.,
Isle of Purbeck, New Forest, Swanage Visitors, and Lul-
worth Lobsters. Two centuries have been made for the
club, Captain Wyld scoring 113 against a strong bowling
side of Hampshire Hogs, and R. W. C. Fisher contributing
100 not out against Downton College. In this latter
match also, H. G. Lewin got within 5 of his century.
Fisher also made good scores of 67 against
Bournemouth United, and 64 against the New Forest,
whilst Captain Wyld contributed 38 against the New
Forest, 60 against Downton College, 67 against Rev. R.
Askew's XI., and 56 against Lulworth Lobsters. Pride
of place in the average table, is taken, however, by E. C.
Duchesne, who batted so consistently in the eight
innings in which he was able to assist the club, that his
average works out at 31. One thing that the statistics
demonstrate is the number of players Captain Wyld has
been able to call to his aid. Very few players have
played in more than half-a-dozen games, as will be seen
by the list below. In bowling the work has been almost
entirely done by Head and R. W. C. Fisher, whose
analyses work out so evenly that it is almost impossible
to differentiate between them. Head, however, is once
again to the front with the largest number of wickets at
the smallest cost, and it would be difficult to estimate
too highly the value of his services. His most note-
worthy performance was perhaps against Swanage Visitors,
when, going on late, he secured the last five wickets in
six overs for no runs. Appended we give the more
important batting and bowling performances in detail :—
