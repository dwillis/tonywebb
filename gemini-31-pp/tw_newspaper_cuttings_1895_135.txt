WATFORD OBSERVER: SATURDAY 24 AUGUST 1895-3.

WEST HERTS v. ST. ALBANS.
The return match between these teams was played at St.
Albans on Wednesday—the second Wednesday of the festival. It
hardly needs to be repeated on this side of the Colne that the
great majority of previous contests both at Watford and St.
Albans have resulted in favour of West Herts, and to reduce
that majority the city eleven determined to do its best. The
game was naturally well patronised, and enthusiasm amongst
Albanians ran higher and higher, when it was seen how affairs
were going. West Herts lost the toss for the ninth time in
succession, and opposed to the bowling of J. Woods and White
were Dr. Norman and T. Sansom. Both batsmen reached
double figures in safety, but within two runs of 40 the Doctor
was bowled by White and Sansom by Woods. G. H. Small, a
Beds County man, and A. A. Coles became associated, and it was
not long before a change of bowling was considered advisable,
R. R. Barker and B. A. F. Astley going on. The alteration had
no effect, and the batsmen continued to carry all before them.
The century was at last hoisted amidst applause, both batsmen
being well set. Nor was a separation effected until the score had
been raised to 176, when Barnett caught Small at third man off
Woods's bowling. His score of 57 was made up of six fours, four
threes, six twos, and only nine singles. He was given no less than
three lives, Little having that number of chances of stumping
him, but for all that it was the day's best display. Gilder had a
short life, but W. T. Westell—the St. Albans Goliath—took his
place and Smalls' too. The second century was soon passed
and the West Herts prospect, as bowler after bowler was tried,
became blacker and blacker. However, when 75 more runs had
been added A. A. Coles was caught third man by Barker, and the
innings was declared closed, it being then 4 o'clock. Coles had
by very fair cricket made 87, after about three hours' play,
scoring eleven fours, five threes, seven twos, and fourteen
singles. He was greatly aided by having a man to run for him.
W. T. Westell, whose average should be high, as he carried out
his bat for 93 against M.C.C., was not out 46. It should be
mentioned that there were no less than 43 extras, 32 of which
were byes. West Herts began their uphill fight at 4.15 by send-
ing in White and C. R. Collins, Pearce and Sansom sharing the
St. Albans attack. Collins was dismissed with the score at 16,
he himself having made four runs, and Cobb came in and an ad-
vance was made to 39 before White was unfortunately given out
lbw to Pearce. Eleven runs later Cobb was caught by Coles's
substitute immediately after 50 had been telegraphed, being
responsible for exactly half. R. O. Barnett and J. Woods only
managed to break their ducks, and most of the scoring was done
by Barker. He, however, found someone to help him when
Astley came in, and over 60 runs had been added when the old
Aldenhamian was dismissed by another catch by the sub-
stitute. The century was passed with Barker and Little together,
but Barker was caught by Sansom when 128 had been reached.
His 30 runs, a most useful contribution, were the result of stylish
though patient cricket. He was at the wickets for an hour and
twenty minutes. Matters had now become very bad, and there
was a quarter of an hour left for play, and only one wicket to
fall. However, Little amply atoned for his shortcomings as
wicket keeper, by keeping up his own wicket until 6.33 when
stumps were drawn. The match was saved, and that was all,
while it was decidedly hard for St. Albans. Score:—

WEST HERTS v. FROGMORE.
West Herts played the last of their home matches on
Saturday against Frogmore. Play did not start until 2 o'clock,
on account of the late arrival of the visitors. The Watford
Club was able to put in a strong side, the only noticeable absentee
being White, who was playing against Norfolk for the County.
Frogmore had their usual eleven, and having won the toss W. M.
Robertson and E. Gentle opened their attack. Gentle was
caught by Little when he had made three, the Rev. F. Sullivan
was bowled by Beaton for six, and Robertson was caught by a
substitute off Astley's bowling for two runs, the result of a
patient quarter of an hour's cricket, the first three wickets only
producing 11 runs. Play became more exciting when F. May was
joined by H. Hobbs, and the score was carried to 50 for the
fourth wicket, Hobbs having contributed 12. W. T. Wright
Millar dismissed with the last ball of his over, and on taking
up again secured three more wickets in successive balls, the
fifth and eighth wicket falling for 83. A. Wright, however, was not
so easily sent back, and it was not until he had knocked up 11
that he was bowled by Burrows. May had until then been
playing cautiously, but there being only one more wicket to fall
he resolved to make hay while the sun shone, and before H.
Cripps was bowled he had put together 31 runs. The innings
closed, after many ups and downs for the fair score of 73. At
twenty minutes to 4, W. R. Collins and F. E. Burrows went out
for West Herts. Home thought to win the match themselves.
Burrows had to go back when he had made six, but a start was
being made by Cobb and Collins, when the former unfortunately
ran himself out with the score at 34. Very nearly half the runs
had been scored, and there were eight more wickets to fall so
that victory seemed certain. J. R. Humbert made a hit for
three, but was caught by Westland, and later at 53 Collins was
bowled for 22. After his departure the remaining batsmen
collapsed, and Beaton, Astley, Millar, and Little were all dis-
missed without the addition of a single run, J. A. Murray's first
and an extra brought the total up to 58, 15 runs behind the
villagers. Only five batsmen failed to score. Frogmore went in
again and did even better than before, as when stumps were
drawn, with four wickets in hand they were only one short of the
total for their first attempt. May was responsible for 19, and A.
Wright was not out 11. The match was thus a win for Frogmore
by 15 runs. Score:—

CLOSING.

THE GROVE v. HEALEY'S BREWERY.
This match was played in the Earl of Clarendon's Park on
Saturday, and resulted in a win for the Grove. Lord Clarendon,
whose love of sport is so well known, takes much interest in the
Grove cricket team, and was present with Lady Edith Villiers,
Lord Hyde, and visitors. The play of Potts, the captain of the
Grove team, was the chief feature in the match, and really won
the game. Score:
