NORTHAMPTON HERALD. SATURDAY 31 AUGUST 1895 - 3.

NORTHAMPTON CRICKET LEAGUE.

FIRST SECTION.
Enterprise v. Excelsior.—This match, brought off on
the Borough Ground on Saturday, ended in favour of
the Enterprise by 22 runs, the scores reading—Enter-
prise 84, Excelsior 62. For the Enterprise (who batted
first) A. E. Coe made 22, S. Field 21, and W. J. Roth
14. Bowling in capital style, P. Cox secured five
wickets for 18 runs, while S. Kirkham took one for 8.
R. Major-Lucas one for 12, P. Kendall one for 12, and
C. Dent one for 19. J. M. Lucas was the only double
figure score on the Excelsior side, contributing 21 (which
included two fives); T. Andrews made 9. A. E. Brad-
ford captured four wickets for nine runs, and S. Field
took four for 22, both in a very fine display taking
respectively two wickets in two balls. W. J. Westmore-
land (one for 11) also bowled.
St. Andrew's v. Star.—A match between teams re-
presenting St. Andrew's and the Star was played on
Saturday on the Borough Ground, the result being
a win for the Star by nine wickets, the score
reading—St. Andrew's 23, and the Star 32 for one
wicket. St. Andrew's played eight men, and the
Star played nine. A. Wilkinson (10) was the only one
to reach double figures for St. Andrew's, who batted
first. P. Cory secured four wickets for 14, and O.
Squires two for 11. The most successful Star bowlers
were F. Warren (16 not out) and W. Moring (15). Birch
captured one wicket for 24.
SECOND SECTION.
Princess-street v. Moss Rose.—Played on the Borough
Ground on Saturday. The Moss Rose sustained a
crushing defeat, Princess-street making 132 for 5 wickets
(innings declared closed) as against 25. C. Penn com-
piled 37 and W. Rogers 22 for Princess-street. W. Kent
took two for 16, H. Bailey two for 25, and A. Garrard
one for 32. No player of the Moss Rose team was able
to reach double figures, thanks to the fine bowling of
Timms, who dismissed six men for eight runs, and
Penn, who captured four wickets for six.
Doddridge v. Excelsior Albions.—These teams met
on the Borough Ground on Saturday afternoon, and a
one-sided match resulted in an easy victory for
the Doddridge team. Owing to the destructive
bowling of J. Smith (five wickets for 31), H. Rowe
(three for six), W. Lack (one for five), and A. Sutton
(one for 20), the Albions were only able to reach 77,
towards which B. Kendall scored 32 and T. Barry
12. C. Sutton (44 not out) and A. Sutton (30 not
out) were the two first to bat for Doddridge, and having
95 to their credit without the loss of a single
wicket they closed their innings.
THIRD SECTION.
St. Giles' v. Wesleyan Star.—This match was decided
on the Borough Ground on Saturday, the scores being
Wesleyan Star 105, St. Giles' 35. For the winning side
J. Lewis (24), York (16 not out), F. Osborn (16), and
Barrows (16) were the principal scorers. The bowling
honours were divided between A. Lloyd (three for 19),
B. Betts (three for 21), P. W. Sharman (two for 11),
and F. Millward (two for 28). P. W. Sharman (13)
was the only double figure scorer for St. Giles'. J.
Lewis and H. Martin each took five wickets for 16 runs.
Blue Shield v. Y.M.C.A.—Played on the Borough
Ground on Saturday. The Blue Shield won easily,
compiling 90 against 13 made by the Y.M.C.A. For
the winners W. Newcome scored 30 not out, W. Shaw
20, and O. Sheehan 12. Coe secured five wickets,
Harris and Tyler two each, and Luland one. E. Chater
succeeded in taking five wickets for four runs and O.
Sheehan five for seven for the Blue Shield.
St. Lawrence's v. Manfield's C.C.—This match was
played on the Borough Ground on Saturday, when St.
Lawrence's won very easily by 97 runs. St. Lawrence's,
batting first, scored 135 runs for eight wickets, and
then declared their innings closed. Vickers (29), Wain-
wright (25), Talbot and Barcock, not out with 25 and
13 respectively, were chief scorers. Warren (three
wickets), Wright and Harding (two each) doing best
for Manfield's in the bowling. On Manfield's going in
to bat they were dismissed for 39, F. Pulley (7) and
E. Wright (10) being chief scorers. W. White (four for
15) and W. Barcock (three for 21) bowling for St.
Lawrence's. Manfield's were unfortunate in losing three
wickets by batsmen being run out.
Wootton v. St. Mary's.—This match was played in
Cow Meadow on Saturday. Scores: Wootton 39, St.
Mary's 29.
Kettering Wanderers beat Kettering Wesleyans on
Saturday by 16 runs. Wanderers, 129; Wesleyans,
113.
Kettering Church Institute 2nd defeated Kettering
Working Men's Club 2nd on Saturday by 21 runs.
K.C.I., 41; K.W.M.C., 20 and 56 for nine wickets.
Kettering Fuller beat Brigstock on Saturday by 12
runs. Fuller, 44; Brigstock, 32.
Kettering Church Institute defeated Kettering Work-
ing Men's Club on the North Park on Saturday after-
noon by four wickets and 103 runs. The Institute,
for six wickets, compiled 165, when they closed their
innings, the highest scorers being Hoare (49), Piggott
(31), Cole (not out 32), and Maycock (24). The
Working Men's Club were all dismissed for 62 (Chap-
lin 17).
Princess-street Excelsior Reserves v. Queen's-road.
—This match was played on the Racecourse on Satur-
day, and, after an exciting finish, ended in a victory
for Princess-street by 4 runs. Scores: Princess-street
Excelsior Reserves, 18; Queen's-road, 14.
Empire Star v. Lifeboat.—This match was played
in the Cow Meadow on Saturday, and ended in a vic-
tory for the former by 29 runs. The chief scorers
for the Empire Star were Oldham (16) and Kent (10).
The bowling honours were shared by Kent (five wickets
for 5 runs, including hat trick) and Miles (five for 5).
Scores: Empire Star C.C., 43; Lifeboat, 14.
Victoria-road v. Cogenhoe.—Played at Cogenhoe on
Saturday last, and won by the former by 7 runs.
Scores: Victoria-road, 35 (T. Garlick 10, A. Scouse
7); and Cogenhoe, 28. T. Garlick took four wickets for
4 runs, F. Roberts four for 12, F. Cooke one for 9.

Henley C.C. v. Grafton Arms.—Played at Baulms-
holme Meadow, the former winning by 31 runs.
Scores: Henley, 61; Grafton Arms, 30.
Queen's Arms, Raglan-street, v. Lord Raglan.—The
return match was played on the Racecourse on Satur-
day, and resulted in the victory of the former by
seven runs—69 to 62. Billingham made 24, Malpas
13, and S. Braines 9 for the winners, Robinson taking
four and S. Braines three wickets respectively. For
the losers Boswell made 15, Ward 10, and Bunting 9,
Ward taking the bowling honours with six wickets,
and Purser three.
Hardingstone v. Borough Employes.—Played at
Hardingstone, and won by Hardingstone by 49 runs.
Scores: Hardingstone, 71; Borough Employes, 22.
St. Sepulchre's Bible Class v. Princess-street
X.L.C.R.—Played on the Racecourse on Saturday, and
won by St. Sepulchre's by 69 runs. St. Sepulchre's
batted first, and scored 106 for the loss of seven
wickets, and then declared their innings closed.
Princess-street were all out for 37. For the winners
R. Osborn (56 not out) and W. Pinkard (17) batted
well, whilst in the bowling J. Dickenson took three
wickets for 2, A. Bradford three for 2, W. Pinkard one
for 4, and H. Botterill one for 7.
Alcombe Rovers v. Uppingham Rovers.—Played on
the Racecourse on Saturday, and ended in a win for
the former by 17 runs. Scores: 30 and 13.
St. Sepulchre's Choir C.C. v. Kingsley Park Wes-
leyans.—Played on the Racecourse on Saturday, and
won by St. Sepulchre's by 47 runs. Scores: St.
Sepulchre's, 62 (Vaughan 16 and Eales 12); Kingsley
Park Wesleyans, 15.
Wellingborough Congregational v. Northampton
College-street.—These teams met at Wellingborough
on Saturday, a low-scoring match ending in a victory
for College-street by 24 runs. The visitors batted
first, but their total was only the very moderate one
of 46 (W. Crask 19, Arthur Robinson 11). J. W.
Dyson took four wickets for 8, and A. Birkett six
for 23. Wellingborough opened with W. Kerry and
C. H. Woolston; but Kerry was sent back by the
first ball delivered, and Woolston was dismissed for
a cypher. J. W. Dyson and A. Birkett made a short
stand, taking the score to 13 before Birkett was caught.
After his departure no one was able to stay against
the bowling of A. J. Robinson, whose analysis was as
follows:—10.1 overs, 7 maidens, 3 runs, 7 wickets.
E. Williams secured the other three wickets for 16,
and the total only reached 22 (J. W. Dyson 10).
St. Paul's v. Catholic Alma.—Played on the Race-
course on Saturday, and won by St. Paul's by 12 runs.
R. Derby (13 not out) was the chief scorer. E. H.
Chater (six wickets for 13) and W. Care (four wickets
for 20) did good work with the ball. H. Forrest (7)
was the highest scorer for the losers. Scores: St.
Paul's, 46; Catholic Alma, 34.
Teachers v. Wollaston.—Played at Franklin's Gardens
on Saturday last, and, strange to say, says a corre-
spondent, ended in a similar way to one of the Senior
League matches, of which the opinion of the M.C.C.
was taken. This high authority on cricket decided
that the League match was a draw, so this match
must be called the same. Wollaston won the toss,
and, batting first, were disposed of for 71. G. Lucy
(16), H. Lucy (14), and A. Horton (10) were the chief
scorers. For the Teachers Warner bowled well, taking
three wickets for 6 runs: Grant obtained five wickets
for 16 runs, and Sheard and Poole each captured a
wicket. The Teachers then batted, and, thanks to
vigorous hitting on the part of Poole (19 not out),
Turner (16), and Mackaness (15), soon obtained the
same number of runs as Wollaston, viz., 71, for four
wickets, when time was called. Hart obtained two
wickets for Wollaston and Brown one.
A number of other matches appeared in our first
edition.
Post Office v. Y.M.C.A.—Played on the Borough
Ground on August 22, and won by the Post Office by
56 runs. The Y.M.C.A., batting first, made 70, Parkin
32 and Turner 27. Bowling for the Post Office, H.
Toyer took three wickets for one run, S. Field four for
16, and T. Pettit three for 30. The Post Office made
126. For the winners S. Field played brilliantly for
84, the other principal scorers being Carter 11 and
Margetts 7.
Brittania Rovers v. Lily of the Valley.—Played on
the Racecourse on Saturday, and won by the former
by 99 runs. Scores: Brittania, 139 (T. Freeman 42 not
out); Lily of the Valley, 40.
