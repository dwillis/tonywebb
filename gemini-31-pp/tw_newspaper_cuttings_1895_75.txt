Killerton v. Exe Valley II.—Played at Killerton
Park, and ended in a easy win for the home
team. Scores:—Exe valley, 20; Killerton, 52 for
3 wickets. Wright and Gould bowled well for
Killerton, the former also batted well for 18.
Devon County Asylum v. Mr. Newbery's XI.—
This match was played at Exminster on Saturday,
and ended in a win for the Asylum by the narrow
margin of 4 runs. Scores:—Devon County
Asylum, 32 and 60; Mr. Newbery's XI., 52
and 36.
Exmouth v. Mr. W. Copleston's Team:—This
two days' match was commenced at Exmouth
yesterday. The visitors were disposed of for 174
runs, the highest scorer being H. Andrew (run
out) 51. Deane took seven wickets. At the close
of play last evening, Exmouth had made 210 for
two wickets down, J. S. G. Grenfell 30, R. C.
Groves 30; J A Gibb (not out) 78.
Wiveliscombe v. Wellington (Home Team).—
This match was played at Wiveliscombe on Satur-
day, and ended in a win for Wiveliscombe by 20
runs. The best scorers for the winners were H. L.
Hodges 25 (not out) and A. D. Hickley 7. For
the losers, T. S. Page 12, and W. H. Brown 6.
Scores:—Wiveliscombe, 62; Wellington, 42.
Bradfield v. Ashill.—This return match was
played on the ground of the former, and won
easily by the home team by 10 wickets. H. Lane
made 10 and 13 for Ashill, and played exceedingly
good cricket, helping his side considerably, and F.
Matthews and W. Miller were of great assistance
to the winners. Ashill, 20 and 36; Bradfield, 65
and 1 for no wicket.
Bridge Mills (Silverton) v. Uffculme.—Played on
the ground of the former on Saturday, and won
by Uffculme by 14 runs. The Rev J F Powning
bowled dexterously, taking six wickets for 17 runs,
and Ponsford the remaining four, for 16 runs.
Frank Welland, for Uffculme, showed brilliant
style for 23. Ponsford played cantious cricket.
Foster was neatly stumped. Scores:—Uffculme—
First innings, 64; second innings, 36 for seven
wickets; Bridge Mills, 40.

SIDMOUTH v. EALING.
This two days' match was concluded yesterday.
Overnight Sidmouth had made 367 in their first
innings, and Ealing had lost two wickets for two
runs. On resuming the visitors raised the total
to 216. The principal feature of the innings was
the 101 by Conry, who was unfortunately run
out, and the fine defence of McGregor for 58. On
going in a second time they did not do quite so
well, and were ultimately beaten by an innings
and two runs. The Ealing scores were as fol-
lows:—
