WEST HERTS v. ABBOTS LANGLEY.
In splendid weather the West Herts Club opened
their cricket season on Saturday afternoon by playing
Abbots Langley, at the West Herts ground. The
visitors went first to the wicket, but were dismissed
for the modest total of 37. West Herts then went to
the wicket, and after doing pretty much what they
liked with the bowling, compiled a score nearly five
times as large as that of their antagonists. The win-
ning point was scored by Lord Essex, and Mr. Hum-
bert contributed a score exactly equal to the entire
Abbot's Langley total. The highest home score was
that of J. A. Murray (44), and the highest Langley
score was that of Parris (11). Abbots Langley went
in a second time, and did a good deal better, having
lost only one wicket when the time was up, and the
score stood at 33.

MELROSE v. BERKHAMSTEAD WORKING MEN.
This match was played on the Recreation Ground,
on Saturday, in beautiful weather, and resulted in a
win for Melrose by 22 runs. Picton won the toss,
and sent the visitors to the wickets first. Mothersole
and Olliffe opened the innings, and they were opposed
to the bowling of Picton (town end) and Collins. The
first ball of the match beat Mothersole, and North
took his place. The first ball in Picton's next over
upset Olliffe's stumps, and from the first ball of
Collin's second over, North was smartly stumped by
Markland—3 for 10, all the runs having been made by
North. After a couple of maiden overs Collins took
two wickets in one over, and the next over saw two
more batsmen dismissed. Collins, after a maiden,
took a couple of wickets in his last over, and Picton
clean bowling Horne with the first ball of his seventh
over, the innings was closed. With the comparatively
small total of 26 runs against them, Melrose took up
the batting. At one time it looked as if there would
be an exciting finish, but on Haskell joining Mark-
ham, a totally different aspect was put on the game
and a total of 48 was reached, so that Melrose were
once more victorious. Score and analysis:

ROAMERS v. CROXLEY.
This match was played at Croxley, on Saturday,
and resulted in an easy win for the Roamers by 62
runs. The Roamers batted first and ran up a total
of 106, of which Holt scored 55. The Croxley bats-
men could do nothing against the bowling of Holt
and Sharpe, A. Raggett being the only one to make a
stand. He knocked up 16 towards the total of 44.

Roxeth were again victorious on Saturday, their
opponents being the Canterbury C.C., whose ground
is at Kilburn. The Kilburn men first took to the
wicket and compiled 31 runs. The bowling of
Phillips and Iredale proving very disastrous, the
former taking 5 wickets for 14 runs and the later 5
for 13 runs.

On Roxeth batting, Iredale played a fine not out
innings of 27, and the total compiled was 65. The
Killburn team's second innings was not quite closed
when stumps were drawn. One wicket had then to
fall and the total compiled was 32—a near escape from
a single innings defeat.

In the second attempt Fleet was most successful
with the ball, taking two wickets for 4 runs, while

Cobb took 5 wickets for 15 runs, and E. Smith 1 for
8 runs.

The Roxeth United Club have an open date so far
as cricket is concerned; but the quoit team will play
Mr. Watkins' team on the ground at Roxeth. More
members are wanted for the quoit section, and as the
subscriptions is only nominal—1s.—those wishing to
join should communicate at once with the secretary,
Mr. Corney.

The Wealdstone Athletic Club ground is to be open
to members on Monday. The field is that just above
the printing factory, and the materials can be procured
at Mr. Mc Adam's up to one o'clock, but not later in
the day. Members are particularly requested not to
go in the long grass if they can possibly avoid it.

There is already a good membership; but others are
wanted, and the secretary, Mr. W. J. Clench, would
be glad at any time to receive the names of any per-
sons wishing to join.

The run of the Harrow Cycling Club on Saturday
was to Amersham. On Saturday next the Club go to
Winchmore, and on Whit Monday to Great Kemble.

The Harrow Athletic and Kodak Tennis Clubs will
play a tennis match on the Athletic Ground on Satur-
day.

Harrow and Harrow Weald met on the Harrow
Weald ground on Saturday, and the visitors won by
20 runs. The home eleven went in first, Body, the
first man, making the highest score—21 runs; C.
Hills put together 20, and Massey made 14 before
being bowled by Page. Petherbridge's average was
4 wickets for 17 runs; Kavanagh took 3 for 27; and
Page 2 for 5. Kavanagh nearly succeeded in doing
the hat trick, taking three wickets with four consecu-
tive balls.

Barker was the main contributor to the 96 of
Harrow, his score being 36. Petherbridge and Page
were the only two others who got into double figures,
making 13 and 12 respectively.
