CHESHIRE COUNTY NEWS: FRIDAY 23 AUGUST 1895-2.

CRICKET.
SATURDAY'S MATCHES.
Stockport v. Werneth.
STOCKPORT WIN BRILLIANTLY.
This match between these old rivals was played
at Cale Green Park in magnificent weather, and
before a large crowd of spectators, it being also
set apart for the benefit of the professionals, Brown
and Disney, who throughout the season have done
splendid work, and it is hoped that they will receive
a substantial reward. It will be remembered that
the first time these teams met at Werneth this
season Stockport won a most interesting match by
eleven runs, due on the whole to the splendid
bowling of the evergreen Sam Brown. On Satur-
day skipper Hartley won the toss, and of course
chose to bat, on a splendid wicket, taking in with
him J. Bates to face the bowling of Hall and
Hodgkinson. The start was none too good from a
Stockport point of view, as Bates in trying to get a
full toss from Hodgkinson to leg was elegantly
caught at slip. Arthur Torkington joined his cap-
tain who sent Hall splendidly for four, but after
scoring ten he was caught at the wicket. Sam
Brown came next and was warmly greeted on the
ground. He immediately got to work, scoring much
faster than his captain. The score had reached 37
when Torkington was caught by Redfern after that
player had missed him. Disney joined his brother
professional and only to see him brilliantly caught
at short slip by Collinson, after playing
a splendid game. H. K. Bromley-Davenport
of Cheshire county and Cambridge University
fame came next, and here a splendid stand took
place, both men defying all the efforts of the
Werneth bowlers. Davenport delighted the
spectators with his big hitting, while Disney, on
the other hand, was playing a most finished game,
his cutting being a treat. At 72 Davenport was
run out in stepping forward to a fast ball from
Searson, which hit the wicket keeper's pads and
came back into the wicket before Davenport
could recover his ground, he had hit up 17 in a
few minutes and he was loudly cheered on retiring.
F. Taylor joined Disney, but shortly after the latter
was caught at mid off for a splendidly played
innings of 21. The telegraph board now
reads 87-6-21. F. Selby came next, but little
needs for further comment with the exception of
Frank Tyler's display, which was a really meritori-
ous performance. His innings of 44 not out included
six four's two three's, and three two's. He was
ably assisted by J. Brewster, who played a nice
innings of eleven before being caught on the
boundary. The innings closed for the respectable
score of 139. After the usual interval Werneth
opened their venture by sending in Wharmby and
Eaton, the attack being entrusted to Sam Brown
and Bromley-Davenport. Brown opened
with a maiden over, but Eaton hit the
amateur all over the [unclear] away for three.
[unclear] after that while he was nicely caught
by Selby at cover point, the first wicket falling at
nine. Bagges took Wharmby's place but with an
addition of six [unclear] Eaton, that player being
defeated by a grand ball from Brown. Redfern
was next in and next out, Selby taking him
beautifully at point off Davenport, and three
wickets were down for 15 runs. Matters never
altered much for with the exception of Heyworth,
Hall, and Searson, no one seemed able to play the
bowling, the innings closing for 70 Stockport
thus being credited with a win over Werneth for
the second time this season. I must just
make a note of the splendid bowling of
Sam Brown and Harry May, the former taking
4 for 18, and the latter 4 for 15 and this
along with the splendid fielding of the Stockport
men, when an odd chance was done in no
small measure to their brilliant victory. The
spectators seemed to fairly enjoy the afternoon's
sport, and especially as to see their own men
come out on the right side.

MANCHESTER v. CHEADLE HOLMS.—Played on
the ground of the latter. The visitors took a
moderate team with them, but proved strong
enough to vanquish the home lot. The game at
times was very exciting, Manchester having seven
wickets down and wanting 30 runs to win, but Mr.
Stills (who played extremely well) on being joined
by Bennett knocked off the runs, the premier club
thus winning by 31 runs and one wicket to fall.
