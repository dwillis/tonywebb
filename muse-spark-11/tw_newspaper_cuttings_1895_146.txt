SWINDERBY AND COLLINGHAM v. LINCOLN LINDUM (A
TEAM).-These teams met at Swinderby on Saturday,
in stormy weather, neither side being fully repre-
sented. Strawson having lost the toss, the Lindum
had to take the field. The home team were first
represented by Wigram and Loveday, the bowling be-
ing shared by the Lindum captain and Fellows.
With the score at nine Wigram was bowled, and Love-
day also lost his wicket three runs later. F. K. Long
and Hylton left at 16, all having been bowled by Fol-
lowes, who had taken four wickets for five runs at
this period of the game. Two runs later White was
bowled by Strawson, and Broadbent, who had fol-
lowed in, returned the next ball to the bowler, six
wickets being down for seventeen. Upon the Rev.
Long and Staley becoming associated runs
came more freely, and it was not until
forty had appeared on the telegraph board that a
separation could be effected, Staley, who had made
13, being the first to leave. At 43 Millns was caught
at the wicket, while four runs later Easton was run
out. When the score had reached 50 Kennedy was
caught at short slip, and the innings closed, Fellows
having taken seven wickets for 26, and Strawson two
for 20. Tweed and S. T. Watkins were opposed by
Wigram and F. K. Long, and with the fourth ball of
the former's first over Watkins was smartly captured
in the slips by White. Weldon filled the vacancy,
and runs came faster. At thirteen Blyton went on
to bowl in place of Long, and at 21 Weldon
was caught by White off Wigram. Fellows then
joined Tweed, and the score continued to rise. At
thirty-eight White was given a trial with the ball,
the change working well, for at 44 Tweed sent one
from the new bowler into E. K. Long's hands, and
one run later Fellows returned one to Wigram. H.
G. Watkins left at 48, and Cousans was joined by
his captain, who, after making a couple of singles,
left it with Cousans to make the winning hits, but the
latter lost his wicket in the same over, Strawson
leaving at the same total. Jeffery left at 56, and
after running a bye Charles was run out, the innings
closing for 67. The home team went in a second time
and had make 24 for three wickets when time was
called. All three wickets fell at ten to Strawson,
who, in trying to catch a sharp return from Staley,
had the misfortune to get his thumb knocked up, and
had to retire from bowling; his three wickets were
obtained for three runs. The Lindum won the match
by seven runs. Score:-

LINCOLN STAMP END v. NEWARK TOWN.-The visit
of Newark Town to the Stamp End ground is looked
upon as the principal home fixture of the season, and
as the locals had obtained the assistance of H. B.
Daft for Saturday's match exceptional interest
attached to the game. Unfortunately for the "gate"
the club anticipated, and for the comfort of those who
love to witness cricket, the weather was most un-
favourable, and it was not until four o'clock that a
start could be made. As soon as it was evident there
would be a game supporters of the club rapidly
appeared on the ground until, considering the weather,
there was a good company. The local captain won
the toss and decided to bat, sending in Harrod and
Mottam to face the bowling of Ringrose and Hollis
(pro.). Runs came steadily, but after making five
Harrod put a ball into the hands of Ryan. Kerrigan
then partnered Mottam, and a good stand was made,
both men playing good cricket. Mottam got several
nasty knocks, and was eventually smartly taken at
point by Jones. H. B. Daft now partnered
Kerrigan, and those present had the pleasure of
witnessing quite a treat in the style of bat-
ting. Daft began carefully until he obtained
complete control over the bowling, and though several
changes were made, it was to no effect. Kerrigan
and Simpson, to the delight of the locals, also showed
their best form, and, although they did not score so
fast as Daft, they played a confident game, and
thoroughly upheld the credit of the club. Whitlock
did not stay long, and then Heywood joined Daft to
give him an opportunity of making his score fifty,
which he was not long in doing, and the innings was
closed with the total at 106, Daft being not out 51.
Daft's driving was both clean and powerful, several
boundary strokes being included in his score, while
he showed a complete command of the bat. But for
the length of the grass in the out-field the scores of
all who made runs would have been larger. The
visitors fared but badly on going to the wicket.
They had about an hour to play, with a chance of
being defeated, while at the best they could only ex-
pect to make a draw. With six wickets down for 32,
rain came on and prevented further play. Richard-
son smartly threw out Dwyer, and a good throw in
by the same player enabled Brown to run out Dr.
Kingrose; a smart catch at the wicket by Brown
getting rid of [unclear] cere. Of the others Heywood
bowled Job, Kerrigan caught and bowled Ryan, and
Cherry was out hit wicket off Harrod. Altogether
the locals are to be congratulated on the game they
played. Score:-

LINCOLN ST. HUGH'S v. MARKET RASEN.-On Whit-
Monday St. Hugh's Club played at Market Rasen
a whole day match on the ground of the latter. Mar-

Kerrigan was not out for a freely played 63, the total

SKEGNESS AND DISTRICT v. NOTTS FORESTERS.-The
opening match of the season came off on Whit-Mon-
day. The weather in the morning was equally and
threatening, but luckily play was not interfered with
during the day. Skegness winning the toss, W. Kit-
wood and the Skegness professional (Dench) opened
for the home eleven, and some good cricket was shown
by the batsmen on a rather tricky wicket, Kitwood
being first to leave after making 13 runs, Dench play-
ing on shortly after to Morley for 14. E. N. Aston
was at the wickets some time before he was c and b
by Hall for a stolidly played innings of 10 runs. T.
Braceby going in sixth wicket down, with the score
at a [unclear] at 17, made matters rather lively, contribu-
ting five fours to the boundary before he was disposed
of for a hard-hit 31 runs, and the total carried to 80.
No further stand was made, the visitors closing for
95 runs. The visitors, after the luncheon in-
terval, sent in J. Mackey and M. P. Smith to face
the bowling of Dr. Cross and Dench. Twenty-one
runs were registered before Mackey was sent back to
a splendid catch low down at mid-off by Kitwood, the
second wicket also falling to another grand catch by
Kitwood-two for 36. A. T. Oliver and M. P. Smith
getting set runs were made at a good pace. Dench
was here relieved by A. M. Wilson, and 73 runs had
been made when Oliver was stumped by Newton off
Cross for a well-played 23-five for 73. The game
now looked in favour of the visiting team who only
wanted 18 runs with five wickets to fall. However, so
well did Dr. Cross bowl, the remaining five batsmen
put on only eight runs, all falling to the Doctor, who
certainly must be congratulated on his maiden venture
with the leather for the Skegness Club, his bowling
analysis reading 16 overs, 4 maidens, 34 runs, 6
wickets, the home team winning their opening match
by the narrow majority of nine runs. Score:-

SKEGNESS AND DISTRICT v. COOPER CORAH
(LEICESTER)-The second match of the season was
played on Tuesday last, and was chiefly remarkable
for another fine bowling performance by Dr. Cross,
who went even "one better" than on the previous
day when playing Notts Foresters, as against the
visitors from Leicester he took nine wickets, at a
cost of only ten runs, the home team winning easily
owing to the fine display of bowling already referred
to. For Skegness W. Kitwood and Dr. Cross opened
against the deliveries of Eastwood and Porring, W.
Kitwood, who was in good form, doing most of the
run-getting. T. Wilson made some good hits for
his ten runs, and E. N. Ashton played steady
cricket for a like number. Dench and Kitwood
played good cricket while together, the latter being
eventually bowled by Eastwood for a well-played in-
nings of 38 runs. The professional's wicket was the
last to fall, after making 24 in good form, the in-
nings closing for 113 runs. The team from Leicester
sent in Towers and Mountney to the bowling of Dr.
Cross and Dench. With the second ball delivery
Epton caught Towers at mid-off, off Cross, and five
wickets were actually down to Cross in the first four
overs delivered for three runs. The only one to
make any stand was Mountney, who made 18 not
out by steady cricket, the Leicester team being all
out for the meagre score of 41 runs. Dr. Cross's
analysis is again worthy of mention, viz. :-Ten
overs, four balls, five maidens, ten runs, nine wickets.
