OAKHURST V WYNSTAY.—Played on Saturday at
Wynstay. The home team were all dismissed for 43, O.
Ormrod being the only one who could make any stand
against the bowling of Fred Gough and H. Aston. Oak-
hurst scored 71. Fred Gough batted well for 23, being the
only one getting into double figures for his side. Oakhurst
won a good match by 28.

SHREWSBURY CLUB V SHREWSBURY SCHOOL.—Played
at the School on July 29 and 30, and resulted in a draw in
favour of the County. This was the final match at the
School, the season having been an eminently successful
one, only one game having been lost. A word of praise is
due to their coaches, Messrs A. Watson (Lancashire), and
G. H. Palmer (late Northants and Worcestershire), who have
undoubtedly worked great improvement in the cricket at the
School. Score:—

OSWESTRY V GUILSFIELD.—Played at Oswestry on
Saturday, in anything but cricket weather. The visitors
batted first, but could do nothing with the bowling of H
T Gough and Sabine, and were all disposed of for thirty-
two. Captain Hayhurst-France and Moore started the
batting for the town, and the score was taken to twenty
when the Captain was bowled by a good ball from A
Robinson, whilst in the next over Moore was smartly stumped
by H Mytton after making eleven. "B T" was bowled
by H M Pryce-Jones for five, and Corfield succumbed to
the same bowler for a like number. Campbell came in
next, and at once seemed to settle down. Moore was
bowled by Makepeace for thirty-two. Minshall, who came
next, was content with defending his wicket whilst Camp-
bell did the hitting, but when the wicket did fall, the latter
was not out with fifty to his credit. Oswestry were
thus left winners by ninety-one runs with five wickets to
spare. Score:—

OSWESTRY V BIRKENHEAD VICTORIA.—Played at
Oswestry on Monday, before a good company. The
visitors were the first to bat, and they started most
disastrously, Gough bowling Brocklebank with his first
ball. The wicket was favouring the bowlers who made
good use of their opportunity, but if one or two chances
had not been thrown away, the score would not have
been so large. France and Clapp opened the Oswestry
innings, and both seemed at once to settle down. The
score was taken to 62, when rain interfered with the
game. On resuming, the necessary runs were made, when
rain again stopped play. After waiting some time it was
decided to abandon the match. Oswestry were thus left
winners by 9 wickets. Score:—
