MR. SHEPHERD'S XI. beat WOOLLEY PARK.
This match was played at Woolley Park on
Wednesday. Woolley going in first put together the
total of 67, of which Haydon and Smith did best, with
17 and 14 respectively. Mr. Shepherd's team then
went in the score at lunch time standing at 61 for
4 wickets, Hutchins and Weaver playing soundly
for their runs. The innings closed for 92, Woolley
going in again put on 56, Mr. Shepherd's team now
requiring 32 runs to win, obtained them with the loss
of 3 wickets, Hutchins again doing well. Both teams
were kindly entertained to tea after the match by Mr.
Wroughton. Score:

INKPEN beat FALKLAND C.C.
The visitors to meet the Inkpen C.C. on their own
ground were rather late in coming, the time fixed for
the match being two o'clock, while the game did not
commence till about four. The home team won the
toss and play quickly began. The chances of winning
looked rather small at one time, four wickets having
fell for 10 runs. E. Rance soon turned the tide by
adding 29 runs in capital style, his score consisting
of a splendid drive for 6, 3, two 4s, and the rest
singles. The Rev. H. D. Butler made a good hit for 4,
and when the innings was closed carried his bat.
G. Butler and J. Hopkins did not bat. The visitors
were dismissed for 28 runs, mainly owing to the first-
rate bowling of T. May, who took 7 wickets, his average
being 1.5. Score:—

LAMBOURN beat CHILTON.
The return match was played on Saturday last, on
the Lambourn ground, and, as the score will show,
resulted in a win for Lambourn, the homesters thus
avenging their defeat in the previous match. The
game did not commence till rather late, and conse-
quently had to be decided on the first innings. The
weather was delightful and the wicket really good.
Chilton won the toss, but elected to send the home
team in. A very poor start was made, as two Lam-
bourn wickets went down for 0, but a wonderfully
different complexion was put on the game, when, after
H. Penfold had scored seven in good style, F. Harris
and J. Bryant got well set. F. Harris made some
splendid hits, and the spectators loudly cheered his
plucky play, his score of 35 being made in a very short
time. J. Bryant played capital cricket for his 38, and
was greatly applauded upon his retirement. V. Hobbs
and C. J. Maberley rendered great assistance to the
score, before the innings was declared closed at 128.
The Chilton team did but little, only one player (S.
Panting) obtaining double figures. The bowling of
Messrs. Hobbs and Bryant was very keen, V. Hobbs
having made two capital catches in the long field.
J. Rumball bowled on, and at the call of time no wicket
had fallen, the score being 51, H. New and Rumball
having scored well during the innings. Score:—

SOUTHINGTON beat MR. A. KINGSMILL'S XII
This highly exciting and closely contested match was
played in lovely weather on the charmingly situated
Sydmonton Cricket Ground by youthful enthusiasts,
the ages being limited to 16, several, however, had many
years still before them ere they reach this degree of
experience. We would draw attention to the excellent
bowling on the Southington side of Koeller and
Poynter, and the batting of these and also Christie and
North. The star of the Sydmonton team was J. Chute
whose bowling was as energetic as his batting, and his
[unclear] was rewarded by the presentation bat offered
for the highest score by Mr. Wyndham Portal. Avery
and Harrison had some mishaps in the downfall of the
Southington wickets, aided by the smart wicket-
keeping of Andrew Kingsmill, in spite of their
strenuous exertions Southington proved successful
by the narrow margin of seven runs. Score:

BURGHCLERE beat NEWTOWN.
Played on the Sandford Priory ground and ended
in a defeat for the home team. Burghclere won the
toss and batted first; three wickets fell quickly, but on
Rev. G. Hicks and G. Kimber getting together runs
were piled on fast, Kimber being run out for a well-
played 19, Rev. G. Hicks carrying out his bat for a
brilliantly-hit innings of 25. Newtown made no stand
whatever, being all dismissed for 41, Allsop alone
getting double figures. This match brought the
Newtown season to a close, after a fairly successful
season, they having played 16 matches, won nine and lost
eight. After a very disastrous start they pulled up in
their last fixtures, winning three out of their last five.

NEWTOWN beat FABER CHURCH INSTITUTE.
This return match was played in the beautiful
grounds of Sandford Park, on Saturday, August 31st,
there being a goodly number of spectators present,
including many ladies. The Family and company,
Newtown went first to the wickets, and by good play
and hard hitting on the part of Riffold (22), and
Gardiner (15), made a total of 68 runs, Gunning's
bowling being too good for them, he being well backed
up by Matthews and Fewtrell. On the Institute
handling the willow, however, they were soon disposed
of for 30 runs, Vickers being top scorer with 16. Gibbs,
Fifield, Allsop, and Baverstock being the bowlers.
Tea was then partaken of, and Newtown again went
to the wickets, but the Institute not being able to go
in again, the home team won a pleasant game on the
first innings by eight runs.

ADBURY HOUSE beat NEWTOWN HOUSE.
On Thursday afternoon a match was played between
elevens of the Adbury House estate and Newtown
House, the game being won by the Adbury House
eleven, a result being mainly due to the good bowling
of H. Barber, F. James, and J. Gasson; G. Cooper
brought off a beautiful catch at short slip, and [unclear]
made a big drive for five runs. The teams M.
Stratford, White, b Gasson and H. White acquitted them-
selves very well. A capital tea had been prepared by
the housekeeper on the grounds, which the players did
justice too. Altogether a most enjoyable afternoon
was spent, and a hearty vote of thanks was carried
with acclaim to Mr. Walter Arkwright for his kindness.
