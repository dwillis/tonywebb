CRICKET.

1ST. SHEPHERD'S XI., beat WOOLLEY PARK.
This match was played at Woolley Park on Wednesday. Woolley going in first put together the total of 67, of which Hayden and Smith did best, with 17 and 14 respectively. Mr. Shepherd's team then went in the score at lunch time standing at 61 for 5 wickets, Hutchins and Weaver playing soundly for their runs. The innings closed for 92, Woolley going in again put on 56, Mr. Shepherd's team now requiring 32 runs to win, obtained them with the loss of 3 wickets, Hutchins again doing well. Both teams were kindly entertained to tea after the match by Mr. Wroughton. Score:

INKPEN beat FALKLAND C.C.
The visitors to meet the Inkpen C.C. on their own ground were rather late in coming, the time fixed for the match being two o'clock, while the game did not commence till about four. The home team won the toss and play quickly began. The chances of winning looked rather small at one time, four wickets having fell for 20 runs. E. Rance soon turned the tide by adding 29 runs more in capital style, his score consisting of a splendid drive for 4, 3, two 2's, and the rest singles. The Rev. H. D. Butler made a good hit for 4, and when the innings was closed carried out his bat. G. Butler and J. Hopkins did not bat. The visitors were dismissed for 29 runs, mainly owing to the first-rate bowling of T. May, who took 7 wickets, his average being 1.9. Score:--

LAMBOURN beat CHILTON.
The return match was played on Saturday last, on the Lambourn ground, and, as the score will show, resulted in a win for Lambourn, the homesters thus avenging their defeat in the previous match. The game did not commence till rather late, and consequently had to be decided on the first innings. The weather was delightful and the wicket really good. Chilton won the toss, but elected to send the home team in. A very poor start was made, as two Lambourn wickets went down for 0, but a wonderfully different complexion was put on the game, when, after H. Penfold had scored seven in good style, F. Harris
and J. Bryant got well set. F. Harris made some splendid hits, and the spectators loudly cheered his blocky play, his score of 35 being made in a very short time. J. Bryant played capital cricket for his 38, and was greatly applauded upon his retirement. V. Hobbs and C. J. Maberly rendered great assistance to the score, before the innings was declared closed at 128. The Chilton team did but little, only one player (S. Ponting) obtaining double figures. The bowling of Messrs. Hobbs and Bryant was very deadly, and V. Harris made two capital catches in the long field. SOUTHINGTON beat MR. A. KINGSMILL'S XII
This highly exciting and closely contested match was played in lovely weather on the charmingly situated Sydmonton Cricket Ground by youthful enthusiasts, the age being limited to 16, several, however, had many years still before them ere they reach this degree of experience. We would draw attention to the excellent bowling on the Southington side of Knoeller and Poynter, and the batting of those and also Christie and North. The star of the Sydmonton team was J. BURGHCLERE beat NEWTOWN.
Played on the Sandford Priory ground and ended in a defeat for the home team. Burghclere won the toss and batted first; three wickets fell quickly, but on Rev. G. Hicks and G. Kimber getting together runs were piled on fast, Kimber being run out for a well-played 12, Rev. G. Hicks carrying out his bat for a brilliantly-hit innings of 25. Newtown made no stand whatever, being all dismissed for 41, Allsop alone getting double figures. This match brought the Newtown season to a close, after a fairly successful season, they having played 14 matches, won six and lost eight. After a very disastrous start they pulled up in their last fixtures, winning three out of their last five. Score:--

NEWTOWN beat FABER CHURCH INSTITUTE.
This return match was played in the beautiful grounds of Sandford Park, on Saturday, August 31st, there being a goodly number of spectators present, including lady friends and family and company. Newtown went first to the wickets, and by good play and hard hitting on the part of Biffeld (22), and Gardiner (18), made a total of 65 runs, Gunner's bowling being too good for them, he being well backed up by Matthews and Fewtrell. On the Institute handling the willow, however, they were soon disposed of for 30 runs, Vickers being top scorer with 16, Gibbs, Biffeld, Allsop, and Bayerstock being the bowlers. Tea was then partaken of, and Newtown again went to the wickets, but the Institute not being able to go in again, the home team won a pleasant game on the first innings by eight runs.

ADBURY HOUSE beat NEWTOWN HOUSE.
On Thursday afternoon a match was played between elevens of the Adbury House estate and Newtown House, the game being won by the Adbury House eleven, a result being mainly due to the good bowling of H. Barber, P. James, and J. Gasson; G. Cooper brought off a beautiful catch at short slip, and Steere made a big drive for five runs. Two notable M's, Stratford, White, b Barber might acquitted themselves with the bat. A capital tea had been prepared by the housekeeper on the grounds, which the players did justice too. Altogether a most enjoyable afternoon was spent, and a hearty vote of thanks was carried with acclaim to Mr. Walter Arkwright for his kindness. Score:
