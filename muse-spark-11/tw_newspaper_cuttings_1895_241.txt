LORD COBHAM'S XI. v. MR. ISAAC'S XI. (UNDER
17).—This match was played on the County Ground
at Houghton on August 14th, in very pleasant
weather. Lord Cobham's boys batted first, and made

a good start, the first five all getting into double
figures, of which Seymour's 26 was the highest score.
Ninety-five was reached when the fifth wicket fell, but
the last five only added 18 runs, B. Foster taking
four for 20 runs. The home eleven made an unfor-
tunate start, losing two wickets for 6 runs.
Thanks, however, to a good innings by R. Mackin-
tosh, and some free hitting by Harkness and F. Isaac,
the total amounted to 95. Lord Cobham's XI in
their second innings made 77 for the loss of four
wickets, of which C. Mackintosh scored an excellent
45 not out, including five 4's and two 3's. The
innings being closed at this point, Mr. Isaac's boys
had to hit for 75 minutes, with a balance of 95 runs to
wipe off. Again they had a very poor start, as three
wickets fell for a single run. J. Isaac and Harkness
put, however, a different face upon matters, and
raised the score materially. Harkness, who left at 36,
hit one 6 in his freely made 22. Isaac was still not
out for 23 when time was called. The match was
decided by the first innings, which gave victory to
Lord Cobham's team by 18 runs. Score:—

LORD COBHAM'S XI. v. MR. GIBBS' XI.—The
annual boys' match at Hagley was played in Hagley
Park, on Aug. 15. Although two of the players
were 17 years and three more were 16, the great
majority were of 15 and 14, while three were only 13.
As by far the greater number of those playing had
taken part in matches during the four preceding
years, the game produced much interest. The day
was fine, though dull; the wickets, in the earlier part
of the day was somewhat difficult. J. Gibbs won the
toss and put his men in first. They made a disastrous
start, losing three wickets for only two runs. P. Lyon and
Jones-Williams improved matters by some steady
play, but the whole side were out for 42, of which
eight were extras, J. Lyttelton going on to bowl in
the last part of the innings, took three wickets for one
run. O. Mackintosh also did well, capturing four
for 13. The opening of the innings of Lord Cobham's
XI, was no better than that of their opponents, as
four men were out for nine runs. Harkness and K.
Mackintosh improved matters considerably, but they
were treated kindly by some of the fielders, since five
catches (not all easy ones) were dropped
in a very short space of time. When
Harkness left at 28, the end was not long in coming,
and the total fell short by two runs of that of their
opponents. P. Isaac, whose bowling and fielding
were alike admirable, took five wickets for 14 runs,
and made four catches. In their second innings the
visitors made a much better show, Jones-Williams
and W. H. Gibbs put on 28 runs for the second wicket,
and O. A. Lyon, aided by his brother, added 31 for
the sixth. The former made the highest score of the
match, a freely hit 23. The other batsmen all did
something, and, with the aid of 14 extras, 86 was
reached when the 10th wicket fell. Of the bowlers in
this innings, N. Talbot came out best, taking in 18.3
overs four wickets for 16 runs. Lord Cobham's team
were left with 90 to make in about an hour and a half.
An excellent catch by P. Lyon off his own bowling
took the first wicket for O. Masters improved some-
what, and Harkness again did much service to his
side by vigorous hitting. Seymour and E. Mackintosh
also got double figures, and their score reached 65 for
seven wickets when barely 10 minutes remained for
play. Two men, however, failed to score, and, as the
last man was absent, the innings was brought to a
close just before time for 70, leaving the other side
victorious, after a very interesting match, by 28 runs.
Apart from the errors mentioned above, the all-round
fielding was good on both sides. Score:—

ST. JOHN'S 2ND XI. v. MALVERN 2ND XI.—The
return match between these clubs was played at
Malvern on Thursday, and resulted in a win for St.
John's by 11 runs. St. John's lost seven wickets for
21 runs, and then the last three put on 22. Mr. L.
James, who was the only one to reach double figures,
hit well for his total. Messrs. Park, Isaac, and Curtis
also batted well. For Malvern, Messrs. W. H. Hart-
land and Homer were top scorers; but, owing to some
smart catches being taken, the total only reached 32.
The bowling of Messrs. Bannister, Robinson and Robinson for
St. John's and Messrs. Clegg and Jenkins for Malvern
was very good. Score:—
