NEWCASTLE DAILY JOURNAL: THURSDAY 11 July 1895.

CRICKET NOTES.
(By "CUSTOS.")

We are frequently told that what spectators like to see are batsmen who make things lively for the bowlers; and, if this is really so, those who saw the game between Captain Nicholls' and Mr Bewicke's teams would enjoy themselves. Though both Vim, Thompson and Butler played exceptionally well, their performances were quite overshadowed by the doings of George Simpson, who, after taking seven wickets for 41 runs, scored 177, and was not out at the close. So far as I could see, Simpson never made a bad stroke until he had reached his hundred, and the chance he gave to Butler at 110 was a difficult one for the fielder to judge. The hitting was, as is usual with Simpson, beautifully timed and clean, and none of the bowlers seemed to give him any trouble, though Walters almost bowled him second hall. V. V. Thompson's 89 was a capital display of steady cricket; and Butler's 82 was obtained in his best style. There was a large gathering of spectators in the field, and after such an interesting game we may expect good crowds at the Constabulary Ground throughout the remainder of the season.

So far this season the Rev. E. W. R. Walters has played in five matches for Mr Bewicke's team, and has an average of over 120 runs per innings. More power to his elbow, say I.

Who is the very superior young man who writes on Northumberland cricket for "Pa- Jack's paper? In his opinion there is only one good team—the Borderers—in the county, and but one competent critic of the game—himself. With regard to the truth, it is a well-known and not very prominent local club, who some years ago came from the south with a great reputation as a player, but with each succeeding season his reputation has grown less, until now, not being able to score off his opponents on the field with either bat or ball, he has taken to the pen, in the hope, I suppose, of playing a winning game. Northumbrian batsmen may not be Graces nor Shrewsburys; but they are able to hold their own with the bowlers which south have, up to now, sent us, if we take the Borderers' "Knight of the pen" as a specimen.

It is pure moonshine for any one to talk of the Borders as representing the best cricketing talent in the county; and no difficulty could be experienced in getting together a much stronger eleven than they put into the field. In fact, what victories they have gained here have been chiefly over school boys; and were the next team chosen to play for Northumberland probably only one Borderer—K Spencer—would be sure of a place in it. At the present time several prominent county gentlemen are trying to improve cricket in these parts. Towards this end they are inviting strong southern teams to visit them; and sarcastic remarks about those connected with the game, and the players, from the pen of the Borderers' superior young man will not help them much. Perhaps, after all, it is not so much as an outside critic, or a first class player, that he—while at play falling off in bowling he attributes to the weakness of local batsmen, as he can only bowl well against good men. What a pity he cannot play in the south.

Some time ago a committee was formed with the intention of resuscitating county cricket in Northumberland, but as the Corporation have refused to grant a field, and negotiations with landowners for one have fallen through, Mr H. O. Mortimer will, I believe, call a general meeting shortly, and lay the matter before it.

I notice that the Elswick Works Club has fixed their match with Benwell on July 27 as [unclear]'s benefit game. A more payable and painstaking professional cannot be found than Elsey, and as a player he ranks among the best in the North. His play on Saturday in polishing off many of their engagements, and I am sure every member will do his best to increase the fund to be handed over to their professional, who, by his ability on the field and conduct off, does as much as any one connected with it to uphold its honour and dignity.

I hope my readers will not forget that the game between South Northumberland and Elswick Works, at Gosforth, on Saturday, is for the benefit of the professional Goodband. This player is certainly one of the best we have ever had in the North of England, and deserves a bumper gate.

On Saturday Stockton visited Darlington, and, thanks to McDonald's 73 run out, defeated the home team easily. Lieut. Coates had another far out against Durham School, and had hard lines in missing a century by 7 runs; while his batting was followed up by some good bowling on the the part of Ferguson. The meeting between South Shields and Tynemouth for Copeland's benefit, furnished some good cricket, but ended in a draw. Wallace could do little with the bowling of Simpson (5 for 13) and Goodband (4 for 12) and were all out for 31. What was the matter with North Durham on Saturday? Did they all get the "bump" together, for there was certainly something wrong with them! For six of them to go down without scoring requires some explanation.

Sunderland, though they succeeded in beating Consett at Consett, could do no better than draw on Saturday at Sunderland; though has the full five hours were not played I believe they intend to claim full match points. A good performance of the day was that of Elsa-v in the match against the Royal Grammar School; for after taking seven wickets for 14 runs he came out top scorer of his side with a well-played 50. Up to the beginning of this month he had got 67 wickets for less than 6 runs each—a bowling feat that will take some beating.

St. George's and Northumberland met on Saturday, in fine weather, before a small gate. Northumberland batted first, and were got rid of very cheaply for 113. Moss took five wickets; Bell, three; and V. Thompson, two for 1 run. Hoare batted well for 38. On St. George's going in a complete rot took place, and they were all out in less than an hour for 28. Not the best form was shown. Every one seemed to think that because Thompson got wickets for nothing there was no necessity for any one else. This won't do. Their cracks cannot come off every time, and the rest of the team must make up their minds to do a fair share in the run getting. The fielding on both sides was very good.

MR BEWICKE'S XI. v. NORTHERN NOMADS.
A two days' match between these teams commenced on the Close House Ground on Tuesday, and finished yesterday in a decisive victory for Mr Bewicke's eleven by an innings and 66 runs. Batting first on Tuesday, the home team made 264, the Rev E W R Walters playing a fine innings of 67, while Guttridge and Captain Wilkinson hit hard for 55 and 45 respectively. After the Nomads had commenced well with Clayton and Mortimer, Guttridge and Walters quickly disposed of several of the other batsmen, and it was not until P Ashworth and Morton came together that a second effective stand was made. Both played steadily, but none of the others could do anything with the bowling, and the side were all out for 149. Following up, they fared badly against Guttridge and A A Mewburn, the professional getting four wickets for 32 runs, while Mewburn's four only cost 16 runs. The innings realised 49, P Ashworth and Clayton alone reaching double figures. Score:—
