St. John's v. Bromsgrove.--This match was played at St. John's on Saturday. A capital game resulting in favour of St. John's by 37 runs. Bromsgrove won the toss, but Mr. Beach Hicks and T. Straw both bowled so well, and were so ably supported by capital fielding, that the visitors were all disposed of for 33 runs, Straw taking five wickets for 7 runs. St. John's on taking the bat soon lost four wickets, through the excellent bowling of Rolleston, for 15 runs, but on Mr. Young joining Mr. Curtis an invaluable stand was made, and, thanks to their excellent batting, the score was taken to 61 before the next wicket fell. Mr. Curtis playing capital cricket for his 16, and Mr. Young an equally good innings for 17. Mr. Park also hit well for his score, and the innings closed for 70. Rolleston bowled splendidly, and Messrs. J. K. and E. Perkins shared the bowling at the other end, and were well backed up by the excellent fielding. Score :--

WORCESTER GRAMMAR SCHOOL v. HARTLEBURY GRAMMAR SCHOOL--Played at Flag Meadow on Saturday. Hartlebury batted first and were all sent back for 20. Of these Vale, who was top score with nine, played very steadily. Thorp took eight wickets for ten runs. Worcester's prospects did not look very bright when two good wickets went for one bye, but on Davis and Thorp being associated runs came more briskly, and their opponents' total was passed with four wickets down. Ultimately the whole side was dismissed for 34 Davis playing very well for his 15. Vale and Dorsett both bowled very well for Hartlebury. Worcester Grammar School have now played nine, won six lost three. Score :--

HANLEY CASTLE GRAMMAR SCHOOL v. WORCESTER CATHEDRAL VOLUNTARY CHOIR.--The return match between these teams was played at Hanley Castle on Saturday, and resulted, as in the first match, in a victory for the Grammar School. The Choir batted first, on a good pitch, but wickets fell rapidly before the excellent bowling of Chadd, who took seven wickets for 7 runs, and Hemus who took three wickets for 4 runs. The innings realised 16 runs. Chadd and Pope started well for the Grammar School, making 22 runs for the first wicket. Hemus and Harwaras also batted in good form against the somewhat weak bowling of the Choir. After tea, which was served on the ground, Chadd continued batting in excellent style, and carried out his bat for 71 runs. Stumps were drawn at 6.30, when the School had made 123 runs for six wickets. Score :--
