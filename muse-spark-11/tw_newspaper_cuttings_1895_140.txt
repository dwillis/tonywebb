HERTFORDSHIRE EXPRESS: SATURDAY 9 NOVEMBER 1895-1.

LOCAL CRICKET AVERAGES, 1895.

BEDS. COUNTY C.C.

With a balance of losses over gains the past season
can hardly be said to have been a thoroughly satisfac-
tory one with this club-although, as yet, and to some
extent, cricket is still played for the sake of the game
among the counties which do not rank as first-class, and
the existence of which consequently does not depend
upon the mere results of matches. The number of
drawn games-two out of eleven-is agreeably small;
for if one thing more than another tends to lessen the
interest of these two-day engagements it is the annoying
frequency of draws. Of the remaining nine Bedford-
shire won four and lost five. That the county's measure
of success was not greater must be put down, more than
to any other cause, to the partial failure of the capt. In
and secretary as a batsman, Mr. Thring's aggregate of
253 in 13 innings falling far short of what was expected
of him in a season so favourable to high scoring. At
the same time he is practically at the head of the list
of averages, Mr. Beasley having played less than half as
many matches as his captain. Messrs. J. Manton and
C. G. Pope have likewise done fairly well with the bat.
Mr. W. F. Brown's record of 64 wickets at an average
cost of 12 runs is a remarkable one for a slow bowler in
such a summer as that of 1895.

HITCHIN C.C.

The Hitchin Club has had a fairly successful season,
having played 19 matches, 10 of which were won, 7 lost
and 2 drawn. The club was successful against Cambridge
Nibillists, St. Albans, City Police, Belgrave, Knebworth,
Arlesey (2), Baldock, Honts. County and the Commer-
cial Union C.C. It was defeated by the Nibillists,
National Provincial Bank C.C., Mr. W. Wright's Notts
XI, Mr. Strauss's Notts XI, Old Northovians, Three
Counties Asylum and M.C.C. The two drawn games
were with St. Albans and Gentlemen of Herts. It was
a source of great disappointment that the club in the
better class of matches-notably the Notts matches-
was very poorly represented; and that this was the case
is little encouragement to the Hon. Secretary to try and
introduce higher standard of cricket in the town. Turn-
ing to the performances of the individual players we
find A. H. Delme-Radcliffe at the top of the batting
averages with an average of 30 for 9 innings. The next
four batsmen, who alone played more than occasionally,
have also excellent figures. Wilson's average of 20,
like his aggregate, is probably the best since the com-
mencement of his long connection with the club. In
Captain E. Rivett-Carnac and his brother the club gained
new adherents whose services with the bat proved
valuable, and the former at times did considerable
execution with his slow underhand bowling. The dry
weather and consequent good wickets have left their
trace in the averages of the bowlers, even Wilson's
capital record showing a falling off as compared with
previous performances.

BALDOCK C.C.

This club has had a very successful season. Ten
matches have been played, seven being won and five lost.
The wins were against Ashwell, Mr. H Tanqueray's
Cambridge XI, Gottered, Three Counties Asylum,
Knebworth and Stevenage (2); the losses were with
Brixton Wanderers, Hitchin and Ashwell. This is the
best record the club has had since the year 1888, when
ten matches were won and two lost. Two second
eleven matches were also played with Graveley, both of
which were won; and two matches with the Baldock
Fire Brigade, one being lost and the other won. The
Fire Brigade match we hope to see made an annual
fixture, and to make it more popular still a whole day
should be allotted to it if possible. W. Bracey wins
the bat with an average of 12.36. H. J. Izzard has the
good average of 21, but has not played in sufficient
matches to take the bat. W. Payne, C. J. Peck and
H. Tanqueray have averages exceeding 10. The ball
presented by Mr. C. J. Vaisey for the best bowling
average is won by H. J. Izzard, F. Pilton not having
taken the required number of wickets.

HITCHIN BLUE CROSS TEMPERANCE
BRIGADE C.C.

During the past season 18 matches were played, of
which nine were won and nine lost. The chief weakness,
as will be seen by a glance at the figures, has been in
the batting, not one player having a double-figure
average, although the wickets for the greater part of
the season were favourable to run-getting. In bowling
Basterfield and Anderson did so well that a change was
rarely needed, and both have creditable records; Hills
(underhand) also proved very useful at times. The
fielding of the team, taking the season all through, left
much to be desired, and with increased smartness in
this department more than one game might have had
a different ending. The majority of the games were
closely contested, and at one time three matches in
succession finished with a difference of only one run
(two lost and one won). The matches won were against
Knebworth, Stevenage 2nd XI, St. Paulswalden (2), St.
Ibbs, Hitchin St. Saviour's (2), King's Cross G.N.R.
and Fosse School, Cambridge. Defeats were inflicted by
Stevenage 2nd XI, Arlesey (2), St. Iotts, Hitchin
Grammar School, Hitchin G.N.R (2), Knebworth and
Fosse School, Cambridge.
