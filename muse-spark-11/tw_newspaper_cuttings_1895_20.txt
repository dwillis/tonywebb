READING STANDARD : FRIDAY 23 AUGUST 1895-2.

READING V. ABINGDON.—That the cricket field is one of the favourite haunts of King Chance was evidenced beyond doubt in this arrangement, which was decided at Reading on Saturday. On the occasion of the first match, played a short time since at Abingdon, Reading were represented by a fairly powerful combination, nevertheless they were the recipients of a decisive "thrashing." The Reading "eleven," however, created a mild surprise in this engagement, as despite that their eleven on paper was particularly weak they turned the compliment they received at Abingdon with slight interest, insomuch as they secured more than an innings' victory. The home captain ordered his colleagues to the wickets first and before Crowthurst and Campion ceased their partnership 41 runs had been obtained. No serious advance in the score was recorded before the promising G Williams handled the willow, he going in third wicket down. Between the third and fourth dismissal 52 chalks were added, but after that stage no rapid scoring was chronicled, and when the umpires declared the homesters' essay at a termination, 143 was the total. Williams with a score of 41 was the not out, the other chief contributors being Crowthurst (17), Campion (32), F Collins (11) and Eynott (11), each of whom provided a good exhibition. On the visitors facing the attack of Campion and Eynott, it was not difficult to forecast that their fate was sealed after but a few minutes play. Dandridge, who is generally looked upon as a safe bat, was caught for one run only had been compiled when three wickets were down three chalks was the grand total. Lovatt scored fourteen, but in doing so had many fresh partners, as might be gathered from the remark that at the fall of fourth wicket the total was 13 ; five, 14 ; six, five ; seven, 20 ; eight, 20 ; nine, 35 ; ten, 39. On following on, the visitors started in healthy style as the first wicket realised 23. A return, however, was noticeable in the first innings, and again apparent, as the board showed that in their second innings only 63 were compiled. Of this number Dandridge notched 22 and A. B. Morland 13. So splendidly did both Campion and H. Eynott trundle, that the home captain was content to rely on the twain only in the trundling department. That his judgment was sound was demonstrated by the captain's fourteen wickets in the match produced: Campion in the first innings bowled 48 balls, and secured one maiden and six wickets at a cost of twenty runs, and in the second delivered 93 balls, and again secured six wickets for 34 runs. Eynott's analysis read: First innings, nine overs, three maidens, 13 runs and four wickets. Second: Twelve overs, three maidens, 23 runs, four wickets. Score:—
