MILTON.

The M.C.C. had their old friends, Leek Church,
as visitors to play them a friendly match, which
resulted in an easy win for Milton. Leek, batting
first, could only make the score of 31, all out,
Charding being the only man to make any show with
the bat against the bowling of Thursfield and Mase-
field, Thursfield taking five wickets for 14 runs, and
Masefield five for 17. The Miltonians did very well,
making 65 for five wickets, Flatt 20, not out, and
Lilley 12, being the chief contributors.

BIGNALL END.

This team were at home last Saturday, engaged
in a League match with Silverdale Second. The
visitors are showing excellent form this season, and a
good number of spectators journeyed to the Bignall
End ground to witness the match. The home club
were not fully represented, A. Dale and R. War-
burton being absentees. The visitors won the toss,
and decided to bat, sending Stanyer and Ellerton to
oppose the bowling of T. Burgess and J. Dale.
The former soon bowled Ellerton in his
first over when he had scored 2, and in his next over
he bowled G. Ellams for a duck. J. Dale caught
and bowled Baston for 1, and T. Burgess got Stanyer
l b w when he had scored 3. This was a very poor
start for the visitors, four good wickets being down
for 9 runs. J. Ellams and F. Dean became part-
ners and a stand was made, the score being run to
20 before F. Dean succumbed to a catch by T.
Burgess off Dale. J. Ellams batted well for 22.
The innings realised 53. If I am not mistaken, this is
the visitors least score this season. The home team
commenced with the usual pair, W. Mayer and E.
Mayer. The latter was caught by Stanyer off
Rushton before he could score, and W. Mayer was
caught off the same bowler when he had made 7; not
a very good start. J. Dale and G. Burgess became
partners, and both batted in good style. Several
changes of bowling were tried but each came in for
punishment. The visitors' score was reached before
the third wicket fell. J. Dale made 13. The
innings realised 72, and we won by 29 runs.
The Second team were at Crewe and got defeated
by 22 runs. J. White made 13 not out, H. Foster
9, E Green 7, A. Warburton and A. Proctor 6 each.
On Monday the League team played the Audley
football team captained by J. Dale. The footballers
made 60, and the League team 150 for seven wickets.
The top scorers were J. Bowley (69) and W.
Mayer (37.)

There was some good scoring done in most of the
League matches played on Saturday. A fine day,
with wickets hard and fast, could scarcely bring
about any other result.
At Stoke the Club and Ground met Leek, and the
"City of the Moorlands" team put on the very re-
spectable total of 135 before the last wicket fell.
Kirkby, who made 46 against Crewe a week ago,
again played a big innings, making 39, his highest
total up to the present in League cricket. Messrs.
Cuntrill, Wright, and Buckwell assisted in bringing
up the total. The Club and Ground replied with
126 for seven wickets, a wonderful performance, con-
sidering the time at the Club's disposal. Messrs.
Heaton, Orallan, Billingzley, and Dawson were the
principal remitters, and when time was called three
wickets had to fall with, only 10 runs to make. On
paper the draw was certainly in favour of the County
Club.
At Burslem the "wooden spoonists" paid a visit,
and against them Burslem totted up what to the
present has been its top score in League matches
this year, viz., 167 for nine wickets. Brownfield
played a good hitting innings for 52, and Hulme,
Lomas, Booth and Ellis, all reached a respectable
score. Norton's response was a very feeble one, not
one of its players even reaching double figures, and
an innings, which was very monotonous, was
finished when 41 runs were totalled.
A rather fluctuating fixture was fought out by
Porthill and Fenton at Wolstanton. When Porthill
had made has the poor score of 43 runs they had
lost seven of their best wickets, but the tail wagged
considerably, and the respectable total of 121 for
nine wickets was at last reached, mainly owing to
the creditable innings by Holdcroft (40, not out),
Bateman (28), and the Rev. F. W. Haden (12).
Fenton's innings was the exact opposite to Port-
hill's, the major portion of the runs being made in
the early part of the innings. Messrs. J. Turner
and C. Merritt put on 43 runs between them, and at
this juncture it must have appeared favourable to
the visitors side. Bennett and Bateman, however,
from this point carried all before them, and the last
wicket put on 66.
Tunstall was visited by Nantwich, and the home
team succeeded in making 81 runs. Matzock, 36, was
the highest scorer. Nantwich made short work of
this, and amassed 145 for seven wickets. Hollowood
carrying his bat for 66 runs, his highest score in
League cricket.
Crewe visited Silverdale, and assisted by an
innings of 63 runs by E. Payne, 38 by Osborne, and
very creditable scores by other players, made 178 for
eight wickets, when the innings was declared closed.
At one time it looked 20 to 1 in favour of a draw,
but a speedy demolition of the last wickets of Silver-
dale brought out Crewe once more the winning
team, Silverdale's total amounting to only 93 runs.
The day's cricket brings out several League
records. For nearly 300 runs to be made on the
Silverdale ground in less than four hours and a half
is certainly a feat that has never been done before,
and that is not likely to be done again for some
considerable time. For three teams to declare their
innings closed in one day is also an item that has
never been chronicled. The attendance at each of
the matches was good, and bespeaks the sustained
interest that this organisation has upon the cricket-
loving public in this district.
To-day only four matches are arranged: County
v. Tunstall, Leek v. Burslem, Nantwich v. Fenton,
and Silverdale v. Porthill.
