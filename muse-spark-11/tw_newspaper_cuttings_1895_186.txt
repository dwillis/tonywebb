OXFORD TIMES: SATURDAY 17 AUGUST 1895-3.

SPORTING NOTES.
[BY THE CAPTAIN.]
THURSDAY.
The fortunes of the county have varied considerably
in the last three encounters, providing a win, a draw,
and a loss. On Wednesday and Thursday last week the
team met Buckinghamshire at Wolverton, but the
visitors had ultimately to submit to defeat by 81 runs.
Our men certainly appear to have a great objection to
the bowling of Nash and Wright, who captured wickets
with the same precision as in the first encounter at
Thame. Arnold once more demonstrated what a useful
man he is, and had it not been for him and Day there
is no saying how low the score of the first innings
might have been.

On Friday and Saturday the eleven went to Bedford
to oppose Bedfordshire, and for the second time this
season came out of the fray triumphant. The home
team had first innings, but could do very little with
P. Rogers, who finally took six wickets for 43 runs.
Only three men got into double figures, and the total
reached 10 short of the 100. Oxfordshire did a great
deal better, and made a capital start with Bassett in
form, and receiving good assistance from Miller and
Smith. Then two or three wickets fell quickly, but
Ward and Wood offered a stubborn resistance, and the
score was finally taken to 223. It was most satisfac-
tory to see Bassett playing in quite his old form, his
65 being put together by good cricket.

On Saturday, therefore, Bedfordshire had to start
their second innings with 133 to wipe off in order to
save the innings defeat. At one period of the game it
looked as if they might accomplish the task, but later
wickets fell so fast that the venture was closed with
the home side still in a minority of 20 runs. As in the
first innings, Brown (89) was top scorer, while Manton
followed him closely with a useful 20. P. Rogers was
again the hero of the bowling department, and was
even more successful than in the first venture, for he
took eight wickets at a cost of only 6.8 runs apiece.

The Worcestershire match, which was down for Mon-
day and Tuesday, at Dudley, had to be left drawn
on the first day, owing to the rain which prevented
any thought of play on the Tuesday. As far as the
match went it was very even indeed. The home county
had first innings, and after quickly losing H. J. Foster
they made a fair start, Millward playing well for 28,
and Arnold and Wilkes both reaching double figures.
With the exception of Bromley-Martin and Jobson, the
rest could do very little, and the total only realised
107. A. Rogers did best in the bowling this time, and
acquired five wickets for 39 runs. The first batsmen
for Oxfordshire failed badly, and for a while it looked
as if the visitors would not nearly approach even the
low score of their opponents. Seven wickets fell, and
only two men had reached double figures, Case (12)
and Ward (16), then P. Rogers laid about him in smart
fashion, and when stumps were drawn for the day was
still not out with 23 to his credit, and the Oxfordshire
score was 96 for the loss of nine wickets.

The Star II. seems to be having better luck than
their first eleven usually have in cup ties, and have
now got through to the semi-final of the City Cup.
Their last match was against Iffley. The latter
had first innings, but unaccountably failed before the
good bowling of Simmonds. A. R. Parker was the only
batsman to make double figures, and he was missed off
the first ball he had; afterwards he hit freely and got
19, so that with a liberal supply of extras (11) the
score reached 56. Simmonds' analysis showed seven
wickets for 23 runs, which is excellent. The Star men
were not long in hitting off the required runs, and
Neale, of Littlemore fame, put together a capital 66.
Galt made a useful 23, and the final total amounted to
126. Either the E.O.C.C. or St. Paul's will be the
Star's opponents in the semi-final.

On Saturday last a match that has lapsed for three
years was renewed, viz., a friendly between the Star
and the Victoria. I am sure that everybody will join
me in feeling very glad that whatever unpleasantness
has existed between these two clubs has at last been
satisfactorily banished. The game itself proved most
enjoyable, and at the end neither side could claim a
victory, though the Victoria were in the better position.
The latter going in first lost six wickets for 142, when
they declared. G. Gray played very nice cricket for
his 42, and R.T. Roberts (30) and Now (29) also made
a good show. On the Star going in only Penney made
much of a display, and he obtained 42, which is dis-
tinctly a good score against the strong bowling opposed
to him. In the end the Star lost five wickets for 76.

The Star played another draw last Thursday with
the E.O.C.C., the game being left in an even and
interesting condition. The Star scored 111 for nine, to
which R. Cummings supplied the most with 23, Wyatt
bowling very well for the E.O.C.C., and taking five
wickets. The Conservatives scored 33 for the loss of
two wickets, when rain stopped the further play.

The Corporation C.C. received a return visit from
the Cheltenham Corporation employes on Saturday, and
a pleasant function it proved. The City Cup-holders
had so astonished the natives of Cheltenham when they
went there in May that their opponents this time
recruited from outside, and brought a pretty good team,
with the result that they had the satisfaction of
returning home victorious. Broughton (33) played a
capital game for the winners and for the losers Brooks
and Tanner shared the honours of top scorers. Tibbetts
and Jennings were much on the spot with the ball for

their respective sides, taking six wickets for 32 and
six wickets for 38. The contest was most exciting at
the finish, the visitors only winning by three runs.
The match was made the occasion of quite a social
gathering between the Corporations of the two towns,
and the visitors professed themselves highly pleased
with the entertainment provided for them.

Next Saturday Cowley will meet Thame on the
ground of the former in the semi-final for the County
Cup, and will be represented by the following:—W.
King, A. Rogers, P. Rogers, G. Polley, F. Arnold, B.
Hinton, E. Burrin, P. King, H. King, W. Hodgkins,
and Colonel Ward. With such a team Cowley ought to
win with comparative ease. By-the-way, I understand
that the D.A.C. were wishful to play their cup-tie
with the City on July 27th, which date they could have
insisted on by the rules, but agreed to a further post-
ponement, and risked the chance of having to scratch,
because the City could not have put a proper team in
the field owing to the match with Mr. Stuart Camp-
bell's XI. I mention it because it was a sportsmanlike
proceeding, and contrasts with what happened at the
time of Mr. Campbell's match last year.
