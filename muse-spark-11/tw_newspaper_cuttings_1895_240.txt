OUR SPORTING COLUMN

What with the rain and the easy wicket Walsall did
not have much chance of showing what their full bat-
ting strength could do in their match on Saturday, al-
though they had quite a [unclear] representative team of
Dudley to deal with. For once in a way the bowling
of Bird looked terrors, and he was treated with very
little ceremony, in fact, the whole team batted with
confidence, and at the conclusion of the play it was
found that Dudley had put together the respectable
score of 129 for nine wickets. S. Morgan-who, by the
way, has not had the best of luck this season-batted a
capital 29 not out, whilst F. Wheldon (21), Mr. Deeley
(15), W. Brazwell (11), F. Brown (12), J. Cropper (14),
and Cole (not out 13) played the bowling without any
apparent difficulty. Smith, too, was evidently in scor-
ing form, but he had the misfortune to return a ball to
Hawke. If the ball had dropped short about an inch
it would have been out of the bowler's reach, and it
was unquestionably a fine catch. The rain undoubtedly
spoilt what would have been a very interesting match.

The Dudley second eleven on Saturday were in ex-
actly the same comfortable position as the first team.
They kept all day second in the field all day, and when
play concluded only six wickets were down for a score
of 115. Some very useful scores were made by A.
Portway (27), E. J. Thompson (27), G. S. Thompson
(35 not out), and J. W. Collins (14), all playing well for
their respective scores.

Dudley hardly expected to win the Charity Shield
with the team they had against Walsall on Monday,
but they made a much better show, all things accounted for.
They hit up 118 and it took six of the best Walsall
wickets to exceed it. Mr. Deeley, the Dudley captain,
was principal scorer for Dudley, with a lively hit 29,
Wheldon was next best with 20 and Smith and Morgan
made 16 and 11 respectively. Better luck another
time.

The rain evidently did not damp the ardour of the
Nethertonians in their match with Darlaston, for the
game was brought to a satisfactory conclusion. Nether-
ton, with three wickets down, knocked up a total of 73,
Gower (18) and Darby (not out 19) being to the fore.
Darlaston were soon disposed of, only 32 runs coming
from them, and out of that small total Thomas obtained
13 runs. Shaw Road beat Flood Street by 40 runs,
the defeated only being able to put together 19. Wood-
setton was another club to take things easily on Satur-
day, the opponents (Slater's) only mustering 35, while they
rattled together 78. Tividale Second (65) and Tipton
(10) were chief scorers for Woodsetton, and for Slater's
T. Price, with 10, was exceedingly useful.

The Brook Grounds, Wednesbury, were on Saturday
and Monday occupied with an interesting pedestrian
handicap, and the grounds were largely patroled.
The distance was 133 yards, and there were 105 entries,
classed into 21 heats. S. Dunn (of Bradley), G. Payne
(of Brookmoor), and R. Weeks (of Can Lane) each won
their heats, whilst in the 100 yards [unclear] Halesowen ran
second to Weeks. After, the first round the 20 appar-
[unclear] man were drawn in four heats, and Payne and
[unclear] managed to win their heats again, and in the
final they came in first and second respectively, after
Weeks had been put back a yard. Payne, who was on
the 30 yards' mark, started at 2 to 1 on, and 7 to 1
against Weeks

The Birmingham League matches suffered in common
with all other games, on Saturday, Stourbridge had a
poor team against Aston Unity, and perhaps it was as
well for them that the rain prevented the game being
played. They were without Foster, Foley, Rhodes, and
Arnold. Five wickets for 63, however, does not [unclear]
quite Unity form; but this was due, probably, to the
the bowling of Hingley, who took four wickets for 28.
The Smethwick and Moseley match was abandoned in
consequence of the rain.
