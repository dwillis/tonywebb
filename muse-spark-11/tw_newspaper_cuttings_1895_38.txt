HIGH WYCOMBE v. WOOBURN INSTITUTE.
This match, which was played on the Wycombe ground last Saturday afternoon, resulted in a draw that, if time had permitted, would have ended in favour of the home team. The visitors were without Barber, the Lannons, and others who generally play for the Institute team, but they had the assistance of H. Bacon, who proved a tower of strength to them in both departments of the game. Wycombe did not play their full strength, more than half their number being reserve men. The home side went in first, and a total of 159 was reached before they were all disposed of. The chief contributor to this score was F. George, whose figures included a 6, six 5's, and seven 4's. Other good batting was displayed by H. George and Page who drove one of Bacon's deliveries out of the field for 6. A word of praise ought to be given to Raffeety among the younger members of the team, who played well for 21; his principal hits being a, 4, three 3's, and three 2's. Bacon was the most successful in bowling, his eight wickets costing a trifle over six runs apiece, and the last three being obtained without a run being scored off him. For Wooburn Institute, Bacon, Bowler and Page made a good start but the second named hitting G. George for a 6. When stumps were drawn the visitors had made 129 for seven wickets, the last three of which had put on but eight runs between them. Page was the most successful with the ball, but his four wickets cost nearly 13 each. Score and analysis:—

MARLOW v. MR. J. MONRO WALKER'S XI.
On Saturday Marlow met an eleven got together by Mr. J Monro Walker, of Bisham Abbey, on the Marlow ground The weather was delightful, and a good day's play was enjoyed. The visitors won the toss and took first innings. Harbottle hit hard for 39, and with smaller contributions from all the side except Lane (who was run out before he had scored), the total reached 133. Harbottle hit a 5 (four for an overthrow) and four 4's. Monro Walker was credited with an 8 (four for an overthrow). Marlow lost Paine and Faulkner cheaply, but with useful scores from Langley, Bower, Baillie, and Ward their total reached 163, with only six wickets down. Eventually the innings closed for 183. One of the Australian visitors staying in Marlow—hit a 6, six 4's, and three 5's in his dashing score of 49. Langley hit five 4's in his 30. Mr. Walkers team went in a second time, and had compiled 113 for six wickets, when stumps were drawn. Marlow therefore won by 30 runs on the first innings. Full score and analysis:—

WYCOMBE CHURCH ROOM v. WYCOMBE ST. JOHN'S.
The third encounter between the above-named teams took place on the Wycombe Rye on Saturday last in splendid weather, and, after a pleasant and interesting game, resulted in a win for the Church Room by 37 runs on the first innings. The St. John's won the toss and elected to bat first, but were disposed of for 40 runs, F. Rolls (11), Ford (8), and Ing (8) being the top scorers. The Church Room then went in, and replied with 77, O. Vickers (not out, 24), Sexton (16), and O. Twitchen (13) being the double-figure contributors. The St. John's then went in again, and showed better form, knocking up 60, W. Rolls (11), Ing (13), and Ford (9) being the chief scorers. Twitchen and Vickers bowled well for the winners, while Rolls and Burnham were the best for the losers.
