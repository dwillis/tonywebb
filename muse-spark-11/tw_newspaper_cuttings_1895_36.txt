FLACKWELL HEATH 2ND XI v. LITTLE MARLOW.
LOW SCORING.

A match between the above clubs was played on the ground of the latter on Saturday last, and resulted in a victory for Flackwell Heath by 25 runs on the first innings. The batting of Musk, Baker, and Smith was very good. Almond accomplished the hat trick, taking 3 wickets in 3 successive balls. Clifford also bowled well. The second innings of the latter proved most disastrous, they being dismissed in 30 minutes for 9 runs. No doubt the result would have been different had time permitted a second innings for the visitors. Davis and Penny batted in good form. In the second innings of Flackwell Heath Almond again accomplished the hat trick, and took 6 wickets for 1 run. Penny obtained 4 for 6 runs. Score:—

MARLOW v. HIGH WYCOMBE.
BIG HITTING BY E. M. MASSIE.

The return match between these old rivals was played at Marlow, on Saturday, in fine weather, on a wicket that favoured the bowlers. It will be remembered that the first match at Wycombe was left drawn much in favour of Marlow. On this occasion the riverside town gained an easy victory, thanks to a fine innings by H. H. Massie, the Australian batsman (who is staying at Marlow this summer), and some excellent bowling by W. H. Lennon and Barber. Marlow batted first, Langley and Barber facing the deliveries of Page and H. S. Parker. Runs came steadily until the total reached 24, when both batsmen were out with a dozen to their credit. Massie and Lennon then added 10 out of 15, when the latter was caught by White, and as Ballis failed to score and Want only hit a 4, five wickets were down for 62. Bower then came to the aid of Massie, and a useful stand was effected. The 100 went up amidst cheers, but with four added a great change came over the game, H. S. Parker secured four wickets, Burton, and Mackenzie in one over. Six, seven, and the last man, Massie was now hitting with tremendous force, and was giving a good display of cutting, a gigantic hit for 6 into Court Garden delighting the onlookers hugely. Morgan batted steadily while the Australian hit until the total was 140, when he was yorked by Parker. Just when it appeared certain that Massie would take out his bat he was captured in the slips, and the innings closed for 146. Massie's dashing score of 69 included two 6's, eight 4's, two 3's, and eight 2's, and was deservedly applauded on retiring to the pavilion. Both Page and H. S. Parker bowled well. Wycombe commenced their innings with F. and G. Parker and Langley. The bowlers were W. H. Lennon and Barber, and a more disastrous start could not well be imagined. Lennon caught and bowled Parker and clean bowled Baines in his first over. Two for 0. G. George scored a couple of singles, and Langley was then caught by Mackenzie off Barber. Three for 3. H. S. Parker helped George to put a better aspect upon affairs, but the former was out at 23, after hitting up 16. Janes followed in and was bowled by Lennon at 26. The brothers George got together and carried the score to 40, when three wickets fell—to the George brothers and White, all clean bowled, Page hitting up 16 by means of a 6, a 4, and two 2's, and the innings closed for 60. Lennon bowled uncommonly well, his seven wickets costing 33 runs each. Wycombe followed on, and made 50 for one wicket. Parker batted well for 35 not out. Marlow therefore won by 86 runs on the first innings—a rather unexpected result, seeing that the team which was in the field was by no means a strong one. Score and analysis:—
