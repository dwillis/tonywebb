South Bucks Free Press: Friday 16 August 1895 - 4.

FLACKWELL HEATH 2ND XI v. LITTLE MARLOW.
LOW SCORING.
A match between the above clubs was played on the ground
of the latter on Saturday last, and resulted in a victory for
Flackwell Heath by 25 runs on the first innings. The batting
of Musk, Baker, and Smith was very good. Almond accom-
plished the hat trick, taking 3 wickets in 3 successive balls.
Clifford also bowled well. The second innings of the latter
proved most disastrous, they being dismissed in 20 minutes
for 9 runs. No doubt the result would have been different
had time permitted a second innings for the visitors. Davis
and Penny batted in good form. In the second innings of
Flackwell Heath Almond again accomplished the hat trick,
and took 6 wickets for 1 run. Penny obtained 4 for 6 runs.

MARLOW v. HIGH WYCOMBE.
BIG HITTING BY E. R. MASSIE.
The return match between these old rivals was played at
Marlow, on Saturday, in fine weather, on a wicket that
favoured the bowlers. It will be remembered that the first
match at Wycombe was left drawn much in favour of Marlow.
On this occasion the riverside town gained an easy victory,
thanks to a fine innings by H. H. Massie, the Australian
batsman (who is staying at Marlow this summer), and some
excellent bowling by W. H. Lennon and Barber. Marlow
losing the toss, Langley and Barber facing the deliveries of
Page and H. S. Parker. Runs came steadily until the total
reached 24, when both batsmen were out with a dozen to their
credit. Massie and Lennon added 19, and about 35, when
the latter was caught by White, and as Ballie failed to score
and Want only hit a 4, five wickets were down for 62. Bower
then came to the aid of Massie, and a useful stand was
effected. The 110 went up amidst cheers, but with four
added a great change came over the game, H. S. Parker
accounting for Bower, Burton and Mackenzie in one over,
six, seven, and eight wickets. Massie was now hitting with
tremendous force, and was well backed up by Morgan, after a
gigantic hit for 6 into Court Garden delighting the onlookers
hugely. Morgan batted steadily while the Australian hit
until the total was 142, when he was yorked by Parker. Just
when it appeared certain that Massie would take out his bat
he was captured in the slips, and the innings closed for 146.
Massie's dashing score of 69 included two 6's, eight 4's, two
3's, and eight 2's. He was deservedly applauded on retiring
to the pavilion. Seven for 90, Parker bowled well.
Wycombe commenced their innings with F. Parker and
Langley. The bowlers were W. H. Lennon and Barber, and a
more disastrous start could not well be imagined. Lennon
caught and bowled Parker and clean bowled Baines in his first
over. Two for 0. G. George scored a couple of singles, and
Langley was then caught by Mackenzie off Barber. Three
for 2. H. S. Parker helped George to put a better aspect
upon affairs, but the former was out at 22, after hitting up
16. Janes followed in and was bowled by Lennon at 28. The
brothers George then got together and carried the score to 40,
when three wickets fell—the George brothers and White, all
clean bowled. Page hit up 16 by means of a 5, a 4, and two
2's, and the innings closed for 60. Lennon bowled uncom-
monly well, his seven wickets costing 15 runs each. Wycombe
followed on, and made 60 for one wicket. Parker batted
well for 35 not out. Marlow therefore won by 86 runs on the
first innings—a rather unexpected result, seeing that the team
which was in the field was by no means a strong one. Score
and analysis:—

BLYTHEWOOD v. CIPPENHAM.
This match, at Blythewood, on Saturday last, proved a very
close one, Cippenham only winning by 5 runs. Going in first
Blythewood were got rid of for 32, A. E. Kent (not out, 9)
doing best. Cippenham got 37, and a remarkable piece of
play was D. Gregory's, who went in first and carried his bat
for only three runs. For the winners F. Woolley took 6
wickets for 15 runs and J. Gregory 1 for 7. In a second
attempt Blythewood obtained 16 runs for the loss of two
wickets. Frost (for Blythewood) secured 4 wickets for 5 runs

TAPLOW STATION v. READING TEMPERANCE
FORWARD.
Played at Reading on Saturday, and, thanks to the splendid
batting (25 runs) and bowling (8 wickets for 12 runs) of G.
Flaber, was won by Taplow by 43 runs to 27. G. Jones
captured 1 wicket for 8 runs and M. Painter 1 for 8. For the
home eleven, Hobbs and Cave were successful with the ball,
but neither of the batsmen could reach double figures.
