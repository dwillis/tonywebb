MR. W. PEARCE'S (WYCOMBE) XI. v. SOUTHALL.
On Saturday last Mr. W. Pearce, of the "Nag's Head,"
London-road, captained an eleven to visit Southall to try
conclusions with an eleven representing the Southall Gas
Works. Although the Bucks men made a fine innings, having
113 runs to their credit, Southall were far and away the best
opponents for the loss of only 3 wickets they had contributed
137 runs, Dixon and Hudson were said to be classed as
belonging to the Middlesex team of Colts, and the way in
which Dixon contributed his 76 showed that he was a batsman
of no mean order. His total included one 5, three 4's, fifteen
3's, and five 2's. William Cooper, too, knocked up eight
boundaries in succession. In fact, against such formidable
opponents the bowling of the Wycombe representatives was
taxed to its utmost, but, nevertheless, a pleasant game was
the result. Both W. Pearce and O. W. Harper showed good
form for the losers. Score and analysis:-

GREAT MISSENDEN v. LEE COMMON.

Thursday, August 22. The visitors went in first, starting

MR. COLLINS CLASS v. BOOKER TEMPERANCE.
The return match between these clubs took place at Booker
on Saturday last, and a pleasant game resulted in a win for
the Class by eight runs on the first innings. The meeting of
the teams has usually been a close game. But last year it was a
win for the Class and a drawn match, and this year the result
of the fixtures has been the same. Batting first, the Class
made 50, J. Keen (32) and Collins (10) being chief contri-
butors. Booker then went in, and the total had reached 42
when the last wicket fell, out of which number Burnham had
made 16. In their second venture the Class could only compile
27, not a single man being able to make a stand against the
under-arm bowling of O. Pearce; Heather (13) was the only
one to get into double figures. Scores:-

MASTER H. PENTON'S XI. v. HEDGERLEY HOME.
A match was played at Chalfont Park on August 22nd,
between XI. got together by Master H. Penton and XI. of
the Church of England Waifs and Strays, Hedgerley Home,
by invitation. The visitors were put in first, Watton batting
well for 30, the whole team being soon dismissed for 69. For
the home side Master H. Penton played a capital innings for
63 (not out); O. Roe and Ranschaert also batted well. Tea
was provided on the grounds by Captain Penton, to which
full justice was done, and a good afternoon's game was
enjoyed. Score:-
