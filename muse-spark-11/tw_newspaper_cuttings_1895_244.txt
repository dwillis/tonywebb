WORCESTERSHIRE CLUB AND GROUND
v. BROMSGROVE.
There was a capital attendance at the above match
played at Bromsgrove on Saturday for Rolleston, the
professional's benefit, and a capital day's cricket was
the result. Bromsgrove won the toss, but put their
opponents in. Burrows and Bowley, who went in
first, batted brilliantly, and made 155 before they
were separated. With other useful and well played
totals from W. S. Caldwell and the Rev. F. A. Bick-
more, the large score of 258 was obtained, when the
innings was declared with six wickets down. Broms-
grove observed strict defensive tactics, and kept their
end up admirably, and only six wickets were down
when the match ended in a drawn game. Score:—

UPTON-ON-SEVERN v. HIGH GREEN.—Played at
Upton on Saturday. L. Longney, winning the toss,
commenced batting, and, being ably assisted by
"extras," took the score to 54 before being bowled,
and the innings closed at that total. For the home
team Gunn was very lucky, but it was owing to his
efforts that the score reached 46, leaving the visitors
winners by 8 runs. The Rev. Morgans and W.
Longney bowled well. The wicket being rough and
hard accounted for the great number of extras given.

CATHEDRAL GUILD v. MR. CONACHER'S XI.—
Played at Diglis, on Saturday, and resulted in a
decisive win for the Guild by 46 runs. The scratch
eleven, winning the toss, elected to bat, but, with the
exception of Mr. C. Riley, could make no stand
against the bowling of S. Smith and Phillips, the
former taking four wickets for 15, and the latter six
for 21. The total absence of extras proved that
Harford at the wickets was well up to his work. On
going to bat, the Guild fared badly, no one reaching
double figures till the eighth wicket was down, but
Walker, Darch, and Morgan put a different com-
plexion on the game, the captain playing extremely
well for his 22. Next Saturday the Guild play their
last fixture with St. John's at Diglis. Score:—

Mr. E. B. Forde, late of Malvern College, who
plays for Somerset, is tenth in the batting averages of
that club with an average of 16.11. He has scored
203 in 14 innings.

St. John's, in their match on Thursday at Boughton,
against the Club and Ground, took a strong lead after
going in first. They made a capital stand, although
the bowling against them was rather warm. Burrows,
Willoughby, and Ryder shared between them what
wickets fell, before Mr. Young declared with nine
wickets down. Mr. H. V. Plum batted very steadily
for 48. Messrs. Curtis and Mansell also performed
creditably, and 123 was the total. This score looked
like being wiped off, for Burrows was in his smiling
humour and hit up 57 in lively fashion. The County
lot were only 13 behind and six wickets down when
time put a stop to proceedings.

Mr. E. P. Gem bossed the team very well, and I
should think he has acted in a similar capacity before,
although I have not known him do so.

It was rather funny that Straw should have been
objected to by the St. John's captain, because I have
an inkling that he has resorted to similar measures
before, viz., when he has been short of players he has
done the best he could to fill the vacancies. Although
he had a right to object to Straw, who had not fielded,
I think he might have given way, but I suppose it
was his customary zeal in trying to win the match that
made him forget previous circumstances.

Mr. W. A. Young performed the hat trick.

Rolleston had his annual benefit at Bromsgrove on
Saturday, and I hope he will have a substantial
sum, for he is a very painstaking cricketer, and
immensely popular with the members. He has done
well for the club, which is second to none in the
district.

I had a good opportunity of looking over the new
pavilion, which is one of the best I have seen, and it
reflects great credit upon Mr. Gadd, who, I was glad
to hear, was getting another ready for the Hands-
worth Wood Club. Others will sure to follow, for at
£115, the total cost, it represents the cheapest and
most comfortable one that could be imagined.

The improved state of the ground struck me as soon
as I got there. I subsequently learned that the
brothers J. H. and E. Perkins, both of whom are in-
dependent gentlemen, set to work and levelled 14,000
feet with their own hands last winter. This sub-
stantiates what I have said of them before, that they
are the right men in the right place. This is
enthusiasm indeed, as those who have had a spell at
that sort of work know very well. The wicket was a
perfect one, and, after all the runs which were made,
was just as good at the close.

I had forgotten to say that there is about £85 yet to
be got to clear the pavilion debt, and I notice a match
will be played there between 22 ladies and 11 gentle-
men. This ought to wipe some of it off, for the
tickets are going off well. If the weather is fine there
will be a good gate and plenty of fun.

Burrows and Bowley established a record, for they
hit up 155 before a wicket fell, and both batted
splendidly. The former only just missed his century.
Master Caldwell played a patient innings, and this
youngster has only to go on the right way to make a
very fine batsman. I like his style very much. The
Rev. F. A. Bickmore was batting first rate when the
innings was declared. The score had reached 258 for
six wickets, and this, against some good bowlers, was
capital. I imagine the local team must have felt
annoyed that they did not bat first after winning the
toss. However, they played a good defensive game,
and kept their end up for two hours and a quarter,
and made a good draw of it.

Tommy Straw took off the gloves and went on to bowl,
I ought to say bowl, but the crowd and the home
captain said he threw, and so I suppose he must have
done. The circumstance caused a deal of flutter, and
John Smith, who was umpiring, had a warm five
minutes, but it all finished up in fun. I must warn
Tommy to stick to his wicketkeeping, for he has few
superiors in that line anywhere.

There are lots of local matches of an interesting
kind during the coming week, and, given a continu-
ance of the present glorious weather, will wind up a
capital season.
