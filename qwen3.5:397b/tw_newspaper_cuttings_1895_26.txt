CRICKET.
UP, SHEPHERD'S XL beat WOOLLEY PARK.
This match was played at Woolley Park on Wednesday. Woolley going in first put together the total of 67, of which Llayden and Smith did best with 17 and 14 respectively. Mr. Shepherd's team then went in the score at lunch time standing at 81 for 4 wickets, Hutchins and Weaver playing good cricket for their runs. The innings closed for Woolley going in again lost on 1st. Mr. Shepherd's team now requiring 32 runs to win, obtained them with the loss of 3 wickets, Hutchins again doing well. Both teams were kindly entertained to tea after the match by Mr. Wroughton. So. re:

IMPER beat FALKLAND C.C.
The visitors to meet the Impern C.C. on their own ground were rather late in coming, the time fixed for the match being two o'clock, while the game did not commence till three-fourths past two, when the tosses and play quickly began. The chances of winning looked rather small at one time, four wickets having fallen for 30 runs. R. Dance soon turned the tide by adding 21 runs more towards his tally, he also getting off a splendid drive for 4, 4, 3, two 2's, and six real singles. The Rev. H. D. Butler made a good hit for 4, and when this innings was closed carried out his bat. G. Butler and J. Hopkins did not bat. The visitors were dismissed for 73 runs, mainly owing to the fine-rate bowling of T. May, who took 7 wickets, his average being 1.5. Score:-

LAMBOURN beat CHILTON.
The return match was played on Saturday last, on the Lambourn ground, and, as the score will show, resulted in a win for Lambourn, the home-sters thus avenging their defeat in the previous match. The game did not commence till rather late, and consequently had to be decided on the first innings. The weather was delightful and the wicket really good. Chilton won the toss, but elected to send the home team in. A very poor start was made, as two Lambourn wickets went down for 0, but a wonderfully different complexion was put on the game, when, after H. Penfold had scored seven in good style, F. Harris and J. Bryant got well set. F. Harris made some splendid hits, and the spectators loudly cheered his 11-oxy play, his score of 35 being made in a very short time. J. Bryant also met for his 8th century was greatly applauded upon his retirement. V. Hobbs and C. J. Maberry rendered great assistance to the score before the innings was declared closed at 129, the Chilton team did but little, only one player (S. Pouting) obtaining double figures. The bowling of Messrs. Hobbs and Bryant was very effective, and F. Harris made two capital catches in the long field. Chilton followed on, and at the call of time no wicket had fallen, the score being 81. H. New and Rumball having scored well during the innings. Score :-

SOUTHINGTON beat MR. A. KINGSMILL'S XII.
This highly exciting and closely contested match was played in lovely weather on the charmingly situated Symondton Cricket Ground by mutual enthusiasm, the age being limited to 16, several, however, had many years still before them ere they reach this degree of experience. We would draw attention to the excellent bowling on the Southington side of Koeller and Poynter, and the batting of these and also Christie and North. The star of the Symondton team was J. Chute whose bowling was so successful in his batting, and his success was rewarded by the presentation hat offered for the highest score by Mr. Wyndham Vivrel. Leyland and Harrison had some share in the downfall of the Southington wickets, aided by the smart wicket-keeping of Andrew Kinsamill, in spite of their strenuous exertions Southington proved successful by the narrow margin of seven runs. BUSSACOMBE beat NEWTOWN.
Played on the Sandiford Priory ground and ended in a defeat for the home team. Burghclere won the toss and batted first. Three wickets fell quickly, but on Rev. G. Hicks and G. Kimber getting together runs were piled on fast, Kimber being run out for a well-played 12, Rev. G. Hicks carrying out his bat for a brilliantly-hit innings of 25. Newtown made no stand whatever, being all dismissed for 41, Allsop alone getting double figures. This match brought the Newtown season to a close, after a fairly successful season, they having played 14 matches, won six and lost eight. NEWTOWN beat PARKIN CHURCH INSTITUTE.
This return match was played in the beautiful grounds of Sandford Park, on Saturday, August 31st, there being a goodly number of spectators present, including Lady Bathurst and family and friends. Newtown went first to the wickets, and by good play and hard hitting on the part of Eldid (22), and Gardiner (15), made a total of 45 runs, Gunning's bowling being too good for them, he being well backed up by Matthews and Fewtrell. On the Institute handling the willow, however, they were soon disposed of for 38 runs, Victoria being top scorer with 16, Gibbs, Fulid, Allsop, and Stevenson being the bowlers. Tea was then partaken of, and Newtown again went to the wickets, but the Institute not being able to go in again, the home team won a pleasant game on the first innings by eight runs.

ADSBURY HOUSE beat NEWTOWN HOUSE.
On Thursday afternoon a match was played between eleven of the Adsbury House estate and Newtown House, the same being won by the Adsbury House eleven, a result being mainly due to the good bowling of H. Barber, F. James, and J. Gascon; G. Cooper brought off a beautiful catch at cover slip, and S. Berry made a big drive for five runs. For the losers M. Stratford, White, Street, and Weight acquitted themselves very well. A capital tea had been prepared by the housekeeper on the grounds, which she placed at the disposal of the players. Altogether a most enjoyable afternoon was spent, and a hearty vote of thanks was carried with acclamation to Mr. Walter Arkwright, f.r.s kindness. Score :
