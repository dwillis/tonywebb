HIGH WYCOMBE v. MR. E. STEVENS' XI.
On Saturday Mr. Edmund Stevens, of The Yache, Chalfont St. Giles, brought over a strong team to oppose the local club, but rain prevented the game from being played out. It was, however, drawn in favour of Wycombe, who had made 63 for the loss of two wickets, when stumps were drawn. No play was possible before luncheon, and a start was not made till past 2 o'clock, when the visitors went to the wicket. Before their innings was concluded, a short shower compelled them to make a precipitate rush to the friendly shelter of the pavilion. The rain holding off for a time allowed the innings to be finished, 57 runs being the result. In Wycombe going in the light was very bad, and the rain came down at intervals in torrents, so that after several attempts to play out time it was thought best to leave the game drawn. At this point the match was left in the position shown by the following score and analysis. The wet did not hinder Balesman from making some good strokes, his 29 including a 6, two 4's, three 3's, and two 2's—

RAYNERS XI v. PERMANENT STAFF OF THE 2nd BATT. OXFORD LIGHT INFANTRY.
On Monday last, August 5th, a match was played on Rayners private ground between the above teams, and, after being hindered a little by the unsettled weather, ended in a decisive victory for the former by an innings and 11 runs. Mr. C. M. Ross captained the home eleven, and, having won the toss, elected to bat. The game was well seen at the commencement, and 4 good wickets were lost for 21, but when the captain and Mr. Powell became associated, the best stand of the day was made, and some good cricket was seen, both playing with confidence. They put on 63 runs between them, when Mr. Ross was caught at the wicket for an invaluable 27. Powell left soon after with a well-played 30 to his credit. Wheeler made 14, and the remaining batsmen being quickly disposed of, the innings closed for 114. An adjournment was now made for lunch, which had been prepared (in a spacious marquee, erected for the occasion) through the generosity of Sir Philip and Lady Kemp, after whom justice had been done to the good things provided, the visitors commenced their innings. Captain Ross and Bagler Wakalin were the only ones to show any resistance to the attack of O. M. Ross and Powell, who were bowling in their best form, and the innings closed for 51. Captain Ross's 16 was far and away the best batting display, which included one 6, a clean drive out of ground. Powell had the best analysis, taking 6 wickets for 23, while Mr. Ross's 4 cost 25 runs. Having to follow on, they failed to reach the number required to save an innings' defeat, Dean taking 6 wickets for 13 runs, and the game resulted as before stated. Full score and analysis:—
