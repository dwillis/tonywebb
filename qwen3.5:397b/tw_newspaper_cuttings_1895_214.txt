Unfortunately the League cricket of last Saturday can be summed up very briefly.
Of the four matches arranged not one was brought to a conclusion. At Xantwich the ground was in such a condition as to preclude the possibility of playing at all, while at Colridge, the important fixture with Porthill was only progressed with to the space of ten minutes, and at Fenton, Crewe succeeded in enough to decide the first innings. The home team had a like measure of success against Tunstall's efforts against the Club ground.
Each club has now, therefore, is compelled to be content with the unsatisfactory one point that attends drawn matches.
Today Leek v. Wakes, is as usual a light one so far as the League fixtures are concerned. The only three matches that are arranged are Tunstall v. Lune-street; Tunstall v. Nantwich v. Crewe, as Nantwich had the County v. Crewe on Monday.
When the League had played over five or six weeks congratulations were made in this column on the utter absence of drawn matches. The elements, however, during the latter part of the season have caused the drawn column to be a singularly heavy one. Leek heads the drawn list, by having failed to complete eight matches out of its thirteen engagements, and led the club been charged on Saturday last it would have created a record, for no club since the institution of the League has drawn nine matches even in the completed season. Silverdale has drawn seven, and bids fair to equal its performance of last year when it had against its name the highest number of draws recorded in the annals of the North Staffordshire and District Cricket League. It must be borne in mind that the action of the League at the commencement of the season which lengthened the time for League matches would have resulted, had fair weather have been the rule instead of the exception, in causing more matches than usual to be played to a finish.

LEEK.
Leek first team had no match at all on Saturday. They were resting after making the record number of single points, with a hope that when they recommence it will run to two points. After the club week they will be able to do with them. Your readers will have noticed that there were no notes from Leek on Saturday. They were written, but the boy who was entrusted with the important mission of conveying them to the station forgot the way. Neither have been heard of since.
Last Saturday was a soaker. It was Ragshaw's benefit, and the match was against our old friends, Buxton, whose faces have not been seen in Leek for a few seasons, but of whose performances in mining we offer congratulations. Everything promised a bumper gate, as the Leek team were not playing, and Kirkby, in a most generous manner, assisted his brother professionals. But it was not to be. The weather that came on just previously to the time fixed for commencing.
The visitors came in grand style in a coach-and-four, and they reached the ground in good time. The spectators were but few, and it showed a good feeling to see so many of the prominent members of the Leek club on the ground. Leek won the toss, and that was about all that was done for an hour; but at last the rain cut, and the Temperance Band played a lively selection, and the game commenced.
Kirkby and Ragshaw took the bowling, but from some cause or other Ragshaw did not come off, and after bowling some dozen overs, which were very expensive, he was taken off. Kirkby was in grand form, and he obtained five wickets for 30 runs—no mean performance against Buxton. Harry Ball made a mistake in missing Howe when this player had only scored 1 run, so his total was 34. Maddock was a patient bat, and kept up his wicket, but failed to add many to the score-sheet. Wordruff had a short and merry career, one of his hits for 6 was a treat. When eight wickets were down for 86 rain again set in, and the match was abandoned.
Leek Highfield have not yet sustained a defeat this season, and their application for permission to join the North Staffordshire League next season ought to be favourably considered.
Leek Reserve were playing Oakamoor on the Leek ground. Leek scored 61, of which number Dr. Hansman was responsible for 50, and G. Tudor 15. When 61 had been obtained for seven wickets, the innings was closed. Oakamoor did not bat long, obtaining 2 runs without the loss of a wicket, when rain prevented any further play.
The Church Club were engaged with Audley on their hundred acre enclosure. The visitors batted first. At least they were the only players who did bat, for when the score was 38 for seven wickets, the gentle rain descended, and the game was abandoned. Out of the 58 J. Leek was responsible for 23.
To-day there will be only one junior match—Leek, and it will be difficult to find eleven players left in the town to do duty, as the usual exodus commenced yesterday, and Leek, that could not very properly be called "Out o' noise," all the places of business having closed for a week.

KEELE.
This week your millions of readers will doubt the authenticity of these notes, as they don't bear the usual "sit agina" forehead. But for our friend Jew Peter Phirius, I have no doubt we should have sat on business as usual under the old sign, but as I wrote in my copy-book years and years ago, "the wheel is suspended to the storm lamb." So it was on Saturday, thanks to Jew Peter. For wind, read Whitmore Rangers (including Whitmore, Silverdale, Newcastle, Porthill, and Madeley) for the storm lamb read Keele, and the parable is complete. In other words, we were in for the usual scorching, but for the rain which damped the scratch. We won the toss and elected to bat. The Rector and T. Jones opened to Lightfoot (Silverdale) and Mawdsley (Porthill). Jones soon retired, Lightfoot being yards too fast for him, and H. Taylor also retired from the same cause. Then Burroughs joined the Rector (who was playing very steadily), and the first change took place, Glover going on vice Mawdsley. Then the final change, and the game was over at 5 15. (33 for two wickets.) Burrough 15 and the Rector 11 (both not out). By some unaccountable means Burroughs played for Keele, when he was put down for Whitmore. I should just like to wonder where the fun comes in, bringing a man like Whitmore Rangers brought against a young and inexperienced team like Keele, in which, as a side, the players are horse grown. Truly, as was said in another column recently, "Sport is dead," but win at any cost seems to be very much alive. At all events, thanks to our umpire and friend "Jew Peter," we gained one point, and he stopped the game at the right time with his watering-can.
To-day, Norton-in-Hales at Keele.

RIGNALL END.
The above club journeyed to Chell last Saturday to play the return League fixture. We won the toss, and sent the home team in to bat. The match had not proceeded far when the rain came on, we stayed out as long as possible, but had to retire when we had succeeded in dismissing nine of the Chell men out for 59 runs. We were anxiously waiting for about an hour and a half for it to clear up so that we might knock up the runs and secure two points, as the fight for championship is a close one.
Nantwich Second team had no match on and they got the advantage of us, we having to lose a point owing to the rain. That is if we should have won. I think from past experience, and the determination of our players we should have pulled off the match. However, we had to be content with a draw. The rain stopping the match when the two last men were in for Chell. Score 59.
M. Statham bowled excellently, taking six wickets for 12 runs, and J. Dale three for 31. To-day Audley.

CLOUGH HALL.
Rain again interfered with the cricket fixture in the League match against Tunstall on Saturday. Dobson lost the toss, and Tunstall went to the wickets. Bull and Webster faced the bowling of Warburton and Parkin. The wicket was a heavy one, and runs came slowly. None of the visiting batsmen made double figures, and their innings closed for a total of 44 when the rain prevented further play. The average of the Clough bowling was as follows—Warburton, 25; Parkin, 09; Broadhurst, 5 0; and Rothwell, who did remarkably well, 1 25.
To-day the team should play away at Silverdale. Thursday's match against Tunstall was a dissertation for the Hall. Tunstall, who batted first, put up a total of 99, and against such a score the chances of the home club were small indeed. The visiting batsmen were too much for the bowlers, and secured a characterless victory. The Hall team succeeded in putting up 54—by no means bad. The bowling of Blatch was very destructive.

MARKET DRAYTON.
Rain fell pretty generally in this district on Saturday, and no cricketers of note came under my notice. Drayton did turn out in their encounter with Whitchurch, but rain fell so heavily, and the ground became so saturated, that although it cleared up about five it was found impossible to proceed with the game. Drayton were assisted by Mr. D. P. Heywood-Jones. The first three or four batsmen could do nothing against the bowling of Cliff, but J. Taylor (not out 12) and W. G. Stephenson (not out 3) broke the spell of fortune which had favoured the visitors. Drayton's fielding was a grand success to Taylor in keeping up the wicket so splendidly, and not in this case, which we are apt to see in local teams, too eager for run-getting. At this period the game was abandoned, a very heavy storm coming on.
To-day Newport play the return at Drayton.

HIGH LANE.
On Saturday we visited St. Paul's, whose ground is situated in "the lake district." Cricket seemed out of the question when we started to the ground. On our arrival, however, the weather soon cleared up. The home side won the toss, and decided to bat, sending Adams and Owen to face the bowling of Whitehouse and Capey. Wickets fell thus: Two for 2, three for 4, four for 7, five for 7, six for 7, seven for 17, nine for 17, nine for 17 (only ten men batting); total, 15. Whitehouse bowled eleven overs, four maidens, taking seven wickets for 9 runs. Capey took two for 5. Heavy rain fell, and we found another pitch and decided to go on with the game. What a disastrous start! Three wickets went down for 1 run, Wallace catching Foster in the first over, and Cartwright bowling two men in the second over. More rain fell, but the sides agreed to continue the game. Horn came as a substitute for Whitehouse with a wet jacket, and St. Paul's assured disinclined to go on. Barnett made the winning hit, but the game continued for three or four minutes, when the players could stand the wet no longer, all having a run for the pavilion with wet jackets.

LAWTON PARK.
Both fixtures on Saturday last with Middleport were postponed, owing to the rain. A start was made, but play was impossible, and the game ceased when Middleport had made 21 runs for the loss of five wickets. To-day Congleton pay the Park a visit in a friendly encounter.

JUNIOR CRICKET GOSSIP.
Saturday last turned out most unfavourable for cricket, and with one or two exceptions no matches could be brought to a definite conclusion owing to the disastrous weather. It is useless attempting to describe any of the games which commenced, and only one match has reached me to hand where the full time was played out. This was Fenton Second v. Crewe Second. The game took place on the latter's ground, and the home-team had whipped together a powerful side including Evanson, Brennan, Heaps, and Lyon, of the first eleven. The home-team batted first and put together 75. Evanson played well for 25, as did Hamner for 14. George Boultlemier was again in evidence with the ball, and captured six wickets for 30 runs, but had the fielding been up to the mark his analysis would have been much better. Boobserson, with his lobs, captured four wickets for 12 runs. I forget to mention that the players on each side numbered twelve. With an hour to get the runs, the visitors showed good form, and were unlucky in not winning, as they put together 56 for six wickets, and were thus robbed of victory, as time was called at six o'clock. Roberta batted splendidly for 27, not out, as did Winkle 16 and Harper 14.
