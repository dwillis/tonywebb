MONTGOMERY v. ANDERTSWITH.—Played at Llymore Park, Montgomery, on Saturday, in rainy weather. The home captain won the toss and sent the visitors in. Williams and A. Eaton, the Montgomery bowlers, were in fine form, taking six wickets for 12; four for 17 respectively, and getting rid of the Yatwylthians for 32, of which E. Davies, a stonemonger substitute, made 12. The Montgomery innings realised 111, towards which A. Eaton (11), O. D. Williams (41), R. T. Harris (13), Shaw (12), and Griffiths (11 not out), were the chief contributors. G. Barton, the Montgomery substitute, bowled well for the visitors, who had a weak team.

WEM v. SHAVINGTON.—Played at Shavington on Wednesday.

Anthony and Clayton went to the wicket first for Wem, and faced the bowling of Lonsdale and Arkinstall. Runs came freely, and the score rose to 67 before Clayton was caught and bowled by Exham. This broke the ice, Anthony being also dismissed in the next over for a freely hit 42. The rest of the wickets fell steadily, and the innings closed for 112 runs. Tea was then partaken of, and on resuming only one over had been bowled when rain came on heavily and stopped the play for the day. Scores :—

WHITTINGTON v. TREDDING.—(RETURN.)—The second fixture between these clubs was played at Teddmoor on Saturday, and resulted in favour of the visitors by 22 runs. For the winners (who only batted nine men) Humphreys played a spirited but somewhat fortunate innings of 24 before being bowled by W Hudson, the total for the first wicket being 23. No one else did much except W Jones who made 12, the innings realising 40. Rowson and Hudson bowled well. The home team made a fairly good start, the first wicket falling for 10, but all were disposed of for 24, no one reaching double figures. MONTGOMERY v. PONTEBURY.—These teams played off their return match at the latter place on Wednesday, in dull weather. The home team, which comprised a strong eleven, batted first, and were all dismissed by A. Katon and Williams for thirty-five, Katon taking five wickets for twelve, and Williams four for seventeen. Amos, who compiled a dozen, was the only man to make any stand. For the visitors the brothers Katon were in rare form, Pryce Katon carrying his bat from first wicket down for an admirable score of sixty-nine, while Alf Katon did yeoman service, and, together with his senior, succeeded in passing the Pontebury score with only a single wicket down. The other Montgomery men were accounted for by Amos and Cartwright, who secured four wickets each.
