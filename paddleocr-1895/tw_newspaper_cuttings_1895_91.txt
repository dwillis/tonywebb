# NEWCASTLE DAILY JOURNAL: THURSDAY 29 AUGUST 1895-2.

## CRICKET.

## NOTICE TO CRICKETERS.

We shall be glad to receive the names and addresses of club secretaries throughout Northumbland as early as possible, in order that the resolution passed at the recent County Club meeting held in Newcastle may be sent to them. We shall also be glad to receive the names of anyone willing to subscribe to the County Club.

To-day Mr Clayton's eleven continues their tour by playing a team raised by Mr R G Mortimer. The match extends over to-morrow, and a star will be made at 11 o'clock this morning. The teams are:

Mr Clayton's XI: F G H Clayton, A T B Dunn, W B Anderson, H Philipson, B N Bosworth-Smith, M G Barlow, J M Quinton, C G Pope, K A Woodward, H D Corbet, and N Malcolmson.

Mr Mortimer's XI: R G Mortimer, J Harrell, C Latimer, C M Henderson, R B Hoare, G Garnett, T Gowans, H J Salmon, C F Blain, with Grithin and another.

## MR A BROWNES XL. M H J SALMON'S XL

Our readers will remember that a short time are “Custs” stated that the meeting between the teams of Mr A Browne of Oxford Hall and Mr H J Salmon of Newcastle at the Constabulary Ground on Monday and Tuesday, September 2nd and 3rd, would provide two days really first-class cricket. At that time the announcement was made to the brothers L C H and R O N Palmaret, as well as C B Fry, the famous Oxford triple Blue, were expected to play in the match; but, unfortunately, none of these celebrated cricketers can come north, and, consequently, Mr Salmon has been more or less compelled to continue himself to local talent. Still, from the names given below, it will be seen that his team is a fairly strong one, in both batting and bowling.

Mr Browns, on the other hand, has spread his net over a wider area, and a better eleven than he is bringing, has not been seen on Tynnside, for it includes Wainwright, the Yorkshire County bowler, and Pongher of Leicestershire, whose performances, especially against Surrey, acupm him as one of the best bowlers of the present day. Besides these, Pickett and F G Bull of Essex are included in the team, so that, if Mr Salmon wins, his men will have to airpass all their previous performances. The teams are as follow:

Mr Brown's XI: A Browns (captain). C Perkins, S Marshall, A R Fuller, F G Bull, G Elser, Lambert, Pickett, Wauwrigon, Pougher, and another (probably Brugs of Lancashire).

Mr Salmon's XI: H J Salmon (captain), G A T Salmon, C O Hall, J S Brown, S C Ferguson, A T B Duon, C F Blain, H Haslam, F G H Clayton, G Simpson, and the Rev E W R Walters.

MR A. C. MACLAREN'S AVERAGE

Including the innings completed on Tuesday morning. A. Maclaren's batting average is a fraction clear 54, and he thus runs a close race with "W. G.," who failed to score for Gloucestershire on Monday. The figures are: W. G. Grace, 54'20; A. C. Maclaren, 54'01.

## MR BEWICKES XI. v. MR F C R CLAYTON'S XI. (By "Custos.")

The match between these teams was resumed at the Close House ground, yesterday, but on account of the rain no definite issue was arrived at. Though the early part of the day was fine, heavy showers fell at intervals throughout the afternoon, driving the players from the field, and ultimately causing the game to be abandoned as a draw, when, in their second innings, Mr Bewicke's team had scored 35 for one wicket. Mr Clayton's eleven were the greater sufferers from the wet weather, their bowlers being thus severely handicapped; though at the finish the rain undoubtedly saved them from defeat. The change from slow to a fast wicket proved too much for most of them, who often played late at Guttridge's fast deliveries, and that lower captured six of their wickets for 32 runs; while Simpson's four cost 35. For the home side Waltz again batted well.

Prompt to time Anderson resumed bowling from the railway end, Beworth-Smith being the other bowler. After four had been scored Guttridge was snarly taken by Pope at extra-cover off Beworth-Smith. Former followed in but had jelly made three when he put one up in the slips which Woodward took. Walters gave Anderson a hot catch at mid-off, which was dropped, and then, with an off drive to the boundary, he sent up the "hundred." His partner, Salman, opened with a lofty 2 through the slips, and, after sitting another single, was caught at long on by Quinton off the Isfunder. C. Perkins filled the vacancy, but lost Welsler, who was taken as short slip by Dunn; and C. Dewick, the last man, came in. The end had now arrived, however, as in attempting a short run the home captain was cut, the innings raising 112, or 28 behind the score of their opponents. With 40 up Guttridge beat Anderson, and Clayton joined Quinton. They stayed together for some time, when Clayton played outside one from Simpson, and was bowled (6 for 59); and an adjournment was made for lunch. A shower of rain caused this interval to be prolonged for about half an hour. On resuming Bowworth-Smith joined Quinton, opening his account with a cut for two; but Guttridge hit his leather stamp when he had scored 7-7 for 68. Two runs later Simpson bowled Quinton. Corbet, the newcomer, was sent back by Simpson after making a single. Moleolron was last man, and increased the score by a couple of 4's when Pope fell to a catch by Simpson in the slips, the total reaching 84, thus leaving Mr Bowwick's team 113 runs to get to win.

Duno and Philipson began Mr Clavten's second innings to the bowling of Gurtridge (house end), and Simpson. Philipson opened the scoring with a single, the first run he has made on the tour. When he had scored three, however, he was out to a brilliant lethargied catch in the slip by Simpson, off Guttridge.
