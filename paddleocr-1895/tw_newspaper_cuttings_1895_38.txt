## WYCOMEE MESERVES v. WEST WYCOMBK.

This match was played at West Wyombe on Saturday afternoon. The visitors went to the wicket first, but offered a very feasible resistance to the bowling of Turner and Fletcher, who each took five wickets for 10 runs. The home side, owing to the good batting of Turner, A. Harman, and Fletcher, reached a total of 70. For the Reserves Ken bowled in good form, taking six wickets for 25 runs. In their second attempt the Reserves did better, and passed their former score before the third wicket fell; but their total was not high enough to avert defeat, as it left the home side only 33 to get to win, which they accomplished with the loss of four wickets.

## HIGH WYCOMBE v. WOOBURN INSTITUTE.

This match, which was played on the Wycombe ground last Saturday afternoon, resulted in a draw that if time had permitted, would have ended in favour of the home team. The visitors were without Earber, the Lunons, and others who generally play for the Institute; but they had the assistance of E. Bacon, who proved a tower of strength to them in both departments of the game. Wycombe did not play their full strength, more than half their number being reserve men. The home side went in first, and a total of 158 was reached before they were all disposed of. The chief contributor to this score was F. George, whose figures included a 4, six 3's, and seven 3's. Other good innings were played by Bird, G. George, and Page who drove one of Bacon's deliveries out of the field for 6. A word of praise ought to 1,000 to Eaffety among the younger members of the team, who played well for 21; his principal hits being a 4, three 3's, and three 3's. Bacon was the most successful in bowling, his eight wickets costing a tride over six runs a piece, and the last three being obtained without a run being scored off him. For Wooburn Institute, Bacon, Bowler, and Plamridge played well, the second named hitting G. George for a 6. When stupe was drawn the visitors had made 129 for seven wickets, the last three of which had put on but eight runs between them. Page was the most successful with the ball, but his four wickets cost nearly 13 each. Score and analysis.

## MABLOW v. MR. J. MONBO WALKER'S XI.

On Saturday Marlow met an eleven got together by Mr. J. Mouro Walker, of Bisham Abbey, on the Marlow ground. The weather was delightfully fine, and an enjoyable game resulted. The visitors won the tics, and took first innings. Harbottle hit hard for 39, and with smaller contributions from all the side except Lane (who was run out before he had scored), the total reached 183. Harbottle hit a 9 (four for an overthrow) and four 4's. Mouro Walker was credited with an 8 (four for an overthrow). Marlow lost Paine and Faulkner cheaply, but with useful scores from Langley, Bower, Railie, and Want, the visitors total was passed with six wickets down. Eventually the innings closed for 183. Want—one of the Australian visitors staying in Marlow—hit a 5, six 4's, and three 5's in his dashing score of 41. Langley hit five 4's in his 30. Mr. Walkers team went in a second time, and had compiled 113 for six wickets, when stumps were drawn. Marlow therefore won by 50 runs on the first innings. Full score and analysis:

## WOOMER KELL'S VUE WANDERERS v. HOLLOWAY'S BOOT OPERATIVES C.C.

The return match between the above-named tams, which took place on the Western Rye last Saturday, exited great interrupt. Mr. E. Colman, who "stripped" the Wanderers, turned out a formidable eleven, while the shomakers were but poorly represented, in fact at the last moment they had to make up with five substitutes. The men of "was and latter" hailed first, and ran up a creditable total of 60; Wilks, Humphrey, Collier, Lillyman, and Oles, contributing their respective scores in fine style. Tibbles, with the ball, was in rare little, his four wickets only costing a fraction over 4 runs a plate; Anger took 2 wickets for 20. The time eventually arrived for the Wanderers' turns at the wicket, Colman and Yousers opening the attack against the destructive deliveries of Humphrey and Mead. Wickets 1 and 2 went down for 16, and at this juncture it looked as though the Wanderers' costingant would collapse, six wickets being captured for 20, the last three of which not adding a run. The score rose slowly, and when Thorne went in the Wanderers' hopes revived, the great sloger piling on his 15 in double quick time. The end eventually arrived with the total at 84, which left the operations winners on the first innings by 12 runs. Both Mead and Hampshire, who bowled throughout the game, captured 4 wickets each for 26 runs a place. The shomen went in a second time, and at the close of the venture 31 runs had been added for the loss of seven wickets. After the match the teams heartily enjoyed a social at the "Belle Vox," Gordon-road. Scores:

## SPEEN v. ICKENHAM.

This match was played at Spoon, a good number of spectators being present to witness the play. It was felt by both teams that it would be a close shave, so each team entered the field ready to its best to win, but, after a very pleasant game, it was found that Spoon had been beaten by 20 runs. The Spoon Brass Band was present, and added to the pleasantness of the day by playing selections of music. A capital tea was provided by Mrs. Gomm for all who cared to partake, and altogether a thoroughly enjoyable day was the result of the club's effort. Score:
