Ginn's XI.—The annual buys' match at Hagley was played in Hagley Park, on Aug. 15. Although two of the players were 17 years and three more were 16, the great majority were of 15 and 14, while three were only 13. As by far the greater number of those playing had taken part in matches during the four preceding years, the game produced much interest. The day was the, though dull; the wicket, in the earlier part of the day was somewhat difficult. J. Gibbs won the two and put his men in first. They made a disastrous start, losing three wickets (r.s) runs. P. Lyon and Jones-Williams improved matters by some already play, but the whole side were just for 42, of which eight were extras. J. Lyttelton going on to bowl in the last part of the innings, too, three wickets for one run. O. Mackintosh also did well, capturing four for 13. The opening of the innings of Lord Cobham's XI, was no better than that of their opponents, as four men were out for nine runs. Harkness and K. Mackintosh improved matters considerably, but they were treated kindly by some of the fielders, since five catches (not all easy ones) were dropped in a very short space of time. When Harkness left at 28, the end was not long in coming, and the total fell short by two runs of that of their opponents. P. Lyon, whose�welling and fielding were alike admirable, took five wickets for 14 runs, and made four catches. In their second innings the visitors made a much better show. Jones Williams and W. H. Gibbs put on 28 runs for the second wicket, and O. A. Lynn, aided by his brother, added 31 for the sixth. The former made the highest score of the match, a freely hit 23. The other between all did something, and, with the aid of 14 extras, 96 was reached when the 10th wickets fell. Of the bowlers in this innings, N. Talbot came out best, taking in 183 over four wickets for 16 runs. Lord Cobham's team were left with 90 to make an about an hour and a half. An excellent catch by P. Lyon off his own bowling took the first wicket for 0. Matters improved somewhat, and Harkness again did much service to his side by vigor as hitting. Seymour and E. Mackintosh also got double figures, and their score reached 65 for seven wickets when barely 10 minutes remained for play. Two men, however, failed to score, and, as the last man was absent, the inning was brought to a close just before time for 70, leaving the other side victorious, after a very interesting match, by 28 runs. Apart from the errors mentioned above, the all-round fielding was good on both sides. Score:
