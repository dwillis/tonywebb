Stockport met Werneth, on the ground of the latter, for the first time this season before a grand gate of over two thousand spectators, and in charming weather. Both teams were well represented. Werneth were rinne the services of Wharmby, one of their pros, but had Brookes, of the Marochoster staff and late of Stockport, assisting them, and who did very well too, taking five wickets for just over nine runs a piece. STOCKPORT v. WARNATH (2nd ELEVEN).—These two old rivals met for the first time this season at Oale Green Park, in glorious weather, and before a fair attendance. The visitors, winning the ties, decided to bat a little after three o'clock, to the bowling of Jones and McLachlan. Jackson oped splendidly with a four, but on the whole runs came slowly, eight wickets being down for 93, when Mareland and Watson became partners and a splendid stand was made, the score being taken. to 170 before either were separated, the score being still the same when the innings closed. After the usual interval Stockport commenced to bat a a quarter past five, with a rather formidable task before them. May soon set to work in earnest. The first wicket compiled 47 towards the number, May, the retiring bateman, accounting for 43 of these. F. Selby, the new man in, seemed rather uncomfortable at first, but he was getting more at home when he lost the service of Royle, the score reading 73-2-19. Marbell joined Selby with almost a hundred runs wanting, and the hand of the clock pointing to six. They had much to do, but the batmen soon set to work, Marshall hitting four 4's in one over. Excitement ran high as time crept on. The spectators became quite riveted to the game, especially the junior portion, who were enthusiastic with delight as each batman sent the leather to the boundary. It was a treat to watch Marshall play each fresh howler. Nothing seemed to come amiss, but another wicket fell before the required runs were reached, Selby being cleaned bowled by the captain for a very useful 46. With ten minutes left to get the remaining nine runs, Jones joined Marshall, the latter soon making the winning hit with a boundary. He was shortly afterwards bowled for a stand 67, comprising 13 fours and 4 twus. Time arrived when the fifth wicket fell, Stockport being victorious by 11 runs and five wickets. Werneth was very strongly represented. This was their first defeat of the season; but the homesters have to come yet.
