# ABINGDON HERALD: SATURDAY 7 SEPTEMBER 1895

## ABINGDON CRICKET AND FOOTBALL

## CRICKET SEASON, 1895.

Matches arranged. 21; played. 17; won. 8; lost. 7; drawn. 2; scratched. 4. The averages of the 1st Xi. in both batting and bowling are appended.

In both batting and bowling are appended. It will be noticed that the late captain, Mr. F. D. Curtis, heads the batting list with an average of 24.50, Payne and Wilson coming next with 16.33 and 16.25 respectively, while Dandridge, Alkin, W. Legge, F. H. Pryce and Skurray all get into double figures. Messrs. A. C. M. Croome and F. Deane appeared once each, and scored over the century, 117 being Mr. Croome's score and 109 Mr. Deane's. In the bowling Payne, Dandridge and H. S. Challenor have borne the brunt, the former taking 27 wickets at an average cost of 5.37 runs, Dandridge, 49 at an average of 7.97 runs, and Challenor 37 wickets at an average of 9.02; all very good performances. Messrs. W. T. Morland, Wilson, Lovett, Legge and Ward have all rendered useful service in this department. Though the 1st XL have achieved successes over Oxford City and Reading, they have not done so well generally, as in other years. The worst feature of this year's proceedings is the number of matches scratched. This we consider a bad omen for the club, as the reason given for the scratchings is the inability of the officials to raise teams. This ought not to be with any club having so many playing members on the books. Each member is supplied with a fixture-list at the beginning of the season, and as the success of the club should be the first thought of all members we greatly wonder at so many members engaging to play other matches on dates fixed for the club matches, but to this can be traced the falling off in the 1st XL performances. If all the subscribing members would pull together, the Abingdon Cricket and Football Club would never lack a team, either 1st or 2nd, that would fail to uphold the honour of the club and the town which supports it, and we hope in future there will be no difficulty in this particular direction. We heartily congratulate the club on the success it has so far achieved and the members may be assured that if the remedies prescribed are properly applied there is a brilliant future in store.

## THE SECOND KLEVEN

although not so successful this year as last, have been able to win more matches than they have lost, and in three cases out of five, had time permitted the drawn games to be played out the gains would most probably have been increased. The batting averages show a slight falling off. Jackson, who last year had an average of 22, takes second place with 15, but Glanville and Steptoe show marked improvement. Heath carries off the honours, but he has only played in six innings. There are several young players who claim attention, and it would be well for older members next season to look to these rising batsmen if they wish the club to prosper. Matcham, who only played three innings, was not out on each occasion, and winds up with an average of 58. In the bowling department a word of commendation is due to Glanville, who has bowled admirably. Heath and Pryce have both bowled well. If a great deal of the net practice was abolished and scratch teams were got together to practice, the fielding would be better. An improvement in that respect is much needed, for if the fielding had been smarter several matches could have been pulled out of the fire this season. Matches played, 12; won, 4; lost, 3; drawn, 5.
