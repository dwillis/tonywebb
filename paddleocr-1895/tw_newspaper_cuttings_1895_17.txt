BISCUIT FACTORY B XI V WHITE O'ROSS (BASINGSTOCK)- On the Factory club ground on Saturday. The home teams batted first and ran up the respectable total of 131 runs, mainly owing to the batting of Crook, Povey and Leaver, the former especially punishing the bowling, he scoring 'no less than seven 4's in succession. In the bowling department, honours fell to W H Phillips, who took eight wickets for eight runs in nine over. The visitors, on following on, made a much better stand, scoring 51 for the loss of two wickets.

Health End v. Sr. Lawrence's (Reading). At Heath End on Saturday, the result of a highly enjoyable game being an easy win for Heath End by 85 runs and two wickets. The batting of Maxwell, White and W Hunt was exceedingly commendable, Maxwell's innings of 72 runs being marred with two mistakes only. The bowling of Maxwell and Roberts was superb, Roberts's analysis reading seven overs, three maidens, nine runs and five wickets; and Maxwell's thirteen, four maidens, fourteen runs and three wickets. After the match, through the hospitality of Mr Hunt, the players partook of tea on the lawn. At the conclusion of the meal the captain with a cap in recognition of his recent performance of the hat trick Mr Hunt referred in eulogistic terms to the excellent services rendered by Mr Roberts, who, in reply, thanked the club for their kindness. Guldhall v Newtown.—On the Newbury ground on Saturday, and resulted in a win for the Guldhall by 75 runs on the first innings. Guldhall, winning the toss, sent Pope and Humphries to the wickets, but when the score was at 21, Humphries was caught and bowled by Williamson. No lengthy stand was then made until Edwards joined Pope; Edwards soon made 16. Drower was the only other man to reach double figures; and by free play he scored 22, the innings closing for 102. Newtown, on handling the willow, made a most disastrous start, six wickets falling for half-a dozen runs. Allsop was the best batman, playing well for his 18 not out. The innings closed for 27, Newtown being 60 runs behind their confres. Allsop and Williamson at their next venture played admirably, scoring between them 101 before being parted. Pope bowled well for G.O.O., doing the hat trick.
