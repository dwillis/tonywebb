# STAFFORDSHIRE SENTINEL: SATURDAY 15 JUNE 1895-1.

## NOTES AND COMMENTS

Radeers will hardly be prepared for the statement of a correspondent that early this morning ice, the thickness of a penny, was found at Fell Brook, near Backnall. It was undoubtedly very cold last evening for the time of the year, and even after the sun had risen this morning the air was unusually keen. Nevertheless, the outlook for crickets as soon approached was fairly satisfactory, and there seemed a very prospect of the various local engagements being entered upon and completed without much interruption from rain or cold.

Staffordsbire played the Seoved Eleven from the Oval on Monday and Tuesday at Stoke, and the home team were beaten by six wickets. There are, however, several conclusions of a consoling mature to be drawn from the game. The first—and by many it will be considered the most important—is that the match does not count in the second-class championship. Had this been otherwise Staffordsbire would most likely have suffered a heavier defeat still, for King (Porthill), who was the main局 of the beating in their first inning, and afterwards proved himself almost as useful with the ball, was not qualified for the county, having been born in Salem, where he came to this district a few months ago.

Another point which will tend to lighten the sorrows of Staffordshire craketers is that the Surrey second spring have not been defeated for three seasons past, and even brought off a drawn game yesterday with Lancashire although the latter lasted the grand total of 483 in their first innings. But the fact that the Staffordshire men have improved all round since their defeat by Northamptonshire a fortnight ago is the circumstances which will afford most pleasure to true craketers, for what matter whether points be won or lost so that play is witnessed worthy to be called cricket. And then to crown all the writer was informed by one of the Surrey eleven years we Staffordshire people under-rate our county in cricket matters; that the County eleven are really a very good lot, and the Surrey men considered them one of the very best among second-class counties. They were sorry they could not say the same for the ground, which, with all due respect to Hanson, usually most successful in the treatment of turf, is must be said was not good. It was bumpy on the first day, while on Tuesday every ball sent up a cloud of dust, and about three yards in front of the hotel wicket a large pics was completely worn out.
