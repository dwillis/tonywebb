SWINDERRY AND COLLINORAM V. LINCOL N LINDUW (A TEAM).—Those teams met at Swinderby on Saturday, in storiny weather, neither side being fully represented. Strawson having lost the loss, the Linauni had to take the field. The home team were first represented by Wigrin and Loveday, the bowling being shared by the Lindum captain and Fellowus. With the score at nine Wigram was bowled, and Loveday also lost his wicket three runs later. F. K. Fellowes then joined Tweed, and the score continued to rise. At thirty-eight White was given a trial with the ball, the change working well, for at 44 Tweed sent one from the new bowler into E. K. Long's hands, and one run later Fellowes returned onto Wigrain. H. G. All three wickets fell at ten to Staloy, who, in trying to catch a sharp return from Staloy, had the misfortune to get his thumb knocked up, and had to retire from bowling; his three wickets were obtained for three runs. The Lindum won the match by seven runs. Score:

NEWARK TOWN—The visit of Newark Town to the Stamp End ground is locked upon as the principal home fixture of the season, and as the locals had obtained the assistance of H. H. Daft for Saturday's match exceptional interest attached to the game. Unfortunately for the "gato" the club anticipated, and for the comfort of those who love to witness cricket, the weather was most unfavourable, and it was not until four o'clock that a start could be made. As soon as it was evident there would be a game supporters of the club rapidly appeared on the ground until, considering the weather, there was a good company. The local captain won the toss and decided to bat, sending in Harrod and Mettam to face the bowling of Ringrose and Hollis (pro.). Itans came steadily, but after making five Harrod pit a ball into the hands of Ryan. Kerrigan then partnered Mettam, and a good stand was made, both men playing good cricket. Mettam got several nasty knocks, and was eventually smartly taken at point by Jones. H. B. Daft now partnered Kerrigan, and those present had the pleasure of witnessing quite a treat in the style of batting. Daft began carefully until he obtained complete control over the bowling, and though several changes were made, it was to no effect. Kerrigan and Simpson, to the delight of the locals, also showed their best form, and, although they did not score so fast as Daft, they played a confident game, and thoroughly upheld the credit of the club. Whitlock did not stay long, and then Heywood joined Daft to give him an opportunity of making his score fifty, which he was not long in doing, and the innings was closed with the total at 106. Daft being not out 51. Daft's driving was both clean and powerful, several boundary strokes being included in his score, while he showed a complete command of the bat. But for the length of the grass in the out-field the scores of all who made runs would have been larger. The visitors fared but badly on going to the wicket. They had about an hour to play, with a chance of being defeated, while at the best they could only expect to make a draw. With six wickets down for 52, rally came on and prevented further play. Richardson smartly threw out Dayer, and a good throw in by the same play enabled Brown to run out Dr. Kingrose; a skitch at the wicket by Brown getting rid of a cre. Of the others Heywood bowled Job, Kerrigan caught and bowled Ryan, and Cherry was out hit wicket off Harrod. Altogether the locals are to be congratulated on the game they played. Recor:

Lincoln St. Hugh's v. Maneet Rasen.-On Whit-Monday St. Hugh's Club played at Market Raven a whole day match on the ground of the latter. Market Raven won the toss and were all out for 38, Mr. Briscoe alone making any stand against the bowling of Melbourne and Kerrigan, the former taking six wickets for 15, and the latter three for 16. On St. Hugh's going on after lunch, a stand of two hours' duration was made by Jackson and Melbourne. On Jackson leaving a further stand was made by Melbourne and Kerrigan, who added 67 more. Melbourne was then bowled for 62, and when time was called Kerrigan was not out for a freely played 52, the total score on the call of time being 117 for seven wickets. Score:

Notts Forestea.—The opening match of the season came off on Whit-Monday. The weather in the morning was equally and threatening, but luckily play was not interfered with during the day. Skegness winning the toss, W. Dench playing on shortly after to Morley for 14. B. N. Aenton was at the wickets some time before he was c and b to Hall for a steadily played innings of 10 runs. T. Brearcy going in sixth wicket down, with the score at 16 at 47, made matters rather lively, contributing five fours to the boundary before he was disposed of for a hard-hit 81 runs, and the total carried to 80. COOPER CORAM (LEICHESTER).—The second match of the season was played on Tuesday last, and was chiefly remarkable for another fine bowling performance by Dr. Cross, who went even “one better” than on the previous day when playing Notte Foresters, as against the visitors from Leicester he took nine wickets, at a cost of only ten runs, the home team winning easily owing to the fine display of bowling already referred to. For Skegness W. Kitwood and Dr. Cross opened against the deliveries of Eastwood and Porring, W. Kitwood, who was in good form, doing most of the running getting. T. Wilson made some good hits for his ten runs, and E. N. Ashton played steady cricket for a like number. Dench and Kitwood played good cricket while together, the latter being eventually bowled by Eastwood for a well-played innings of 38 runs. The professional's wicket was the last to fall, after making 24 in good form, the innings closing for 113 runs. The team from Leicester sent in Towers and Mountney to the bowling of Dr. Cross and Dench. With the second half delivery Epton caught Towers at mid-off, off Cross, and five wickets were actually down to Cross in the first four overs delivered for three runs. The only one to make any stand was Mountney, who made 18 not on the other in the 1st round. The Leicester team being all out for the meagro score of 41 runs. Dr. Cross's analysis is again worthy of mention, viz. —Ten overs, four balls, five maidens, ten runs, nluowickets. Score: —
