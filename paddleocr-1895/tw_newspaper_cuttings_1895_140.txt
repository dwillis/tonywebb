# HERTFORDSHIRE EXPRESS: SATURDAY 9 NOVEMBER 1895-1

## LOCAL CRICKET AVERAGES, 1895.

## BEDS. COUNTY O.O.

With a balance of losses over gains the past reason can hardly be said to have been a thoroughly satisfactory one with this club—although, as yet, and to some extent, a ticket is still played for the sake of the game among the counties which do not rank as first-class, and the existence of which consequently does not depend upon the more results of matches. The number of drawn games—two out of eleven—is agreeably small; for if one thing more than another tends to lessen the interest of these two-day engagements it is the varying frequency of draws. Of the remaining nine Bedfordshire won four and lost five. That the county's measure of success was not greater must be put down, more than to any other cause, to the partial failure of the cap. In and secretary as a between Mr. Thring's aggregate of 253 in 13 inning falling for short of what was expected of him in a season so favourable to bligh scoring. At the same time he is practically at the head of the list of averages, Mr. Bessley having played less than half as many matches as his captain, Meers. J. Manton and O. O. Pope have likewise done fairly well with the bat. Mr. W. F. Brown's record of 64 wickets at an average cost of 12 runs is a remarkable one for a slow bowl in such a summer as that of 1895.

## HITOBIN O.O.

The Hitchin Club has had a fairly successful season, having played 19 matches, 10 of which were won, 7 loot and 2 drawn. The club was successful against Cambridge Nihilists, St. Albans, City Police, Belgrave, Knebbworth, Arlesey (2), Baldock, Hants, County and the Commercial Union O.O. It was defeated by the Nihilists, National Provincial Bank O.O., Mr. W. Wright's Notts Xi, Mr. Strauss's Notts Xi, Old Northovians, Three Counties Asylum and M.O.O. The two drawn games were with St. Albans and Gentlemen of Horts. It was a source of great disappointment that the club in the better class of matches—notably the Notts matches—was very poorly represented; and that this was the case in little encouragement to the Hon. Secretary to try and introduce higher standard of cricket in the town. Turning to the performances of the individual players we find A. H. Delm6-RedeliJo at the top of the batting averages with an average of 30 for 9 innings. The next four batsmen, who alone played more than occasionally, have also excellent figures. Wilson's average of 20, like his aggregate, is probably the best since the commencement of his long connexion with the club. In Captain E. Rivett-Carnas and his brother the club gained new adherents whose services with the bat proved valuable, and the former at times did considerable execution with his slow-underhand bowling. The dry weather and consequent good wickets have left their trace in the averages of the bowlers, even Wilson's capital record showing a falling off as compared with previous performances.

## BALDOOK 0.0.

This club has had a very successful season. Ten matches have been played, seven being won and five lost. The wins were against Ashwell, Mr. H. Tanqueray's Cambridge XI, Gotterod, Three Counties Aylum, Koeworth and Stevenage (2); the losses were with Briton Wanderers, Hishin and Ashwell. This is the best record the club has had since the year 1888, when ten matches were won and two lost. Two second eleven matches were also played with Gravely, both of which were won; and two matches with the Baldock Fire Brigade, one being lost and the other won. The Fire Brigade match we hope to see made an annual fixture, and to make it more popular still a whole day should be allotted to it if possible. W. Bracey wins the best with an average of 1236. H. J. Izzard has the good average of 21, but has not played in sufficient matches to take the bat. W. Payne, O. J. Peek and H. Tanqueray have averages exceeding 10. The tall presented by Mr. O. J. Vessay for the best bowling average is won by H. J. Izzard, P. Flitton not having taken the required number of wiskets.

## HITOHI IN BLUE OROSS TEMPERANCE BRIGADE O.O.

During the past season 15 matches were played, of which nine were won and nine lost. The chief weakness, as will be seen by a glance at the figures, has been in the batting, not one player having a double-figure average, although the wickets for the greater part of the season were favourable to run-getting. In bowling Besterfield and Anderson did so well that a change was rarely needed, and both have creditable records; Ellie (underhand) also proved very useful at times. The fielding of the team, taking the season all through, left much to its desired, and with increased smartness in this department more than one game might have had a different ending. The majority of the games were closely contasted, and at one time three matches in succession finished with a difference of only one run (two lost and one won). The matches won were against Knebworth, Stevenage 2nd XI, St. Paulswalden (2), St. Ibbes, Hitchin St. Savior's (2), King's Cross G.N.R. and Perse School, Cambridge. Defeats were inflicted by Stevenage 2nd XI, Arlassy (2), St. Ibbes, Hitchin Grammer School, Hitchin G.N.R. (2), Knebworth and Perse School, Cambridge.
