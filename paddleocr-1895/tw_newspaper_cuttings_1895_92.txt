## Mr MORTIMER'S XI. v. Mr CLAYTON'S XL. (By "Custos.")

Yesterday the meeting between these teams on the Constabulary Ground, Jesmond, drew together a good number of spectators. The heavy rain overnight made the wicket soft to start with, but the sun soon affected it, and it became very difficult as the day wore on. Under these circumstances winning the toss conferred a great advantage. Dame Fortune decided in favour of Mr Mortimer in this matter, and he, of course, took first innings. Taken altogether the batting was moderate, that of Griffin standing out by itself as the best of the day. This professional opened with a very fluky stroke over point's head; but quickly settling down, he scored freely off all the bowlers, and did not make another mistake until he played on to Quinton. His 63 included a 6, eight 4's, a 3, and five 2's. The finest performance of the day, however, was that of C Latimer, who obtained eight wickets for 30 runs. Helped by the ground, and making the most of his opportunity, Latimer took a great amount of watching, and managed to "stick up" almost every batsman. The fielding also was smart. Some of Mr Clayton's teams shaped very poorly; and at the finish, with three of them out in their second innings, they are still 47 runs behind.

Winning the toss Mortimer decided to ball, taking Garnets with him to the wickets to open the inning. The bowling was entrusted to Pope and Anderson, while Woodward kept wicket. Off the last ball in the MCC man's first over Mortimer tried a short run. Dunn, however, threw Garnett's wicket down from extra cover, so that the experiment proved a costly one. The captain then started the scoring with a boundary through the slips off Pope. Hansell filled the vacancy and commenced with a 3 to the off; but was taken in the slips by Anderson off Pope immediately afterwards. Griffith was next man in, and getting several 4% off Pope brought on Dunn as the cemetery end when the score stood at 3t, while with 43 up Corbet relieved Anderson. This change proved effectual, as Corbet bowled Mortimer, who had played good crickets for 19, the partnership having called 43 runs. Cumberlego followed in; and Bosworth-Smith went on in place of Dunn. The new-comer was missed from a return by Corbet. Griffin did nearly all the scoring and with 89 up Clayton relieved Corbet. Quinton going on at the other end. A 4 to the professional sent up the "century" and then Quinton bowled him. The retiring batsman had played a capital innings for 65 without a chance. V. Thompson succeeded, and with 110 on the board an adjournment was made for luncheon. On returning Bosworth-Smith started the howling. Cumberlego left his crease to attempts a leg-bye, and was promptly run out. (5 for 111.) House did not stay long, being caught as mid-off by Corbet off Clayton after making 9. (6 for 127.) Henderson was the new-comer. After six runs had been added Bosworth Smith dismissed Thompson with a fine one-handed catch, low down, a extra cover. Latimer failed to score; and with 140 up Anderson was tried again, but the separation came from the other end, Bosworth-Smith bowling Henderson. (9 for 156.) Salmon was last man in, but Gowans monopolized the scoring until he was caught at extra-cover by Barlow off Bosworth-Smith, the inning closing for 161. Clayton took Philipson to the wickets with him to open their innings. Griffith and Latimer bowling. The captain started merely, getting the professional's first two deliveries to the off-boundary, while Philipson also scored a 4 off Latimer. When he had made 11 Clayton was taken at the wicket by Salmon off Latimer (one for 15). Bosworth-Smith's wicket was saved by a wide throw in from Gowans and then Philipson played on to Griffin (two for 27). Barlow followed in, but at 33 lost his partner, who was sent back by Latimer. Anderson was bowled for a single, and Dunn was caught in the slips off the first ball he received. Quinton and Barlow carried the score to 60, when Latimer clean bowled the last-named batsman, repeating the performance to Woodward before the latter had scored. Quinton fell a victim to Griffin immediately after; and Pope had only made a single when he put his leg in front. Corbet, the last man, smoked a 4 as a start, and was then beaten by the Northumbrian, the total reaching only 74.

Being 87 runs behind, Mc Clayton's eleven followed on, the captain going in first with Barlow, Griffin and Latimer again bowling. Barlow returned one to Latimer when he had made 2, and Clayton left at 24; and with the score at 40 Bosworth-Smith was given out leg before. Then, as the light had become very bad, it was decided to draw stumps for the day. Play will be resumed at eleven o'clock this morning. Score:

ACKLINGTON AND DISTRICT v. ALNWICK. This match was played at Alnwick on Wednesday, in cold and threatening weather, on behalf of the Alnwick Crickers Club, before a moderate attendance of spectators. The visitors won the loss, and elected to that on a soft wicket, which became more and more tricky as the game went on. The start was most disastrous, two wickets being down for two runs; but on the two Graves getting together they were not separated until 50 runs had been registered by good cricket. The score finally reached 57, of which number J Acheson Gray was responsible for 33, six of the side failing to score. The home aides fared even a worse, as only Shoomsille offered any resistance to the bowling of C'Riddell and H Rudge, the former capturing three and the latter six, and the whole side were good rid of for the small score of 54.
