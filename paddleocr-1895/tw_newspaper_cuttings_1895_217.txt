# STAFFORDSHRE SENTINEL: SATURDAY 24 AUGUST 1895-2

## BASFORD v. KLELE.

Roele visited Baxford this afternoon to play off the return encounter with the team that beat them decisively in July. The weather was beautifully fine, but the wicket was treacherous, and having won the toes the home captain put his opponents in. A. Walklate and W. Beaton opened the game to the bowling of J. G. Hughes and G. Shrubsole. One run was knocked by the first-named batsman, off Hughes, when he was bowled by the last ball of the over, Boustoff having added 1 more run. N. A. Cogbill came in and matters improved, 24 being reached before the incoming batsman was caught at mid-on by Rev. A. Paice. Benton was caught by Sutton at mid-off without addition, and H. Taylor, who had succeeded Coghill, fell at 26. G. Sutton and the Rev. F. C. Stanner became associated, but the latter had his middle pegshot down without scoring. F. A. Winstanley joined Sutton, who was caught by Shrubsole behind off a peculiar stroke. A. Capper did not stay long, Shrubsole accounting for him when he had made a single. F. J. Harford and J. Taylor then got together, the former being bowled by Shrubsole at 44. W. Bowers whipped in and got a couple, when he was smartly caught by A. D. Stewart, the innings reasoning 47 runs and having lasted an hour. Shrubsole took five wickets for 11 and Hughes five for 35. The Basford innings was commenced at 4:35 by the brothers Stewart, Archie taking the first over from Walklate. A disastrous start was made, the batsman in cutting the howler's third delivery being taken at points by Coghill. A. B. Wallinooke came in, and he opened the scoring by snicking the Silverdale man to the boundary for a couple. Person locked at the other end, and Stewart was bailed without scoring, two wickets thus falling for 3 runs. A. English took his place. He and his partner put a more favourable aspect on the game. A. slight shower came on but quickly cleared off, and no stoppage was effected. Only 8 was reached when English was bowled by Benton. Before the next batman, G. Shrubsole could get to the wicket, the rain again descended, this time heavily, and the players had to seek shelter. After twelve minutes' rest a resumption was made, sawdust being requisitioned. Walbrooke, having knocked 6 since he went in, was bowled without any addition to the total. Shrubsole and the Rev. Mr. Paice played well, the former hitting very freely before getting leg-before. The last wicket had put on 25 runs. Sutton ran himself out, and Paice was bowled soon after. An interesting encounter was won by the homesters on the eighth wicket.

## NANTWICH v. BIGNALL END. (LEAGUE.—2ND DIVISION)

These teams contested the championship of the Second League at Nantwich in beautiful weather on a splendid wicket. Bunnall End have 24 points, and this was their final match. Nantwich, with 21 points, have yet to meet Leek Church and Tunstall. The home team won the toss, and sent in Shenton and Hinde. A leg eye resulted off Burgess's opening delivery, with another bye from the next ball in Dale's over. Each batsman scored a single. Hinde drove nicely to the boundary for 4. Runs came freely, Hinde cutting for a boundary. followed by a smart stroke for 3. Thirty runs were put on in as many minutes. Statham relieved Burgess, and in the first over disposed of Hinde for a well earned 22, which included three boundaries. (One for 35.) Edwards joined Shenton, but the latter made all the runs, despite frequent changes of bowling. At 50 for one wicket Nantwich entertained strong hopes of wiping out the heavy defeat received at Audley. The visitors folded well. Edwards ultimately settled down to some steady batting, but gave a high ball, which was caught. (Two for 70.) Shenton, with a boundary, recorded his 50 and sent up the century for his side at the same time, for which he was loudly applauded. Hereabouts the game was interrupted by rain.

## TRENTIIAM v. BARLASTON.

This match was played at Trentham this afternoon before a large number of spectators. The refreshing rain of yesterday had rendered the ground in splendid condition, and as usual the home team had to take the field. Barleston opened their innings with A. Mear and C. Meakin, the bowling being shared by Moore and Blair. This partnership was but short, as Moore was bowling a very good ball, and in his second over he took Meakin for 1. Meakin was home joined by Stevenson; he likewise met the same fate off Blair for a single also. Again Meakin had Sutton as a partner, but runs came in very slowly, the home fielding wall, and gave very few chances. About twenty minutes play as the third wicket fall for 15, Meakin being taken by Moore, this vacancy was filled by H. Walthe. Both hatmen played a very careful game, but missed no chances, and during the partnership they succeeded in taking several boundaries, which helped up the score nicely. Walthe, however, stayed but for a short time, he being caught and bowled by Moore at this point. The homesters seemed to be revenging themselves for their defeat of last Saturday, Greatrex being caught by Collins of Moore from his first ball, this being 5 for 23.

## NEWCASTLE v. WHITMORE

Played on the ground of the former this afternoon. Newcastle won the toss, and rent in Dean and J. Booth; the Whitmore attack being opened by Smith from the factory end. The scoring was opened in the third over, when Booth nicked Smith for a single, and matters continued slow until Dean made a capital hit for 4. In the next quarter of an hour 7 only was knocked up, and when the board showed 15, Sanders went on vice Tideswell in the visitors bowling department. The Newcastle wickets fell rather quickly, the only player to make a stand up to the time 40 was telegraphed being J. Booth, who played a steady game, and bid fair to make a good score. Sanders's slow bowling had been doing all the execution, but Calow hit him for 3 in his first stroke. Run interfared with the play, and shortly after the resumption J. Booth was bowled for 24, after seeing the fall of seven of his companions' wickets. Newcastle innings closed for 88 exactly at 530 p.m.

## CLOUGH HALL v. PURSI.EM. (LEAGUE—2nd DIVISION).

This League fixture was down to bo played this afternoon. However, at four o'clock no appearance was made by Burstman, yet after a stormy morning the weather was fine and the ground was in good condition for players.

## AUDLEY v. LEEK CHURCH (LEAGUE—2ND DIVISION.)

This match came off this afternoon in fine weather and on a splendid wicket, at the Kent Hills ground. The visitors won the toss and sent Chapman and Wood to the wickets, who had to face the bowling of Henshall and George Timmis. Audley were minus four of their first men, and had to fill up with second eleven men. The innings opened out very nicely, and runs were scored rather freely. Chapman contributed 8 and Wood 6 before they returned to the pavilion. Bryne on going to the wickets played in dashing style, and by hits of three singles, four 2's, two 3's, two 4's, and one 6 be made 31 runs, after which he was caught by Leek off Henshall. Haines, Milwood, and Ball were not so fortunate in playing the slow bowling.
