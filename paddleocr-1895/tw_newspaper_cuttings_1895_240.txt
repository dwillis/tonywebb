## OUR SPORTING COLUMN

What with the rain and the sory wicket Walall did not take much chance of showing what their full battling strength could do in their match on Saturday, along with they had by the means representative team of Dudley to deal with. For once in a way the bowling of Bird lost its terrors, and he was treated with very little ceremony. In fact, the whole team batted with confidence, and at the conclusion of the play it was found that Dudley had put together the respectable score of 129 for nine wickets. S. Morgan—who, by the way, has not had the best of luck this season—batted a capital 29 not out, whilst F. Wheldon (21), Mr. Deley (15), W. Brazenall (11), F. Brown (12), J. Croper (14), and Cole (not out 13) played the bowling without any apparent difficulty. Smith, too, was evidently in scoring form, but he had the misfortune to return a ball to Barnsby. It is bad had dropped short about an inch it would have been out of the bowler's reach, and it was nepotistically a fine catch. The rain undoubtedly not that would have been a very interesting match.

The Dudley second team on Saturday were in ex-sally the same comfortable position as the first team. They kept Walsall second in the field all day, and when play concluded only six wickets were down for a score of 115. Same very useful scores were made by A. Ferray (27), E. J. Thompson (27), G. S. Thompson (28) and J. W. Collins (14), all playing well for their respective scores.

Dudley hardly expected to win the Charity Shield with the learn they had against Walsall on Monday, but they made a good show, all things accounted for. They hit up 110, and it took it of the best Walsall wickles to exceed it. Mr. Dealey, the Dudley captain, was principal coorer for Dudley, with a lively hit 39. Waldow was next beat with 29 and Smith and Morgan made 16 and 11 respectively. Better luck another time.

The rain evidently did not damp the ardeur of the Netherionians in their match with Darlasten, for the game was brought to a satisfactory conclusion. Netherion, with fire wickets down, knocked up a total of 73, General (21) and Darby (not out 19) being to the fore. Darlation was soon disposed of, only 32 run coming from them, and out of that small total Thomas obtained from the. Shaw Road beat Flood Street by 40 runs, the defeated only being able to put together 19. Woolsetton was another club to take things easily on Saturday, the opponents (Slater's) only making 26, whilst they studied together 79. W. Baker (SS) and T. Walters (10) were chief scorers for Woodsetton, and for Slater's nine-hour with 10 was exceedingly useful.

Leabrook Grounds, Wednesday, were an Saturday and Monday occupied with an interesting pedestrian handicap, and the grounds were largely patrouished. The distance was 133 yards, and there were 105 entries, closed into 21 beats. S. Dunn (of Bradley), G. Payne (of Brockmoor), and H. Weele (of Can Lane) each won their heats, whilst T. Hulston (of Halesowen) ran second to Weele. After the first round the 20 approaches were re-drawn in four heats, and Payne and Weele managed to win their heats again, and in the final they came in first and second respectively, after Weele had been put back a yard. Payne, who was on the 30 yards' mark, started at 2 to 1 ou, and 7 to 1 against Weele

The Birmingham League matches suffered, in common with all other names, on Saturday, stourbridge had a poor team against Aston Uity, and perhaps it was as well for them that the rain prevented the game being played. They were without Foster, Foley, Rhodes, and Arnold. Five wickets for 64, however, does not seem quite Unity form; but this was due, probably, to the bowling of Hingley, who took four wickets for 28. The Smithwick and Moseley match was abandoned in consequence of the rain.
