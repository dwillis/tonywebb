# HERITORDSHIRE EXPRESS: SATURDAY 18 MAY 1895-1.

## PROSPECTS OF THE LOCAL CRICKET SEASON.

The glorious weather of the past fortaught has enabled ericketers to commence the national summer pastime under the best of conditions. In first-class cricket there has been a marked absence of the low scores which generally mark the matches played in the month of May, when soft wickets and wet days are the rule. The same is doubtless true of local cricket, and whatever the rest of the season may be like its opening has certainly been an auspicious one. With these few introductory observations, we will proceed to glance at the prospects of local cricket in 1895.

## HERIB. COUNTY O.O.

The recent adoption of the new ground at St. Albans as the "county ground" occurred too late to influence the venue of the present season's matches. The only fixture assigned to this fine enclosure is the return succounter with Bedfordshire. Watford, as for several summers past, gets the lion's share of the matches, although whether it subscribes more to the funds than any other district may be open to question. But just now the wishes of subscribers seem to be a matter of little or no concern to the committee of the club. At the meeting whereat the resolution was passed adopting Olarence Park, St. Albans, as the county ground, Hitchin and Hartford were both mentioned by the secretary as places where certain of the matches would continue to be played notwithstanding the resolution above referred to. But neither town is favoured with a county fixture, nor has been for several years past, although a not inconsiderable part of the club's income must be derived from subscribers living in these divisions of the county, and although the former admittedly has an excellent ground. The novelties in this year's programme are out and home engagements with the counties of Worcester and Barks. These are scarcely likely to prove very interesting, while distance is a decided disadvantage in the case of the former. The eleven is likely to consist for the most part of the cricketers who have represented the county for several seasons past, for there is still, we regret to hear, a complete dearth of young professional talent. The age of the men upon whom the county now chiefly relies makes the outlook for the next few years a gloomy out. Uicketers, even of the "overgreen" variety, cannot go on for ever. ## BEDFORDSHINE ORIOKET CLUB.

This club has added Wilshire to the list of its opponents, and will have to make the journey to Trowbridge to carry out the first of the two fixtures with that county. An interesting match should be that with the Uppingham Rivers, whose opponents will be the amateurs of the county; if the visitors should be well represented it will be safe to predict some long scoring. For the rest the engagements are as last year, and provided Mr. Thring experiences no great difficulty in getting his elevens together, the county is likely to have a successful season. All or nearly all of last year's players will be available again this. The lack of change howlers, however, is again likely to handicap the county. ## HITOBIN O.O.

This club's long list of engagements includes a number of very interesting fixtures, and the season promises to be one of considerable interest. Two University teams are among the visitors, and a well known Cambridge town club will also send over an eleven. The two-days match with a Nottingham eleven, one of the best in last season's programme, again finds a place on the card; and another two-days fixture with Mr. Strauss's eleven is likely to prove almost equally interesting. Another innovation is the match with the San Ofice, but this, In common with the fixtures with Baldock and Arlesey, is a half-day engagement; more of these will, it is stated, be arranged during the season. Wilson, who has been with the club upwards of twelve years, will take a benefit on the occasion of the match with Gentlemen of Herts; this, by the way, forms the first of the fixtures in what we may term a shortened cricket week, and possibly the four days' continuous cricket will be supplemented by an additional fixture for the Saturday.

## HITCHIN NIGHTINGALE O.O.

We regret to bear that it has been found necessary to bring the career of this club to a close. Of late years the membership has gradually decreased, until it became a matter of difficulty to gale elevens for the matches. There was, of course, a corresponding decline in the club's funds, and it has been carried on mainly by the aid of honorary subscribers. In these circumstances it was adjudged best to let the club drop out of existence, and a resolution to this effect was passed at the recent annual meeting.

## STEVENAGE O.O.

With the list of fixtures of this club we have received no information as to the probable strength of the club in 1895. Probably it will for the greater part rely upon those players who have given this service in recent years. One or two very providing players were seen in their ranks last season, and if they have only "come on" the eleven is likely to be stronger, especially, perhaps, in the department of bowling, than in 1894. We notice that the club has not reammed amicable relations with its neighbours, Hitchin, for which perhaps the latter club is to blame. Whatever the facts of the dispute may be, it's prolongation redounds little to the credit of the club's concerned, and next summer, we trust, we shall once again see these old rivals trying their strength together. The younger members of the club have been well cared for, as many as seven matches for the second eleven having been arranged; the encouragement of the "colts" is sound policy, and will doubtless bear fruit in after years in the shape of useful additions to the first team. The ordinary club engagements differ little from those of recent seasons. One fixture in the list, "versus Three Counties," induces the thought at first sight that Steveago have ambitious notions of meeting the full strength of Hertfordshire, Bedfordshire and Huntingdonshire, or some other triple combination of shirze. ## THREE COUNTIES ASYLUM

Though many familiar faces remain, the Asylum eleven this season will differ considerably from that which represented the institution in 1894. The loss of Neville, whose slow left-handed bowling proved so useful during the two previous seasons, will be severely felt, as will also the loss of Hopkins, a fast bowler who was in the eleven one season only. The non-comers include Brockishurst and Berry, both left-handed bowlers; and Woodhouse, who is expected to be of service as a batman.
