## HIGH WYOOMBE v. MR. E. STEVENS' XI.

On Saturday Mr. Edmund Stevens, of The Vacha, Obalfont St. Alice, brought over a strong team to oppose the local club, but rain prevented the game from being played out. It was, however, drawn in favour of Wycombe, who had made 45 for the loss of two wickets, when stumps were drawn. No play was possible before lunchbook, and a start was not made till past 3 o'clock, when the visitors went to the wicket. Before their innings was concluded, a smart shower compelled them to make a precipitate run to the friendly shelter of the pavilion. The rain holding off for a time allowed the innings to be finished, 87 runs being the result. On Wycombe going in the light was very bad, and the rain came down at intervals in torrents, so that after several attempts to play out time it was thought best to leave the game drawn. ## RAYNERS XI v. PERMANENT STAFF OF THE 3rd BATT. OXFORD LIGHT INFANTRY.

On Monday last, August 5th, a match was played on Haynes private ground, between the above teams, and, after being hindered a little by the unsettled weather, ended in a decisive victory for the former by an inning and 11 runs. Mr. O. M. Rose captained the home eleven, and, having won the loss, elected to bat. The game was very slow at the commencement, and 4 good wickets were lost for 21, but when the captain and Mr. Powell became associated, the best stand of the day was made, and some good cricket was seen, both playing with confidence. They put on 66 runs between them, when Mr. Rose was caught at the wicket for an invariable 57. Powell left soon after with a well-played 39 to his credit. Wheeler made 14, and the remaining batmen being quickly disposed of, the inings closed for 114. An adjournment was now made for lunch, which had been prepared (in a spacious marquee, erected for the occasion) through the generosity of Sir Philip and Lady Rose. After ample justice had been done to the good things provided, the visitors commenced their inings. Captain Rose and Bagler Waklin were the only ones to show any resistance to the attack of O. M. Rose and Powell, who were bowling in their best form, and the inings closed for 51. Captains Rose's 18 was far and away the best batting display, which included one 6, a clean drive out of the ground. Powell had the best analysis, taking 6 wickets for 23, while Mr. Rose's 4 cost 25 runs. Having to follow on, they failed to reach the number required to save an inning's defeat, Dean taking 6 wickets for 18 runs, and the game resulted as before stated. Full score and analysis

## MAKLOW v. ORIKL

Played at Markow on Monday is very shoery, so that The local club found it difficult to raise a side, and but firms some help from Wycombe, would probably have had to play short. The team which took the field included H. E. Mank, who is staying in Marlow, the bowling being the weak point. Marlow won the toss and batted. Mank, Langley, and T. Thurlow all compiled useful scores, but the stand of the innings was made by Bower and Barton, who carried the score from 113 for eight wickets to 13 for nine. Burton hit a six, three 4's, and two 5's in his 50. The innings allied for 175. The visitors made an excellent start with Arundel and H. G. Owens. Rane came steadily from the bowling of Mank and Coleman, and 70 went up before Billie offered a separation by catching Owens off his own bowling. Afterwards the Oried men did badly. Arundel was cut next at 50, bowled by Coleman. His dashing innings of 54 included a 6, six 4's, a 2, etc. Langley took up the bowling with the total at 107 for four wickets and met with great success, securing five of the last six wickets at a cost of 15 runs. The innings closed for 196, and Marlow therefore won by 40.
