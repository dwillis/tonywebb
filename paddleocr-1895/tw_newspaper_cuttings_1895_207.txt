The Caverswall club has had a fairly busy time of it this Whitsunside, but they have just reason to be satisfied with the results of their matches. Of four played, including last Saturday's encounter, not one has been lost or drawn; all of them have ended in victories. Against Cheddton they scored 111, the visiting club being dismissed for as near half as it was possible to get of that total, viz., 56. Against Purthill on Monday the team ran up a total of 182 against which the visitors made 134. Means, Bowers, Bridwood, Young, Bettaney, and Cooper were the chief scourers. At Demione on Tuesday, 102 runs were scored, the Collegians being dismissed for 76. Mr. Morgan's bowling played havoc with the students, five of whom fell to him for 29. On Wednesday, Fenton were the visitors, and they ran up the score of 122 before the last wicket fell. The home club, however, were more than equal to the occasion, the Fenton total being passed with five wickets in hand. Mr. J. H. Copastike has proved a most valuable acquisition to the team. The old Longton and district player is in splendid form just now, and his batting displays always give pleasure to the oillokers, science and dash being combined in his play. Book, the pro, is also doing capital service in all departments. On Saturday he took six wickets for 19 runs, and on Tuesday he knocked 20 runs at Denstone. His stumping of the Fenton captain on Wednesday was a very smart bit of work, and all round the Purthill man has proved himself to be a good investment.
