# NEWCASTLE DAILY JOURNAL: THURSDAY 11 July 1895.

# CRICKET NOTES.

## (Hy "CORTOA.")

We are frequently told that what spectators like to see are batmen who make things lively for the bowlers; and, if this is really so, those who saw the game between Captain Nicholls and Mr Hewick's teams would enjoy themselves. Though both Vin. Thompson and Butler played exceptionally well, their performances were overshadowed by the doings of George Simpson, who, after taking seven wickets for 41 runs, scored 177, until was not out at the close. So far as I could see, Simpson never made a bad stroke until he had reached his hundred, and the chance he gave to Butler at 110 was a difficult one for the fielder to judge. The hitting was, as is usual with Simpson, beautifully timed and clean, and none of the bowlers seemed to give him any trouble, though Walters almost bowled him second half. V. Thompson's 89 was a capital display of steady cricket; and Butler's 82 was obtained in his best style. There was a large gathering of spectators in the field, and after such an interesting game we may expect good crowds at the Constabulary Ground throughout the remainder of the season.

## ——

So far this season the Rev E. W. H. Walters has played in five matches for Mr Bewicke's team, and has an average of over 129 runs per inning. More power to his elbow, say I.

Who is the very superior young man who writes on Northumberland cricket for "Ps" Jacken's paper? In his opinion there is only one good team—the Borderers—in the county, and but one competent critic of the game—himself. If rupour speaks the truth, it is a well-known member of a prominent local club, who some years ago came from the south with a great reputation as a bowler; but with each succeeding season his reputation has grown less, until now, not being able to score off his opponents on the field with either hat or ball, be has taken to the pen, in the hope, I suppose, of playing a winning game. Northumbrian batman may not be Graces nor Stoftarts; but they are able to hold their own with the bowlers which south have, up to now, sent us, if we take the Borderers' "Knight of the pen" as a specimen.

## --

It is pure moonshine for any one to talk of the Borders as representing the best trickling talent in the county; and no difficulty would be experienced in getting together a much stronger eleven than they put into the field. In fact, what victories they have gained here have been chiefly over school boys; and were the next team chosen to play for Northumberland probably only one. Harderer—K Spencer—would be sure of a place in it. At the present time several prominent county gentle men are trying to improve cricket in these parts. Towards this end they are inviting strong southern teams to visit them; and sarcastic remarks about those connected with the game, and the players, from the pen of the Harderers' superior young man will not help them much. Perhaps, after all, it is not county so much as afternoon to play for theocr quakes that this criter patroness, while his own falling off in bowling, ne attributes to the weakness of local hatmen, as he can only bowl well against good men. What a pity he cannot play in the south.

## - - -

Some time ago a committee was formed with the intention of resuscitating county cricket in Northumberland, but as the Corporation have refused to grant a field, and negotiations with landowners for ogo have fallen through Mr H. G. Mortimer will, I believe, call a general meeting shortly, and lay the matter before it.

I notice that the Elawick Works Club has fixed their match with Renwell on July 27 as Elaw's benefit game. A more capable and palmstaking professional cannot be found than Elaw, and as a player he ranks among the best in the world. His bowling has pulled the club through many of their engagements, and I am sure every member will do his best to increase the field to be handed over to their professional, who, by his ability on the field and conduct off, does as much as any one connected with it to uphold its honour and dignity.

## ——

Sunderland, though they succeeded in beating Consett at Consett, could do no better than draw on Saturday at Sunderland; though has the full five hours were not played I believe they intend to claim the match. One of the best performances of the day was that of Eley in the match against the Royal Grammar School; for after taking seven wickets for 14 runs he came out top scorer of his side with a well-played 30. Up to the beginning of this month he had got 67 wickets for less than 6 runs each—a bowling feat that will take some beating.

St. George's and Northumberland met on Saturday, in fine weather, before a small gate. Northumberland batted first, and were got rid of very cheaply for 113. Miss took five wickets; Bell, three; and V. Thompson, two for 1 run. Hoare batted well for 38. On St. George's going in a couple rot took place, and they were all out in less than an hour for 28. Their cracks cannot come off every time, and the rest of the team must make up their minds to do a fair share in the run getting. The fielding on both sides was very good.

## CONSETT WEDNESDAY v. CHISTERLE STREET WEDNESDAY.

Beautiful weather favoured this match, which came off at Chester-le-Street yesterday. ## MR BEVICKE'S XI. v. NORTHERN NOMADS.

A two days' match between these teams commenced on the Close House Ground on Tuesday, and finished yesterday in a decisive victory for Mr Bewick's eleven by an inning and 66 runs. Betting first up Tuesday, the home team made 264, the Rev E W R Walters playing a fine inning of 67; while Guttridge and Captain Wilkinson his hard for 55 and 45 respectively. After the Nomade had commenced well with Clayton and Mortimer, Guttridge and Walters quickly disposed of several of the other batmen, and it was not until P Ashworth and Morton came together that a second effective stand was made. Both played steadily, but none of the others could do anything with the bowling, and the side were all out for 149. Following on, they fared badly against Gustridge and A A Mowburn, the professional getting four wickets for 32 runs, while Mowburn's four only cost 16 runs. The inning realised 49, P Ashworth and Clayton alone reaching double figures. Score: —
