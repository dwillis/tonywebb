## WORCRSTERSHIRE CLUB AND GROUND

## v. BROMSGROVE.

There was a capital attendance at the above match played at Bromagrove on Saturday for Rolleston, the professional's benefit, and a capital day's cricket was the result. Bromagrove won the toss, but put their opponents in. Burrows and Bowley, who went in first, batted brilliantly, and made 155 before they were separated. With other useful and well played totals from W. S. Caldwell and the Rev. F. A. Bickmore, the large score of 258 was obtained, when the innings was declared with six wickets down. Bromagrove observed strict defensive tactics, and kept their end up admirably, and only six wickets were down when the match ended in a drawn game. Score:

Mr. R. B. Porch, late of Malvern College, who plays for Homococca, is tenth in the batting averages of that club with an average of 16.11. He has scored 203 in 14 innings.

St. John's, in their match on Thursday at Bcurhton, against the Club and Ground, took a strong lead after going in first. They made a capital stand, although the bowling against them was rather warm. Burrows, Willonghy, and Ryder shared between them what wickets fell, before Mr. Young declared with nine wickets down. Mr. H. V. Plum batted very steadily for 48. Messrs. Curtis and Mansell also performedί creditably, and 125 was the total. This score looked like being wiped off, for Burrows was in his amiting thiuour and hit up 57 in lively fashion. The County lot were only 13 behind and six wickets down when time put a stop to proceedings.

Mr. E. P. Gem bossed the team very well, and I should think he has noted in a similar capacity before, although I have not known him do so.

It was rather funny that Straw should have been subjected to by the St. John's captain, because I have an inkiing that he has resorted to similar measures before, viz., when he has been short of players he has done the best he could to fill the vacancies. Although he had a right to object to Straw, who had not folded, I think he might have given way, but I suppose it was his customary zeal in trying to win the match that made him forget previous circumstances. I had a good opportunity of looking over the new gavilion, which is one of the best I have seen, and it reflects great credit upon Mr. Gadd, who, I was glad to hear, was getting another ready for the Handsworth Wood Club. Others will sure to follow, for as 6115, the total cost, its represents the chaspost and mass comfortable one that could be imagined.

## Mr. W. A. Young performed the hat trick.

The improved state of the ground struck me as soon as I got there. I subsequently learned that the brothers J. K. and K. Perkins, both of whom are independent gentlemen, set to work and levelled 14,000 feet with their own hands last winter. This substantiates what I have said of them before, that they are the right men in the rights place. This is enthusiasm indeed, as those who have had a spell at that sort of work know very well. The wicket was a perfect one, and, after all the runs which were made, was just as good at the close.

Riposteron had his annual benefits as Bromagrove on Saturday, and I hope he will have a substantial sum, for he is a very painstaking cricketer, and immensely popular with the members. He has done well for the club, which is second to none in the district. I had forgotten to say that there is about $15 yet to be got to clear the pavilion d'oe, and I notice a match will be played there between 22 ladies and 11 gentlemen. This ought to wipe some of it off, for the tickets are going off well. If the weather is fine there will be a good gate and plenty of fun.

Burrows and Rawley established a record, for they his up 155 before a wicket fell, and both batte d. The former only just missed his century. Master. Oldwell played a pertinent junngs, and this young-ter has only go on the right way to make a very fine batsman. I like his style very much. The Rev. Y. A. Bickmore was buting first rate when the Inning was declared. The score had reached 258 for six wickets, and this, against some good bowlers, was capital. I imagine the local team must have felt annoyed that they did not bat first after winning the toss. However, they played a good defensive game, and kept their end up for two hours and a quarter, and made a good draw of it.

Tommy Straw took off the gloves and went on to, well, I ought to say bowl, but the crowd and the bone captain said he threw, and so I suppose he must have done. The circumstance caused a deal of flutter, and John Smith, who was umpiring, had a warm five minutes, but it all finished up in fun. I must warn Tommy to stink to his winkkeeping, for he has few superiors in that line anywhere.

There are lots of local matches of an interesting kind during the coming week, and, given a continuance of the present glorious weather, will wind up a capital season.
