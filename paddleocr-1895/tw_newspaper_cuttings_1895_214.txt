## LEEK.

Look first to an had no match at all on Saturday. They were resting after making the record number of single points, with a hope that when they re-commence it will run to two points. After the club week they will be able to do with them. Your readers will have noticed that there were no notes from Leek on Saturday. They were written, but the boy who was entrusted with the important mission of conveying them to the station forgot the way. Neither have been heard of since.

Last Saturday was a soaker. It was Bagshaw's benefit, and the match was against our old friends, Burton, whose faces have not been seen in Leek for a few seasons, but of whose performances this season we offer congratulations. Everything promised a bumping gate, as the Leek team were not playing, and Kirkby, in a most generous manner, assisted his brother professional. But it was the wretched weather that came as the disappointment, a heavy slower coming on just previously to the time fixed for commencing.

The visitors came in grand style in a coach-and-four, and they reached the ground in good time. The spectators were but few, and it showed a good feeling to see so many of the prominent members of the Leek club on the ground. Look you the boss, and that was about all that was done for an hour; but at last the sun came out, and the Temporance Band played a lively selection, and the game commenced.

Kirkby and Bagshaw took the bowling, but from some cause or other Bagshaw did not come off, and after bowling some down overs, which were very expensive, he was taken off. Kirkby was in grand form, and he obtained five wickets for 30 runs — no mean performance against Baxton. Harry Ball made a mistake in missing Howe when that player had only scored 1 run, as his total was 31. Maddock was a patient last year and kept an six wicket, but failed to add many to the score sheet. Woodrutt had a short and merry career, one of his hits for 6 was a treat. When eight wickets were down for 30 rain again set in, and the match was abandoned.

Leek Highfield have not yet sustained a defeat this season, and their application for permission to join the North Staffordshire League next season ought to be favourably considered.

Leok Reserve were playing Oakamoor on the Leak ground. Leak scored 61, of which number Dr. Hassaman was responsible for 30, and G. Tudor 18. When 61 had been obtained for seven wickets, the innings was closed. Oakamoor did not but long, obtaining 2 runs without the loss of a wicket, when rain prevented any further play.

The Church Club were engaged with Audley on their hundred score onclosure. The visitors batted first. At least they were the only players who did but, for when the score was 58 for seven wickets, the gentle rain descended, and the game was abandoned. Out of the 58 J. Look was responsible for 23.

Today there will be only one junior match—Leek, and it will be difficult to find eleven players tof in the town to do duty, as the usual exodus commenced yesterday, and Leek this week-end could very properly be called 'Owt of noise," all the places of business having closed for a week.

## RIGNALL END.

The above club journeyed to Chell last Saturday to play the return League fixture. We won the loss, and sent the home team in to Lab. ## MARKET DRAYTON.

Rain fell pretty generally in this district on Saturday, and noerieket of note came under my notice. Drayton did turn out in their encounter with Witchchurch, but rain fell so heavily, and the ground became so saturated, that although it cleared up about five a.m. it was found impossible to proceed with the game. Drayton were assisted by Mr. J. P. Haywood-Lousdale. The first three or four latismen could do nothing against the bowling of Cliff, but J. Taylor (not out 12) and W. G. Stephenson (not out 8) broke the spell of fortune which had favoured the visitors. Stephenson was a grand success to Taylor in keeping up the wicket so splendidly, and not in this case, which we are apt to see in local teams, too eager for run-getting. At this period the game was abandoned, a very heavy storm coming on.

## HIGH LANE

On Saturday we visited St. Paul's, whose ground is situated in "the lake district". Crokets seemed out of the question when we started to the ground. On our arrival, however, the weather soon cleared up. The hero side won the toss, and decided to be, sending Adams and Owen to face the bowling of Whitehouse and Capey. Wickets fell thus: Two for 2, three for 4, four for 7, five for 7, six for 7, seven for 11, eight for 17, nine for 17 (only ten men batting); total, 19. Whitehurst bowled eleven over, four mailers, taking seven wickets for 9 runs. Capey took two for 5. Heavy rain fell, and we found another pitch and decided to go on with the game. What a disastrous start! Three wickets went down for 1 run, Wallace catching Foster in the first over, and Cartwright bowling two men in the second over. More rain fell, but the side's agreed to continue the game. Runs came as well as could be expected on such a wicket, and St. Paul's season disinclined to go on. Barnett made the winning hit, but the game continued for three or four minutes, when the players could stand the wet no longer, all having a run for the pavilion with wet jackets.

## LAWTON PARK.

Both fixtures on Saturday last with Middleport were postponed, owing to the rain. A start was made, but play was impossible, and the game ceased when Middleport had made 21 runs for the loss of five wickets. Today Congleton pay the Park a visit in a friendly encounter.
