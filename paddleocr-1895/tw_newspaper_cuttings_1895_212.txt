## MILTON.

The M.C.C. had their old friends, Leek Church, as visitors to play them a friendly match, which resulted in an easy win for Milton. Look, batting first, could only make the score of 31, all out. Garding being the only man to make any show with the last against the bowling of Thurfield and Macfield, Thurfield taking five wickets for 14 runs, and Macfield five for 17. The Miltonians did very well, making 65 for five wickets, Flatt 20, not out, and Liller 13, being the chief contributors.

## BIGNALL END.

This team were at home last Saturday, engaged in a League match with Silverdale Second. The visitors are showing excellent form this season, and a good number of spectators journeyed to the Bignall End ground to witrums the match. The home club were not fully represented, A. Dale and R. Warburton being absentee. The visitors won the tax, and decided to ball, sending Stanyer and Ellerton to oppose the bowling of T. Burgess and J. Dale. The former clean bowled Ellerton in his first over when he had scored 2, and in his next over he bowled G. Ellams for a duck. J. Dale caught and bowled Sutton for 1, and T. Burgess got Stanyer 1 b when he had scored 3. This was a very poor start for the visitors, four good wickets being down for 9 runs. J. Ellams and F. Dean became partners and a stand was made, the score being run to 20 before F. Dean snounced to a catch by T. Burgess off Dale. J. Ellams tatted well for 22. The innings realized 13. If I am not mistaken, this is the visitors least score this season. The home team commenced with the usual pair, W. Mayer and E. Mayer. The latter was caught by Stanyer off Rehton before he could score, and W. Mayer was caught off the same bowler when he had made 7; not a very good start. J. Dale and G. Burgess became partners, and both tatted in good style. Several changes of bowling were tried but each came in for punishment. The visitors' score was reached before the third wicket fell. J. Dale made 13. The innings realized 72, and we won by 29 runs.

The Second team were at Crow and got defeated by 22 runs. J. White made 13 not out, H. Foster 9; R. Green 7, A. Warberton and A. Proctor 6 each. On Monday the League teams played the Audley football team captained by J. Dale. The football team made 60, and the League team 150 for seven wickets. The top soners were J. Rowley (49) and W. Mayer (51).

There was some good scoring down in most of the League matches played on Saturday. A fine day, with wickets hard and fast, could scarcely bring about any other result.

At Stoke the Club and Ground met Leek, and the "City of the Moorlands" team put on the very respectable total of 136 before the last wicket fell. Kirkby, who made 46 against Crowo a week ago, again played a big innings, making 59, his highest total up to the present in League cricket. Means. Cantrill, Wright, and Buckwell assisted in bringing up the total. The Club and Ground replied with 126 for seven wickets, a wonderful performance, considering the time as the Club's disposal. Means. Heaton, Crallan, Billingsley, and Dawson were the principal emitters, and when time was called three wickets had to fall with, only 10 runs to make. On paper the draw was certainly in favour of the Country Club.

At Buralem the “ wooden spoonists ” paid a visit, and against them Burdem totted up what to the present has been its top score in League matches this year, viz. 137 for nine wickets. Brownfield played a good hitting innings for 32, and Hulme, Lemm, Booth and Ellis, all reached a respectable score. Norton's response was a very feeble one, not one of its players even reaching double figures, and an innings, which was very monotonous, was finished when 41 runs were totalled.

A rather floutuating fixture was fough but by Porthull and Fenton at Wolstanon. When Porthill had made but the poor score of 43 runs they had lost seven of their best wickets, but the tail wagged considerably, and the respectable total of 121 for nine wickets was at least reached, mainly owing to the creditable innings by Holdcroft (40), not out), Bateman (28), and the Rev. F. W. Haden (12). Fenton's innings was the exact opposite to Porthill's, the major position of the run being made in the early part of the innings. Messrs. J. Turner and O. Merritt put on 43 runs between them, and at this juncture it must have appeared favourable to the visitors' side. Bennett and Bateman, however, from this point carried all before them, and the last wicket fell as 69.

Tunstall was visited by Nantwich, and the home team succeeded in making 91 runs. Maccock, 36, was the highest scorer. Nantwich made short work of this, and amassed 145 for seven wickets. Hollowood carried out his bat for 66 runs, his highest score in League cricket.

Crews visited Silvordale, and assisted by an innings of 63 runs by E. Payne, 38 by Osborne, and very creditable scores by other players, made 174 for eight wickets, when the innings was declared closed. At one time it looked 20 to 1 in favour of a draw, but a speedy demolition of the last wickets of Silvordale brought out Crewe once more the winning team, Silvordale's total amounting to only 93 runs.

The day's cricket brings out several League records. For nearly 300 runs to be made on the Silverdale ground in less than four hours and a half is certainly a feat that has never been done before, and that is not likely to be done again for some considerable time. For three teams to declare their innings closed in one day is also an item that has never been chronicled. The attendance at each of the matches was good, and betokens the sustained interest that this organisation has upon the cricket-loving public in this district.

To-day only four matches are arranged: County 7. Tunstall, Leek v. Buralem, Nantwich v. Featon, and Silverdale v. Porthill.
