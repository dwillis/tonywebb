LAWTON PARK.

This club won its protest against Silverdale, and the match has to be replayed. As was pointed out last week it is most important that the measurement of the ground and pitch should be taken accurately, and especially in Leagues, Combination, or Alliance fixtures, so as to avoid the unpleasantness which arises from disputes. More need not be said regarding the match. Saturday's match was again a victory for the Park club, which so far has done well in the Combination, not having lost a match. To-day, the club meet Middlewich, both at home and away, and it is probable that another success will have to be recorded.

PORTHILL PARK.

On Saturday Porthill 2 Tun still gave about the best batting display which they have ever given, and their score of 152 for three wickets was a performance which reflected credit on all those who had a share in it. King justified the expectations of all who have seen his form, and his score of 65 was made without fault. H. P. Wood once again also proved his real value to his side, as he stayed with King until the score was 81, of which his share was 35. The previous week, against Club and Ground, the first team had made 30 without being parted, when rain stopped play, so that in the two games they had made 152, being repeated. Almost a record for North Staffordshire League cricket. Dean followed in, and soon began to make matters lively, and well possibly have stayed to help Bennett occupy the innings out on the part of the first Tunstall association. His score of 21 was got in his usual vigorous style, and the fifth that got rid of him for less than 10 runs may be considered fortunate. The first 120 runs were scored on the rather small ground in just over the hour, and then the tide of scoring was reduced somewhat, but that did not suit Bennett, and he paid little respect to any of the bowlers, and in about twenty-five minutes made the score of 52, not out, at which point the innings was closed, having Tunstall much time to bat than the visiting team had taken to complete their rather formidable score.

Some mention has been left, in former editions to the impression that the Porthill batting sadly lacked force and run-getting power. This is certainly no longer correct, as in last Saturday's display no opportunity was neglected to score runs, and the display altogether was singularly free from risky stroke. The Tunstall men stood their long outing well, and the fielding all through was smart, especially so in some instances.

The Tunstall innings was conspicuous for a very good innings on the part of Walker, who made 26, and a prolonged stand for the last wicket by Brierley and Tater, who took the score from 35 to 102, as which number Tater effected a chance off bowling field 1 to King, which no doubt, would have been Bennett's account for four wickets, and the absence of W. S. White, who was fielding, gave King an opportunity to show that his bowling will be useful if he is called on, as he also took four wickets.

All old members of the Porthill Club would notice with pleasure, that once again that sterling cricketer Farmer Walker was back again in their ranks, after his absence of some or eight years, and although the big performance of some of the other players did not give him a chance on Saturday, there is no doubt he will not be long in the team before he makes his presence felt in the old style which so many Wolstanton people recall with pleasure.

It is also interesting to notice with what great success Bennett made his first appearance in Derbyshire county cricket this season, his aggregate of 35 and bowling analysis, both being the best performances of the side at, Hampshire. Porthill also supplied the Staffordshire wicket-keeper in the person of J. Poole, and the absence of these two, coupled with the holidays, caused the team which was sent to Caverswall to be mainly composed of second eleven men, who on the whole acquitted themselves well, scoring 134 to Caverswall 120, and seeing that King was once in a way dismissed without scoring, the total must be considered a good one. The Hon. F. W. Hanlen was conspicuous by a very well played innings of 39, other double figures being: G. Evans 15, J. Dean 12, G. Wood 17, and S. Mawdsley 20 not out. A very pleasant day was spent at Caverswall, the only regret being that owing to the holidays so many of the first team were absent. The eleven team had a very easy victory on Saturday v. Audley, scoring 104 for four wickets, and disposing of their opponents for 39. S. Mawdsley once again taking his full share of wickets. To-day, Fenton visit Porthill, and for the first time for four years. Fenton plays against Porthill, with what result may be looked for in another column. Next Saturday we visit Silverdale, and the week after the great fight of the season, Burslem at Porthill.

FENTON.

[BY "RAMBLER."]

Fenton visited Norton last week for the purpose of annexing points, and were successful, at least, this is what I believe. The scores were—Fenton 94, Norton 96. This ought to entitle Fenton to count two points. Norton will probably agree that it does, but others may think otherwise.

Some people have an idea that I supported the action of Fenton in their match v. Silverdale. How this is arrived at I cannot imagine. I stated sufficiently plain for an average reader to understand that I blamed the rules, and that if the rules allowed one team to "bar" another nobody could blame the players. This is, to my mind, clear enough. I am of the same opinion now. There who have seen a match at Trent Bridge, when Nottingham been playing for a draw will never forget it. How to waste time is shown to perfection. Players leaving the wickets for a cup of tea and other such little tricks, disgust those who go to see sport. The ways in which a batsman can waste time are numerous, and the fact the remark applies. I hold to the opinion that the apparent easy method should be empowered to extend the time for such period as they consider has been deliberately wasted.

The game of cricket is one of the oldest on record which perhaps accounts for the old-fashioned ways of its legislators. No serious effort is made to advance with events. This is a mistake which is made clearly apparent by the actions of the spectators when a player like Stoddart goes in to bat. His presence acts like magic on a crowd. There is no "cudding around" about him. He goes in to get runs not to waste time, and it must be annoying to a sportsman like him when a bowler uses the power given him by the rules to waste time to the disadvantage of the latter.

The members of the Cricket League may think reform such as I have suggested impracticable, or if possible say they have no power in the matter. That's just where the deadlock is. Nobody appears willing to accept a move. Last year Mr. J. T. Fenton suggested that (instead of mere luck deciding which team should have choice of innings) the visitors should have the option of batting or fielding. There was nothing in the world to prevent this being done in League matches, but the committee wouldn't have it. The majority of the committee preferred to trust to luck to assist in winning matches. Winning the toss has now to do with a Saturday afternoon match than most people imagine, and if a captain is fortunate enough to guess the upper side of a penny in both matches he stands a good chance of scoring four points, or at the least the opportunity of making his opponents take the field the best portion of the time.

Luck is an element which ought not to be counted upon as an assistance to score victory, and although not a thought-reader, I venture to think that 99 out of every 100 spectators will agree with what I say. On a bitterly cold day six or eight thousand people will put in an appearance at Sable to see a good football match, because they know that they will get a full hour and a half's sport. Waste of time is not permitted there, and luck is reduced to a minimum. The cleverest team as a rule wins the game, and that is a lot more than can be said about present-day cricket.

Having concluded the portion which was to be continued, as per last week's issue, I will now revert to the Fenton v. Norton match. Somehow or other, no matter how badly Fenton is playing against other clubs, they rise to the occasion against Norton. Last year Mouel went with damaged optics, and although unfit to play he scored more than a third of the Norton team put together. On Saturday the team was a sort of six-and-ten. Winning the toss, the usual pair took their places at the wickets. Merritt played in his usual careful style, and Turner applied the wood hard and often, and had 16 to his credit when a separation came. Breeze made things lively, but eventually stopped a ball with his foot, and retired at the request of the umpire, with as much satisfaction as is usual in such cases. Skeldon, who has been studying how to get balls eyes, outers, and other things whilst training as Bechfield, soon got an "outer," and also made some smarter applied to W. Turner and Pooley. Walton made a couple, and things were looking rosy for Norton, but Dean, Birks, and Rhodes altered the complexion of the game, the score at the close reading 94.

Birks is to be complimented upon his success. Fate has for some time been against the cheery Alsatian batsman, but now a turn has been made all Fentonsians hope it will be a long way to the nearest stopping place.

Norton commenced badly against the deliveries of Turner and Breeze, catches for once in a way being accepted. Boulton was the only man on the side to get into double, and his score shows what a careful batsman he is. The end came with the total at 56, Fenton winning comfortably at the finish.

On Wednesday a fairly representative team visited Caverswall, the paradise of cricketers, to oppose Mr. W. K. Bowers' eleven. It was a day out for the Turners, who put on 50 runs in about half-an-hour, J. having 39 to his credit and W. 18. Rhodes chipped in with a useful 15 and the other eight divided a level score between them, the respective patrons varying from nothing to 7.

The home captain, assisted by Aynsley, Morgan, and Booth, raised the Fenton score, 44 runs being drawn before J. Morgan, Taylor, Young, and Blair had a chance of showing what they could do. The outing, as usual, was much enjoyed, and some of the players are already consulting an almanac in the hopes of finding out when Whit Week is due in 1896.

MOUNT PLEASANT.

On Saturday we went to Birches Head to meet Tabernacle Mission. The Missionaries batted first, facing the bowling of Edwards and Hammond. T. Prince made a good stand, being finally caught by Burchell after making 21 runs. The last wicket fell for 72 runs.

On our side A. Smith and W. Edwards were in good form, the former hitting in splendid style, and making 52 runs, which included three 5s. The innings closed for 80 runs, and we won by 8 runs. Our second team received the Y.M.C.A. at Mount Pleasant. The visitors won the toss, and had to thank Hardwick and Salmon for their victory. Hardwick made 34 and Salmon 11 runs out of a total of 61. Hardwick's bowling was deadly, and Hardwick gave long catches, but was missed each time. Our men made a good stand. Phillips (10), Deakin (7), and Bimilster (2) were the best. The innings closed with the score at 81. It was a close game throughout, and thoroughly enjoyed.

On Whit-Monday we were pitched to play against Hanley Tabernacle. Owing to the holidays only three of our first team turned up, and consequently we made a poor appearance, The Tabernacle brought up a fearfully cautious and good wicket. Result: Tabernacle 55 (of which W. Richards made 25); Mount Pleasant 9.

LEEK.

Lovers of cricket were well catered for last week and the beginning of this week in Leek. Both the Leek and the Leek Highfield teams had very good engagements on Saturday, and they were both well patronised, the attendances being about equal. A very childish practical joke was carried out either in the very hot hours of the evening of Friday week or the early hours of Saturday morning, and was intended to do damage to the Leek Highfield Cricket Club. Once almost all the greens of the Highfield C.C. had announced the was included in their list of fixtures for Saturday last, having upon them the words "The Leeks and milk walks." Whoever was the joker may take all the credit he may think reflects upon him and be welcome to it.

LEEK CLUB.

Mr. Brent and the other members of the executive of the Leek Club are to be congratulated on the opening on Saturday last of what I unhesitatingly say is one of the best grounds in North Staffordshire. The attendance on the ground on the opening day was a large one, although the weather was threatening. The arrangements for the accommodation of the spectators was all that could be desired, but the representatives of the press had to sit outside, and rain falling several times made this very unpleasant. I understand that a portion is to be erected at a cost of about £2,500, probably the press will be considered.

The captain of the Crewe team won the toss, and made a great mistake in allowing the Leek team to bat first on a good wicket, and they found it all before the game was over. Brent and Dr. Middle had first, and the doctor from the first ball scored a single. Both batsmen played a safe game, and the bowling was taxed, but Price and Pickford being taken off before a wicket was taken. This good start, had a visible effect upon the company, the ladies, of whom there were a good number present, congregating the gentlemen upon the successful start, and the applause manifested in delight at there being an ample supply of all kinds of provisions.

Brent was unfortunately out leg before, but Kirkby played a splendid game, and was put up a number of runs. The Doctor was caught out, and Wright and Kirkby got together and runs came rapidly, the visitors having now found out their mistake in putting Leek in first. Wright played a vigorous game, but Riley, who was bowling in fine form bowled him with a grand ball, Kirkby ought to have obtained his 50 runs, but Payne with a marvellous catch disposed of him for 45. He had played a good game. Backwell foolishly ran himself out, and the innings closed with a total of 111. Riley was the most successful bowler, taking seven wickets for 28.

Crewe commenced with Osborne and Lyon, to the bowling of A. H. Wardle and Kirkby. Osborne was bowled 1st, Kirkby missing taking the wicket, but he retired next howler for having him caught off the bowling by Cantrill, and then commenced to fall rapidly, and it was a feature of the Crewe, as they had four good wickets down for 39.

A Noats eleven were the players on Monday and Tuesday at Westwood-lane. It seems that there had been some misunderstanding as to the right of the visitors to be invited an eleven of Notts, and the secretary of the Notts County team had been corresponded with. Whoever they were they proved themselves good players. They provided two days good cricket. The attendance on both days must have cleared the hearts of the Executive. Monday's attendance was like that in the old time, when Ashmoue Park used to play Leek. The ladies were present in goodly number, and the bright colours there were added to the scene.

There was a capital arrangement, and that was the late start. The visitors had missed their train at Stoke, and only arrived in time for late game.

A start was made at two o'clock, and it was soon seen that the Leek team were in for an afternoon's fielding. Goodacre and Whitlock played steady cricket, and completely mastered the bowling. The left-hander was, however, run out, the score standing at 53. Bennett, who has played with Notts County, joined Goodacre, and again the Leek bowling was punished. Bennett has a very free style with him. He obtained three boundaries, and the round wicket which was smartly taken by Cantrill behind the wickets fell for 75; Skelton, who was responsible for the team visiting Leek, partnered Bennett, and the score was taken to 91, when Wardle clean bowled Bennett. Wardle did not remain long. He hit a six and a four, and Wright then displaced his wicket. The visitors were out for 145. The Leek team had tried eight bowlers, all of the team, and Wardle and Wright were the most successful.

Leek commenced with Messrs. Heaton and Jones. The former was soon out, but Jones and Kirkby played good cricket, and the score was taken quickly up. Jones played an exceptionally good innings, some of his strokes being very pretty. Kirkby was out leg before, and Wright did not stay long, the day's play ending with 52 for three wickets. Jones was not out 25, and the Rev. J. Darling not out 8. The state of the game brought down a good number of spectators on Tuesday, but the day's play was not so interesting, the Leek batting falling off considerably. Vickerman and C. Wardle were the highest scorers, the total reaching 126. The visitors replied with 105 for eight wickets, so the game was drawn. Whitlock scored 59 and Bell 25. The visitors of the game was the clever wicket-keeping of Cantrill.
