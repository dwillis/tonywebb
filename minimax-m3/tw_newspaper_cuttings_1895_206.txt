CLOUGH HALL.

The Thursday's team against Bucknall Church were again successful. It would appear so far that this team is stronger than that of the Second League team, and yet it has not the same opportunities for practice. Bucknall had little luck at the commencement of the game, and but for the staying powers of Berrisford would have made a sorry show. The last man played a slow but sure game, and was not out for 17. Warburton and Tipton both bowled well, and the visitors were disposed of for 44 runs. Thursday would appear to have been Warburton's day, and he was ably supported by Wright when wielding the bat, and the last wicket was won with the loss of two wickets for 42, though play was and the hospitality they received at Clough.

Continued on Monday the club were successful in their competition against Audley, but it was evident that the attractions at the Hall drew away the spectators from the match. To-day the team has the pleasure of meeting Stone club, and it is to be hoped that it will prove successful. HANLEY Y.M.C.A.

Two matches and two victories in one week is good business. A Thursday team having been formed they played their first match on May 30th against Stoke Post Office. The Y.M.C.A. at the score of 103 for two wickets declared their innings closed, Hardwicks and Salmon retiring with 57 (not out) and 20 (not out) respectively to their credit. The Post Office were all got out for 57, leaving us winners by the handsome majority of eight wickets and 46 runs. For the Post Office, Boullemier (8), Shirley (12), and Bigley (5) were the highest contributors. On Saturday last we visited Keeles to play our return match with Mount Pleasant. We were rather surprised, however, to find they had made a poor second considering that Alliance matches have to be played thereon, but we played the match nevertheless, and after an exciting game won by 10 runs, the totals reading: Y.M.C.A., 61; Fenton, 51. For the Y.M.C.A., Hardwicks (34) and Salmon (11) were again the highest scorers, while the bowling analysis was: Hardwicks six for 17; Parry, two for 5; and Richardson two for 18.

CREWE.

For two weeks rain has interfered with two of the most important fixtures on the Alexandra list, and it is still a mystery whether Crewe would have beaten Nantwich, or whether Lock would have knocked the champions off their perch. Still the fact remains that the Moorlanders had made 111, while Crewe had lost four of their very best bats for 32.

Judging from the remarks which have been made by the Crewe contingent, it would appear they had a most enjoyable outing in the Moorlands, and a feature to predict the it would not take much persuasion to entice them into that District again. They speak highly of their drive to Bunstall, and also for the hospitality they received at the hands of the Lock cricketers, who did everything to make their visit a convivial one. I am given to understand "Little Jerry" was the recipient of a hearty vote of thanks for his assiduous attention towards his guests.

Speaking from a cricket point of view, the most successful man on the Crewe side appears to be Riley, who certainly distinguished himself as a bowler on Saturday against Lock. He took seven wickets for 28 runs, while on Monday against Highfield he was also responsible for five wickets for 20 runs. The others have not done anything extraordinary.

THE ALEXANDRA "A" TEAM.

If there was any doubt existing as to the merits of either the Alexandra "A" team or the Athletic, I should imagine this has been removed, for the Alexandra fairly got the upper-hand of their opponents in a friendly match, played on the ground of the former. The batting of the homesters was the feature of the play, and the score which was obtained by S. Peel is worthy of recognition. He became completely master of the situation, and turning out right and left he managed to get 53 (not out), which he himself was declared closed at 138 for eight wickets. Robins also made 34 in good style, while Livemore (23) and Boardman (23) also rendered material assistance towards this big total.

The visitors commenced very badly, as seven wickets fell in rapid succession; but when W. Hinckley and Hayes got together a stubborn stand was made. Twenty-four runs were occupied during their association. Still they were all dismissed for 50, and the Alexandras "A" team won very easily. The Peemian-at-Way had no fixture.

The High Town Wolesyans opposed the Primitive on the latter's ground, and got slightly the worst of the argument, as the home representatives knocked up 29 for six wickets, and closed their innings, Clarke being not out 31, and Hazell 32.

The Wesleyans had also lost six wickets for 43 when stump were drawn, leaving the match incomplete.

Abraham, the financial secretary of the Alexandra Cixic, has just issued his annual financial statement and report for the past year. From a brief perusal it would appear that this important club is in a flourishing condition. Last year the balance carried forward was £59 5s. 11d, while on the present occasion the balance is £119 6s. 5d. This is a matter for congratulation, as no club or organisation can expect to prosper without good financial prospects, which can only be obtained by good management, and this is one especial feature in the history of the Crewe Alexandra Club.

Don't forget the Alexandra sports next week. There will be several important events, the presence of Charley Perry, the English champion in the 100 and A H Drower, the Scottish champion in the 100 yards scratch, will be worth witnessing; while E. C. Breden, the half-mile champion, and other high-class luminaries in the athletic world should make the forthcoming festival of the Crewe Alexandra one of the best yet held.

BIGNALL END.

This club had no League fixture last Saturday, the vacancy being filled up by the League team playing twenty-two members of the Wesleyan adult class, which is composed of players out of both Bignall End and the Audley clubs. J. White, the hon. soc. of the home club, and a member of the adults class, had charge of the twenty-two. The League team won the toss and decided to bat. E. Mayer and W. Mayer going in the wickets to oppose the bowling of White and Taylor. A good start was made, 25 being scored before the first wicket fell. The League team all batted well, compiling 111 runs before they were dismissed, several bowlers were tried and all came in for their share of punishment. We all sincerely hope that he will soon recover from the effects, and that he will soon again be found in the Forge Mills team.

MACCLESFIELD.

I like Sandbach. There is a quaintness about its figures and surroundings that lends a familiarity which always makes a visit pleasant. Moreover its ground and surroundings are eminently suited for followers of our summer sport. Mr. Sun, Mr. Peacock, or anyone else who has had the good fortune to be placed at square leg from the top wicket will readily bear me out. The amount of speed a bowler can put into a sprint after a ball towards the Wheel Steaf is astonishing.

It was a sorrowful party which left Macclesfield home, and beauty, westward bound, on Saturday at 1:30, for Humpy, the pride and flower of the town, was minus. Sun was ditto, but the news of the latter's absence having been broken to the team early in the week the edge of the grief was blunted. An air of indefinable and melancholy mystery hung around, and on the way Nature, in a sympathetic mood, wept with us for the loss of the missing one. Now if there is one pleasant sensation in all the world it is that of riding in an open brake when it is raining. It beats tobogganing into fits. The preliminary canter on the part of J.
