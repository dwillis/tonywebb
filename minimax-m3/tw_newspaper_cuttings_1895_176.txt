Northampton Herald : Saturday 17 August 1895-2.

NORTHAMPTON TOWN LEAGUE.
SECOND SECTION.
Victoria-road v. Excelsior Albions. — Low scoring ruled in this match, which was played on the Borough Ground on Saturday, Victoria-road compiling 21, King's 8 being the highest contribution. Cryer took four wickets for 25 and Tate four for 10. The Excelsior Albions, who only batted eight men, were dismissed for 26, W. Darby (9) being the highest scorer. Victoria-road thus won by five runs. per Victoria-road F. Roberts obtained three wickets for 16, and F. Cooke four for 10.

Princess-street v. Duddridge. — Princess-street suffered another reverse on Saturday on the Borough Ground, Duddridge replying with 19 to their opponents' 61. J. Ingram, Princess-street's "stonewaller," who scored 24, was the only one to get into double figures against the destructive bowling of J. Smith (eight wickets for 20 runs), W. Laid (obtained one wicket for 28, and A. Britton one for 8. The chief contributors to the Duddridge total were A. Jones (15), C. Pulten (14), C. Jones (13), and W. Thurley (19). For Princess-street, W. Doxford drove three wickets for 25, F. Smith one for 12, W. Saxey two for 5, W. Rogers two for 22, and F. Brooks two for 25.

Moss Rose v. Pioneers. — The Moss Rose administered a crushing defeat to the Pioneer on the Borough Ground on Saturday. Batting first, the former "declared" when its score was 105 for art wickets, W. Kent, who played an excellent not out innings of 44, and W. Langley (36) being the chief contributors. Stanford (two for 35), A. Knight (one for 18), Wilkinson (two for 16), and H. Wright (one for 20), trembled for the Pioneer. The Pioneer were all out at 68, Knights (11) and A. Knight (10) being the only batsmen to reach double figures. Bert Goodall (six wickets for 32) and Garrard (four for 21) were the bowlers for the Moss Rose.

BEDFORDSHIRE v. OXFORDSHIRE.
This return match was concluded at Bedford on Saturday, when the home county had to bow to defeat to the tune of an innings and 26 runs. It was very evident after Bedfordshire's first venture that their opponents were too strong for them, and Oxfordshire brought the day's play to a close with the respectable total of 273, thus leaving Bedfordshire the task of securing 133 to save an innings defeat. On Saturday they took up their second stand, but the Brothers Rogers's bowling was so destructive that they were all dismissed for 103, P. Rogers taking eight wickets for 53 runs. Oxfordshire thus gained their second victory over Bedfordshire this season by an innings and 26 runs.

M.C.C. AND GROUND v. BUCKINGHAMSHIRE.
It did not take long on Saturday to bring the match at Lord's between these teams to a conclusion, Buckinghamshire beating the M.C.C. by an innings and 26 runs—some such result having been foreshadowed by the state of the game overnight. St. James' Excelsior C.C. v. St. James' Hall.—Played at St. James' End on Thursday, and won by the former by 45 runs. The Hall batting first, had to do nothing against the bowling of Dillinger (four wickets for 30 runs) and Wright (five for eleven runs). On the Excelsior batting they made 54. C. Wright scored 17 and A. Kirton 6. Scores: Excelsior, 54; St. James' Hall, 9.

St. Paul's v. Princes-street K.I.C.C.—Played on the Racecourse on Saturday, and won by St. Paul's by 240 runs. Scores: St. Paul's 446, Princes-street 86.

St. Paul's v. Wellingborough All Saints' Juniors.—Played at Wellingborough on August 7th, and won by St. Paul's by 35 runs. Scores: 95 and 60.

Wellingborough All Saints' v. Kettering Working Men's Club.—Played at Wellingborough on Saturday, and won by the home-team, who scored 120 by eight wickets. Leonard, who took seven wickets for 12, A. Gass 15, W. Payne 24, F. French 18, not out, looked well for the home team. S. Browning, not out, 12, did best for the visitors.
