WILTSHIRE TIMES: SATURDAY 31 AUGUST 1895—2.

SPORTS AND PASTIMES.

Football.—Trowbridge and District Cup Competition.—A meeting will be held at Mr. Shallam's Office, Church Street, on Monday evening next, at seven o'clock, for the purpose of receiving entries and arranging details for the ensuing season's competition. Secretaries of all junior clubs in the district invited to attend. [1839]

Essex won five matches, lost seven, and drew four, Carpenter 31-06 per innings, is the premier batsman of the team, and Mead, 15-80, carries off the bowling honours.

Messrs. N. F. Druce and W. Mortimer (old Marlburians) sailed from Southampton on Saturday, with the other members of Mr. P. Mitchell's University team.

Lansdown beat the Ishmaelites on Tuesday by an innings and 19 runs. For the winners, English (103) and Poynton (71) played finely, and the bowling honours were divided between Miller and Scobell.

Out of the 16 matches on the Notts programme three were won, ten lost, and five drawn. Shrewsbury has a batting average of 48-46, and Gunn one of 33-36. Hardstaffe 15-35 and Attewell 16-07 have been the best bowlers.

Derbyshire won five of their 16 matches, lost four, and drew seven. Eaglshaw takes the batting honours with 30-47 per innings, Chatterton being next with 29-14. The two best bowlers are Davidson, 15-69, and Bennett, 16-03.

Corsley met Dilton Marsh, at Corsley, on Saturday, the home side winning easily. Scores: Corsley 63 (H. Moody 19, S. Minty 15); Dilton Marsh 29 (E. Jones 12). For Corsley, H. Ball took five wickets for 10 runs.

C. L. Townsend, the famous young Gloucestershire bowler, told an interviewer the other day that he found his break came naturally, simply by copying so far as he could the action of bowlers in the best matches.

At no time did it appear likely that any definite result would be arrived at in the Notts and Kent match, so well did the batsmen play on either side. In the course of three short days' play eighteen wickets realised an aggregate of 629 runs.

At Taunton, on Wednesday, Tyler, the Somerset bowler, accomplished the hat trick, and quite turned the game against Yorkshire in favour of his side. The batsmen to suffer were Brown, Denton, and Tunnicliffe, and at the time of the occurrence Yorkshire had hit off nearly half the runs they required for the loss of two batsmen.

Lancashire defeated Middlesex on Tuesday, at Lords, by an innings and 100 runs. The Metropolitan County has fallen away greatly in batting strength. Their efforts against the Northern county were represented by 83 runs in the first innings and 77 in the second. For the winners, A. C. Maclaren played a fine innings of 103.

Yorkshire have played 26 matches, of which 14 were won, seven lost, and five drawn. Moorhouse heads the batting average with 35-69, F. S. Jackson being next with 37-46. F. W. Milligan has the best bowling figures, with 14-50, though the brunt of the work has been done by Peeb, whose 138 wickets have been obtained for an average of 14-80 runs each.

In a recent match at Leighton a question was raised as to the validity of the deliveries sent down by Eldridge. One gentleman asserted that the professional's action constitutes a throw, but this opinion did not find much support. If Eldridge was guilty of throwing, something would surely have been said about the matter at Lords, where he has bowled in six or seven matches.

Although set the tremendous task of getting 295 to win, Yorkshire played up in grand style against Somerset on Wednesday, Tunnicliffe and Wainwright making a brilliant effort to win the match. Each scored 82 runs, but they received little support and suffered defeat, after an exciting finish, by 39 runs. L. C. H. Palairet, who overnight had obtained 148, increased these figures to 165, the Somerset total realising 353.

Five first-class cricket matches were concluded on Saturday. At Taunton, Somerset gained a brilliant victory over Surrey, who, however, still head the list in the competition for the championship with eleven points. Yorkshire, who held the second place last week, were beaten by Gloucestershire, while Lancashire, who were third, scored a win over Notts, the result being that Yorkshire and Lancashire now had eight points each, though the latter county had played fewer matches. In the other results on Saturday, Sussex beat Kent, and Hampshire won their game with Leicestershire.

Some doubt is expressed whether the Wiltshire Cricket League has answered the purpose intended, and it is said that a little more enthusiasm will have to be manifested by the various cricket clubs if the competition is to be continued another season. It will be a pity if the League is allowed to die from sheer inaction. There are sufficient cricket clubs in Wiltshire to make it the success which is asked for. Perhaps something might be done in the way of establishing a second division of the League. There is plenty of talent amongst junior clubs which only needs developing.

Although represented by a weak eleven, Swindon C.C. were successful in defeating Oxford Cowley on Saturday by a substantial margin. Nine of the Oxford wickets fell for 31 runs, but the last two batsmen made a good stand, 50 runs being put on before they were parted. Brown took five wickets for 21 runs, and Overton five for 34. Swindon started badly, four wickets being down for 19 runs. Kinner improved matters, but the eighth wicket fell when the total stood at 59. When Currant joined Major the winning hit was soon made, and afterwards both batsmen played freely, Brown also rendering useful aid.

No bowler in England, we should say, has been more ruthlessly punished this year than has Tyler, the slow left-hander of Somersetshire. For reference apply to A. C. MacLaren and company. Remembering this one would scarcely have looked to Tyler accomplishing the rare feat of capturing all the ten wickets in an important match, and what's more, those wickets Burrey's. He took eight on the Thursday and the remaining two the next morning, and so delighted were the good people who foregathered at Taunton that they subscribed the handsome sum of £35 for the lucky bowler. He is fortunate, for the crowd collected £40 for him when he took nine Notts wickets three years ago.

When future historians are writing of big hitters they will most assuredly have to include the name of G. H. Jessop. In recent years nearly all the big smiting has been done by Australians—Massie, Bonnor, McDonnell, and Jack Lyons—but we hardly think that any of these ever equalled the wonderful feat of Jessop against Yorkshire last week. When he went in the wickets Yorkshire were having the best of the game with Gloucestershire, but he altered this almost in the twinkling of an eye, knocking up 63 in 35 minutes. We should say the Yorkshire bowling never received such scurvy treatment before, and Messrs. Peel, Hirst, and Smith are not likely to forget it for a day or two.

Warminster, for the second time this year, made a very poor show against Westbury on Saturday, losing the game by 79 runs and six wickets. Westbury first took the bat, and quickly compiled 223 for the loss of four wickets, F. W. Laverton, Shortland, and Attfield each playing a brilliant and profitable game. Warminster were then sent to the wickets, but the only one to make any stand against the bowling was Coates, who once more proved, by some vigorous batting, to be the back bone of the team. The innings resulted in a total of 144, leaving Westbury the winners as above described. Warminster were rather unfortunate in losing one of their men through an accident, as the ninth wicket went down just before the call of time, so that if another man had been ready the game might have been made a draw.

The Leighton season came to a conclusion on Tuesday, with the match between Mr. Harris' eleven and Leighton. The visitors defended the wickets first, and compiled the respectable score of 258, to which Stephens (missed when he had made 50) was the highest contributor with 114, Badcliffe (got out before he got into double figures) and Miller following with 59 and 47 respectively. The home team started poorly, five wickets falling for 66 runs, but the remaining batsmen raised the total to 279, which included a well made 63 from A. G. Stancomb, 59 from Newman, and 53 from F. W. Stancomb. In the second innings of Leighton, seven wickets went down for 123 runs, when time put a stop to the game, which thus ended in a draw, slightly in favour of Leighton.

Wednesday's results do not materially alter the positions of the counties, Somerset, by their hard-earned victory over Yorkshire being the able to go up a point. Surrey just failed to defeat Gloucestershire, and the other engagements at Derby and Nottingham ended in drawn games. Yorkshire, Notts, Derbyshire, and Essex have now concluded their engagements. The final match to decide the championship of the Wiltshire League was played on the County Ground, at Trowbridge, on Saturday, Corsham, winners of the Southern Division, and Swindon, who hold a similar position in the Northern Division, meeting to decide the question of supremacy. Two strong elevens were placed in the field, perhaps the most prominent absence being Andrews, who did not assist the Swindonians. Batting first on an excellent wicket, prepared by Jeynes, Corsham ran up the tall score of 194 for seven wickets, when the innings was declared closed. S. F. Kennie, who has recently been displaying high class form, placed 78 runs to his credit, and was not out at the close. A. G. Kinner also played well for 40, and serviceable contributions were made by O. J. Mayo (25), O. F. Speckman (23, not out), and H. Harris (19). Seven of the Swindon players had a turn with the ball, Warner, who took two wickets for 26 runs, came out with the best figures. Hayward obtained three for 41 runs. Wainwright's one cost 37, and Dinsdall's solitary success was secured for 36 runs. Marks had 27 runs, his innings, his bowling, Preston 17, and Williams 12. Swindon made a fair start the first wicket not falling until 12 runs had been registered. The next two then dropped for the addition of one run, and other batsmen making a weak display it was soon evident that victory would go to Corsham. Bradford wind up the season with a match against Beckington, to-day, on the Beckington ground.

The Avon Vale took a somewhat weak team to Bradford on Saturday, and paid the penalty. Going in first the home team ran up the good score of 104 for five wickets, Uncles, Holbrook, Clarkson, and Reynolds all scoring freely, the bowling of the visitors being very weak. Avon Vale were disposed of for 40. With the exception of Adair who played a good defensive game no one showed to advantage. The home team allowed themselves only an hour to dispose of the visitors and succeeded in pulling the game off within five minutes of time. The visitors arrived an hour late.
