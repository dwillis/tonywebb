STAFFORDSHIRE SENTINEL: SATURDAY 24 AUGUST 1895-2

**Left Column:**

**BASFORD v. KEELE.**

Keele visited Basford this afternoon to play off the return encounter with the team that beat them decisively in July. The weather was beautifully fine, but the wicket was treacherous, and having won the toss the home captain put his opponents in. A. Walklate and W. Breston opened the game to the bowling of J. G. Hughes and G. Shrubsole. One run was knocked by the first-named batsman, till Hughes, when he was bowled by the last ball of the over, Boastoff having added 1 more run. N. A. Coghili came in and matters improved, 24 being reached before the in-comer Latham was caught at mid-on by Rev. A. Page. Breston was caught by Sutton at mid-off without addition, and H. Taylor, who had succeeded Coghill, fell at 26. G. Sutton and the Rev. F. C. Stainer became associated, but the latter had his middle pegs put down without scoring. F. A. Winstanley joined Sutton, who was caught by Shrubsole behind off a peculiar stroke. A. Capper did not stay long. Shrubsole accounting for him when he had made a single. F. J. Bamford and J. Taylor then got together, the former being bowled by Shrubsole at 44. W. Powers whipped in and got a couple, when he was smartly caught by A. D. Stewart, the innings realising 47 runs and having lasted an hour. Shrubsole took five wickets for 11 and Hughes five for 35. The Basford innings was commenced at 4.35 by the brothers Stewart, Archie taking the first over from Walklate. A disastrous start was made, the batsmen in cutting the bowler's third delivery being taken at point by Coghill, A. D. Walkbride came in, and he opened the scoring by snicking the Silverdale man to the boundary for a couple. Breston bowled at the other end, and Stewart was bailed without scoring, two wickets thus falling for 3 runs. A. English took his place. He and his partner put a more favourable aspect on the game. A sight shower came on but quickly cleared off, and no stoppage was effected. Only 6 was reached when English was bowled by Breston. Before the next batsman, G. Shrubsole, could get to the wicket, the rain again descended, this time heavily, and the players had to seek shelter. After twelve minutes' rest a resumption was made, sawdust being requisitioned. Walkbride, having knocked 6 since he went in, was bowled without any addition to the total. Shrubsole and the Rev. Mr. Page played well, the former hitting very freely before getting out. After the last wicket had put on 26 runs. Sutton ran himself out, and Page was bowled soon after. An interesting encounter was won by the home-sters on the eighth wicket.

**NANTWICH v. BIGNALL END.**
(LEAGUE.—2ND DIVISION.)

These teams contested the championship of the Second League at Nantwich in beautiful weather on a splendid wicket. Bignall End have 24 points, and this was their final match. Nantwich, with 21 points, have yet to meet Leek Church and Tunstall. The home team won the toss, and sent in Shenton and Hinde. A leg bye resulted off Burgess's opening delivery, with another bye from the next ball in Dale's over. Each batsman scored a single. Hinde drove nicely to the boundary for 4. Runs came freely. Hinde outing for a boundary, followed by a smart stroke for 3. Thirty runs were put on in as many minutes. Statham relieved Burgess, and in the first over disposed of Hinde for a well earned 22, which included three boundaries. (One for 35.) Edwards joined Shenton, but the latter made all the runs, despite frequent changes of bowling. At 50 for one wicket Nantwich entertained strong hopes of wiping out the heavy defeat received at Audley. The visitors fielded well, Edwards ultimately settled down to some steady batting, but gave a high ball, which was caught. (Two for 70.) Shenton, with a boundary, recorded his 50 and sent up the century for his side at the same time, for which he was loudly applauded. Hereabouts the game was interrupted by rain.

**Middle Column:**

**TRENTHAM v. DARLASTON.**

This match was played at Trentham this afternoon before a large number of spectators. The refreshing rain of yesterday had rendered the ground in splendid condition, and as usual the home team had to take the field. Darlaston opened their innings with A. Meakr and C. Meakin, the bowling being shared by Moore and Blair. This partnership was but short, as Moore was bowling a very good ball, and in his second over he took Meare for 1. Meakin was here joined by Stevenson; he likewise met the same fate off Blair for a single [unclear]. Again Meakin had Sutton as a partner, but runs came in very slowly, the home bowling well, and gave very few chances. About twenty minutes play saw the third wicket fall for 15, Meakin being taken by Moore, this vacancy was filled by H. Waltho. Both batsmen played a very careful game, but missed no chances, and during the partnership they succeeded in taking several boundaries, which helped up the score nicely. Waltho, however, stayed but for a short time, he being caught and bowled by Moore at this point. The home-ters seemed to be revenging themselves for their defeat of last Saturday, Trestraux being caught by Collins off Moore from his first ball, this being 5 for 23.

**NEWCASTLE v. WHITMORE.**

Played on the ground of the former this afternoon. Newcastle won the toss, and sent in Dean and J. Booth; the Whitmore attack being opened by Smith from the factory end. The scoring was opened in the third over, when Booth nicked Smith for a single, and matters continued slow until Dean made a capital hit for 4. In the next quarter of an hour 7 only were knocked up, and when the board showed 15, Sanders went on vice Tideswell in the visitors' bowling department. The Newcastle captain, and sent a third slip, the only player to make a stand up to the time 40, exchanged being J. Booth, who played a steady game, and bid fair to make a good score. Sanders's slow bowling had been doing all the execution, but Calow hit him for 3 in his first stroke. Rain interfered with the play, and shortly after the resumption J. Booth was bowled for 24, after seeing the fall of seven of his companions' wickets. Newcastle innings closed for 88 exactly at 5.30 p.m.

**Right Column:**

**AUDLEY v. LEEK CHURCH II.**
(LEAGUE—2ND DIVISION.)

This match came off this afternoon in fine weather and on a splendid wicket, at the Kent Hills ground. The visitors won the toss and sent Chapman and Wood to the wickets, who had to face the bowling of Henshall and George Timmis. Audley were minus four of their first men, and had to fill up with second eleven men. The innings opened out very nicely, and runs were scored rather freely. Chapman contributed 9 and Wood 6 before they returned to the pavilion. Byrne on going to the wickets played a rattling style, and hits by the visitors, four 2s, two 3s, two 4s, and one 6 he made 31 runs, after which he was caught by Leek off Henshall. Haines, Milwood, and Hall were not so fortunate in playing the slow bowling.

**BUCKNALL CHURCH v. TUNSTALL II.**
(LEAGUE—2ND DIVISION.)

These teams met at Bucknall this afternoon, Bucknall won the toss, and sent in T. Edge and R. Beaville to the bowling of the brothers Lockett. In attempting a short run from the first ball, Leige was hurriedly thrown out by Rhodes. This let in Beresford, but two overs later Beaville was bowled by a good ball, the total being 3. A. Beresford followed, but A. Lockett, who was bowling a good length, soon shattered his wickets. Disasters continued, and Beresford, Williams, and Egerton were out with the score standing at 10. None of the batsmen so far had been able to play the bowling. Lowe relieved the monotony by hitting A. Lockett to leg for 2, but the bowler had his revenge in the next over. A little later Tomkinson dismissed Capewell by a splendid catch. (Seven for 12.) The three last wickets only added 5 runs. The bowling analysis was very good, A. Lockett securing five wickets for 2, and S. Lockett four wickets for 11.
