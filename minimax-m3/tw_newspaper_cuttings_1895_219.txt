The above is the completed League table for 1895, and on Saturday last the League flag, which remains in possession of the winning team for a season, was hauled on the Pirehill ground.

That Pirehill deserves its position no one will gainsay, and, as a matter of fact, the club have once made points since the commencement of the League; than any other club, although it has never before been actually first, in 1894-3-9 it occupied the second position. Burslem and Crewe are a slightly inferior position to that occupied last year. The greatest improvement has been shown by Leek and Silverdale, who this year occupy positions far in advance of those they held last season. Pearton, the County Club, and Tunstall have all slightly deteriorated.

In consequence of the unfavourable state of the weather in July and August the number of points obtained do not bear a favourable comparison with those of former years, and the list of drawn fixtures is the heaviest on record since the institution of the League, notwithstanding the fact that the time for play has been extended for the season.

Burslem, in 1891, proved its ability to go through a season undefeated, and it has claimed for Norton to establish the untenable record of going through a full season without losing a single wicket. The four points standing to the credit of the club is the lowest season's aggregate ever made.

CONGLETON.

The last game of the season of the Forge Mills was decided at Congleton on Saturday, in the return match with Knypersley. The visitors won the toss, and batted first. Bourne and Shufflebotham played first-class cricket for 63 and 34 respectively. Knypersley declared their innings closed when five wickets were down for 101. The Forge had only an hour to bat, and at the call of time had five wickets down for 42 runs. A. Minshull was the top scorer with 16.
