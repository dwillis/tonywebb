STAFFORDSHIRE SENTINEL: SATURDAY 15 JUNE 1895.—1.

NOTES AND COMMENTS.

Readers will hardly be prepared for the statement of a correspondent that early this morning, ice, the thickness of a penny, was found at Fell Brook, near Bockwell. It was undoubtedly very cold last evening for the time of the year, and even after the sun had risen this morning the air was unusually keen. Nevertheless, the outlook for cricketers as noon approached was fairly satisfactory, and there seemed every prospect of the various local engagements being entered upon and completed without much interruption from rain or cold.

Staffordshire played the Second Eleven from the Oval on Monday and Tuesday at Stoke, and the home team were beaten by six wickets. There can, however, several conditions of a consoling nature to be drawn from the game. The first—and by many it will be considered the most important—is that the match does not count in the second-class championship. Had this been otherwise Staffordshire would most likely have suffered a heavier defeat, still, for King (Porhill), who was the mainstay of the batting in their first innings, and afterwards proved himself almost as useful with the ball, was not qualified for the county, having been born in Nelson territory. He came to this district a few months ago.

Another point which will tend to lighten the sorrows of Staffordshire cricketers is that the Surrey second eleven have not been defeated for three seasons past, and even brought off a drawn game yesterday with Lancashire although the latter batted the grand total of 428 in their first innings. But the fact that the Staffordshire men have improved all round since their defeat by Northamptonshire is a fortnight ago is the circumstance which will afford most pleasure to true cricketers, for what master whether points be won or lost or that play is witnessed worthy to be called cricket. And then to crown all the writer was informed by one of the Surrey eleven that we Staffordshire people underrate our coming six cricked matters; that the County eleven are really a very good lot, and the Surrey men considered them one of the very best among second-class counties. They were sorry they could not say the same for the ground, which, with all due respect to Hantson, usually more successful in the treatment of turf, is thus be said was not good. It was bumpy on the first day, while on Tuesday every ball sent up a cloud of dust, and about three yards in front of the hotel wickets a large piece was completely worn out.

MACCLESFIELD.

The week called Whit, when straw stackers and multi-coloured ties abound, is finished. To those who congregate here in the happy valley the finish was as brilliant as could well wished for, but we, Macclesfield way, cannot congratulate ourselves. When Bollington adopts the fixture-card we generally expect a slashing; but when it became known that both Turner and Hawley were off the team for Saturday, the classic brow of Mr. Senn was unwrinkled in his contentment, for he thought we had a chance. We always have with Bollington; perhaps if we hadn't we should do better. It was not a victory, but a regular slaughter.

We have been more fortunate with the toss than the matches lately, and on Saturday again went to the wickets first. Johnstone and Shatwell were not long together, the former's wicket falling after the first run, Kenyon working the trick with a catch. This was a little more fortunate, but after having half a dozen with his first two hits Kenyon quickly down to single, and he also was dismissed by Kenyon for 5. Shatwell meanwhile had contributed half a dozen, so that the second wickets fell at 13.

Sheldon joined Shatwell and we content for some little time to let his partner appropriate the runs. Twenty was soon telegraphed, but 6 runs later Sheldon retired for 4, and his place was filled by Trodd. Trodd seemed to shape about as well as the rest, and after playing three singles off Pointon let one of the Bollington pro.'s in, and Tommy after performing a gymnastic feat with his hat secured.

Kenyou also altered his style of bowling, and adopted Pointon's nice and easy go-as-you-please kind of delivery. Result, fifty increased the score by 2 and sought seclusion in the woodlawn's shade awaiting his turn to field. (5 for 36). This was glorious, and we bid fair to lose all the wickets for something under 50. Rothwell, however, made a stand, and before Shatwell, who had been batting from the commencement, left him, had pulled the total up to 44. Another of Pointon's tempter's drew the hundredth, but he failed to get fairly hold of the ball, which slipped into Green's hands, and he retired after the best innings of the day (for Macclesfield) for 20. Smile only managed a boundary and Sheldon even less. This left Frost and Hopkinson in with the score at 59 and one wicket to fall. Hopkinson was content with singles, but Frost flew for higher game. After getting the first two balls, and giving us all a match, he got Pointon away for 3 and sent 60 up. His next hit also was a boundary, and after a single he landed another boundary, which completed the 70. It was too good to last, however, for after Hopkinson had again got away, Pointon drove him out, and Furness seizing the opportunity lifted the bails, and finished the innings, the Macclesfield total standing at 74.

The memory of a few other encounters Bollington way is still pretty green, and hopes of anything like a decent finish were at zero. Pointon and Halloway opened the batting against Trodd and Frost, the first over of each produced 4 runs and the second 4 and 5 respectively. Our 74 began to look sick. Twenty was soon signalised, and at 25, Halloway received his quietus, the hands of Trodd for 11. Armitt joined Pointon at the wicket, but before long Trodd again effected a separation, and after completely beating Pointon twice with the third ball took his wicket.

The crowd applauded, and the cries of "well done Tommy" indicated that Trodd has even yet a certain amount of popularity Bollington way.

Rothwell has replaced Frost, but seemed to get on little better, for both Armitt and Furness fairly polished the bowling. Ten after ten kept tottering up until at last 50 was reached amidst applause. At 61, however, Rothwell bowled for a catch and got it. Furness retiring for 17.

Pointon, brother to the pro., followed on, and after a goodly sprinkling of extras, together with a small matter of 24 by Armitt, had raised the total to 94. Armitt retired caught and bowled by Shatwell. Our total had none since been passed, and what was left of the Bollington team went in for increasing their average. Green was with Pointon, jun., and at 5 10, 100 went up, and more in sorrow than anger I left them to fight it out.

A glance at the score book shows a magnificent innings by Green of 52, not out, 19 by Pointon, and 17 by Olaye, bringing the Bollington total up to 187 for eight wickets. What a pity it did not rain.

After the display at Sandbach the fielding of the team was simply wretched not only in but in the majority of positions, and he only wonder is that Bollington did not score more. Twice Pointon was missed before he had started scoring, and towards the finish another couple of ridiculously easy catches were thrown away. The extras given numbered 30! Truly a splendid accompaniment for any wicket-keeper.

BASFORD.

We should have gone to the High School on Saturday to play the boys, but owing to their ground not being good we arranged for them to visit us, and both sides had the pleasure of batting on a wicket that was as good as possible. Matters having been cleared by Toss sending us the brothers Stewart. A. Stewart began to hit soon, but at 9 was bowled by Tallwright in trying to hit a good one to leg. Farkes was caught in the slips by Nichols. English then joined J. H. Bosware, and play was steady for a time, English being caught at the wicket after putting 21 to his credit. Shrubsole now came in, and after playing steadily for a few overs both batsmen made things lively, Stewart at last being caught and bowled by Lawrence. Hubbard made 70 by really first-class cricket, and well deserved the applause he was received with. The Rev. A. Paice did not stay long, being bowled for 4, and Boswall made 2 when the chances was applied, Shrubsole being not out for a well-played 35. Total 151 for five wickets. The School began with Chadwick and M. G. Nichols. Chadwick was bowled by Hughes with the score at 5. L. R. Nichols now joined his brother, and a long stand was made, and a telegram showed 56 before R. G. was bowled. He had played a very steady game, while his brother was scoring well. Shewell was caught as the wicket for 7, and Barnes was soon bowled by Rev. A. Paice. Time was called when the score was five for 63. J. R. L. Nichols being not out 85, made a good style.

DRESDEN.
CHUBCH.

Saturday last found the above club at Sandyford to play the return match. After losing on the previous Saturday the committee chose the best men in the hope of a win, but that hope died a natural death. Sandyford commenced to bat, against the bowling of Williams and Sadler. Four wickets soon fell, but the fielding was not up to the mark. W. Coggins after a time relieved Williams, and Cuparstire took Sadler's place. When the score reached 49 the last wicket was taken. Whitehaw took five wickets for 19 runs, and W. Coggins four wickets for 4 runs, and Sadler one wicket for 2 runs. Dresden commenced to bat against excellent bowling, and a procession was witnessed with much pleasure by the spectators to and from the pavilion. The team were all out for 21. G. Baker, the Sandyford bowler, was in grand form with the ball and took six wickets for 5 runs.

The second team played Caverswall Amateur at home, and they shared the same fate as the first team. Potter took wickets for 11 runs.

To-day the first team have pleaded Milton for their visitors, and the second team visit Longton Zion to play their return match.

AUDLEY.

We went to Chell on Saturday to play the Chell club in connection with the North Staffordshire Second League. We were rather late, and a start was made at 3 45. We batted first, scoring in D. Henshall and J. Timmis to face the bowling of Davis and Lewis. Henshall was caught by Wood for a duck. Timmis and H. Lawrence started well, and the 25 of Lawrence helped us up. Lucky 5 were scored by the other nine. The innings closed at 60, extras 7. The Chell team made a very bad start, the first six wickets falling for 15 runs, and it looked a sure win for us. However, Hargreaves commenced to lash out, and punished our bowling. We missed several chances, and luck was against us. Chell won by 6 runs, and deserve the points for their plucky play. We went down as Chell last year. We play Bignal End to-day at home. We have no more league matches at home till nine weeks to-day, when we receive Nantwich.

The game took place at Nantwich, and batting first the visitors did not make a good start, as three wickets were down for 17. Upon Hall and Webster joining, however, the score was put up 26, when Hall was run out, having contributed an invaluable, but somehow has luckily W. Webster continued to score and was bowled by A. Lockett, whilst the last fielding of the visitors gave 32 runs, a feet which in itself clearly shows the slovenly manner in which the batsmen went about the field. Webster obtained 22 and 8. Lockett 21 runs respectively.
