**CRICKET.**

UPP. SHEPHARD'S XL. beat WOOLLEY PARK.

This match was played at Woolley Park on Wednesday. Woolley going in first put together the total of 67, of which Hayden and Smith did best with 17 and 14 respectively. Mr. Shephard's team then went in the score at lunch time standing at 31 for 4 wickets. Hutchins and Weaver playing good cricket for their runs. The innings closed for 129, Woolley again going in made a spirited attack, scoring 39 runs to win, obtained them with the loss of 3 wickets, Hutchins again doing well. Both teams were kindly entertained to tea after the match by Mr. Wroughton. Score:—

INKPEN beat FARINGDON C.C.

The visitors to meet the Inkpen C.C. on their own ground were rather late in arriving, a time fatal for the match being two o'clock, whilst the game did not commence till about four. The home team won the toss and play quickly began. The chances of winning looked rather small at one time, four wickets having fell for 10 runs. Mr. Reece soon turned the tide by adding 32 runs more in capital style, ably supported by a splendid drive by T. Lee, and two fine hits by J. Attwood. The first six was 63, not out for 6, and when the innings was closed carried out his bat. G. Butler and J. Hopkins did not bat. The visitors were dismissed for 29 runs, mainly owing to the fine bowling of T. May, who took 7 wickets, his average being 1·9. Score:—

LAMBOURN beat CHILTON.

The return match was played on Saturday last, on the Lambourn ground, and, as the score will show, resulted in a win for Lambourn, the homesters thus avenging their defeat in the previous match. The game did not commence till rather late, and consequently had to be decided on the first innings. The weather was delightful and the wicket really good. Chilton, who lost the toss, but elected to send the home team in. A very poor start was made, as two Lambourn wickets were down for 0, but a wonderfully different complexion was put on the game, when, after H. Penfold had scored a run in good style, F. Harris

[Column 2]

and J. Bryant got well set. F. Harris made some splendid hits, and the spectators loudly cheered his plucky play, his score of 35 being made in every form. F. Bryant played capital cricket for his 35, and was greatly applauded upon his retirement. V. Hobbs and C. J. Maberley rendered great assistance to the score, before the innings was declared closed at 129. The Chilton team did but little, only one player (S. Panting) obtaining double figures. The bowling of Messrs. Hobbs and Bryant was very effective, and F. Harris made two capital catches in the field. Lambourn had a splendid start, and at the call of time no wicket had fallen, the score being 61. H. New and Rumball having scored well during the innings. Score:—

BURGHCLERE beat NEWTOWN.

Played on the Sandford Priory ground and ended in a defeat for the home team. Burghclere won the toss and batted first; three wickets fell quickly, but on Rev. G. Hicks and G. Kimber getting together runs were piled on fast, Kimber being run out for a well played 12. Rev. G. Hicks was not out for 9, these made a little score of 38. Newtown made no stand whatever, being all disposed for 41. Allsup alone getting double figures. This match brought the Newtown season to a close, after a fairly successful season, they having played 18 matches, won six and lost eight. NEWTOWN beat PANBR CHURCH INSTITUTE.

This return match was played in the beautiful grounds of Sandford Priory on Saturday, August 31. Newtown had a slender number of spectators present, including Lady Butler and family and company. Newtown went first to the wickets, and by good play and hard hitting on the part of Elfield (32), and Gardner (15), made a total of 85 runs. Gunning's bowling being too good for them, he being well backed up by Matthews and Newcomb. On the Institute handling the willow, however, they put in an appearance for 30 runs, Wicks and Newcomb with 18, Gibbs, Elfield, Allsup, and Baverstock being the bowlers. Tea was then partaken of, and Newtown again went to the wickets, but the Institute not being able to go in again, the home team won a pleasant game on the first innings by eight runs.

ARBURY HOUSE beat NEWTOWN HOUSE.

On Thursday afternoon a match was played between elevens of the Arbury House estate and Newtown House, the game being won by the Arbury House eleven, a result being mainly due to the good bowling of E. Barber, F. James, and G. Cooper, who brought off a beautiful catch, 11, also J. James' slip, and a Barry Whitten a big drive for five runs. For the losers M. Stratford, White, Street, and Weight acquitted themselves very well. A capital tea had been prepared by the housekeeper on the grounds, which the players did justice to. Altogether a most enjoyable afternoon was spent, and a hearty vote of thanks was carried with acclamation to Mr. Walter Arkwright for his kindness. Score:—
