Ux. SHEPHERD's XL. beat WOOLLEY PARK.

This match was played at Woolley Park, on Wednesday. Woolley going in first put together the total of 67, of which Hayden and Smith did best with 17 and 14 respectively. The innings closed for 78, Woolley again going in scoring 64. Mr. Shepherd's team, securing 39 runs to win, obtained them with the loss of 3 wickets, Hutchins again going well. LAMBOURN beat CHILTON.

The return match was played on Saturday last, on the Lambourn ground, and, as the score will show, resulted in a win for Lambourn, the homesters thus avenging their defeat in the previ us match. The game did not commence till rather late, and consequently had to be decided on the first innings. The weather was delightful and the wickets really good. Chilton won the toss, but elected to send the home team in. A very poor start was made, as two Lambourn wickets went down for 0, but a wonderfully different complexion was put on the game, when, after H. Penfold had scored a-von in good style, F. Harris and J. Bryant got well set. F. Harris made some splendid hits, and the spectators loudly cheered his play. His score of 35 being made by 7 boundaries, &c. Bryant played capital cricket for his 36, and was greatly applauded upon his retirement. W. Hobbs and C. J. Maberley rendered great assistance to the score, before the innings was declared closed at 129. The Chilton team did but little, only one player (S. Panting) obtaining double figures. The bowling of Messrs. Hobbs and Bryant was very effective, and F. Harris made two capital catches in the field. Mr. Spiers followed on, and at the call of time no wicket had fallen, the score being 61, H. New and Rumball having scored well during the innings. Score:—

NEWTOWN beat PARKER CHURCH INSTITUTE.

This return match was played in the beautiful grounds of Sandford Priory, on Saturday, August 31, when a splendidly number of spectators present, including Lady Butler and family and company. Newtown went first to the wickets, and by good play and hard hitting on the part of Eifield (27), and Gardiner (15), made a total of 68 runs, Coombs's bowling being too good for them, he being well backed up by Matthews and Newcomb. On the Institute handling the willow, however, they were all disposed of for 29 runs, the only player with double figures being 16, Gibbs, Eifield, Allson, and Beaverstock being the bowlers. Tea was then partaken of, and Newtown again went to the wickets, but the Institute not being able to go in again, the home team won a pleasant game on the first innings by eight runs.

ADBURY HOUSE beat NEWTOWN HOUSE.

On Thursday afternoon a match was played between crews of the Adbury House estate and Newtown House, the game being won by the Adbury House eleven, a result being mainly due to the good bowling of E. Barber, F. Haynes, and P. White, the latter brought off a beautiful catch, and a sharp slip, and c. Berry Fields, a big drive for five runs. For the losers M. Stratford, White, Street, and Wright acquitted themselves very well. A capital tea had been prepared by the houskeeper on the grounds, which the players did justice to. Altogether a most enjoyable afternoon was spent, and a hearty vote of thanks was carried with acclam to Mr. Walter Arkwright for his kindness. Score:—
