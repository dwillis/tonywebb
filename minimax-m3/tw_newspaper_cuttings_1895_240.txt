**Column 3:**

OUR SPORTING COLUMN

What with the rain and the envy wicket Walsall did not have much chance of showing what their full batting strength could do in their match on Saturday, although the best by of means preventable team of Dudley to deal with. For once in a way the bowling of the visitors, and he was treated with very cold immunity, in fact, the whole team batted with confidence, and at the conclusion of the play it was found that Dudley had put together the respectable score of 129 for nine wickets. S. Morgan—who, by the way, has not had the best of luck this season—batted a capital 29 not out, whilst F. Wheldon (21), Mr. Dooley capt. W. Brunwell (11), F. Brown (12), J. Cropper (14), (8), Cole (not out 12) played the bowling without any apparent difficulty. Smith, too, was evidently in scoring form, but he had the misfortune to return a ball to Baresby. If the team had dropped short about an inch it would have been out of the bowler's reach, and it was unquestionably a fine catch. The rain undoubtedly spoilt what would have been a very interesting match.

The Dudley second team on Saturday were in easily the same comfortable position as the first team. They kept Walsall second in the field all day, and when play concluded only six wickets were down for a score of 115. Some very useful scores were made by A. Feenley (27), E. J. Thompson (27), G. S. Thompson (8) not out, and J. W. Collins (14), all playing well for their respective scores.

Dudley hardly expected to win the Charity Shield with the team they had against Walsall on Monday, but they made a good show, all things accounted for. They put up 118, and it took 16 of the best Walsall wickets to exceed it. Mr. Dawley, the Dudley captain, was principal scorer for Dudley, with a lively 24. 24. Wesley, who was next best with 22, and Morgan made 16 and 11 respectively. Better luck another time.

The rain evidently did not damp the ardour of the Nethertonians in their match with Darleston, for the game was brought to a satisfactory conclusion. Netherton, with five wickets down, knocked up a total of 72, Gower (34) and Darby (unbeaten 9) being to the fore. Darleston only made in the first Innings—put in with Thomas and Gower being put together 19. Wooleston was another club to take things easily on Saturday, the Congregational (Slater's) only making 56, whilst they raised together 72. W. Baker (35) and T. Walters (10) were chief scorers for Woodsetton, and for Slater's Hockey (15) at the first team.

Leabrook Grounds, Wednesday, were on Saturday and Monday occupied with an interesting pedestrian handicap, and the grounds were largely patronised. The distance was 100 yards, and there were 100 entries, classed into Longstone, St. Union (of Bradley), G. Payne (of Essington), and E. Woods (of Cos Lane) each won their heats, whilst T. Hinton (of Halesowen) ran second to Woods. After the first round the 20 successful men were redrawn in four heats, and Payne and Woods managed to win their heats again, and in the final they came up with a yard. Payne, who was in 30 yards' start, started at 2 to 1 on, and 7 to 1 against Woods.

The Birmingham League matches suffered, in common with all other Saturday fixtures, accordingly had a per team against Aston Unity, and perhaps it was as well for them that the rain prevented the game being played. They were without Piser, Foley, Rhodes, and Arnold. Five wickets for 64, however, does not [unclear] on quite Unity form; but this was due, probably, to the bowling of Higgs, who took four wickets for 22.

The Smethwick and Dudley match was abandoned in consequence of the rain.
