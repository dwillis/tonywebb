**OUR SPORTING COLUMN**

What with the rain and the very wicket Walsall did not have much chance of showing what their full batting strength could do in their first match on Saturday, although the best the home team could do, in a way the bowling Dudley to start with. For once in a way the bowling was once in the old terrors, and he was treated with very little ceremony. In fact, the whole team batted with confidence, and at the conclusion of the play it was found that Dudley had put together the respectable score of 129 for nine wickets. S. Morgan—who, by the way, has not lost sight of luck this season—batted a capital 26 not out, whilst F. Wheldon (21), Mr. Dooley (4), W. Bramhall (11), F. Brown (12), J. Cropper (14), and Cole (not out 13) played the bowling without any apparent difficulty. Smith, too, was evidently in scoring form, but he had a total Thomas amounted to about an inch in his back. The rain had damped the ardour of the Barnsby. If it was to look short four wickets to the fore, it would have been out of the bowler's reach, and it was unquestionably a fine catch. The rain undoubtedly spoilt what would have been a very interesting match.

The Dudley second team on Saturday were in exactly the same comfortable position as the first team. They kept Walsall second innings closed all day, and when play ceased only six wickets were down for a score of 115. Some very useful scores were made by A. Ferday (27), E. J. Thompson (27), G. S. Thompson (24 not out), and J. W. Collins (14), all playing well for their respective scores.

Dudley hardly expected to win the Charity Shield with the team they had against Walsall on Monday, but they made a good show, all things accounted for. They put up 118, and it took [unclear] of the best Walsall wickets to exceed it. Mr. Dooley, the Dudley captain, was principal scorer for Dudley, with a likely kit 39, Wheldon was next best with 29 and Morgan 11. Weale made 16 and 11 respectively. Batting luck another time.

The rain evidently did not damp the ardour of the Nethertonians in their match with Darleston, for the game was brought to a satisfactory conclusion. Netherton, with five wickets down, knocked up a total of 72, Gower (34) and Darby (not out 19) being to the fore. Darleston were soon disposed of, only 32 runs coming from the bat and out of that small total Thomas (c) rained 25 runs. Shaw Road beat Flood Street by 40 runs, the defeated only being able to put together 19. Wooleston was another club to take things easily on Saturday, the opponents (Slater's) only making 38, whilst they raised together 78. W. Baker (35) and P. Walters (19) were chief scorers for Woodsetton, and for Slater's Sinclair with 10 was exceedingly useful.

Loobook Grounds, Wednesday, were on Saturday and Monday occupied with an interesting pedestrian handicap, and the grounds were largely patronised. The distance was 120 yards, and there were 105 entries, classed into Leacrofts, S. Linon (of Bradley), G. Payne (of Essington), and E. Weale (of Can Lane) each won their heats, whilst T. Hulston (of Halesowen) ran second to Weede. After the final round the 30 successful men were redrawn in four heats, and Payne and Weele managed to win their heats again, and in the final they came off here and second respectively, after Weele had been put back a yard. Payne, who was on the 30 yards' mark, started at 2 to 1 out, and 7 to 1 against Weede.

The Birmingham League matches suffered, in common with all others, on Saturday, owing to the inclement weather. Mr. Thomas Aston Unity, and perhaps it was as well for them that the rain prevented the game being played. They were without Fraser, Foley, Rhodes, and Arnold. Five wickets for 64, however, does not seem quite likely form; but this was due, probably, to the bowling of Bingley, who took four wickets for 28. The South Staffordshire match was abandoned in consequence of the rain.
