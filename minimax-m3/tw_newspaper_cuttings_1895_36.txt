South Bucks Free Press: Friday 16 August 1895 - 4

BLACKWELL HEATH 2ND XI v. LITTLE MARLOW.
LOW SCORES.

A match between the above clubs was played on the ground of the latter on Saturday last, and resulted in a victory for Blackwell Heath by 23 runs on the first innings. The batting of Musk, Baker, and Smith was very good. Almond accomplished the hat trick, taking 5 wickets in 3 consecutive balls. Clifford also bowled well. The second innings of the latter proved most disastrous, they being dismissed in 20 minutes for 8 runs. No doubt the result would have been different had time permitted a second innings for the victors. Davis and Penny batted in good form. In the second innings of Blackwell Heath Almond again accomplished the hat trick, and took 5 wickets for 1 run. Penny obtained 4 for 6 runs. Score:—

MARLOW v. HIGH WYCOMBE.
BIG HITTING BY E. E. MASSIE.

The return match between these old rivals was played at Marlow, on Saturday, in fine weather, on a wicket that favoured the bowlers. It will be remembered that the first match at Wycombe was left drawn much in favour of Marlow. On this occasion the riverside town gained an easy victory, thanks to a fine innings by E. E. Massie, the Australian batsman (who is staying at Marlow this summer), and some excellent bowling by W. H. Lannon and Barber. [unclear] won the toss, Langley and Barber facing the deliveries of Page and H. E. Parker. Runs came steadily until the total reached 28, when both batsmen were out with a dozen to their credit. Massie and Lannon advanced the total to 54, when the latter was caught by White, and as Bailis failed to score, and went out on a 4, five wickets were down for 38. Brown then came to the aid of Massie, and a useful stand was effected. The 100 went up amidst cheers, but with four added a great change came over the game. H. S. Parker sending back Brewer, Burton, and Mackenzie in one over. Six, seven, and eight for 104. Massie was now hitting with tremendous force, and was especially severe on Page's gigantic hit for 6 into Court Garden delighting the onlookers hugely. Morgan batted steadily while the Australian hit until the total was 149, when he was yorked by Parker. Just when it appeared certain that Massie would take out his bat he was captured in the slips, and the innings closed for 146. Massie's dazzling score of 137 included two 6's, six 4's, two 3's, and eight 2's. He was deservedly applauded on leaving the pavilion. Both Parker and Keen bowled well. Wycombe commenced their innings with F. M. Parker and Langley. The bowlers were W. H. Lannon and Barber. A more disastrous start could not well be imagined. Lannon caught and bowled Parker and clean bowled Baines in his first over. Two for 0. George scored a couple of singles, and Langley was then caught by Mackenzie off Barber. Three for 5. H. S. Parker helped George to put a better aspect upon affairs, but the former was out at 26, after hitting up 16. Jones followed in and was bowled by Lannon at 28. The brothers George then got together and carried the score to 40, when three wickets fell—the George brothers and White, all clean bowled. Page hit up it by means of a 6, a 4, and two 2's, and the innings closed for 50. Lannon bowled magnificently well, his seven wickets costing 51 runs each. Wycombe followed on, and made 50 for one wicket. Langley batted well for 35 not out. Marlow therefore won by 65 runs on the first innings—a rather unexpected result, seeing that the team which was in the field was by no means a strong one. Score and analysis:—

BLYTHEWOOD v. CIPPENHAM.

This match, at Blythewood, on Saturday last, proved a very close one, Cippenham only winning by 5 runs. Ucing in first Blythewood were got rid of for 35, and E. Kent (not out, 2) doing best.
