**WYCOMBE RESERVES v. WEST WYCOMBE.**

This match was played at West Wycombe on Saturday afternoon. The visitors went in to the wickets first, but offered a very feeble resistance to the bowling of Turner and Fletcher, who each took five wickets for 10 runs. The home side, owing to the good batting of Turner, A. Harman, and Fletcher, made a total of 78. For the Reserves Keen bowled in good form, taking five wickets for 26 runs. In their second innings the Reserves did better, and passed their former score before the third wicket fell; but their total was not high enough to avert defeat, as it left the home side only 33 to get to win, which they accomplished with the loss of four wickets. **HIGH WYCOMBE v. WOOBURN INSTITUTE.**

This match, which was played on the Wycombe ground last Saturday afternoon, resulted in a draw that, if time had permitted, would have ended in favour of the home team. The visitors were without Barber, the Lenmore, and others who generally play for the Institute; but they had the assistance of H. Bacon, who proved a tower of strength to their side, both departments of the game. Wycombe did not play their full strength, more than half their number being reserve men. The home side went in first, and a total of 156 was reached before they were all disposed of. The chief contributor to this score was F. George, whose figures included a century and seven 5's. Other good innings were played by Bird, G. George, and Page who drove one of Bacon's deliveries out of the field for 6. A word of praise ought to be given to Ratty among the younger members of the team, who played well for 21; his principal hits being a 4, three 3's, and three 2's. Bacon was the successful in bowling, his analysis for Institute being a trifle over six runs apiece, and the last three being obtained without a run being scored off him. For Wooburn Institute, Bacon, Bowler, and Plumeridge played well, the second named hitting G. George for a 6. When stumps were drawn the visitors had made 129 for seven wickets, Bacon having had put on but eight of the 1st and 3rd. For the rest the short successful with the ball but his four wickets cost mainly 13 each. Score and analysis:—

**WYCOMBE CHURCH ROOM v. WYCOMBE ST. JOHN'S.**

The third encounter between the above-named teams took place on the Wycombe Rye on Saturday last in splendid weather, and, after a pleasant and interesting game, resulted in a win for the Church Room by 37 runs on the first innings. The St. John's won the toss and elected to bat first, and compiled the moderate total of 40. S. Free (18), Burnham (8), and Ing (4) being the top scorers. The Church Room then went in, and replied with 77. Visiton (not out 28), Sexton (16), and G. Twitchen (13) being the double-figure contributors. **WYCOMBE BEES v. WANDERERS v. HOLLOWAYS BOOT OPERATIVES C.C.**

The return match between the above-named teams, which took place on the Wycombe Rye last Saturday, resulted in a victory for Mr. E. Coleman, who "skippered" the Wanderers, turned out a formidable eleven, while the olympians were rather mixed, and the last moment they had to take up with five substitutes. The Bees, who came in last, and ran up a creditable total of 86, with Sharp, Golder, Humphery, Collings, Luffyman, and Colea, contributing their respective scores in five figures. Tibbles, with the ball, was in rare fettle, his four wickets only costing a fraction over a shilling a run. Going in, the Wanderers started badly, the only really serviceable player being Tibbles, and at the fall of the fifth wicket Coleman and Young's opening stand against the destructive deliveries of Humphery and Mead. Wickets 1 and 2 went down for 18, and as this juncture it looked as though the Wanderers would not reach the ordinary six wickets being down for 22, the third stand, for 33, not adding a run. The score ran slowly, and when the total closed the Wanderers' hopes revived, the grandest player giving on his 13 in double quick time. The end eventually arrived with the total at 84, which left the operatives winners on the first innings by 22 runs.
