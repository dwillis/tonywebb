HIGH WYCOMBE v. MR. E. STEVENS' XI.

On Saturday Mr. Edmund Stevens, of The Vache, Chalfont St. Giles, brought over a strong team to oppose the local club, but rain prevented the game from being played out. It was, however, drawn in favour of Wycombe, who had made 45 for the loss of two wickets, when stumps were drawn. No play was possible before luncheon, and a start was not made till 2.30 o'clock, when the visitors went to the wickets before their innings was concluded. Rain greatly modified the desire to make a precipitate rush to the friendly shelter of the pavilion. The rain holding off for a time allowed the innings to be finished, 87 runs being the result. On Wycombe going in the light was very bad, and the rain came down at intervals in torrents, so that after several attempts to play out time it was thought to be best to leave the game drawn, and point the match was left in the position shown by the following score and analysis. BAYNES XI v. PERMANENT STAFF OF THE 2ND BATT. OXFORD LIGHT INFANTRY.

On Monday last, August 5th, a match was played on Bayners private ground, between the above teams, and, after being blindered a little by the unsettled weather, ended in a decisive victory for the former by an innings and 11 runs. Mr. C. M. Ross captained the home eleven, and, having won the toss, elected to bat. The game was very slow at the commencement, and wickets fell rapidly until 11.30, when the captain and Mr. Powell became associated, the best stand of the day was made, and some good cricket was seen, both playing with confidence. They put on 62 runs between them, when Mr. Rose was caught at the wicket for an invaluable 37. Powell left soon after with a well-played 39 to his credit. Wheeler made 14, and the remaining batsmen being quickly cleared off, the innings closed for 116. An adjournment was now made for lunch (which had been prepared in a spacious marquee, erected for the occasion) through the generosity of Sir Philip and Lady Rose. After ample justice had been done to the good things provided, the visitors commenced their innings. Captain Rose and Bugler Wakelin were the only ones to show any resistance to the attack of C. M. Rose and J. Lans, every wicket in their best form, and the innings closed for 51. Captain Rose's 16 was the highest score in the batting display, which included one 6, a clean drive out of the ground. Powell had the best analysis, taking 6 wickets for 23, while Mr. Rose's cost 25 runs. Having to follow on, they failed to reach the number required to save an innings defeat, Dean taking 6 wickets for 18 runs, and the game resulted as before stated. Score and analysis:—
