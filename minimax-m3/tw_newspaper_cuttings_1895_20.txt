READING v. ABINGDON.—That the cricket field is one of the favourite haunts of King Chance was evidenced beyond dispute in this engagement, which was decided at Reading on Saturday. On the occasion of the first match, played a short time since at Abingdon, Reading were represented by a fairly powerful combination, nevertheless they were the recipients of a decisive thrashing. The Berks county town, however, created a mild surprise in their engagement, as despite that their eleven on paper was per- fectible they returned the compliment they received at Abingdon with slight interest, inasmuch as they secured more than an innings victory. The home captain ordered his colleagues to the wicket first and when 16 runs had been obtained Campion ceased his partnership, 41 runs had been obtained, No serious advance in the figures was recorded before the promoting G Williams handled the willow, he going in third wicket down. Between the third and fourth dismissal 32 chalks were added, but after that stage no rapid scoring was chronicled, and when the umpires declared the homeators essay at a ter limation, 143 was the total, Williams with a score of 41 was the not out, the other chief contributors being Crowhurst (17), Campion (32), Pott (19) and E Eynott (11). On Eynott and F Bynott relinquishing their partnership to the visitors, the score of the debut before they opened the second time and with Wilson, the team, but in doing so had many fresh partners, as might be gathered from the remark that at the fall of fourth wicket the total was 13; five, 14; six, 16; seven, 20; eight, 29; nine, 35; ten, 39. On following on, the visitors started 80 runs off with the first wicket, the grand total, however, the rot set in during their first innings was again apparent, as the board showed that in their second innings only 63 were compiled. Of this number Dandridge notched 23 and A.D. Morland 15. So splendidly did both Campion and E. Eynott trundle the home captain was content to rely on the bowling only in the trending department. That his judgment was sound was demonstrated by the capital record each secured, which is here produced: Campion in the first innings bowled 43 balls, and secured one maiden and six wickets at a cost of twenty runs, and in the second delivered 63 balls, and again secured six for 34 runs.
