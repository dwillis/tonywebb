WESTERN DAILY MERCURY: THURSDAY/FRIDAY 15/16 AUGUST 1895-2

BEDMINSTER C.C. v. PLYMOUTH GARRISON.

Continuing their tour in Devonshire, Bedminster opened a two days' encounter with the Plymouth Garrison at Mount Wise, Devonport, yesterday. There was but a moderate attendance when the game started at noon, but during the afternoon, notwithstanding the numerous counter attractions, the crowd increased. It was decided to play twelve aside, and winning the toss the Garrison decided to take the first innings. Captain Curteis and Lieutenant Collins being the first pair to face the bowling of McCarthy and Bucknell. The former's first over proved disastrous to Captain Curteis, who had only scored a single. The vacancy was filled by Lieutenant Perry-Ayscough, who soon secured a mastery over the bowling, necessitated a change. Ayscough was the first to go, falling a victim to a good ball sent up by Hemmens. He had made 40, which included six 4's. Lieutenant Collins did not long survive his partner, as he was well caught in the same manner's successive over. Lieutenant Taylor and Lieutenant Sparks were together for a very short period, and it was not until Lieutenant Hobbs partnered the latter that anything like a resistance was offered to the bowling. Bucknell had again gone on bowling, and in his first over dismissed the Garrison captain with a ball which broke in from the off. The outgoing man had made 16, which included a six and two fours. Lieutenant Harrison and Captain Robinson were the next pair to cause trouble, but the former should have been caught when he had made 20. Proffiting by the let-off both batsmen signalised the event by each hitting a 3, but eventually Harrison was well caught in the long field for 24. The innings closed for 167.

At a quarter to four the visitors commenced their batting. Cook and Lawson facing Harrison (from the Pavilion end) and Finlaison. Runs came slowly, and the result of half an hour's play was 21 runs. Lawson was then caught, and with two runs added three more wickets fell in quick succession. Brown and McCarthy made a stand, and when the former was run out the score was 55. McCarthy did not long survive, as he had the misfortune to play on in the next over. T. Hemmens and J. Bucknell now became associated, and offered a

stubborn resistance to the bowling for less than six being tried. At length Hemmens fell to a good catch by Captain Robinson, who, also, a few minutes later, accounted for the downfall of G. Hemmens, by running about forty yards and bringing off a magnificent catch in the deep field. The innings closed for 117. Play will be resumed at 11.30 this morning. Score:—
