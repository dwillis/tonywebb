NEWCASTLE DAILY JOURNAL: THURSDAY 11 JULY 1895.

CRICKET NOTES.
(By "CASTOR.")
We are frequently told that what spectators like to see are
batsmen who make things lively for the bowlers; and, if this is
really so, those who saw the game between Captain Nicholls' and
Mr Bewicke's teams would enjoy themselves. Though both
Vin. Thompson and Butler played exceptionally well, their per-
formances were quite overshadowed by the doings of George Simp-
son, who, after taking seven wickets for 41 runs, scored 177, and
was not out at the close. So far as I could see, Simpson never
made a bad stroke until he had reached his hundred, and the
chance he gave to Butler at 110 was a difficult one for the fielder
to judge. The hitting was, as is usual with Simpson, beautifully
timed and clean, and none of the bowlers seemed to give him any
trouble, though Walters almost bowled him second ball. V.
Thompson's 89 was a capital display of steady cricket; and
Butler's 82 was obtained in his best style. There was a large
gathering of spectators in the field, and after such an interesting
game we may expect good crowds at the Constabulary Ground
throughout the remainder of the season.

So far this season the Rev E. W. R. Walters has played in five matches for Mr Bewicke's team, and has an average of over 120 runs per innings. More power to his elbow, say I.

Who is the very superior young man who writes on Northum-
berland cricket for ' P. I. '? Is he a player ? In his opinion there
is only one good team—the Borderers—in the county, and but
one competent critic of the game—himself. If rumour speaks
the truth, it is a well-known member of a prominent local club,
who some years ago came from the south with a great reputation
as a bowler; but with each succeeding season his reputation has
grown less, until now, not being able to score off his opponents on
the field with either bat or ball, he has taken to the pen, in the
hope, I suppose, of playing a winning game. Northumbrian
batsmen may not be Graces nor Stoddarts; but they are able to
hold their own with the bowlers which south have, up to now,
sent us, if we take the Borderers' "Knight of the pen" as a
specimen.

It is pure moonshine for any one to talk of the Borderers as re-
presenting the best cricketing talent in the county; and no
difficulty would be experienced in getting together a much
stronger eleven than they put into the field  In fact, what
victories they have gained here have been chiefly over school
boys, and were the best team chosen to play for Northumberland
probably only one Borderer—K Spencer—would be sure of a
place in it  At the present time several prominent county gentle-
men are trying to improve cricket in these parts. Towards this
end they are inviting strong southern teams to visit them; and
sarcastic remarks about those connected with the game, and the
players, from the pen of the Borderers' superior young man will
not help them much. Perhaps, after all, it is not county so much
as afternoon tea party cricket that this critic patronises, while
his own falling off in bowling he attributes to the weakness of
local batsmen, as he can only bowl well against good men.
What a pity he cannot play in the south.
Some time ago a committee was formed with the intention of
resuscitating county cricket in Northumberland; but as the
Corporation have refused to grant a field, and negotiations with
landowners for one have fallen through, Mr R. G. Mortimer
will, I believe, call a general meeting shortly, and lay the matter
before it.
I notice that the Elswick Works Club has fixed their match with
Benwell on July 27 as Lisey's benefit game. A more capable and
painstaking professional cannot be found than Lisey, and as a
player he ranks among the best in the north. His bowling has
pulled the club through many of their engagements, and I am
sure every member will do his best to increase the fund to be
handed over to their professional, who, by his ability on the field
and conduct off, does as much as any one connected with it to
uphold its honour and dignity.
I hope my readers will not forget that the game between
South Northumberland and Elswick Works, at Gosforth, on
Saturday, is for the benefit of the professional Goodband. This
player is certainly one of the best we have ever had in the North
of England, and deserves a bumper gate.
On Saturday Stockton visited Darlington, and, thanks to
McDonald's 75 not out, defeated the home team easily. Lieut.
Coates had another day out against Durham School, and had
hard lines in missing his century by 7 runs; while his batting
was followed up by some good bowling on the part of Fergu-
son. The matches between South Shields and Tynemouth for
Copeland's benefit, furnished some good cricket, but ended in a
draw. Wallsend could do little with the bowling of Simpson (5
for 13) and Goodband (4 for 12), and were all out for 31. What
was the matter with North Durham on Saturday ? Did they all
get the "hump" together, for there was certainly something
wrong with them. For six of them to go down without scoring re-
quires some explanation.
Sunderland, though they succeeded in beating Consett at Con-
sett, could do no better than draw on Saturday at Sunderland;
though as the full five hours were not played I believe they
intend to claim the match. One of the best performances of the
day was that of Eley in the match against the Royal Grammar
School; for after taking seven wickets for 14 runs he came out
top scorer of his side with a well-played 28. Up to the beginning
of this month he had got 57 wickets for less than 6 runs each—a
bowling feat that will take some beating.
St. George's and Northumberland met on Saturday, in fine
weather, before a small gate. Northumberland batted first, and
were got rid of very cheaply for 113. Moss took five wickets;
Bell, three; and V. Thompson, two for 1 run. Hoare batted well
for 32. On St. George's going in a complete rot took place, and
they were all out in less than an hour for 38. Not the best form
was shown. Everyone seemed to think that because Thompson
got a duck there was no necessity for their getting any either.
This won't do. Their cracks cannot come off every time, and the
rest of the team must make up their minds to do a fair share in
the run getting. The fielding on both sides was very good.

CONSETT WEDNESDAY v. CHESTER-LE-
STREET WEDNESDAY.
Beautiful weather favoured this match, which came off
at Chester-le-Street yesterday. Both teams were fairly
strong, especially in bowling, which held the upper hand
all through—a fact in great part due to the crumbling
wicket. Child both bowled and batted well for the home
club; while James and Joseph Andrews batted and bowled
in good form for Consett, who won an interesting game

MR BEWICKE'S XI. v. NORTHERN NOMADS.
A two days' match between these teams commenced on
the Close House Ground on Tuesday, and finished yes-
terday in a decisive victory for Mr Bewicke's eleven by
an innings and 66 runs. Batting first on Tuesday, the
home team made 264, the Rev E W R Walters playing
a fine innings of 67; while Guttridge and Captain Wilkin-
son hit hard for 55 and 45 respectively. After the
Nomads had commenced well with Clayton and Mortimer,
Guttridge and Walters quickly disposed of several of the
other batsmen, and it was not until P Ashworth and
Morton came together that a second effective stand was
made. Both played steadily, but none of the others could
do anything with the bowling, and the side were all out
for 145. Following on, they fared badly against Gutt-
ridge and A A Mewburn, the professional getting four
wickets for 32 runs, while Mewburn's four only cost 16
runs. The innings realised 49, P Ashworth and Clayton
alone reaching double figures. Score:—
