STAFFORDSHIRE CHRONICLE : SATURDAY 15 JUNE 1895-2

STAFFORD v. WOLVERHAMPTON SCHOOL.—There
were some splendid performances with the ball in
the match between Stafford and Wolverhampton
School at Stafford on Wednesday. Taking first
han's, Stafford started badly, C. Baker being
bowled by the first ball, but on Williams and T.
S. Fishwick coming together a prolonged stand was
made. Both playd fine cricket. Fishwick scored
at a rapid rate, and his hitting all round the
wicket was very strong. His score included two
sixes for two lofty drives over the boundary rails.
While not making runs at anything like the pace of
his partner, Williams gave an attractive display of
batting, and his engagement promises to be one of
considerable advantage to the club from a run-
getting point of view. Before Fishwick was run
out, the score was taken to 174, of which the out-
going batsman's share was 114. When the separa-
tion was effected three wickets fell rapidly with
only an increase to the score of five runs. At the
fall of the fifth wicket, Stafford adopted the clo-
sure. The School eleven made a disappointing dis-
play against Turner and Weetman, they all being
disposed of for the small score of 32. A feature of
the innings was that all the batsmen were bowled,
Weetman capturing six wickets for fourteen runs
and Turner four for eighteen. Score:

SHUGBOROUGH PARK v. ABBOTS BROMLEY.—The first match of this season between these old opponents was played at Abbots Bromley on Saturday. The home team batted first, but were unable to face with any success the deliveries of C. Marsden and C. Westman, and in 10 overs and 1 ball the whole eleven were dismissed for 22 runs—nine of which were extras. No fewer than 8 batsmen had the unenviable "0" attached to their names. The visitors then compiled 76 for 7 wickets, when time was called. It must be stated that Abbots Bromley were without the services of one of their regular bowlers.
