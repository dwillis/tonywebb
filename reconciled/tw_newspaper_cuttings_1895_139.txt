WATERS PLACE v. POLES.
This match was played at Poles on Saturday in glorious weather. Waters Place won the toss, and batted first on a splendid wicket. The first two men were soon sent back, but on Pratt joining Perkins the bowling was mastered, and both men were well set when through a misunderstanding the latter was unfortunately run out for a useful 14. None of the other batsmen offered much resistance to the bowling, and when the last wicket fell the total was 62. Pratt played vigorously for his 30 runs. On Poles handling the willow they found run-getting rather difficult owing to the good bowling of the brothers Salmons and Perkins, and were all disposed of for 38 runs, Mr. Hanbury being the only one to reach double figures. The visitors' second innings was very similar to their first, Perkins again playing well. The total

reached 71, which left the homesters 95 to win in about
an hour, and although they made a great effort to win
time was called with the score standing at 64 for three
wickets. Richards played grandly for his 30 not out.
Waters Place thus won their last match of the season
by 24 runs on the first innings. A sumptuous tea
was kindly provided for the teams by Mr. Hanbury.

WARE TOWN CLUB v. WARE PARISH CHURCH CHOIR.
This match was played on Saturday and resulted in a
win for the Town. The Choir were considerably
weaker than was anticipated, owing to several members
being away for their holidays. Waller played well for
the Town and compiled a useful 26. The team was
eventually out for 58, which looked like a promising
total for the Choir to compete against, but against the
bowling of Pearce and Waller the stumps fell for less
than a dozen. Still, the afternoon was a pleasant one to

GREAT v. LITTLE MUNDEN.
On Wednesday a match was played between Great
and Little Munden at Libury by the invitation of Mr.
Jearrard, where a sumptuous spread was prepared for
the visitors and friends, who spent a very pleasant
afternoon. After the game finished votes of thanks
were recorded to Mr. and Mrs. Jearrard for their kind-
ness. The scoring was very low, viz., Great Munden,
1st innings, 6; 2nd innings, 43 (H. Kite 15, and G.
W. Cooper 13). Little Munden, 1st innings, 26;
2nd innings, 68 for 4 wickets (A. Sinclair 21, G.
Washington, not out, 10).
