CRICKET.
MR. SHEPHERD'S XI. beat WOOLLEY PARK.
This match was played at Woolley Park on Wednesday. Woolley going in first put together the total of 67, of which Hayden and Smith did best, with 17 and 14 respectively. Mr. Shepherd's team then went in the score at lunch time standing at 46 for 3 wickets, Hutchins and Weaver playing good cricket for their runs. The innings closed for 92, Woolley going in again put on 58. Mr. Shepherd's team now requiring 32 runs to win, obtained them with the loss of 3 wickets, Hutchins again doing well. Both teams were kindly entertained to tea after the match by Mr. Wroughton. Score:

INKPEN beat FALKLAND C.C.
The visitors to meet the Inkpen C.C. on their own ground were rather late in coming, the time fixed for the match being two o'clock, while the game did not commence till about four. The home team won the toss and play quickly began. The chances of winning looked rather small at one time, four wickets having fell for 10 runs. E. Bance soon turned the tide by adding 25 runs more in capital style, his score consisting of a splendid drive for 5, three 4's, and the rest singles. The Rev. H. D. Butler made a good hit for 4, and when the innings was closed carried out his bat. G. Butler and J. Hopkins did not bat. The visitors were dismissed for 29 runs, mainly owing to the first-rate bowling of T. May, who took 7 wickets, his average being 1.3. Score:-

LAMBOURN beat CHILTON.
The return match was played on Saturday last, on the Lambourn ground, and, as the score will show, resulted in a win for Lambourn, the homesters thus avenging their defeat in the previous match. The game did not commence till rather late, and consequently had to be decided on the first innings. The weather was delightful and the wicket really good. Chilton won the toss, but elected to send the home team in. A very poor start was made, as two Lambourn wickets went down for 0, but a wonderfully different complexion was put on the game, when, after H. Penfold had scored seven in good style, F. Harris
and J. Bryant got well set. F. Harris made some splendid hits, and the spectators loudly cheered his plucky play, his score of 35 being made in a very short time. J. Bryant played capital cricket for his 38, and was greatly applauded upon his retirement. V. Hobbs and C. J. Maberley rendered great assistance to the score, before the innings was declared closed at 129. The Chilton team did but little, only one player (S. Panting) obtaining double figures. The bowling of Messrs. Hobbs and Bryant was very effective, and F. Harris made two capital catches in the long field. Chilton followed on, and at the call of time no wicket had fallen, the score being 61, H. New and Rumball having scored well during the innings. Score :-

SOUTHINGTON beat MR. A. KINGSMILL'S XII.
This highly exciting and closely contested match was played in lovely weather on the charmingly situated Sydmonton Cricket Ground by youthful enthusiasts, the age being limited to 16, several, however, had many years still before them ere they reach this degree of experience. We would draw attention to the excellent bowling on the Southington side of Kneller and Poynter, and the batting of these and also Christie and North. The star of the Sydmonton team was J. Chute whose bowling was as energetic as his batting, and his prowess was rewarded by the presentation bat offered for the highest score by Mr. Wyndham Portal. Leyland and Harrison had some share in the downfall of the Southington wickets, aided by the smart wicket-keeping of Andrew Kingsmill, in spite of their strenuous exertions Southington proved successful by the narrow margin of seven runs. BURGHCLERE beat NEWTOWN.
Played on the Sandleford Priory ground and ended in a defeat for the home team. Burghclere won the toss and batted first; three wickets fell quickly, but on Rev. G. Hicks and G. Kimber getting together runs were piled on fast, Kimber being run out for a well-played 43, Rev. G. Hicks carrying out his bat for a brilliantly-hit innings of 56. Newtown made no stand whatever, being all dismissed for 41, Allsop alone getting double figures. This match brought the Newtown season to a close, after a fairly successful season, they having played 14 matches, won six and lost eight. After a very disastrous start they pulled up in their last fixtures, winning three out of their last five. Score :-

NEWTOWN beat PARISH CHURCH INSTITUTE.
This return match was played in the beautiful grounds of Sandleford Park, on Saturday, August 31st, there being a goodly number of spectators present, including Lady Butler and family and company. Newtown went first to the wickets, and by good play and hard hitting on the part of Fifield (22), and Gardiner (13), made a total of 43 runs, Gunning's bowling being too good for them, he being well backed up by Mathews and Fewtrell. On the Institute handling the willow, however, they were soon disposed of for 30 runs, Vickers being top scorer with 15. Gibbs, Fifield, Allsop, and Baverstock being the bowlers. Ten minutes previous to end Newtown again went to the wickets, but the Institute not being able to go in again, the home team won a pleasant game on the first innings by eight runs.
ADBURY HOUSE beat NEWTOWN HOUSE.
On Thursday afternoon a match was played between elevens of the Adbury House estate and Newtown House, the game being won by the Adbury House eleven, a result being mainly due to the good bowling of H. Barber, F. James, and J. Gasson; G. Cooper brought off a beautiful catch at short slip, and C. Berry made a big drive for five runs. For the losers M. Stratford, White, Street, and Waight acquitted themselves very well. A capital tea had been prepared by the housekeeper on the grounds, which the players did justice too. Altogether a most enjoyable afternoon was spent, and a hearty vote of thanks was carried with acclaim to Mr. Walter Arkwright for his kindness. Score:
