WEST HERTS AND WATFORD OBSERVER: FRIDAY 25 AUGUST 1939-5.

**Entertaining Batting**

STANMORE (7 wkts.) 162 .. LEAVESDEN (8 wkts., dec.) 203

THE BATTING in this match was always entertaining. Lambkin profited by his early caution while some of his partners, who hit out from the word "go," paid the penalty of exuberance. The model of batsmanship came from W. T. Palmer. His runs were scored all by clean, powerful shots to all parts of the field. Stanmore's main support was F. Richards, who again helped to retrieve fortunes after a bad start. Marsland was the only bowler to enjoy continued success for the wicket was always on the side of the batsmen. Score: —

★STARRED SELECTIONS

IN the ideal cricket weather that only the start of the football season can bring, few matches were brought to a definite result. Abbots Langley preserved their unbeaten record by the skin of their teeth, beating Chipperfield by eight runs, Hemel Hempstead won very comfortably, but West Herts, Bushey, Chorleywood, Stanmore, Leavesden and Kings Langley were among the teams that drew their fixtures.

Of the five centuries that were scored, Don Yates obtained two—hitting 108 not out on Saturday, and 100 not out on Sunday. Yet another Yates achievement.

THEIR REPUTATION NOT ENHANCED

Southern Railway (9 wkts., dec.) 160...West Herts (7 wkts.) 91

West Herts, one man short, did nothing in this match at Rayner's Park to enhance their reputation. Missed catches and poor fielding helped to swell their opponents' score to 160 for nine, when the closure was applied.

Replying, West Herts were given a good send off by Lane and Dedman, who put on 45, but apart from Kendrick's modest 19, the rest batted with caution. Score: —
