BEDFORDSHIRE TIMES: FRIDAY 25 AUGUST 1939-4.

VICTORY BY ONE RUN

Kempston's Struggle with Lynton Works

There was an exciting finish to the Lynton Works-Kempston game at Kempston on Saturday, the home side winning by one run. Both sides were below strength.

Lynton, batting first, lost three wickets for six runs. Scutchings then joined Everett and helped to put on 14 runs. Curchin (8) and Pestell (7) carried the score along. At this point Everett, who had opened, was out after a very steady innings. The remaining wickets quickly fell and Lynton's final score was 54. For Kempston, Curtis took five for 23 and Cambers three for 16.

Kempston opened with Tipton and Sid Smith. At 7 Smith was bowled by Spindler. Tipton followed at 10. Dodson and Draper put on 14, but the next two wickets fell very quickly. Then Draper and Cambers, by very careful batting, took the score to 46, when Draper (17), was out. Cambers now did all he could to get the bowling. J. Smith (1) and Godson (2) brought the score to 50. With two wickets to fall and nine runs needed, the match became exciting. Cambers, with R. Smith holding his end up, took the score to 59. With the winning hit made Cambers had a go and was very quickly out. Kempston thus won a very keen game by one run. The collection for Club funds came to 10s. Scores:—

THE GUILE OF VETERANS

Redman and Geary Play Well for Clarence

Excellent bowling by the veterans, Redman (five for 15) and G. Geary (three for 29), helped to give the Clarence a comfortable victory over the Beds. and Herts. Depot at Kempston Barracks on Saturday. Minns had two wickets for no runs, and the only batsman to offer serious resistance were Kendall and Buckley. A LATE STAND

Saves Flitwick Ramblers from Defeat

Markyate batted first when they visited Flitwick Ramblers on Saturday. They declared at tea-time with the score 143 for six wickets.

When the Ramblers batted J. Godfrey was bowled without a run being scored. Wickets fell very quickly to the fine bowling of A. Palmer and R. Cain, and eight men were out with only 36 runs scored. E. Bonner joined H. Gazeley and they made a stand. While Gazeley did most of the scoring Bonner kept his end up. Gazeley reached his half-century and just later the hundred went up. But with the third ball of the last over H. Gazeley was bowled. The Ramblers' captain's innings included eight fours and one six. His ninth-wicket partnership with E. Bonner realized 76 runs. J. Parker came in to play the last three balls, thus saving the Ramblers from defeat. Scores:—
