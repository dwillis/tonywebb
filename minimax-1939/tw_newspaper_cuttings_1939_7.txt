BEDFORDSHIRE TIMES: FRIDAY 11 AUGUST 1939-4.

SPORT OF THE WEEK
—————————
Wickets Down Like Nine-Pins — Eagles' New Players and Plans

After ruining outdoor sports engagements on Saturday, it looked as though rain was going to repeat its evil on Monday in the early part of the morning. The main sport fixture was the opening match of the County Cricket Week on the Bedford School playing field against Oxfordshire. The pitch was sodden after the torrential rain of Sunday, but by midday on Monday the weather brightened and a start was made on a newly-prepared crease to the left of the pavilion—away from the School, at about 1.40 p.m. The play that followed was in direct contrast to the weather, being bright and exhilarating to the Bedfordshire supporters. By winning by an innings and 89 runs Bedfordshire made a grand start to the Week. How they fared against Buckinghamshire will be seen by the report in another column.

**Familiar Faces Missing**

Oxfordshire's team seemed a strange one to many of us, writes A. R., for so many familiar faces were missing. Territorial camps and other claims had robbed the visitors of old stalwarts and the opportunity was taken of introducing some new blood. The newcomers had an unfortunate introduction to Bedford.

Although it was on the board that the wicket might play tricks later on it was a surprise to see Oxfordshire wickets going down like ninepins in the very early stages. The wicket was as sodden as a pudding and the bowlers had a job to gain a foothold. Yet there were Payne and Oliver having a real bank holiday. Payne bowled Money with the fourth ball of the first over with only one run on the board, and without addition to the score and in the same over the footballer [unclear] Parton lbw. Not to be outdone, Oliver sent back Lithgow in his first over. August taking a catch in the slips. Williams, who followed, survived one ball and had his wicket broken with the next from Oliver. Four for two runs, one run being from a no ball.

No big reason for the collapse could be found at this stage in the state of the pitch; it must have been due to the swing the bowlers could impart with the new ball.

**"Do-or-Die" Effort**

And so Oxfordshire's sorry plight went on. Two left-arm spin bowlers, Haywood and Owen, the latter bowling over the wicket, replaced the medium-fast attack of Payne and Oliver, and they, too, met with success, but not of such phenomenal order as the opening pair, the ball not turning fast enough to do justice to the wiles of the slow men. With eight wickets down for 28 Oxfordshire wanted a "do-or-die" run, and they had one in O'Shea. While Bowtell went on in a steady patient way O'Shea tried an aggressive bat. He had all the luck in the world for some time. Owen tied him up in knots without being able to get his wicket. Oliver and Markham had spells with the ball, but it was Haywood who broke the partnership, which put on 56 for the ninth wicket. The Three Counties Hospital player "spun" Bowtell out. The ball almost stood before it turned and made the Oxford man give the easiest of catches to Markham. He had scored a valuable 22. O'Shea went on hitting out and compiled 42 not out. It was through him and Bowtell that his side's total reached 85.

**Eloquent Testimony**

Payne had the best return of the home bowlers with three wickets for nine runs. Oliver having three for eighteen, and Haywood three for 23. These returns tell a story, but the fall of the wickets are more eloquent testimony of the way the game went—1 for 1, 2 for 1, 3 for 1, 4 for 2, 5 for 12, 6 for 12, 7 for 22, 8 for 28, 9 for 64, 10 for 85.

Bedfordshire made an exceedingly good start through August and Oliver. These two young players were steady and orthodox and were together until 64 had been scored Oliver was the first to go for 28. Owen came in next and made his bow to the Bedford public as a batsman. The spectators liked him for his enterprise and fine—sometimes almost impudence—running between the wickets. Owen saw to it that the score was advanced at a rapid rate and made the imperturbable August scurry. August would play in a Test match without turning a hair, for he has that temperament. The Old Bedfordian reached his fifty, but added only one more run before he was out. He played back too late to a ball which turned sharply. The score was 93 for two.

**The Brightest Partnership**

The brightest partnership of the day followed. Payne was promoted in the batting order so that the policy of going for the runs could be maintained. It was. The Chelsea footballer made 15 in double quick time, and, with Owen, treated us to an exhibition of quick running between the wickets. Owen sent up the hundred off a no-ball.

Winlaw followed Payne and the scoring rate dropped. The Bedfordshire captain took no risks and in a streaky innings he had a life when short-leg dropped a catch right into his hands. Owen left at 136 and Poole came in. A. B. at once hit out, but it was some minutes before he broke his "duck", for he could not get the ball through the well-placed field. Then Winlaw gave another catch to the man who had missed him, and this time Florey was not so generous and Winlaw was out for 12. Poole got going nicely and collected his runs at a rapid pace. He and John were together at the close of play when the total stood at 193 for five and Poole's score at 39. The wicket was badly worn.

**Last Five Wickets Soon Fall**

Poole and John continued confidently on Tuesday morning, and it was a surprise, when, at 199, Poole's off stump was knocked back by Bowtell. With an off-drive Browning sent the two hundred up after the home County had been batting about three-and-a-half hours.

O'Shea came on with the new ball at Bowtell's end. Runs came at a good pace, John being particularly strong on the on side, but Browning seemed a little uncertain when facing Bowtell, who had taken up the attack at the School end, and at 213 a leg break hit the Bedford County master's off peg. The soft wicket was obviously to Bowtell's liking. Markham shaped stylishly for a few minutes, but with six runs added this young batsman went forward to Bowtell and the stumps were spreadeagled. Haywood succeeded first ball. John and Page took the score to 253, at which total the innings closed. Five Bedfordshire wickets had gone in fifty minutes for the addition of sixty runs. Bowtell's figures on Tuesday were four wickets for 10 runs. All told he took six for 64.

**Another Oxfordshire Decade**

Faced with the considerable task of getting 141 runs to avoid an innings defeat, Oxfordshire opened with Lithgow and [unclear] to the bowling of Payne, Lithgow and Oliver. With the last ball of the first over Oliver induced Lithgow to step across and the batsman was out lbw, one run having been scored. Payne had to have had Pete taken in the slips, and then with the score at two Oliver sent a fine ball through Pete's defence. The total stood at three when the third wicket fell, a ball from Payne taking Parton's bails. Frequently deliveries from both bowlers reared disconcertingly and Money and Bowtell were anything but comfortable. Twice Bowtell almost played on to Payne.

At [unclear] Oliver had Money caught at [unclear], the Luton bowler having [unclear] on three wickets for six runs in three [unclear] and two balls. The first aggressive [unclear] of the innings was a four to leg by Storey off Payne. Florey decided that an attacking policy was the best in the circumstances and he took thirteen runs off an over by the Chelsea footballer, who was now bowling round the wicket. Florey went on collecting runs by attractive strokes, and he and Bowtell had added 27 when the latter was caught at the wicket.

**All Over**

Markham relieved Payne, and Williams was lbw to his second ball (37-6-1). A 49 Florey's free-scoring venture came to an end; Haywood, who had gone on for Oliver, had him caught behind the wicket. He hit four boundaries. Page was in his best "appealing" form, at 54 the umpire's finger went up in response to another inquiry from the diminutive stumper, and out went Nunn. Carr tried to hit Haywood for six and was bowled, the score being 59. Oliver came into the attack again and immediately took the last wicket.

The game was over shortly before lunch, and Bedfordshire had won by an innings and 89 runs.

Oliver's exceptional bowling figure were: 6.2 overs, 2 maidens, 8 runs, 3 wickets. Haywood had three wickets for eight runs in three overs. Markham took one for 14, and Payne one for 28

**Against Suffolk**

Bedfordshire's programme next week opens with a match against Suffolk on the Vauxhall Motors ground, Luton, on Monday and Tuesday. This is a new fixture, and a return match will be played at Felixstowe in the following week.

Bedfordshire's team will be the same as the one that met Buckinghamshire this week, with the exception of Payne, whose place is to be taken by H. Radice, a Bedford boy who is at Marlborough School. Radice took four wickets for 43 runs against Rugby a few days ago. Suffolk's side is to include the former Surrey professional, Jack Daley, spin bowler. The team will be: H. P. Waugh (capt.), H. L. Porter, B. H. Belle, A. G. Powell (wicketkeeper), G. W. Hockey, J. E. Forrest, R. A. Henley, D. T. Birks, Mead P., Perkins G. C., and Daley J. V.

**Hospital Cup Final**

In common with nearly all cricket on Saturday, the County Hospital Cup final between Marston and Shire Hall Seconds was wiped out by rain.

It has been decided to play the final on the Bedford Modern School ground on Saturday, 9th September. Apparently the teams are waiting for the sunshine.
