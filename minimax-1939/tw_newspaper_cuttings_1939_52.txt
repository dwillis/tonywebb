WEST HERTS AND WATFORD OBSERVER: FRIDAY 4 AUGUST 1939-2

★ STARRED SELECTIONS

BATSMEN took a back seat and bowlers blossomed into brilliance in club cricket during the week-end. In contrast to recent weeks not a single century was scored, although F. Richards, of Stanmore, came precariously near to that distinction with a well hit 95. These runs were gathered at the expense of the Ickenham attack. Another Stanmore player, K. H. Chapman, against Radlett, put up about the deadliest turn of wicket destroying bowling seen on the Common for some time. Taking a wicket with the first ball of the match, he bowled for five overs without conceding a run, and secured five victims. His final analysis was:

         O.    M.    R.    W.
         8     6     8     7

Attackers in the game between Watford and Scammells did pretty well as much as they liked. They had two innings apiece and no fewer than twenty-two "ducks" are set out on the score sheets. Poultry-farming at Sedgwick's Meadow!

WEST HERTS CHECKED AFTER GOOD START

WEST HERTS (9 wkts. dec.) 177 .. NORTH LONDON (5 wkts.) 102

NEITHER side could claim definite superiority when stumps were drawn, North London faring much better on the arrival of S. Machray. Until then West Herts were on top with three down for 16 and four back for 64.

But for Machray's effort Hawes (three for 14) might have run through the side.

West Herts' middle men provided the side with most of their runs. Mitchell, Foxen, Kendrick, and Stimpson sharing the honours. Mitchell missed his half-century by three, and in doing so hit a six and seven fours. Grimsdell, after the loss of Burgess for two and following Lane's departure at 15, pulled the home team out of a discouraging start.

His steadiness after the uncertain start helped to lay the foundation of a very useful score. Score:

HEMPSTEAD'S DOGGED DRAW

HEMEL HEMPSTEAD 115 (for seven) .. LEIGHTON BUZZARD 174 (for seven dec.)

DECLARING at 174 for seven wickets, Leighton Buzzard appeared to be in a strong position when they met Hemel Hempstead on the Heath Park pitch on Saturday, but the home side offered determined resistance and had lost only seven wickets when stumps were drawn at 7.30 p.m. with 115 runs on the board.

Although Wildman, with 56, gave the visitors a useful start, Leighton Buzzard would not have fared too well had it not been for A. B. Poole, who contributed 89 not out. Missed early in his innings, he went on to bat confidently and scored 15 fours and one six. Hemel Hempstead started poorly, and only 65 runs were on the board when the sixth wicket fell. For their successful reply to Leighton Buzzard's score, they were chiefly indebted to H. H. Grange, who batted No. 2. and scored 51 runs without being defeated. His innings included three fours and one six. He was partnered by Gilkes when the time limit was reached, with the result that the match was left drawn. Score:

WATFORD CO-OP. MEET THEIR WATERLOO

Watford Co-op. 108 .. London Co-op. (7 wkts.). 291

This semi-final tie resulted in a heavy defeat for Watford. London Co-op. winning by 183 runs. For Watford, A. Smith batted patiently and well for 39 against a varied attack from the London side (selected from 18 teams of their own league).

The excellent London batsmen made the Watford attack look quite commonplace, all bowlers being treated alike. Linsell's 100 was an excellent effort, and the Watford total was passed with only two wickets down. Score:
