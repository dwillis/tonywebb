West Herts and Watford Observer: Friday 25 August 1939 - 4.

**THEY REMAIN UNSHAKEN**
'ABBOTS LANGLEY (5 wkts.) 157.......HERTS RAMBLERS 154

HERTS RAMBLERS paid Abbots Langley a compliment on Sunday by including in their side players who are outstanding in their respective clubs. Their attack included Watson and Ward, of Leavesden Hospital, Burgess and Kendrick, of West Herts, Edmonds of Kings Langley, and Brandon, the Hemel Hempstead skipper.

With Watson immediately operating, Abbots Langley, facing a total of 154, lost three wickets for 20, before Jones and A. W. Briers became associated in a stand which turned the game against the visitors. Briers received valuable help from B. Crownshaw, and the winning hit came in the last over but one. Against such powerful opposition victory by five wickets was no mean achievement, and it stamps the unbeaten Abbots Langley as the outstanding local team of the season. Briers had 7 fours and one 6 in his 74, which took him 98 minutes. Best home bowler was R. Crownshaw, with 4 for 51. Score:

**Useful Team to Test Herts!**

Denis Compton, the England and Middlesex cricketer, is bringing an International team to meet a Herts XI. at the Old Albanians ground, St. Albans, on Sunday, September 3, for Ted Roberts' benefit fund. Compton will captain a side which will be selected from the following: J. M. Sims, W. J. Edrich and F. Price (England and Middlesex), J. Hardstaff (England and Notts), W. E. Bowes (England and Yorkshire), R. P. Nelson (Northants), K. James (New Zealand and Northants), J. Hulme, J. Robertson, J. Smith, L. H. Gray, A. Fowler, L. Compton, G. E. Hart and S. M. Brown (all of Middlesex).

The Herts team will be selected from: P. G. T. Kingsley, C. B. Fordham, E. Roberts, A. Grimsdell, F. Appleyard, M. A. Wiggs, W. Lismore, L. E. McLean, A. S. Tee, F. K. Butler, C. W. Ford, J. Adams and T. Watson.

Frank Chester and D. Tregear will be the umpires, and play commences at 2 p.m.

**Australians Went in Singles to Victory**
Rickmansworth 127...London Australians 171

Rickmansworth entertained the London Australians (who last year called themselves Australia House) on Sunday, and lost by 44 runs. The home side batted first and scored only 127 on a good hard wicket. Shepherd and Odams were the best performers in the Rickmansworth side. At tea, London Australians had lost five wickets for 63 and the game appeared to be very even, but Hall and Scrivener dug themselves in and scored in singles until well set, took the score to 140 before Scrivener was bowled for 31.
