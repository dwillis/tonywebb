WESTERN DAILY PRESS: MONDAY 21 AUGUST 1939 - 1.

CRICKET MATCHES

**Fine Century by R. C. Robinson for Flax Bourton**

RUNS were fairly plentiful in local cricket, in Bristol and district, on Saturday, but in some cases bowlers returned excellent figures.

In the match at Golden Hill between Y.M.C.A. and Bedminster, which was drawn, over 400 runs were scored. When stumps were drawn, the Y.M.C.A. wanted eight runs to win, having made 198 for seven wickets, declared, in response to Bedminster's 205 runs for seven wickets, declared.

Bedminster took first knock and were indebted chiefly to L. Champeny (53), and R. J. Simons (42 not out). The Y.M.C.A. had an hour and threequarters in which to get the runs, and collected the first 100 in just under the hour. H. Rowlands was in fine form with 54 not out, while C. J. Axe (45), D. C. Johnson (33), and V. R. Davies (25), batted exceptionally well.

A fine century by Rupert C. Robinson, who hit up 101 not out enabled Flax Bourton to declare at 210 runs for five wickets, and was largely instrumental in his side's overwhelming victory against Stoke Bishop.

Stoke Bishop were tumbled out for 43 runs, by J. Lansberry, who came through with the remarkable analysis of seven wickets for 11 runs.

**STINCHCOMBE BEATEN**

United Banks journeyed to Stinchcombe, and easily accounted for the home team by eight wickets. Stinchcombe went in first, but were dismissed for 38 runs, G. Crossman being top scorer with 22. The damage was done by A. C. Tanner, who took seven wickets for 35 runs.

United Banks lost two wickets for 14 runs, but with M. Arnold and Tanner together Stinchcombe's total was passed. Tanner had a wonderful match, following up his splendid bowling with a great halfcentury.

There was an intensely thrilling finish to the match between Bridgwater and Imperial at Bridgwater, the home team winning by four wickets off the last ball of the game. Imperial batted first and declared at 181 for four wickets, thanks chiefly to A. H. M. Derrick (52), W. H. Rossiter (45) and N. R. Poole (36 not out).

Bridgwater started steadily, P. Thorn and D. Norton scoring 31 and 25 runs respectively, and then came a hurricane innings by A. M. Pearce of 67 runs in 28 minutes, including 11 fours and a six. While Pearce and K. Cotham (31) were together 83 runs were added.

A. H. M. DERRICK.

Schoolmasters scored a fine victory over Taunton, on the latter's ground, by six wickets. Taunton went in first and made a strong start, S. Wigginton making 21 and H. Pear, 34. Taunton declared at 129 runs for nine wickets.

**GAME ENDS IN TIE**

Schoolmasters got off the mark with a grand opening partnership and secured their runs easily. S. G. Wills played a fine innings of 90 and A. R. Curtis scored 47.

It is not often that a tie occurs, but such was the result at Coombe Dingle, in the match between Bohemians and Frys.

Frys batted first, and thanks to A. Woolacott (64, not out), and L. H. Carter (36), they totalled 130. Then the Bohemians made a poor start, losing two wickets for a few runs, but A. Bull (21), C. R. Lucas (38), and H. J. Alpass (31), pulled things round. However, the bowlers got on top again, and with the last pair together it was with the Amid great excitement that R. F. P. Holloway levelled the scores, but in attempting the winning run, the last man was run out, and the game ended at 130 runs apiece. P. W. Setchell, and R. D. French took four wickets for 28 runs and three for 21 respectively, for Bohemians.

Stapleton went over to Long Ashton and inflicted a heavy defeat on the Somerset side, by nine wickets. H. N. Curwen bowled so well for Stapleton, that Long Ashton were all out for 115. Curwen taking six wickets for 35 runs.

**CLIFTON WELL BEATEN**

Stapleton were never in any sort of trouble with the bat, and passed their opponents' score for the loss of only one wicket. F. Weeks was undefeated for a brilliant 57.

Making his first appearance this season, for Westbury, E. H. Staddon scored 20 not out, and took four wickets for 22, and so helped materially in his side's easy victory over Clifton, at Henbury. Westbury took first knock, and totalled 146.

Clifton cut a sorry figure before the bowling of Staddon and R. C. Phelps, and were tumbled out for 74, of which J. W. Burrough contributed 46!

The Optimists beat Claverham by 53 runs at Blaise Castle. C. F. Bolt scored 43 for the Optimists, out of a total of 115, and in reply Claverham could only total 62 runs.

In a benefit match for George Hunt, the Weston-s.-Mare groundsman and player, Weston beat Chard by 88 runs. Consistent batting by Weston enabled them to declare at 191 runs for five wickets, G. Hupt scoring 37, and W. W. Birtill 58. L. C. Hawkins and Hunt bowled so consistently, getting six and four wickets respectively, that Chard were dismissed for 103 of which H. F. Golledge made 32
