BEDFORDSHIRE TIMES : FRIDAY 18 AUGUST 1939-3.

BEDFORD BOWLERS PUNISHED

Weakened Side Trounced at Peterborough

A much-depleted Bedford team travelled to Peterborough on Saturday and were heavily beaten. Among the absentees were Browning, Markham, Bailey, Rawlins, Percy, Rogers, and Morris, and it was decided not to weaken the second eleven by calling on their regular players. The Peterborough team included A. W. Snowden, the Northamptonshire batsman, who scored a brilliant 96 before he was bowled by Meldrum, a newcomer to the Bedford team. Kenney, another new player in the side, showed his worth by scoring an extremely useful 27.

Peterborough batted first on a soft wicket. Snowden and Collins were soon complete masters of the bowling, and 94 runs were on the board before Collins was brilliantly caught by Howard at short leg. Powdrill soon followed him to the pavilion, but Snowden, Lane, Patching, and Laxton took the score to 205 for five wickets before Peterborough declared.

Bedford were soon in difficulties, for Audley was bowled with only five runs on the board, but Kenney and Dodd added 40 runs for the next wicket. The highlights of the Bedford knock were fine sixes by Howard and Dexter. Howard was unlucky to be caught on the boundary in attempting to lift a ball out of the ground. Dexter was dismissed by the next ball, which ended the over, and Ferriman was clean-bowled by the first ball of the following over. There was a complete collapse, and the last six Bedford wickets fell for 15 runs, giving Peterborough the victory by 103 runs. Scores:—

CHEAP WICKETS

Peterborough 2nds Defeated at Bedford

Bedford Town second team scored another comfortable victory on Saturday, easily defeating the Peterborough second eleven at Newnham Lane. Peterborough batted first and, thanks to some fine bowling by P. Humphreys, they were dismissed for the low total of 74. Humphreys captured seven wickets for 38 runs in eight and a half overs. Last week he took six wickets for seven runs against Royston. The last six Peterborough batsmen added only four runs to the total.

The Town second team began very steadily. Stokes soon mastered the Peterborough bowling, and scoring freely all round the wicket, made a brilliant 66 before being caught in the deep. The Peterborough total was passed with seven wickets in hand, but it was decided to bat on, and when stumps were drawn the Bedford score stood at 121 for seven wickets. The scores were:—

STEWARTBY LOSE

St. Neots Declare and Win

St. Neots were away to Stewartby on Saturday, a new fixture, and came away winners by 63 runs, after declaring. St. Neots did not start their innings well, losing the wickets of Owen and Bunnage with only 11 on the board. Craig and Jones batted well, however, and put on 47 for the third wicket before Jones was bowled after making a sound 24, his highest for St. Neots first eleven. After this, four St. Neots wickets fell quickly, but Sillett joined Craig, and this pair added 64 for the eighth wicket. Craig made 67 before being bowled. He batted with great steadiness. Sillett also made his highest score of the season and his 31 included some excellent drives. The St. Neots innings was declared closed at 154 for nine.

Stewartby made an excellent start in their reply, Tysoe and Hudson putting on 55 before being parted, but after the opening partnership was broken the rest of the batsmen collapsed and it was a question as to whether St. Neots would beat the clock and dismiss Stewartby before 7.30, the time fixed for the close of play. With two overs to go St. Neots had still to get rid of two remaining London Brick Company batsmen, but they managed to do it with three balls to spare. Craig, by taking five for 20, completed a good all-round performance for St. Neots. Scores:—

CLOPHILL'S SUCCESS AT HENLOW

Henlow seconds were beaten at home by Clophill on Saturday. Batting first, they scored 144, Horwood making 30, Mabbitt 27, and Payne 26. Clophill replied with 183 for eight wickets, of which Shotbolt hit a lusty 61, including eleven fours and a six, Taylor 35, Burnage 34, and Nicholls 21, also batted well. Mabbitt was Henlow's most successful bowler, capturing four wickets, including the hat-trick. R. Legate, a youngster with a future, took three for 17. For Clophill, Mann took three for 26, Billington three for 33, and Burnage two for 34.
