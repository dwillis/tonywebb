THE CHRONICLE FRIDAY AUGUST 4 1939

CRICKET
MINOR COUNTIES
CHAMPIONSHIP
BERKS DRAW WITH HERTS

BERKSHIRE opened their Minor Counties programme on Monday, when Hertfordshire were the visitors to the Kensington Road ground at Reading. It was Hertfordshire's fifth match, however, they having defeated Bucks outright, winning two and losing two of the remaining games on the first innings. Winning the toss, Hertfordshire batted first, but runs came very slowly at the start. It was not until McLean came in that there was any really bright batting. McLean, in fact, took less than a third of the time to compile his 29 than Roberts took for 31. Berkshire bowlers met with little success until Krabbe came on. Although hit about in his first over, he had Roberts caught on the leg side. The score gradually mounted, the best stand being that for the fifth wicket, Kempster and Ford putting on fifty runs. The score would not have been so great had the Berkshire fielders held somewhat easy chances. Ford, who compiled forty, was missed by Bradley off Cawston when only nine, and Wiggs was dropped by Lewis off Relf. It was not until Cawston came on that wickets began to fall with any regularity, and his final analysis was five for 42. Berkshire were greatly assisted by Stokes, who, playing behind the stumps, caught three and stumped one.

Cawston and Timewell opened the Berkshire innings, but the former was never really comfortable. It seemed a mistake to put a hitter in so early, especially as he had just completed bowling 19 consecutive overs. After having a snick at one or two balls which were well off the wicket, Cawston was caught behind the stumps. Stokes was getting going when Roberts came on with his spinners. Missing one of these, the Berkshire captain was well out of his crease when Grimsdell put down his wicket. A smart catch at mid-on disposed of Grisied, and Timewell went next, caught at silly mid-on in trying to hook a short ball to leg. Krabbe was, in fact, the only Berkshire batsman really to get going, and he contributed a valuable 42 before he was out to what was a sheer piece of bad luck. He put the ball round to the leg boundary, but in doing so stepped into his own wicket. The best assistance he received came from Girling, who bats left-handed but bowls right-handed. The innings closed with Berkshire being 43 runs in arrears.

An Expensive Miss

This left Hertfordshire with ten minutes to bat before close of play, and Roberts and Tee, although failing to score, played out time. This ten minutes was not without its shocks, however, for Tee was narrowly missed at fine leg. On Tuesday morning Berkshire met with early success, for Relf had Tee l.b.w. in his second over. Roberts and Fordham, however, carried the score to 65 before they were separated. Timewell should have held a lofty catch from Kempster off Girling when the Herts man had only compiled five runs. He went on to score all round the wicket. Girling clean bowled McLean, who had been responsible for some hard hitting in the first innings, and Ford was well run out, Wolton hitting the stumps with a throw-in from the pavilion sight screen. After this Kempster and Ledger remained together to put on 114 runs, Kempster, missed at five, getting a total of 82 before being clean bowled by Cawston. His innings included two sixes—both off Krabbe—and eight fours. Ledger did not stay long after him, succumbing to a brilliant catch by Krabbe, who ran in yards from the boundary to hold the ball almost as it reached the ground. Grimsdell declared the innings closed at three o'clock, leaving Berkshire with three and a quarter hours—should the extra half-hour be demanded—to get 310 runs to win, an almost impossible task.

Berkshire were not allowed to set about their task in too vigorous a fashion, the bowling being too good to allow them to take any liberties. Lewis and Timewell had been in half-an-hour for 19 when the latter was caught at the wicket. At four o'clock ten minutes heavy rain rendered the pitch water-logged and at 5.30 p.m. the umpires decided that further play was impossible. The match was therefore drawn with Herts taking the points on the first innings.
