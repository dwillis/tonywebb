BEDFORDSHIRE TIMES: FRIDAY 4 AUGUST 1939-4.

BEDFORD GO FOR THE RUNS

Another Good Win Away from Home

Bedford Town scored another first-class victory when they defeated Harrow Town by four wickets at Harrow on Saturday. Bedford were considerably weakened by the absence of Bailey, Chas. and Phil Rawlins, and Percy, but once again their batsmen rose to the occasion and scored 216 for six wickets in a little under two hours.

Harrow batted first and scored 205 runs for nine wickets in 125 minutes, Browning (three for 62) and Kenney (two for 19) being the most successful bowlers.

Bedford were given two hours to bat in a failing light, but they were soon on top of the bowling. Browning and Markham scored 53 for the first wicket, Browning hitting seven 4's in a knock of 35. Markham began steadily, but when seeing the ball, hit nine 4's in succession before he was caught in the deep. A sixth-wicket stand between Gompertz and Rogers added 77 to the score, and the Harrow total was passed in the last over. Scores:

ASPLEY GUISE'S BIG SCORE

On an easy wicket at Luton Aspley Guise batsmen on Saturday recorded their highest score so far this season—251 for six. They were playing against Barclays Bank. Two wickets were down for 28 when Barnwell and Bowron began a splendid partnership which added 142 before Barnwell was bowled when only nine short of his century. He gave a fine display of forceful and stylish batting, including fifteen fours. His partner, a newcomer to the Aspley team, was playing in his third match. He was undefeated with 104 when the innings was declared closed at 251 for six. B. C. Barnwell followed up his batting success by a good performance with the ball, taking seven wickets for 44 runs in ten overs. Aspley were easy winners. Scores:-

Aspley Guise: H. W. Mayne 8, B. C. Barnwell 91, H. A. Price 2, R. Bowron not out 104, W. King 8, J. J. Blofeld 0, J. L. Royden 34, C. M. Stone not out 1, extras 3, total 251 for six wickets.

SEVEN TIMES CHAMPIONS

On Friday evening in Bedford Park Goldington Road School, seven times champions of the Bedford Elementary Schools Cricket League, achieved what no other champion side has achieved; they beat the Rest, thanks in the main to a fine captain's knock by K. Hullatt, who reached the boundary five times in one over.

Mr. J. Arnold Whitchurch afterwards presented to the champions the shield that he gave to the League when it was instituted eighteen years ago. This fact was commented upon by a former Goldington Road School master, Mr. A. J. Mayhew, and Mr. H. R. Neale spoke in praise of the cricket played by the boys.

The Rest declared at 71 for six and left Goldington Road an hour in which to get the runs. Harlow and Webster gave them a fine start, and then Hullatt arrived to hit the Rest bowling vigorously and give the champions a win by six wickets.

Joe Payne, the Chelsea forward and Bedfordshire County cricketer, had a field day for Luton against the R.A.F. (Henlow) last week, taking five wickets for 29 runs and scoring 61 not out. He hit a six and eight fours. R.A.F. gave a splendid exhibition of fielding. Scores: R.A.F. Henlow 85, Luton Town Wednesday 169 for six wickets.

HOSPITAL CUP FINAL

The final of the Hospital Cup competition is to be played on the Bedford Modern School cricket ground to-morrow. The finalists will be Marston and the Shire Hall 2nd XI. Mr. J. Arnold Whitchurch will present the cup and medals.
