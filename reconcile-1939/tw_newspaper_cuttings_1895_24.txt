THE CHRONICLE FRIDAY AUGUST 18 1939

MINOR COUNTIES CRICKET
BERKSHIRE MISS THEIR CHANCE
MISTAKEN TACTICS AT HIGH WYCOMBE

At 11 a.m. last Saturday Berkshire had victory within their grasp at High Wycombe in their Minor Counties match with Bucks. Until then the game was open, for on Friday both sides had gone through an innings with Bucks leading by 13. For the last half-hour that evening Berkshire scored 11 runs for the loss of one wicket. Heavy rain had made the pitch difficult, but by Saturday morning it had dried considerably, and when they went into bat, Berkshire had everything to gain by going for the bowling. Instead, they were content to "nibble" at it, with the result that wickets began to tumble like ninepins.

Sayles had marching orders when the score was only 14, and Stokes left before he had scored. Sayles got out through an in-swinger from Battcock, and Stokes was l.b.w. to the same bowler. Sixteen for three was a poor start, but Hubble rose to the occasion and started to hit out. Partnered by Crisfield, these two sent the score up and up, but after Crisfield had gone, Caudle was out for 9, and the rot had set in. The innings closed at 103.

With 91 runs to get to win, Bucks sent their opening batsmen in on an easy wicket, although the ball turned rather sharply at times. However, Franklin was out for 2, and Rachliffe and Baker put up a stubborn resistance until 29. Peters stayed long enough to make the Berkshire bowling look easy, and after lunch things began to happen. Bucks had to fight every inch of the way to get runs, and until Battcock arrived the game was in the balance. However, he settled the issue at shortly before 2.30 to give Bucks the win when he hit a four to square leg.

New County Cricket Caps

Two Berkshire County cricketers, O. P. F. Sutton and R. T. Dedman, were awarded their County caps this week for their general performances this season, and particularly for their display against Dorset at Dorchester.

Owen Sutton went to school at Harrow, but, although he played for his house, he did not get his place in the school team. When he left school he forsook cricket for tennis. He was living in Norfolk for a time, and on returning to Reading he took up cricket again, playing for the Reading Cricket Club Sunday eleven and for Sutton's. He had not played for Berkshire prior to this season. He is a keen golfer and is a member of the Reading and Sonning Clubs.

R. T. Dedman is A.R.P. sergeant in the Berkshire Police Force at Newbury. He was an enthusiastic cricketer as a schoolboy, and on leaving school became groundsman to the Alton Club. At one time he received an invitation to join the Hampshire County Club, but this he declined. He has been a member of the Berkshire County Police since 1928.


MINOR COUNTIES CRICKET
HEAVY DEFEAT FOR BERKSHIRE
SUTTON'S USEFUL 65

Excellent fielding and splendid length bowling by Relf and Dedman characterised the early stages of the game at Dorchester on Monday and Tuesday, when Berkshire were defeated by Dorset by 161 runs. Dorset's first three players were out for only 24 runs, when Carnegie-Brown and Taylor made a splendid stand at a time when the game was in a most critical position.

Soon after lunch on the first day, however, Carnegie-Brown was bowled by Hubble, and Tovey followed him to the pavilion two overs later.

When Berkshire opened their innings, the wicket seemed still to retain plenty of run-getting propensities, but Foot and Badham kept the Berks batsmen rather subdued, and only 19 runs had been scored after 30 minutes play. Two hours after the tea interval Berks were all out for 134, giving Dorset a first innings lead of 81.

In the second innings there was no holding Dorset, and they declared with the score at 278 for 5. Sutton was the mainstay of the Berks second innings, scoring 65 before hitting his own wicket. Berkshire, however, were all out for 198, to lose by 161 runs.
