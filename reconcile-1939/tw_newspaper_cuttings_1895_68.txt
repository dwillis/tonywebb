WEST HERTS AND WATFORD OBSERVER: FRIDAY 25 AUGUST 1939-3.

Mills Collects Welwyn Wickets
KINGS LANGLEY (6 wkts.) 128...WELWYN GARDEN CITY (7 wkts. dec.) 177
KINGS LANGLEY'S journey to Digswell Park on Saturday resulted in a drawn game. Batting first Welwyn put together a score of 177 for seven wickets, an excellent second wicket partnership between Carter and Pakes putting on 97 runs. With the first ball of his second spell Mills bowled Carter, and in the course of a further five overs dismissed six of the Welwyn side for 26 runs, giving him a final analysis of six for 57.
Bennett returned to form with the bat for Kings Langley, scoring 57 runs by good cricket, and received sound support from Townley and Foster. The Welwyn attack, however, was too accurate to permit of fast scoring, and at close of play the visitors with four wickets in hand still required 50 runs to win. Score:

Thrilling Finish at Abbots Langley
Abbots Langley 133 .. Chipperfield 125
The visit of Chipperfield to Abbots Langley on Saturday provided the most thrilling game seen on the Manor Ground this season, for with only two wickets to fall the home side required 17 runs to win, and these were obtained without further loss, although at the finish only 8 runs separated the two scores.
For Chipperfield only R. Smith and Joiner batted with confidence, the former scoring an attractive 67 in a total of 125. R. Crownshaw again bowled well in taking five wickets for 35, and Glenister, too, had the fine analysis of four wickets for 22 runs. Abbots Langley seemed well set for victory after scoring 84 for three wickets, Jones playing a really fine innings of 53, but eight wickets were down for 109, and until Arnott and P. Briers became associated, the home record of freedom from defeat this season seemed lost. Goodman bowled exceptionally well for Chipperfield, taking five wickets for 35 in 17 overs. Score:

BOWLERS CREATE BATSMEN'S PANIC
West Herts II 88 .. Harefield Sanatorium 59
Finishing an hour before its time, this game remarkable for its low scores on a wicket full of runs, developed into a battle of wits between a slow left-hand bowler on both sides and non offensive batsmen, with the exception of Connolly's bright this-is-the-way-to-it innings. Thanks to Husselbee and Wood the visitors had only quite a moderate total to achieve for victory, but with Deane's superb spell of bowling and the support of Woolf, and keen hands in the field, the home side went to victory by 29 runs. A short, sweet, but enjoyable treat. Score:
