# BEDFORDSHIRE TIMES: FRIDAY 4 AUGUST 1939-2.

# SPORT OF THE WEEK

# FACILITIES FOR RECREATION — CRICKET WEEK — SIME BACK — BRIGHT BEDFORD

## All Set

All is set for the County Cricket Week at Bedford next week, and given good weather there should be capital sport. I dealt fully with prospects and personnel of teams last week, so I shall content myself, and you, with repeating the fixtures and teams. All three matches will be played on the Bedford School field.

Monday and Tuesday, v. Oxfordshire: R. de W. K. Winlaw, A. B. Poole, G. L. B. August, J. A. R. Oliver E. Page, N. E. Browning, A. Haywood, J. Payne, J. G. Owen, D. John, and E. D. Markham; twelfth man, D. B. Coleman.

Wednesday and Thursday, v. Buckinghamshire; Friday and Saturday, v. Hertfordshire; R. de W. K. Winlaw, A. B. Poole, G. L. B. August, J. A. R. Oliver, A. W. Winlaw, N. E. Browning, A. Haywood, J. Payne, J. G. Owen, D. John, and E. D. Markham; twelfth man, D. B. Coleman. Hours of play are: first day, 11:30 a.m. to 7 p.m.; second day, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m.

It is interesting to note that Roy Crompton, the son of the County Secretary, Mr. Frank Crompton, has been awarded the Bedford School fielding prize presented by the County captain.

## Arnold Sime's Good Form

People who watched Arnold Sime's nine batting for the Old Bedfordians against Bedford School on Friday and Saturday must have regretted that he is no longer available for the County. When runs have to be got in a hurry Sime is the man for the occasion, and his forcing of the pace in the Old Bedfordians' second innings will long be remembered. The position was this: the Old Bedfordians compiled 314 (Sime 77) in their first knock and got the School out for 187. The School were not dismayed and, following on, scored 203 for nine wickets, leaving the Old Bedfordians with 77 needed for victory. Normally, this would have been an easy task for a side possessing such batting talent as the Old Bedfordians, even in the fourth innings, but time was the important factor. Only thirty-five minutes remained for play.

## A Sporting Decision

Doubleday, the School captain, could easily have let his side bat on for a draw, and could hardly have been criticized had he done so, but he unexpectedly declared. This very sporting decision caused quite a stir and put new life into the game. Sime and Oakley hurried out and they proceeded to score regularly off good-length bowling. It was not so much big hitting, but the process of taking cheeky singles and making ones into twos and twos into threes that kept the scorers busy. Both men played nicely-judged strokes and ran intelligently, but it was obvious that Sime was the brains of this partnership.

## Captain's Good Service

So much for the people who think that school cricket is dull! Doubleday, the Bedford School captain, may not be a particularly attractive batsman to watch, but he has done yeoman service and has not lacked enterprise in connexion with his leadership. The most successful bowler of the match was (as it he had not already done enough with the bat) Sime. A. E. Cook had the same number of wickets to his credit, but had more runs scored off him. A word or two of commiseration for Harrington, the School wicketkeeper, who had to be replaced because he broke a finger in the early play on Friday. It is wretched luck to miss a game so eagerly awaited.

## Bedford's Enterprise

The Bedford Town first eleven (minus four of their regular players) enhanced their reputation for bright cricket still more when they visited Harrow Town on Saturday. Bedford were late in arriving owing to car trouble which involved nearly half the team, and only four hours' play was possible. However, in these four hours the spectators were treated to some of the most sparkling cricket they have seen this season. Harrow scored freely at the rate of a hundred runs an hour and compiled a very useful 205 for nine wickets.

This gave Bedford about two hours of batting in a failing light, but right from the start they went out for runs and were soon on top of the Harrow bowling. E. D. Markham, the young ali-rounder, hit nine fours in succession, but when he was dismissed Bedford were still 96 runs behind and only threequarters of an hour was left for play. Then Rogers and Gompertz came together and by taking every possible scoring chance they sent the score along rapidly and the Harrow total was passed in the last over, after only 115 minutes' batting.

These Bedford games are certainly worth watching. If you haven't been to the Newnham Avenue ground on a Saturday when the first eleven are at home do so at the earliest opportunity. Look at the fixture list published in our columns each week.

## Martlets' Tour

The Bedford Marlets Cricket Club left Bedford this week for their fifth annual tour of Kent. The seventeen members who make up the party have their headquarters at East Farleigh, Maidstone, and the teams to be played are in a thirty-five mile radius of this place. The tour opened with a match against the Royal Marines, Chatham (yesterday), and there is a match on each day (excepting Sunday) until 12th August. The other fixtures will be against Tunbridge Wells, Barming, Whitstable and Tankerten, Frindsbury, Gravesend, Tonbridge, Hythe, and Cranbrook. The touring team is: E. J. Cooper (captain), W. Moore (Cheltenham), E. S. Volz, F. Marshall, D. T. Stevens, H. Steele-Smith (Selwyn College), N. B. Larby (O.B.M.), P. H. Thomas (Selwyn College), K. J. Petts (Welwyn), D. C. H. Wright (Southgate), W. M. E. White (the Army), R. Chatterton (Mill Hill School), V. A. Gubbins (Southgate), N. E. Gillham (Southgate), B. C. Barnwell (O.B.M.), K. C. Campin (Modern School), and D. R. Goodwin (Modern School). In 1938 nine matches were played, of which seven were won by the Martlets.

## Campin's Year

It will be interesting to see how the young Modern School medium-fast bowler, Campin, fares on the tour. He was outstanding in the School games and captured forty-five wickets—a record number—out of the seventy-six wickets taken. Of 171 overs he sent down forty-three were maidens, and the average cost of each wicket was only 7.8. His figures prove his consistency: v. King Edward School, seven for 51 (thirty-one overs); v. Royal Masonic, five for 37; v. Emanuel, four for 35; v. Wellingborough, five for 23; v. Oakham, five for 19; v. Reading, eight for 49; v. Forty Club, seven for 86; v. Northamptonshire Amateurs, four for 54. Total, forty-five for 354; average, 7.8.
