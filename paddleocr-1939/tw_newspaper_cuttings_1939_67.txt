# $ \underline{\text{Do YOU Agree That}} $— CLUB CRICKET Lacks Personalities?

Is club cricket as attractive to watch to-day as it was a decade ago?

I apologize for treading this well-worn path of controversy (writes "Cassio") but I hand on an interesting opinion—that of an ex-Herts batsman, who has forsaken the playing arena for the spectators' benches.

As keen and enthusiastic as ever for the welfare of the game, he is seriously perturbed. "Where are the personalities in the sides to attract the crowds?" he asks.

The reason, he submits, for the alleged falling off in interest amongst the public.

## Safety Measures

Blaming the cricket schools run by professionals, his words are interesting. He says:

“Most young players coached at these schools in London learn to adopt safety measures. Their style of play is built on the foundations of defence, and they argue, quite correctly, that runs will come if they stay at the wicket. This may be true of county cricket, but I am certain that it does not pay in club matches.”

"It is the same with the bowlers. They bowl to keep runs down, and if they begin to get hit, trundle balls down outside the off-stump.

“I am not claiming that we were better cricketers, but the game a decade or so ago was far more attractive. Of that I am certain.”

# STARRED SELECTIONS

DON YATES, who is the terror of every local bowler, hit his 44th century on Saturday. Since 1928, when he started his brilliant career with Watford Clarence, Yates has been a prolific scorer. With Oxhey Grange, Waterlows, Odhams and North Watford Conservatives, he has hit 1,000 runs (or more) each year since 1929. He has already reached this number this season. One summer on behalf of Waterlows his tally reached 2,000.

His average to date for this wet summer is 67.

What a great pity it was that Yates's talents were not recognised, and some effort made to give him an opportunity to develop in a higher class of cricket. It is obvious that Yates has exceptional ability, and would have made his mark in higher company had he been brought along on the right lines.

It seems a crying shame that such talent is wasted—it wouldn't happen in Australia.

The excellent battling of W. Townley, of Kings Langley, has been attracting a lot of attention. He is well worth a trial for the county. Less able players than he have often appeared.
