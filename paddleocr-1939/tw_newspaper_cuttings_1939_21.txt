# THE CHRONICLE FRIDAY AUGUST 11 1939

## CRICKET

## MINOR COUNTIES CRICKET

## BERKS LOSE BY AN INNINGS

## BUCKS BOWLER'S THIRTEEN WICKETS

CONTINUING their Minor Counties cricket programme at the Kensington Road ground, Reading, on Monday and Tuesday, Berkshire put up a sorry show against Buckinghamshire, who were last season's champions. Previous to coming to Reading the Bucks team had done nothing very great this season, having defeated Bedfordshire by two runs on the first innings, and losing by an innings to Hertfordshire. On this occasion Berkshire, failing both from the bowling and batting point of view, lost by an innings and fifteen runs.

Stokes, the Berkshire captain, lost the toss for the third consecutive time, and Bucks went on to bat on what proved to be a batsmen's wicket. Play was delayed on Monday morning owing to the condition of the pitch following heavy rain, and sawdust had to be liberally applied even then to make it playable. Berkshire secured two easy wickets, but this brought watchlife and Peters together to send the score along at a good pace. Peters, who was the top scorer of the match, was missed twice in one over from Bradley before he had reached double figures, first by Relf, who failed to reach a hard chance in the slips, and then by Caudle in the gulley. This pair sent up be hundred in an hour and a quarter, and it was not until they had put on 92 runs that it had made a brilliant catch in the slips to disclose of Ratcliffe. Walker joined Peters for his pair to take the score from 121 to 170, but hereafter wickets fell at fairly regular intervals, and the innings closed for 222. Some idea of the consistency of the Bucks batting may be found from the fact that their first eight batsmen all reached double figures.

Berkshire were left with three and a half hours in which to bat, but in that time they were literally skittled out by Bucks, and also lost four second inrings wickets after being forced to follow on. Bucks opened the bowling with Ratcliffe and Lund, the former having four men in the slips, whilst the latter had a leg trap of a similar number. Caudle, this year's Reading School captain, proved to be top scorer of the innings, whilst Owen Sutton came next with nineteen. Sutton, in fact, fully justified his selection by compiling the best aggregate on the Berkshire side. Wolton was the only other Berkshire player to reach double figures. Rutter, the Bucks fast bowler, seemed unplayable to many of the Berkshire men. At one period he had taken four wickets for seven runs, and his final analysis was six for 23. Berkshire were all out for 88, leaving 34 to get to avoid the innings defeat. Although they certainly did better in the second innings they did not succeed in reaching this figure.

Left with 65 minutes for their second inning: Monday Berkshire lost four wickets for 58 runs, and an hour's play on Tuesday sufficed to finish the game. Except for one over by Edwards, Batcock and Rutter bowled throughout this innings unchanged, Rutter securing even wickets for 55 runs. Some of the Berkshire players showed a tendency to play the ball with their pads instead of with the bat. Bayles was out in the second innings in this way, and Stokes was 1.b.w. in both innings. His second innings Stokes played the first ball he received with his pads, and there was a confident appeal against him. He was given not out on this occasion, but when he did almost exactly the same with the next ball the ample gave a decision against him. Sutton and Wolton made the best stand, putting on 19 for the fifth wicket. Relf scored about the quickest 21 of the match, and it was a catch a big hit from him which closed the match.

# MINOR COUNTIES CRICKET

## BERKSHIRE FLATTER BUT TO DECEIVE

## DEFEAT BY SUFFOLK BY NINE RUNS

Berkshire staged a splendid recovery in their Minor Counties match against Sulolk at Felixstowe on Wednesday. Batting first on an easy-paced wicket, they made a disastrous start, losing their first three wickets for 27 runs, but the later batsmen attacked the bowling and, thanks to their enterprise, the substantial total of 297 was reached. H. G. Hubble, with 12 fours as his best strokes, batted with admirable skill and imitate for his 88 runs, scored in 100 minutes. Daley, with 7 for 118, was Sulfolk's most successful bowler.

Suffolk, after a promising start, were always in difficulties, and Relf, taking half their wickets for 44 runs, brought about a collapse from which they never recovered.

Following on 168 behind, Suffolk scored three without loss before stumps were drawn. Berkshire fared badly on Thursday, when Suffolk ran up a big score in their second innings, despite some good bowling by Cawston. Their total of 281 left Berkshire with 114 to get for victory. Waugh, however, skittled Berkshire out, and the last wicket fell four minutes from time, leaving Suffolk the winners by nine runs.
