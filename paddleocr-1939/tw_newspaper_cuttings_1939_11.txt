# BEDFORDSHIRE TIMES: 5R1DAY 18 AUGUST 1939-4.

## CRICKET IN THE RAIN

## Martlets' Tour of Kent Nearly Ruined

The Bedford Martlets' annual cricket tour of Kent was this year nearly ruined by rain. It was not until the last two days that summer weather prevailed.

The matches against the Royal Marines at Chatham, K.C.M.H., Barming, and Tonbridge were abandoned without a ball being bowled. The first game at Linden Park, Tunbridge Wells, was played on a wet, but drying wicket, and the Martlets decided to bat after some hesitation. Having practice on concrete at their headquarters the Martlets could not settle down and they were out for 50. Heavy rain threatened to render further play impossible, but a fresh wicket was cut for Linden Park's innings. Off the first ball N. B. Larby made a catch off Campin (3 for 17) at short leg, and the home side lost three for 10 and four for 28. Rain began to fall again but Linden Park reached 53 for five.

## EASILY DISMISSED

Whitstable and Tankerton were easily dismissed for 81 (W. M. E. White two for 26, K. C. Campin three for 23, and K. J. Petts four for 14). R. Chatterton hit hard for 60, but after winning for the loss of only one wicket the Martlets were all out for 132.

At Frindsbury the touring side lost three for 28, but W. Moore (25) and B. C. Barnwell (45) retrieved the position. D. C. H. Wright (20) and W. M. E. White (36) hit hard. Sound batting took the total to 198, and then rain stopped play.

The Martlets, facing their strongest opponents on the County ground at Gravesend, were rather unfortunate to be without W. M. E. White, who was called to Lords to play for the Army against the Public Schools. Before lunch Gravesend took 95 minutes to score 69 runs off some very accurate bowling by Campin and F. Marshall, but in the end they made 246. Campin took five for 60 and K. J. Petts five for 68. Barnwell made a particularly fine catch in the deep. After tea the light became poor and the Martlets lost four for 27. W. Moore (52) and P. R. Thomas (34) made a good stand to save the side. With rain falling the game was given up as a draw, the score being 124 for seven.

At Tonbridge a misty rain swept the ground all the morning and with conditions failing to improve another match had to be abandoned.

## CAMPIN'S BOWLING

The following day was gloriously fine, but at Hythe overnight rain made play impossible before lunch. Hythe sent the Martlets in to bat. Four wickets were down for 17. P. R. Thomas (61) and E. S. Volz (36) put on nearly 100 for the next wicket, and the tail wagged sufficiently to take the score to 152. Hythe were left with 153 runs to make in 105 minutes. Some fine bowling by Campin on a quickly drying wicket banished their hopes. Seven wickets were down for 48, and then a stubborn stand brought the game within ten minutes of the close. A draw looked certain when D. C. H. Wright held a smart left-handed catch in the gully, and the last ball but one from Campin was a beauty and beat the batsman. He took seven for 36. W. M. E. White, who played for Hythe in this game, said that Campin's bowling was superior to any of the Public School bowling at Lords. Hythe, who lost a batsman through injury, were all out for 78.

The final game, against Mr. Evans's XI on the Cranbrook School ground, was a curious affair. Before lunch the home team lost eight for 47 against Marshall and Campin, but after the interval the School professional, H. G. Draper, hit a hard 38 and Cranbrook reached 110 (Campin seven for 41, Marshall three for 26). By patient batting W. Moore and Volz took the score to 54, but the absence of Thomas and Barnwell was keenly felt, and the tourists' total was only 74.

Two matches were won. two lost, one drawn, and four abandoned.

## HENLOW BATSMEN

## FAIL

## Bedford Rugby Player in Letchworth Stand

Henlow were beaten by 60 runs at Letchworth on Saturday. Their batsmen, with the exception of R. Lynn and F. Thorogood, failed dismally. Lynn played a grand innings, making 32, including one glorious hit for six. He is regaining his form after his long period of enforced absence. Alexander bowled brilliantly to capture five for eighteen and Grey returned three for thirty. Henlow started off well, Russell bowling Diss first ball, but that was the extent of their success until their total had been passed, Grey and Taylor adding 92 in fifty minutes for the second wicket. Grey, the Bedford Rugby Club full-back, batted brilliantly for his 51, including six fours and a six. Taylor was more subdued in making 28. Fenton (25) also batted well. A. R. Carlisle, the Henlow skipper, with his tantalizing slows, was their best bowler, taking six for 43. Russell took two for 26. Scores:

## BOWLERS' GOOD RETURNS

## Biggleswade Defeated by Aspley Guise

Aspley Guise completed the double against Biggleswade when they won by 46 runs at Biggleswade on Saturday.

The visitors were given a good start by King and Stone after Barnwell had left without scoring. Both players scored freely, the return to form of W. King being very welcome after rather a bad patch. He hit two sixes and three fours in his 31—and followed this up by stumping three opponents later in the game.

The accuracy of the Biggleswade bowling is shown by the fact that the wickets were hit seven times in Aspley's innings of 130. Giles took four for 16.

But for a fine not out innings of 40 by R. Griffiths, Biggleswade would have been routed by Blofeld and Bedford. The former at one time captured three wickets for one run and finished with four for 10. Bedford took four for 23.
