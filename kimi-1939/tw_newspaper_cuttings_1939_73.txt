Cricket Notes. By "Will Lowe"

A. KING'S 102
IN 88 MINUTES

Time Thwarts Hitchin
at Bedford

A brilliant century by Tony King,
of Hitchin, was the outstanding indi-
vidual feat in North Herts and South
Beds cricket circles on Saturday. His
score of 102 was compiled in eighty-
eight minutes, and it included sixteen
4's and nine 2's.
Opposing Bedford, on the latter's
ground, Hitchin had an early shock
when J. D. Waters was lbw to Kenney
for a duck with only one run on the
score-board. W. V. Stanley then helped
King to take the score to 132 before be-
ing caught, and when King left four
runs later he had made 102 out of the
136. Subsequently Sid Brown made 35
(which included six boundaries) and
Barker hit five four's in a useful contri-
bution of 26, so that Hitchin finally to-
talled 222.
D. D. Audley, who opened for Bedford
with R. Kenney, played a grand fighting
innings of 52 before being run out with
the score at 109 for four wickets, but
five more wickets fell while 15 runs
were being added, and Hitchin looked
like snatching a thrilling victory. How-
ever, they were thwarted by a last-wicket
stand by R. Hawkes and R. Ferriman,
and had to be content with a draw,
though they held the overwhelming lead
of 83 runs when stumps were drawn.

REVENGE FOR THREE COUNTIES
Visiting Leavesden without Eric Bir-
cumshaw on Wednesday last week,
Three Counties Hospital squared ac-
counts with their hosts, who had al-
ready won at Three Counties. After
dismissing Leavesden for 167, Three
Counties had lost six wickets for 60 and
eight for 102.
Then Pogmore joined Mayes, and
these two set about their job with
grim determination and eventually
won the game for their side.
Both played grand cricket, and de-
served every credit for a great victory.

HENLOW RECOVER.
Another good recovery, though not
quite so thrilling because it came in the
first innings of the match, was that of
Henlow against the Cardington Airmen.
After losing five wickets for 28 runs,
Henlow were saved by a sixth wicket
partnership of 51 by Jordan and R. V.
Lynn, and eventually made 123.
The Airmen had scored 52 by the time
their third wicket fell, but after that
they steadily lost ground and were out
for 92.
Russell had his best day for the
Henlow Club, taking seven wickets for
18 runs in 8.7 overs.
Henlow II also won their match against
Three Counties "A" at Arlesey, and
here again two players changed the
whole complexion of the game. Batting
most disappointingly, Henlow had seven
wickets down for 47 when R. Russell and
R. Leggatt came to the rescue, and they
were chiefly responsible for the total
reaching 93. Russell had five fours in
his 26 not out.
With a lowly total up against them,
Three Counties collapsed sensationally
against the bowling of Mayes, who took
the first seven wickets for 22 runs, and
the side were skittled out in under the
hour for 69. Of these, Bramall was re-
sponsible for no fewer than 47, carrying
his bat right through the innings. He
had a most fortunate existence, how-
ever, being missed six times.

CLARKSON AGAIN
Once again P. Clarkson has been the
saviour of the Stevenage side. At
Broxbourne on Saturday, he played a
fine innings of 56 not out and thus
helped his club to put together the re-
spectable total of 132.
Midway through their innings, Brox-
bourne appeared certain to pass this
score, but the tail failed to wag, and
they fell short by nine runs. L. Watson
took five of their wickets for 32 runs,
and G. Field 3 for 28.
Stevenage II. completed a "double"
for the club by an overwhelming victory
at home against Little Wymondley. The
villagers were put out for 88, C. Minnis
taking 6 for 20 in nine overs, and the
early Stevenage batsmen soon exceeded
this total, J. Allison making 58, and J.
Boorman and D. Walker 32 and 38 re-
spectively.

NARROW VICTORIES
Thanks to Dearden's innings of 51, a
sterling solo effort when the other bats-
men were coming and going all too
quickly, Knebworth Park totalled 120
against Hertford II at Hertford, after
being 42 for 5.
Dearden also bowled with success at
the start of the Hertford innings, and
he and Scott had dismissed eight men
for only 80 when Clifton and Ellis
made a stand which carried the home
side to a one-wicket win.
Another one-wicket victory was that
of Hitchin United at Graveley in a match
featured by big hitting
Seven 6's and forty 4's were recorded
during the day. For the United, Alf.
Wingate hit three 6's and six 4's in his
47, and Les Armitage seven 4's in his
43 not out, and W. Pettit (Grateley)
made seven 4's in his 44.
The United's victory was an exciting
one, being principally due to a last-
wicket stand by Armitage and B.
Bridges, the latter making an invalu-
able 13 not out. A. Mead, of the Hitchin
team, was the most successful bowler,
with 6 for 37.

KIMPTON'S POOR SEASON.
In their last home game, Kimpton
suffered a heavy defeat against Luton
Boys' Club, and from a results point
of view they have had an unusually
poor season. Bowling unchanged for
the visitors, Matthews and Jones ran
through the Kimpton team for the
meagre score of 45, and only Gerald
Gain, who hit a six, got double figures.
Slack fielding was partly responsible
for the Luton team making 80 for
five wickets, although two of their
players were run out in attempting to
steal short runs.

KING'S WALDEN WIN.
With eight second eleven players in
the side, King's Walden triumphed over
Barclays Bank by two wickets, after a
good fighting finish, the scores being 142
and 148 for eight wickets. For the win-
ners, J. Parkyn hit up 57, and H.
Maltby 35.
E. Harper captured seven wickets for
21 runs in helping Offley to beat Luton
Hoo by 95 to 62. For the losers, Bram-
ner also took seven wickets, at a cost
of 39 runs.

S. WRAY AGAIN IN FORM.
S. Wray, of Preston, was again in
form with the ball against Weston, who
were defeated on their own ground by
135 to 49, but the best all-round per-
formance of the game—and one of the
best of the day in the district—was that
of B. Lubbock, of Preston, who scored
a half-century before being run out, and
then took six Weston wickets for only
24 runs.

THE ICKNIELD WAY!
For a pleasant game of skittles, how-
ever, the match at Old Hale Way,
Hitchin, between Ransom's C.C. and the
Icknield C.C., takes the biscuit this
week.
Ransom's were out for 55, Massey
taking 5 for 28 and Darlow 5 for 12
and the Icknield lads were then dis-
posed of for a mere 25, J. Sworder
(who, incidentally, made top score
of 17 for his side) taking 6 for 6, and
J. Chappelle 2 for 10. Seven of the 11
players got ducks!
