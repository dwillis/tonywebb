22-Stone Recruit

A. W. Deadman, who played for
West Herts for the first time on
Monday and hit a fine 56, is
reputed to weigh 22 stones. If he
reproduces this form he will pro-
vide future West Herts opponents
with a weighty problem!

Glenister "Hat Trick"
—Jones's Six 6's

Abbots Langley
(5 wkts.) 262:... ..Mr. G. Aldridge's XII 130

Dismissing their opponents for 130, Abbots
Langley scored an easy victory on the Manor
Ground on Sunday, passing the visitors' total
for the loss of one wicket. Against the varied
Abbots Langley attack the visiting batsmen
could make little impression with the excep-
tion of E. Turner, who scored an entertain-
ing 50.

F. Glenister was the most successful
bowler in the home side, taking four wickets
for 19 runs, including the "hat trick."

Although Abbots Langley had an early shock
in the loss of Glenister before a run had been
scored, Edwards and Jones entertained the
spectators with some lively cricket, the
latters 83 including six sixes. Lambkin and
Simons also batted confidently and when
stumps were drawn 262 runs for five wickets
had been scored in just over two hours.

A. W. BRIERS'S TURN ON MONDAY

Abbots Langley Mr. G. Aldridge's XI
(5 wkts. dec.) 201 ...........(7 wkts.) 111

The return game with Mr. G. Aldridge's
XI played at Abbots Langley on Monday was
unfortunately interfered with by rain, and
play did not commence until 3 p.m. The
home batsmen were again in good form, parti-
cularly A. W. Briers, who played a very sound
innings of 81 not out. P. K. Briers and E.
W. Jones also showed good form in scoring
50 and 39 respectively.

Declaring at 201 for five wickets, Abbots
Langley left the visitors two hours for bat-
ting, and though they lost three wickets for
22 runs Hoddinott and Mott stopped the rot
and prevented the home side from repeating
the victory achieved the previous day. When
stumps were drawn the visitors had scored
111 for the loss of seven wickets. Score:
