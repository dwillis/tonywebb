SUFFOLK'S FINE RECORD

Some high scoring was witnessed at Cintra
Ground, lent by Messrs. Sutton and Sons, when
Berkshire entertained Suffolk for the first time
at Reading in the Minor Counties Championship. This was the m [unclear] county match to take
place at Cintra for a number of years. Suffolk
fielded a strong eleven, including seven players
who had had experience of first-class county
cricket, among them being Phil Mead, who
played for England and Hampshire.

Winning the toss, Berkshire went in to bat
on a very easy wicket. Runs came very rapidly
at first, but wickets also fell fairly regularly.
Stokes was clean bowled by a short-pitched
ball from Belle, whilst Wolton was smartly
stumped after going forward to a ball from the
same bowler. It was not until Timewell and
Crisfield came together that anything of a stand
was made. They carried the score to 89 before
Timewell put up a high catch in the outfield.
Crisfield, who had been missed at four, followed soon afterwards, having contributed a
very useful 40 to the score. Hubble started
well, but played on to his wicket when he
seemed to be getting set. The best batting display was given by Cawston, who in his 98 hit
five 6's and eight 4's. He punished Daley very
severely, hitting him for three sixes and a four
off consecutive balls. Once Cawston had been
disposed of, however, the Berkshire tail failed
to wag, and the last three wickets fell for the
addition of only five runs. Perkins had been
the most successful Suffolk bowler.

Suffolk were left with an hour to bat before
close of play, and they made full use of their
time, being only 81 runs behind the Berkshire
total with six wickets in hand. Porter compiled
a useful 63 of their total. Mead was out to a
brilliant catch by Wolton, who held a ball that
was going away from him over his head.

Berkshire bowlers were more successful on
Thursday morning, and they disposed of the
remaining Suffolk batsmen in a little over an
hour. Dedman once again was responsible for
the collapse, claiming four of the remaining
six wickets. His final analysis figures were four
for 62. Altogether, Relf captured five wickets
for 73 runs. The Suffolk total of 242 left Berkshire with a first innings lead of 18.

Berkshire started their second innings
disastrously, Stokes being stumped with only
two on the board, and Timewell following
nine runs later. Crisfield was the next victim,
and the Suffolk bowlers seemed to be definitely
on top. Until Hubble hit a four off Daley to
make the score 26, all of the runs had come in
singles. Hubble managed to stay in whilst two
other wickets fell, but he was bowled eventually
by a ball from Perkins, which broke back and
took his leg stump. Taylor indulged in some
hard hitting, but was given three lives early in
his innings, and was eventually bowled by
Daley. Relf was caught off the first ball he
received. The Berkshire innings closed for 83
runs, and had lasted for only an hour and
three-quarters. Perkins had the remarkable
bowling figures of six for 15, and altogether
had taken 12 wickets for 73 in the match.

With plenty of time in which to compile 102
runs for victory, Suffolk had obtained the
runs by the tea interval for the loss of only
two wickets. Suffolk, who are at the top of the
Minor Counties championship table, have now
played six matches, won four, and won one on

the first innings, there being no result in one
game.
