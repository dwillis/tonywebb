WESTERN DAILY PRESS: MONDAY 12 JUNE 1939 - 1

ANOTHER VICTORY

Old Bristolians Easily Defeat United Banks

SCHOOLMASTERS, who have become one of the strongest sides in local cricket, recorded another victory on Saturday, when they beat St. George at the County Ground by six wickets.

St. George batting first made a disastrous start, and lost six wickets for 32, but staged a fine recovery. Two of the young batsmen of the side, A. Poole and G. Green, scored 96 runs in a seventh wicket partnership, and the St. George total, due to their efforts, reached 136.

A peculiar feature of the innings was that every player was clean bowled. The bowlers who did the damage were F. Golledge, who took six for 38 runs, and G. Dewfall, who had the other four for 30 runs.

Schoolmasters hit off the necessary runs for the loss of four wickets, and A. R. Curtis was top scorer with 55.

Clifton were concerned in an interesting finish which was totally unexpected. At Henbury they were set only 122 runs to get to win by Trowbridge, and started in such a confident manner that at one stage of the innings the score was 90 runs for one wicket and an easy victory seemed a certainty. Then D. Ferris, a clever leg-break bowler, began to get to work, and wickets tumbled so that when the eighth wickets fell runs were still needed.

RUNS SECURED

Hastily the 10th man had to change back into his flannels and put on his pads and go unexpectedly out to bat. The runs were secured all right, thanks to some sound batting by P. C. Awdry, and Clifton won by two wickets.

K. G. Kinnersley and J. F. Robinson had given the Clifton Club a good start, each scoring a half-century. Clifton dismissed Trowbridge for 121, J. L. Taylor taking six wickets for 22 runs.

Cardiff brought a strong side to oppose Imperial at Knowle, but they gained little advantage over the home side and the match was drawn. In a big partnership for the fourth wicket, N. K. Contractor and F. M. Pullen scored 146 runs. Cardiff declared at 216 for four wickets. Contractor hit seven fours in his 89, and Pullen had 14 boundaries in his 86 not out.

Imperial made a poor start, losing three wickets for 42 runs, but then N. R. Poole (68) and A. W. Reed (26) effected a recovery, and put on 77 runs for the fourth wicket. At the close Imperial had made 164 for six wickets.

Despite a good innings of 58 by H. J. H. Alpass, the Bohemians could not avert defeat at Bridgwater, where they were beaten by 105 runs. The home side scored heavily, putting up a total of 237 for seven wickets, declared, to which the Bohemians replied with 132.

CONSISTENT BATTING

There was consistency in batting by the Old Bristolians at Tyndall's Park, where they scored 231 for four wickets, declared. The six batsmen who went in all scored over 20, with the best innings coming from P. Hazard and J. W. Box, who got into the seventies.

To that big score the United Banks made a spirited reply, but they were dismissed for 146, which left the Old Bristolians the winners by 145 runs.

There were many interesting features in the game at Stapleton between the local side and Bedminster, although the match was drawn with honours even. In the first place, Bedminster put together the good total of 215 runs, due mainly to a fine knock of 61 by W. Carr.

J. Howse, the Stapleton wicket-keeper, had the distinction of taking part in the dismissal of half the Bedminster side. He made two catches behind the wickets, and stumped three batsmen. Altogether he had a good match, for when Stapleton went in he scored 66. A similar number was hit by F. J. Weeks, and at the close the home side were 182 for eight wickets.

Knowle secured a creditable win at home on Saturday, when they beat Stinchcombe Stragglers by 88 runs. There was some good bowling by B. Williams, who took six wickets for 53 runs, but despite this, the two Knowle batsmen, W. G. Essex and E. Hathway were in brilliant form, and both recorded half-centuries. This built up the home side's score of 186 for nine wickets declared; Stinchcombe Stragglers were dismissed for 98.

THORNBURY TRIUMPH

Once again W. Harries and R. Mayo were in great form with the ball for Thornbury, and their fast attack put Flax Bourton out for 80 runs. Of that total A. E. Robinson was responsible for 51. Thornbury easily hit off the runs and went on to score 228 for nine wickets, R. Mayo being top scorer with 64 retired.

Westbury-on-Trym scored 166 when at home to Weston-super-Mare, but it was not big enough, and the visiting side won by four wickets. Making his first appearance, Norman Thompson was responsible for a particularly fine innings for Westbury, hitting a six and six fours in 51. It was G. Hunt who had as much to do with Weston winning this game, for he scored 74 out of his side's total of 173 for six.

One of the most convincing wins of the day was secured by Bristol Y.M.C.A. who at Golden Hill, beat the Optimists by seven wickets.

The Optimists put together a total of 135 despite the deadly bowling of R. Cave, who took six for 40 runs in eight overs. The Y.M.C.A. batsmen were in excellent form, with C. J. Axe 46, V. R. Davies 60, and H. Hooper 41.

Although they were given just over two hours to beat Lansdown's total of 114 Bath found runs hard to get, and in an exciting finish were only two runs behind. In the last over of the day Bath needed seven runs to win, but could only make three, Ruddick being caught off the last ball when he attempted to hit a six.
