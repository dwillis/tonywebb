A SPORTING FINISH

Old Bedfordians End
their Week

Old Bedfordian Week has come and
gone once again, and its enjoyable social
events and [unclear] encounters pass
into pleasant [unclear]. The Old Boys
beat the boys at cricket—an exciting
victory—were be[unclear] by the boys and by
the masters at tennis, and had their usual
Sunday golf match with the Bedfordshire
Club. There were [unclear] swimming and row-
ing contests with the School. On Sunday the
annual meeting of the Old Bedfordians
Club was held, and the Head Master gave
a garden party, and on Friday there was
an "At Home" followed by the Old Bed-
fordians' Ball, which is reported on an-
other page.
This season's Bedford School cricket
ended on a high note with the two-day,
twelve-a-side match against the Old Bed-
fordians. Although forced to follow on,
the School struck back to such effect in
their second innings that at 6.25 p.m. on
Saturday Doubleday, the captain, was in a
position to make a sporting declaration.
He left the Old Bedfordians with seventy-
seven to score in thirty-five minutes. The
Old Bedfordians promptly accepted the
challenge and sent out Sime and Oakley
with instructions to get a move on. And
Sime and Oakley did get a move on. They
got the runs with some minutes to spare!

AN INNINGS DEFEAT

Old Modernians Lose to
the School

The annual reunion of Old Bedford
Modernians was held at the Modern
School field on 27th July, when a large
number of Old Boys, parents, and scholars
attended. The full week-end pro-
gramme included a two-day cricket
match with the School, a swimming dis-
play, and tennis and swimming matches
against the School on Saturday. The re-
union tea was served under the auspices
of the Modern School Parents' Associa-
tion and the Old Bedford Modernians
Club.
The cricket match ended in a victory
for the School by an innings and six runs.
The game was notable for a fine not-out
century by the School captain, D. R.
Goodwin, who scored an attractive but
steady 145. Scores:—

CRICKET WEEK

A Letter from a Former
County Captain

A former captain of the Bedford County
Cricket Club, Mr. H. R. Orr, of Bedford,
has sent the following letter to the Sports
Editor in connexion with next week's
County cricket games:—

Sir,—The County Cricket Week begins
next Monday and the playing prospects
are definitely bright (weather permitting).
The Eleven made an excellent start this
season when they put up such a good
performance at Luton against Sir Julien
Cahn's strong team. The match was
drawn, the County leading on the first
innings—fine all-round cricket, good bat-
ting, and steady, good-length bowling,
backed up by excellent fielding, which,
perhaps, was the most pleasing feature
of the game. In these days of plumb
wickets it is essential, and only fair to the
hard-working bowlers, that special at-
tention should be paid, when selecting
a team, to the important department of
fielding.
The Eleven will be at full strength
next week, with R. de W. K. Winlaw
making his first appearance. He is a
first-class captain and his brilliant field-
ing is always an inspiration to his side.
There are several reasons for optimism
concerning the present and the future
of Bedfordshire cricket. To begin with
it is most gratifying to notice the much
keener interest Luton people are now
taking in the game, and they have several
players up to County form who have
proved acquisitions to the team.
There is also the important fact that
the men we are now playing are all
young, which surely augurs well for days
to come. When J. A. R. Oliver and G.
L. B. August were selected for the
County both were still at school, and
both made good at once. They are now
two of the most consistent players in the
side. Another boy, E. D. Markham, who
has been showing consistently good form
for the newly-formed Bedford Town Club,
is having a well deserved trial. If a
young player has the ability, plus the
right temperament, it is wise to give
him a chance with a view to the future.
The hon. secretary, Mr. F. Crompton,
continues his good work, and by his
untiring efforts has secured many new
subscribers during the past year.
In conclusion, I would like to add that
it gives great pleasure to notice that the
County Club, which was re-formed forty
years ago, is now in such a flourishing
condition. May the weather improve for
the Week and may bright cricket and
"bumper" gates be seen.

H. R. ORR.
22 Pemberton Avenue.
Bedford.
