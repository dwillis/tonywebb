THE CHRONICLE FRIDAY AUGUST 11 1939

CRICKET

MINOR COUNTIES
CRICKET

BERKS LOSE BY AN
INNINGS

BUCKS BOWLER'S THIRTEEN
WICKETS

CONTINUING their Minor Counties
cricket programme at the Kensington
Road ground, Reading, on Monday and
Tuesday, Berkshire put up a sorry show against
Buckinghamshire, who were last season's cham-
pions. Previous to coming to Reading the
Bucks team had done nothing very great this
season, having defeated Bedfordshire by two
runs on the first innings, and losing by an
innings to Hertfordshire. On this occasion
Berkshire, failing both from the bowling and
batting point of view, lost by an innings and
fifteen runs.
Stokes, the Berkshire captain, lost the toss
for the third consecutive time, and Bucks went
in to bat on what proved to be a batsman's
wicket. Play was delayed on Monday morn-
ing owing to the condition of the pitch follow-
ing heavy rain, and sawdust had to be liberally
applied even then to make it playable. Berk-
shire secured two easy wickets, but this brought
Ratcliffe and Peters together to send the score
along at a good pace. Peters, who was the
top scorer of the match, was missed twice in
one over from Bradley before he had reached
double figures, first by Relf, who failed to
reach a hard chance in the slips, and then
by Caudle in the gulley. This pair sent up
the hundred in an hour and a quarter, and it
was not until they had put on 92 runs that
Relf made a brilliant catch in the slips to dis-
pose of Ratcliffe. Walker joined Peters for
his pair to take the score from 121 to 170, but
thereafter wickets fell at fairly regular intervals,
and the innings closed for 222. Some idea of
the consistency of the Bucks batting may be
gained from the fact that their first eight bats-
men all reached double figures.
Berkshire were left with three and a half
hours in which to bat, but in that time they
were literally skittled out by Bucks, and also
lost four second innings wickets after being
forced to follow on. Bucks opened the bowl-
ing with Ratcliffe and Lund, the former having
four men in the slips, whilst the latter had a
leg trap of a similar number. Caudle, this
year's Reading School captain, proved to be
top scorer of the innings, whilst Owen Sutton
came next with nineteen. Sutton, in fact, fully
justified his selection by compiling the best
aggregate on the Berkshire side. Walton was
the only other Berkshire player to reach
double figures. Rutter, the Bucks fast bowler,
seemed unplayable to many of the Berkshire
men. At one period he had taken four wickets
for seven runs, and his final analysis was six
for 23. Berkshire were all out for 88, leaving
134 to get to avoid the innings defeat. Al-
though they certainly did better in the second
innings they did not succeed in reaching this
figure.
Left with 65 minutes for their second inn-
ing, on Monday Berkshire lost four wickets for 58
runs, and an hour's play on Tuesday sufficed
to finish the game. Except for one over by
Edwards, Battcock and Rutter bowled through-
out this innings unchanged, Rutter securing
seven wickets for 55 runs. Some of the Berk-
shire players showed a tendency to play the
ball with their pads instead of with the bat.
Bayles was out in the second innings in this
way, and Stokes was l.b.w. in both innings.
In his second innings Stokes played the first
ball he received with his pads, and there was
a confident appeal against him. He was given
not out on this occasion, but when he did al-
most exactly the same with the next ball the
umpire gave a decision against him. Sutton
and Walton made the best stand, putting on
49 for the fifth wicket. Relf scored about the
quickest 21 of the match, and it was a catch
off a big hit from him which closed the match.

MINOR COUNTIES
CRICKET

BERKSHIRE FLATTER BUT
TO DECEIVE

DEFEAT BY SUFFOLK BY
NINE RUNS

Berkshire staged a splendid recovery in their
Minor Counties match against Suffolk at Felix-
stowe on Wednesday. Batting first on an easy-
paced wicket, they made a disastrous start,
losing their first three wickets for 27 runs, but
the later batsmen attacked the bowling and,
thanks to their enterprise, the substantial total
of 297 was reached. H. G. Hubble, with 12
fours as his best strokes, batted with admirable
skill and initia[unclear]ve for his 88 runs, scored in
100 minute[unclear]. Daley, with 7 for 118, was
Suffolk's most successful bowler.
Suffolk, after a promising start, were always
in difficulties, and Relf, taking half their
wickets for 44 runs, brought about a collapse
from which they never recovered.
Following on 168 behind, Suffolk scored
three without loss before stumps were drawn.
Berkshire fared badly on Thursday, when
Suffolk ran up a big score in their second
innings, despite some good bowling by Cawston.
Their total of 281 left Berkshire with 114 to get
for victory. Waugh, however, skittled Berk-
shire out, and the last wicket fell four minutes
from time, leaving Suffolk the winners by nine
runs.
